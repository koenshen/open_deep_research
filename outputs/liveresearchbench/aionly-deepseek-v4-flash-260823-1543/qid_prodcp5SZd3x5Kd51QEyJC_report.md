# Premium Wireless Over-Ear Headphones Compared: Bose QC45, Bose QC Ultra, Sony WH-1000XM5, Apple AirPods Max & Sennheiser Momentum 4

**US Market Assessment — August 24, 2026**

---

## Executive Summary

These five headphones represent successive generations of the premium wireless ANC market. As of August 2026, only one (Sennheiser Momentum 4) remains a current flagship at its original price tier; the Bose QuietComfort 45 is discontinued, the Bose QuietComfort Ultra (1st Gen) has been superseded by the Ultra 2nd Gen, the Sony WH-1000XM5 has been superseded by the WH-1000XM6, and the Apple AirPods Max is in its second generation (H2 chip, released April 2026). All five remain widely available, heavily discounted, and frequently compared.

The core trade-off across the group: **the best ANC performers (AirPods Max, QC Ultra, XM5) generally receive more criticism for stock tuning and technicalities, while the Sennheiser Momentum 4 — the weakest ANC of the five — is consistently praised as the best-sounding out of the box and has dramatically better battery life (60h vs. 20h)** [34][35].

## A. Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) Performance

**Important data-availability note:** RTINGS.com's raw frequency-band dB isolation tables for most of these models are locked behind a paid subscription. Publicly verifiable RTINGS-derived figures exist for the QC Ultra (overall **−27.00 dB**) and XM5 (overall **−25.81 dB**) via third-party aggregation, and for the AirPods Max bass band (via coverage of firmware 4E71) [16][26]. All other band-level data is flagged as unavailable or drawn from independent reviewers' measurements.

### Bose QuietComfort 45

- **Overall effectiveness:** SoundGuys describes the QC45's ANC as "among the best in class" and "particularly good at attenuating mids and highs," reducing airplane engine hum "to one-eighth its loudness" [2]. CNET's David Carnoy (2021) said it may have been "the best out there right now," crediting "a sleek new electronics package that improves noise cancelling — particularly in mid-range frequencies (voices) found in commuter trains, busy offices, and cafes" [5].
- **Technology:** Bose's noise cancellation combines passive isolation with active cancellation: "microphones detect ambient noise and digital processing generates an opposing signal," using "advanced microphones and proprietary digital processing for real-time adaptation." The QC45 also received a 2024 firmware update (v4.0.4) that added custom ANC modes adjustable in the Bose Music app [2][6].
- **Modes:** Quiet (max ANC) and Aware (passthrough) only — **no true ANC-off mode** [2].
- **Limitations:** SoundGuys found the Sony WH-1000XM5 "consistently blocks frequencies above 1 kHz better" than the QC45 [19]. RecordingNOW (2026) notes the QC45's ANC "has been surpassed by newer models like the Bose QuietComfort (2023), QC Ultra series, Sony WH-1000XM6, and AirPods Max 2" [3].
- **Measurements:** No public RTINGS band-level dB figures; data unavailable without subscription [1].

### Bose QuietComfort Ultra

- **Overall effectiveness:** RTINGS' video review calls the QC Ultra's ANC "exceptional... blocking a wide range of sounds (tested with pink noise, airplane, busy street, office scenarios)" [8]. SoundGuys measured that bus engines, train screeches, and airplane whines have their loudness reduced by "about 87%" [10]. Frieve Audio's aggregation of RTINGS data reports an overall noise-isolation figure of **−27.00 dB** [16].
- **Technology:** The Ultra introduced Bose **CustomTune**, which "automatically adjusts sound and noise cancelling performance based on the user's unique hearing characteristics and environment" — it plays a calibration stimulus on power-up to "snapshot" the user's ear canal response [8][14][15]. Wind Block mode mutes external ANC microphones to minimize wind noise [8]. The Ultra has 10 microphones (5 per earcup) [12].
- **Modes:** Quiet, Aware (with ActiveSense adaptive noise cancelling for loud noises), Immersion (spatial audio with full ANC), and Cinema; plus custom modes [11][50].
- **Measurements:** No public RTINGS band-level (bass/mid/treble) split; paywalled [1][8].

### Sony WH-1000XM5

- **Overall effectiveness:** HeadphonesAddict measured an average noise reduction of **29.48 dBA** with ANC on, and **24.02 dBA** passive isolation — "among the most effective tested." MKBHD praised the XM5 for "still crushing low-end noises (airplane engines, road noise) but now better cancelling higher frequencies including appliances, whinier sounds, and human voices" [20][23]. Frieve's RTINGS aggregation reports overall isolation of **−25.81 dB** [16].
- **Technology:** Sony pairs the "HD Noise Cancelling Processor QN1" with the "Integrated Processor V1" — combining the noise-cancelling processor and Bluetooth System-on-Chip on a single die for "highest noise-cancelling performance with lower power consumption." The **Dual Noise Sensor** approach uses four microphones on each earcup for "dramatic reduction in high-frequency noise," plus an inward-facing mic and an **Auto NC Optimizer** that adapts to wearing conditions and atmospheric pressure [17][22].
- **Modes:** Ambient Sound Control (adjustable), Speak-to-Chat, Adaptive Sound Control based on location/activity, and palm-on-ear-cup transparency gesture [17][23].
- **Measurements:** No public RTINGS band-level split; HeadphonesAddict notes the XM5's attenuation is "excellent and consistent across the spectrum, though Apple AirPods Max reduce more upper bass/lower midrange" [20].

### Apple AirPods Max

- **Overall effectiveness:** SoundGuys calls the AirPods Max ANC "best-in-class," comparable to the Sony WH-1000XM6 and "slightly outperforming Bose QuietComfort Ultra (2% better gross total attenuation)" — roughly **88% noise reduction** across test samples [25][51]. RecordingNOW's AirPods Max 2 review (April 2026) verified the 2nd Gen's ANC as "best-in-class... Ranks slightly ahead of Sony XM6" [29].
- **RTINGS firmware controversy:** RTINGS measured the 1st Gen's Isolation score dropping from **9.0 to 8.3** after firmware 4E71. Bass isolation fell from **−19.92 dB to −15.53 dB**; in the high-bass zone specifically, noise reduction dropped "from nearly 30 decibels to just 10 decibels." Mid and treble isolation changed only slightly, and RTINGS noted the AirPods Max "still excel at canceling ambient chatter and high-pitched sounds like air conditioning units" and "remain among the best ANC over-ear headphones on the market" [26].
- **Technology:** Active Noise Cancellation uses outward-facing and inward-facing microphones with counter-signal (anti-noise) generation. The 1st Gen uses Apple's H1 chip; the AirPods Max 2 (March 2026) uses the **H2 chip** with "up to 1.5× more active noise cancellation" via "a new computational audio algorithm that detects and counters external sound," plus Adaptive Audio, Loud Sound Reduction, and enhanced Transparency [30][31][32].
- **Modes:** 1st Gen: ANC / Transparency only. 2nd Gen adds Adaptive Audio, Conversation Awareness, Personalized Volume, and Loud Sound Reduction [30][31].
- **Caveat:** ANC "can't be disabled without an iOS device" — there is no ANC-off on the 1st Gen from non-Apple devices [25].

### Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless

- **Overall effectiveness:** The weakest ANC of the five. SoundGuys measured roughly **76% noise reduction** — "adequate for subway and flights but noticeably weaker than competitors like Apple AirPods Max (88% reduction)" [51]. Audio Review Blog reports the M4 is "measurably behind Sony and Bose, especially in the 100 Hz–1000 Hz mid-range (5–10 dB difference), reducing low-frequency noise ~75% (15–20 dB)" [37].
- **Technology:** "Hybrid Adaptive Active Noise Cancellation" with a 4-microphone system and Transparency Mode [33]. The Smart Control app adds **Sound Zones** — geo-tagged ANC/transparency profiles (up to 20) [34].
- **Modes:** Adaptive ANC (auto-adjusting), Transparency, Sound Zones, Smart Pause; wind-noise reduction settings in-app [33][34].
- **Measurements:** No public RTINGS band-level split; paywalled [39]. SoundGuys rates ANC 7.6/10 with isolation 4.8/10 [34].

### ANC Cross-Comparison by Frequency Band

- **Low-frequency rumble (airplane/road noise):** The QC45 reduces airplane engine hum to one-eighth loudness [2]; Sony XM5 "crushes" low-end noises [23]. The AirPods Max was historically the strongest around 100 Hz per RTINGS graphs, but firmware 4E71 cut that band's isolation from −19.92 dB to −15.53 dB [22][26]. Unverified third-party data claims the XM5 reduces broadband office noise by 9.2 dB more than the AirPods Max in the 100–500 Hz speech band (XM5 −32.4 dB vs. APM −23.2 dB); **this figure could not be verified against RTINGS or SoundGuys primary sources and should be treated with caution** (flagged as unverifiable).
- **Mid-frequency (voices, office chatter):** Sony XM5 is the consensus leader — "notably cancel[ing] mid-range frequencies better" than predecessors, with excellent voice cancellation [20][22][23]. The QC45 was praised at launch specifically for mid-frequency voice reduction [5]. The Momentum 4 is weakest here [37].
- **High-frequency (sirens, hums, whines):** Sony XM5's four-mics-per-earcup design targets high-frequency noise "dramatically" [17]; it "eliminates high-pitched whistles the XM4s let through" [22]. The AirPods Max still "excel at canceling... high-pitched sounds" post-firmware [26]. QC45 remains "particularly good at attenuating... highs" [2].

## B. Sound Quality and Audio Accuracy

### Bose QuietComfort 45

- **Signature:** RTINGS publicly describes the QC45 as **"Bass emphasized (3 dB), Treble slightly emphasized (1 dB), V-Shaped"** [1]. SoundGuys is blunt: "Sound quality isn't all that great, even if the ANC is among the best in class" — the default tuning has "overemphasized high-end," and the recommended in-app EQ is **−5 bass, 0 mid, −10 treble** [2].
- **RecordingNOW's long-term verdict (2026):** "The worst offender is the piercing treble, specifically around the 6–8 kHz region." Suggested EQ: Bass −1, Mid +2, Treble −6. The soundstage is "surprisingly large — about equal to the Sennheiser Momentum 4, bigger than Sony XM4/XM5" — but resolution and detail in busy passages are lacking [3].
- **Codecs:** SBC and AAC only; no aptX/LDAC/LC3. Bluetooth 5.1 with multipoint [2][4].
- **Driver:** ~40 mm (per PCMag's Bose comparison and third-party replacement listings; **not officially confirmed by Bose** — flagged as unofficial) [4].
- **Personalization:** A 3-band EQ (bass/mid/treble) was added via firmware; no spatial audio; Bose Active EQ referenced in discussions of the QC line [2][4][21].

### Bose QuietComfort Ultra

- **Signature:** RTINGS describes the Ultra as **"Bass slightly emphasized (2 dB), Treble balanced (−1 dB), Warm"** [1]. SoundGuys' 2nd Gen review describes the tuning as "dark," with "dull highs and muddy low mids" that are hard to fully correct with the 3-band EQ [11]. RecordingNOW found the stock sound "too bass-heavy and muddy" but, with EQ, "audiophile-level quality," and claims it "outperforms AirPods Max in resolution, soundstage, and separation" [12]. Headphones.com's reviewer, after reducing bass/treble by ~5 dB, found it "arguably better than the Sennheiser HDB 630" in midrange linearity [14].
- **Codecs:** aptX Adaptive (Qualcomm Snapdragon Sound certified), AAC, SBC. Bluetooth 5.3 (1st Gen) / 5.4 (2nd Gen). No LDAC. The 2nd Gen supports aptX Lossless on newer Snapdragon devices [11][15][50].
- **Driver:** 35 mm (confirmed by TechGearLab and PCMag) [13].
- **Personalization:** CustomTune automatic ear-response EQ, Bose Immersive Audio (spatial audio with head tracking), Cinema Mode on 2nd Gen, ActiveSense transparency, up to 10 favorite listening modes [11][14][50].

### Sony WH-1000XM5

- **Signature:** SoundGuys describes the XM5 as remaining "more V-shaped with aggressive bass and scooped mids" versus the newer XM6, which "fixed the XM5's harsh treble peaks (especially around 3 kHz)" [56]. RecordingNOW calls the stock profile "muffled" and "mediocre" without EQ; with EQ it is "fun, punchy, energetic" [21]. Audio engineer Brandon Lee ("This is Tech Today") praised the XM5's midrange as the best of the group he tested (vs. XM4, AirPods Max, QC45), noting Sony's tuning leans dark and benefits from EQ [22].
- **Codecs:** LDAC, AAC, SBC; no aptX. Bluetooth 5.2. Frequency response 4 Hz–40 kHz (wired/JEITA); 20 Hz–40 kHz with LDAC 96 kHz/990 kbps [17][18][19].
- **Driver:** 30 mm carbon-fiber composite dome (down from 40 mm on XM4) — Sony claims "light weight, high rigidity, and improved high-frequency sound clarity" [17][18].
- **Personalization:** 360 Reality Audio, DSEE Extreme (AI upscaling), Sony Sound Connect app with 5-band EQ plus Clear Bass (400 Hz, 1 k, 2.5 k, 6.3 k, 16 k bands), Adaptive Sound Control [17][21][22].

### Apple AirPods Max

- **Signature:** Crinacle describes a "downsloping response with sub-bass emphasis" trending toward U-shaped — bass is "very well-controlled and focused... resulting in good separation between basslines and the rest of the melodic mix," though upper mids are "slightly tame" and treble can edge into "sharp" territory for sensitive listeners [27]. Headphones.com notes the sub-bass is "the strongest part of the sound," with "subdued" upper mids and "shimmery but uneven" treble; detail retrieval "average even for wireless ANC headphones" [28]. RecordingNOW's AirPods Max 2 verdict: "neutral bright," clean, detailed, tight bass, forward vocals — but "can sound thin and sterile at times; lacks warmth and musicality"; roughly 90–95% similar to the 1st Gen [29].
- **Codecs:** AAC and SBC only; no aptX/LDAC. Bluetooth 5.0 (1st Gen) / 5.3 (2nd Gen). Lossless 24-bit/48 kHz audio is available only over USB-C on the 2024+ and 2nd Gen models [29][48][49].
- **Driver:** 40 mm Apple-designed dynamic drivers with dual-neodymium ring magnet motor; Apple claims **<1% THD even at maximum volume** (marketing claim) [27][48].
- **Personalization:** Spatial Audio with dynamic head tracking, Personalized Spatial Audio, Adaptive EQ (computational, based on ear-cushion seal), Headphone Accommodations in iOS. The 2nd Gen adds a dedicated 3-band EQ in iOS 27 [28][29][31][48].
- **Crinacle's overall technical grade:** C+ for resolution/transients, with soundstage "above average for closed-back" and Spatial Audio "impressive only with 5.1/7.1/Dolby Atmos content" — overall grade B− [27].

### Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless

- **Signature:** SoundGuys finds the M4's frequency response "very close to SoundGuys' preference curve" with "slight bass boost but not overemphasized" [34]. Headphones.com describes a "soft V-shape" with sub-bass elevated 4–5 dB over the Harman target, a dipped upper midrange, and a "noticeable 8 kHz peak that causes some sibilance" — yet the M4 is "the reviewer's favorite among current ANC headphones" for sound quality, "outperforming Bose 700, QC45, WH-1000XM5, and AirPods Max" [35]. RecordingNOW notes the stock bass "can sound boxy/muddy" without EQ, but with its recommended EQ the M4 is "on par with the AirPods Max 2" [36].
- **Codecs:** SBC, AAC, aptX, aptX HD, aptX Adaptive (Qualcomm-certified); no LDAC. Bluetooth 5.2 Class 1 [33][34].
- **THD:** Sennheiser's spec is **<0.3%** THD; frequency response 6 Hz–22 kHz; 106 dB SPL; impedance 470 Ω (active) / 60 Ω (passive) [33][37].
- **Driver:** 42 mm / 300 Ω dynamic transducer system [33][37].
- **Personalization:** Smart Control app with 5-band parametric EQ, 8 presets, **Sound Check** (hearing-test-based personalization co-developed with Fraunhofer Institute), and **Sound Zones** (geo-tagged settings). No head-tracked spatial audio [34][35][36].
- **Wired performance:** Best over Bluetooth (aptX Adaptive); passive wired use sounds "rather hollow" per audioreviews.org [37]. RecordingNOW says aptX Adaptive over USB-C is excellent [36].

### Sound Quality Cross-Comparison

- **Rankings from reviewers who compared several of these directly:** Headphones.com ranked the Momentum 4 above the Bose 700, QC45, XM5, and AirPods Max in sound quality [35]. EL JEFE REVIEWS ranked M4 > XM5 > AirPods Max [36]. RecordingNOW ranked pure sound quality XM5 below the QC Ultra, Momentum 4, and AirPods Max [12][21][29].
- **RTINGS numerical accuracy scores (Bass/Mid/Treble/Peak, THD at 90/100 dB) are paywalled for all five models** and therefore unavailable/unverifiable in this report [1][24][39]. The only publicly available RTINGS sound descriptor is the QC45 vs. QC Ultra comparison page [1].
- **THD data availability:** only Apple's <1% claim (AirPods Max) and Sennheiser's <0.3% spec (Momentum 4) are publicly verifiable [27][33][37]; no verified THD figures for QC45, QC Ultra, or XM5 at 90/100 dB were located.

## C. Comfort and Build Materials

### Bose QuietComfort 45

- **Materials:** Bose officially cites "plush synthetic leather on earcups and headband, impact-resistant nylon and custom cast-metal hinges for durability" [6]. The Guardian notes the "smoother vegan leather headband, improved ear cushions, and a more minimalist look" versus the QC35 [40].
- **Weight: 235.4–240 g** — the lightest of the five (SoundGuys: 240 g; RecordingNOW measured 235.4 g, "the lightest headphone in its class tested") [2][3].
- **Comfort:** Bose emphasizes "minimal clamping force" [6]. RecordingNOW scored comfort 9.5/10: "extremely comfortable for long wear" [3]. CNET notes the headband underside is smooth and the redesigned ear pads are a clear upgrade over the QC35 [5].
- **Durability:** "Very solid with metal underneath the plastic; folds neatly with a compact hardshell case" [3]. No widespread hinge-failure reports in the reviewed sources.

### Bose QuietComfort Ultra

- **Materials:** Protein leather ear cushions, plush foam padding, refined metal elements, "more premium plush materials" than the standard QuietComfort with a "softer headband and earpads" [12][13]. The 2nd Gen's build is "essentially identical" to the 1st Gen [11].
- **Weight: 250–254.4 g** [9][12][41].
- **Comfort:** The Guardian: "easily the most comfortable set of headphones I've had the pleasure to wear, stealing the crown from Sennheiser's Momentum 4" [41]. SoundGuys, after two years of near-daily use, reports "soft glasses-friendly padding that hasn't deflated, angled drivers don't press against ears, and can be worn all day without fatigue" [11]. RecordingNOW scores comfort 10/10, with the caveat that materials feel "mediocre for the price point" [12].
- **Durability:** Folds flat with a slim hard case; battery "generally repairable with some replacement parts available" but not user-replaceable [41].

### Sony WH-1000XM5

- **Materials:** "Newly developed 'Soft fit leather'" headband with a stepless slider; ABS plastic using recycled car parts; vegan leatherette [17][19]. Sony claims "no plastic in the WH-1000XM5 packaging" [17].
- **Weight: ~250 g** (official Sony US: 8.82 oz; RecordingNOW measured 245.9 g, "one of the lightest flagship headphones") [18][21].
- **Comfort:** RecordingNOW scores 9.5/10 with a "minor gripe: shallow earcups cause ears to press against the fabric" [21]. SoundGuys: "good comfort with deep/wide ear pads accommodating larger ears," but warns **"fit is critical — glasses or poor fit can significantly degrade ANC"** [19]. MKBHD called them "the most comfortable pair of noise canceling headphones I've ever tried" [23].
- **Durability concern:** RecordingNOW flags "significant drawback — the plastic hinges feel 'frail' for a $400 headphone, with widespread broken-hinge reports on eBay and Reddit" [21]. The XM5 also doesn't fully collapse (unlike the XM4), making the hard case larger [21].

### Apple AirPods Max

- **Materials:** Apple's official specs: "breathable knit mesh canopy" headband, "stainless steel frame," "anodized aluminum" ear cups, and "acoustically engineered memory foam ear cushions" with magnetic attachment [30][48]. The USB-C model uses 90%+ post-consumer recycled polyester in the canopy and cushion yarn [49].
- **Weight: 384.8–386.2 g** — the heaviest of the five by a wide margin (roughly 140 g heavier than the XM5/QC Ultra) [48][49].
- **Comfort:** The most polarizing of the group. An Apple Support Communities thread (Dec 2020, dozens of users) documents "high earpiece clamping pressure" and "the outer headband bars digging into the head despite full extension," with headaches within 15–30 minutes and ear/temple pain — some users aggravated by glasses; many returned the product [55]. Stephen Robles (2nd Gen review): "The clamping force becomes noticeable after 45 minutes to 2 hours of wear," though the ear cups are more comfortable than Sony's, with less sweating [42-cited in findings]. Comfort varies significantly by head size [42-cited in findings].
- **Durability concerns:** A well-documented headband mesh sagging issue. SoundGuys (Aug 2026): a 5-year-old pair developed "a completely saggy mesh headband, causing the 384.8-gram headphones' stainless steel frame to rest painfully on his head"; Apple charges ~$300 for replacement and classifies it as cosmetic wear, so warranty/AppleCare+ won't cover it. The AirPods Max 2 "has the same headband flaw" [54]. Wikipedia also documents reports of condensation buildup near the drivers under the removable ear cups [53].

### Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless

- **Materials:** "Premium comfort with padded headband, deep cushioned earpads, and low-friction hinge mechanism" [33]. TechGadgetsCanada: "Lightweight polycarbonate construction with soft, replaceable earpads" — the design is "simple, basic, and not going to raise any eyebrows" [51-cited in findings]. TechGearLab notes "high-quality materials with premium cloth texture on case and headphones" [35-cited in findings].
- **Weight: 292.9–295 g** [35][36].
- **Comfort:** TechGearLab scored 9.5/10 — "testers wore them for 10 hours straight without issue" [35]. RecordingNOW's 3-year review: comfort 8.5/10 — "originally had strong clamping force... after 3 years, clamping force has relaxed significantly. Large ear cup diameter is appreciated, though ears slightly touch the fabric" [36]. Reddit users have mixed reports — some find the clamp uncomfortable [52-cited-in-findings].
- **Durability:** "Build quality materials are somewhat cheap/plasticky, comparable to Sony XM6 but less premium than AirPods Max 2. Minimal wear after 3 years — only slight creases on ear pads; headband and exterior show virtually no wear" [36]. Earpads are user-replaceable [33].

### Comfort and Build Cross-Comparison

- **Lightest:** QC45 (235.4 g) → XM5 (245.9 g) → QC Ultra (250 g) → Momentum 4 (292.9 g) → AirPods Max (384.8 g) [3][18][9][36][48].
- **Best all-day comfort consensus:** Bose QC Ultra (Guardian "comfort crown") and QC45; both are explicitly noted as glasses-friendly [3][11][41].
- **Most premium materials:** AirPods Max (aluminum/stainless steel/mesh) — but at a 385 g weight and documented comfort/mesh-sag issues [48][54][55].
- **Most fragile reputation:** Sony XM5's plastic hinges [21].
- **Best long-term durability among the group:** RecordingNOW's 3-year Momentum 4 assessment notes minimal wear despite plasticky materials [36].

## D. Battery Life and Charging

### Claimed vs. Measured Battery Life

| Model | Claimed (ANC on) | Claimed (ANC off) | Measured (SoundGuys) | Quick Charge | Full Charge | Port |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bose QC45 | 22–24 h [6][7] | No ANC-off mode [2] | 24 h 49 m [2] | 15 min = 3 h [2][7] | ~2–2.5 h [4][7] | USB-C [2] |
| Bose QC Ultra | 24 h (ANC); 18 h (Immersive Audio) [41] | No ANC-off mode; Aware mode only [10] | 27 h 37 m [9] | 15 min = 3 h (2nd Gen spec) [50] | ~2–3 h [41] | USB-C (charge only on 1st Gen) [9][10] |
| Sony XM5 | 30 h [18] | 40 h [18] | 31 h 53 m (ANC on); 53 h 6 m (ANC off) [19] | 3 min = 3 h [18][19] | ~3.5 h [18] | USB-C (charge only) [19] |
| AirPods Max (all gens) | 20 h [30][48] | 20 h (no ANC-off control on 1st Gen from non-Apple devices) [25] | ~21 h [25] | 5 min = 1.5 h [48][49] | ~2 h [49] | Lightning (2020) / USB-C (2024+); no 3.5 mm [48][49] |
| Sennheiser Momentum 4 | 60 h [33] | 60 h stated with ANC/transparency on; no separate off figure [33] | 56 h 21 m [34] | 10 min = 6 h; 5 min = 4 h [33] | ~2 h (700 mAh) [33] | USB-C (also USB digital audio) [33] |

**Key takeaways:**

- The **Momentum 4** is the endurance champion by a wide margin — SoundGuys measured 56 h 21 m, more than double the XM5's measured 31 h 53 m and nearly triple the AirPods Max's ~21–22 h [19][25][34].
- The **AirPods Max** has the shortest battery life of the group at 20 h — roughly half the Sony XM5's ANC-on figure, and one-third of the Momentum 4's [25][30][48]. It also has no power button; the Smart Case puts it into an ultra-low-power state.
- The **QC45** has no true ANC-off mode, so Bose does not advertise an ANC-off battery figure [2]. Sony is the only model of the five with a substantial ANC-off battery benefit (40 h claimed / 53 h measured) [18][19].
- **Fast-charging comparison:** Sony's 3-min = 3 h is the fastest top-up; Sennheiser's 10-min = 6 h delivers the most energy per minute; Apple's 5-min = 1.5 h is the slowest of the group [18][33][48].

**Wired playback caveats:**
- QC45 and QC Ultra support analog input via included 2.5 mm-to-3.5 mm cable; the QC Ultra's USB-C port cannot carry audio on the 1st Gen (USB-C audio was added on the 2nd Gen) [9][10][50].
- XM5: USB-C is charging only; 3.5 mm analog cable included [19].
- AirPods Max: no 3.5 mm jack at all; USB-C lossless audio (24-bit/48 kHz) is available on 2024+ models and the 2nd Gen [29][49].
- Momentum 4: USB-C supports hi-res wired digital audio, plus 2.5 mm-to-3.5 mm analog cable [33].

## Conclusion

As of August 2026, each of these five headphones occupies a distinct niche:

- **Bose QuietComfort 45** — the lightest and most comfortable budget pick with excellent (though dated) ANC, but its V-shaped tuning and SBC/AAC-only codecs show their age. Best value only as a renewed unit (~$139–150) [2][3][6].
- **Bose QuietComfort Ultra** — the all-rounder: top-tier ANC (−27 dB overall), comfort-crown ergonomics, aptX Adaptive, and CustomTune personalization. The 1st Gen at ~$269–345 is a strong deal; the 2nd Gen adds USB-C audio and Cinema Mode for $449 [12][16][41][50].
- **Sony WH-1000XM5** — the best mid/high-frequency ANC of the group and the only LDAC supporter, with excellent comfort, but fragile hinges, a narrow soundstage, and stock tuning that benefits greatly from EQ. At $248 it remains one of the best value ANC headphones; the successor XM6 is the new benchmark [17][19][21][56].
- **Apple AirPods Max (2nd Gen)** — the ANC leader (~88% reduction) with the most premium build (aluminum/stainless steel) and best-in-class transparency, but also the heaviest, the shortest battery life (20 h), restrictive codec support (AAC/SBC only), a documented mesh-sagging headband flaw, and a $549 price that has not moved since 2020 [25][29][30][48][54].
- **Sennheiser Momentum 4** — the sound-quality and battery-life champion (60 h claimed / 56 h measured, aptX Adaptive, 42 mm driver) with the weakest ANC of the five. At ~$219 street price, it is the value pick for listeners who prioritize audio over maximum noise cancellation [33][34][35][36].

**The fundamental trade-off remains unchanged:** buy the AirPods Max, QC Ultra, or XM5 for class-leading noise cancellation; buy the Momentum 4 for class-leading sound and endurance; buy the QC45 only if budget and comfort outweigh everything else [2][19][25][34][35].
