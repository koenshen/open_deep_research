# Food Delivery Market in North America and Europe (2025–2026): Comprehensive Research Report

## Executive Summary

The food delivery market in North America and Europe continued to expand in 2025–2026, though precise sizing depends heavily on methodology: estimates range from narrow "meal delivery revenue" definitions (tens of billions of US dollars) to broad grocery-inclusive revenue measures (hundreds of billions or over a trillion dollars globally). North America remains the largest regional market (31–38% of global revenue depending on the source), while Europe is the second-largest and most fragmented region. The biggest structural story of the period is consolidation: **DoorDash acquired Deliveroo (completed October 2025)**, **Prosus acquired Just Eat Takeaway.com (delisted November 2025)**, and **Grubhub was sold by Just Eat Takeaway to Wonder Group (January 2025)** for a fraction of its 2020 purchase price.

Demand is being driven by urbanization, entrenched convenience habits (over one-quarter of Americans order delivery weekly), the rapid expansion of grocery and quick-commerce delivery (now a larger revenue pool than meal delivery per Statista), and subscription programs. Key challenges include persistent profitability pressure, a wave of labor regulation (the EU Platform Work Directive, New York City minimum pay, California's Prop 22 litigation), commission caps in major US cities, and intense competitive rivalry that has triggered aggressive price competition and M&A.

This report provides: (1) market size, growth, and segmentation with cited figures and methodological caveats; (2) demand drivers and challenges; (3) a quantitative competitive comparison of DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub, and Deliveroo across market share, delivery-time performance, and commission structures; (4) flagship partnerships and geographic differentiators; and (5) a full source list. Where data is unavailable, it is explicitly flagged as not specified rather than estimated.

## 1. Market Size, Growth Rate, and Segmentation

### 1.1 Global Market Context (2025–2026)

Global market estimates diverge widely because research firms use different definitions (platform commission revenue vs. Gross Merchandise Value vs. grocery-inclusive total revenue):

- **Statista** estimates global online food delivery revenues approached **US$1.4 trillion in 2025**, more than double pre-pandemic levels, with the **grocery delivery segment now contributing more revenue than meal delivery**; China is the largest market and the United States the second-largest, followed by India, the UK, and Japan [1][2].
- **Mordor Intelligence** values the global market at **US$257.74 billion in 2025**, **US$284.73 billion in 2026**, projected to reach **US$468.51 billion by 2031** at a **10.47% CAGR (2026–2031)**; North America is the largest region, with 37.54% of 2025 revenue [3].
- **Euromonitor International** reports delivery accounted for **22% of global consumer foodservice spending in 2025**, up from 9% in 2019; global foodservice reached **US$3.36 trillion in 2025**, and delivery is expected to surpass **US$1 trillion by 2029** [4].
- Other global estimates include: **US$319.99 billion (2025) → US$350.63 billion (2026)** at 9.58% CAGR (Fortune Business Insights) [5]; **US$387.6 billion (2025)** at 12.8% CAGR (DataIntelo) [6]; **US$326.4 billion (2026)** at 13.8% CAGR (Persistence Market Research) [7]; and **US$182.60 billion (2025)** at 11.2% CAGR (Maximize Market Research) [8].

**Comparability caveat:** These figures are not directly comparable. Statista's figures include grocery and retail delivery; other firms measure only restaurant meal delivery or platform service revenue. All figures below are reported with their source and definition.

### 1.2 North America: United States, Canada, Mexico

**Regional overview.** North America is consistently ranked the largest regional market, with share estimates ranging from 27.4% (DataIntelo) to 37.54% (Mordor) of global revenue [3][6]. Fortune Business Insights assigns North America 31% of the global market [5].

**United States.** Statista estimates the US generated approximately **US$432 billion in online food delivery revenue in 2025**, with over US$100 billion from the meal delivery segment and grocery delivery accounting for nearly two-thirds of the total; the US counted over **180 million meal delivery users in 2025** [9][10]. By contrast, IMARC Group's narrower definition sizes the US market at **US$34.88 billion (2025) → US$38.00 billion (2026)**, projected to reach US$75.36 billion by 2034 at an **8.94% CAGR (2026–2034)** [11]. The US competitive landscape is highly concentrated: IMARC puts DoorDash at roughly **67%** of the meal delivery segment, Uber Eats at **23%**, and Grubhub at **6%** [11].

**Canada.** Canada's online food delivery market generated approximately **US$18.9 billion in revenue in 2024**, projected to reach about **US$28.64 billion by 2030** (7.7% CAGR, 2025–2030) [12][13]. About 27% of Canadians use food delivery at least monthly, with average order spends of C$32–C$35; usage surveys rank **SkipTheDishes first (55% usage)**, ahead of Uber Eats (46%) and Domino's (43%) [13].

**Mexico.** Mexico's online food delivery market reached **US$10.2 billion in 2025**, projected to reach **US$33.5 billion by 2034** at a **14.16% CAGR (2026–2034)** — the fastest-growing major market in North America [14]. Mordor Intelligence notes Mexico's grocery delivery segment is on track for a 15.95% CAGR through 2031, supported by Walmex's expansion plans and 25% e-commerce sales growth in 2024 [15].

**North America grocery delivery.** The North American online grocery delivery market (a key segment of overall food delivery) was valued at **US$180.2 billion in 2025** and an estimated **US$195.7 billion in 2026**, projected to reach US$295.34 billion by 2031 at an **8.60% CAGR (2026–2031)**. The US holds 85.6% of this market; same-day services captured 45.35% of 2025 revenue, and instant delivery (under 2 hours) is expected to grow at 25.9% CAGR [15].

**North America quick commerce.** The broader quick-commerce market (groceries, convenience, prepared food delivered in under ~30–60 minutes) was valued at **US$61.69 billion in North America in 2025** (33.43% of the global total of US$184.55 billion), with the US projected at US$55.68 billion in 2026 [16].

### 1.4 Segmentation by Type of Food Delivery

**Restaurant-to-consumer (first-party) vs. platform-to-consumer (aggregator/marketplace):**
- **Persistence Market Research:** restaurant-to-consumer delivery is the dominant segment (~65% share in 2025); platform-to-consumer is the fastest-growing [7].
- **Fortune Business Insights:** platform-to-consumer holds 58% share; restaurant-to-consumer 42% [5].
- **Mordor Intelligence:** restaurant-to-consumer channels held 68.75% of global market share in 2025; aggregator-to-restaurant models are expected to grow at 11.47% CAGR through 2031 [3].
- **Market Data Forecast (Europe):** platform-to-consumer dominated in 2025; restaurant-to-consumer is the fastest-growing segment at 18.5% CAGR (direct-ordering apps, cloud kitchens) [17].

**Meal delivery vs. grocery delivery:**
- Per Statista, **grocery delivery is now the larger revenue pool globally and in the US**, where it accounts for nearly two-thirds of the market [1][10].
- Fortune Business Insights splits business models at 59% meal delivery / 41% grocery delivery globally [5].
- North American online grocery delivery alone is nearly 5× the size of the narrow US meal-delivery revenue estimate (US$180.2B vs. US$34.88B) [15][11].

**Platform-to-consumer grocery and quick commerce:**
- Quick commerce globally: **US$184.55 billion (2025) → US$385.36 billion by 2034** (8.55% CAGR); Europe holds 31.95% and Asia-Pacific the fastest growth; groceries are the largest product segment (43.78% share) [16].
- Platform multi-vertical strategies are scaling: at Delivery Hero, customers ordering from both food and quick commerce generated nearly half of Group GMV and spent 5.2× more than food-only customers [25]; at DoorDash, DashPass members placed ~75% of Total Orders in US grocery/retail, and New Verticals accounted for over 25% of user orders in Q2 2025 [25][26].

**Channel and payment segmentation:**
- Mobile applications dominate ordering: 72.3% (US, IMARC) [11], 74% (global, Fortune BI) [5], 65.8% (Europe, Market Data Forecast) [17], 82.76% (global, Mordor) [3].
- Online payments represent 67–82% of transactions depending on the source; cash on delivery remains meaningful in some markets (18–32%) [3][5][6][11].

**Delivery-speed segmentation (North America grocery):** same-day services captured 45.35% of 2025 market size; instant delivery (under 2 hours) is expected to advance at 25.9% CAGR; retail (store-based) delivery led with 62.38% share [15].

## 2. Demand Drivers and Challenges

### 2.1 Demand Drivers

**Urbanization.** Urban density is the single most powerful structural driver of delivery demand. According to the UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025, **45% of the world's population lives in cities and 36% in towns**, with megacities quadrupling from 8 (1975) to 33 (2025) [27]. In Europe, **>75% of the population lives in urban centers** (Eurostat, cited by Market Data Forecast) [17]. YouGov data shows **39.2% of urban US dwellers use food delivery apps weekly, vs. 24.2% in suburbs and just 15.3% in rural areas** [28][29].

**Consumer convenience preferences.** Delivery has become a permanent convenience utility rather than a pandemic-era novelty:
- **28.2% of Americans use food delivery apps at least once a week** (January 2025); nearly **40% of Gen Z and Millennials order weekly**, vs. 21.5% of Gen X and 10.0% of Baby Boomers [30].
- Gen Z orders **2.3 times per week on average** (vs. 1.1 for older generations); **60% of Americans order food online at least once weekly**; **52% of US consumers consider food delivery "essential"** [31][32].
- 95% of DoorDash orders come through mobile; **79% of delivery orders come from repeat customers** and **73% stick with the same app** [33].
- The average American spends over **US$1,566 annually on food delivery** (~3.7 orders per month at ~US$35.42 per order) [29].

**Inflation pressures.** Inflation cuts both ways — it raises costs but reinforces demand for value and convenience:
- The **USDA Food Price Outlook (July 2026)** projects 2026 all-food price inflation of **+3.1%** (food-away-from-home +3.5%), with beef/veal prices up 10.7% and fresh vegetables up 6.8% [34].
- The **cumulative cost of a delivered meal rose ~50% between 2021 and 2025**, versus ~16.5% overall inflation, driven by menu price inflation, delivery fees, service fees, and tips; a two-person meal delivered twice monthly costs ~US$28 more per month than pickup [35].
- On apps, consumers pay an average **all-in premium of ~81% over in-store prices** (FinanceBuzz 2024, cited by MakeMyReceipt) [36].
- Despite this, demand has proven resilient: DoorDash reported record GOV and "all-time high average order frequency" in 2025 [37], and Uber reported record Monthly Active Platform Consumers (180M in Q2 2025, 202M by Q4 2025) [38][39]. Euromonitor describes consumers as "deeply value-minded" but still willing to pay for convenience, with delivery fees rising from 9% of foodservice spend (2019) to **14% (2025)** [4].

**Post-pandemic normalization.** US meal-delivery sales growth decelerated from a pandemic peak of +162% YoY (April 2020) to **+8% YoY by March 2024** — a normalization to durable single-digit growth, not a collapse [40]. Delivery's share of global foodservice spending grew from 9% (2019) to 22% (2025), indicating that pandemic-era habits were retained [4].

**Grocery and quick-commerce expansion.** Grocery delivery is now the main growth engine of the overall food delivery market [1][2]. North American online grocery delivery is projected to grow at 8.60% CAGR to 2031, supported by SNAP online purchasing expansion (41 million participants), same-day fulfilment infrastructure, and dark-store economics [15]. Subscription programs amplify order frequency: subscribers order ~2.5× more per month than non-subscribers [41].

### 2.2 Challenges

**Profitability.** The industry has shifted from "growth at all costs" to a disciplined profitability regime, but unit economics remain thin:
- **DoorDash** achieved GAAP net income of US$193M in Q1 2025 and US$285M in Q2 2025; Adjusted EBITDA reached US$590M (Q1 2025) and US$914M (Q2 2026), though its EBITDA margin as a share of GOV is only ~2.4–2.7% [37][42][43].
- **Uber's Delivery segment** became EBITDA-profitable for the first time in 2023; Delivery Adjusted EBITDA reached US$873M (4.0% margin) in Q2 2025 and passed US$1.0B in Q4 2025 [38][39][44].
- **Deliveroo** recorded its **first-ever annual profit in 2024 (£3 million)** and H1 2025 Adjusted EBITDA of £96.3M (2.5% of GTV) [45][46].
- **Just Eat Takeaway.com** improved FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA to €460 million but still reported a net loss of €1.645 billion, driven by a €1.002 billion Grubhub impairment [47].
- **Grubhub's** sale price collapse — from a **US$7.3 billion acquisition in 2020 to a US$650 million enterprise value in January 2025** — illustrates how quickly marketplace value can deteriorate [48].
- **Delivery Hero** reported its first-ever positive Group Operating Result in H1 2025 (€5M), but adjusted FY2025 guidance downward by ~€110M due to FX headwinds and Glovo legal-risk provisions [25].

**Labor regulation and gig worker classification.** Regulatory risk intensified on both sides of the Atlantic:

- **EU Platform Work Directive ((EU) 2024/2831):** adopted 23 October 2024, in force 1 December 2024, with Member State transposition due by **2 December 2026**. It establishes a **rebuttable presumption of employment** for platform workers, restricts algorithmic management (e.g., bans processing emotional state, private conversations, data outside working hours), requires human review of significant decisions (termination, account suspension), and imposes transparency obligations. It applies to an estimated 28+ million platform workers, of whom up to 5.5 million may be falsely classified as self-employed. Transposition is a "patchwork" — Belgium, Spain, and Portugal are broadly compliant; the Netherlands passed its VBAR law (effective January 2026); France, Germany, Ireland, and Italy are still drafting [49][50][51].
- **California Prop 22:** the California Supreme Court **upheld Proposition 22 unanimously in July 2024**, preserving app-based drivers' status as independent contractors (with 120% minimum earnings, health stipends, and accident insurance). This shields Uber Eats, DoorDash, and Grubhub from AB5-style reclassification in their largest US state [52].
- **New York City minimum pay:** NYC requires delivery apps to pay at least **US$22.13 per hour (2025–2026 rate, excluding tips)** — the first municipal minimum wage for delivery workers in the US — affecting more than 60,000 couriers [53][54].
- **Spain's Rider Law** (2021) mandates courier employee classification and is a template for other EU transpositions [17]. Delivery Hero took provisions for Glovo rider classification risks in Italy in April 2025, cutting FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA by ~€57 million [25].
- **United Kingdom:** the **Employment Rights Act 2025** (Royal Assent December 2025) introduces day-one SSP, zero-hours contract reforms, and a consultation on a single "worker" status; the new **Fair Work Agency** will absorb minimum-wage enforcement by 2027. In May 2026, **more than 7,000 Just Eat couriers launched employment tribunal claims** seeking worker status, minimum wage, and holiday pay [55][56][57].

**Commission caps and fee regulation.** US cities have imposed structural limits on platform commissions:
- **New York City** (permanent since 2022): **15% delivery fee cap, 5% basic service fee cap, 3% transaction fee cap** (NYC Administrative Code § 20-563.3). A **May 2025 amendment (Local Law 2025/079)** allows an optional "enhanced services" fee of up to 20%, creating a theoretical maximum of ~43% per order if restaurants opt in [58][59].
- **San Francisco** enacted the nation's **first permanent 15% commission cap** (2021), though it was weakened in November 2022 to allow higher fees if platforms offer a "core delivery service" at ≤15% [60].
- **Washington D.C.** made its cap permanent; Seattle passed caps that were legally challenged [41][60].
- Platform responses ("regulatory response fees," "city fees," tiered partnership plans) have shifted costs to consumers, with total consumer costs often running **30–40% above restaurant menu prices** in capped markets [41][60][61].

**Competitive intensity and consolidation.** The market has consolidated dramatically:
- **DoorDash acquired Deliveroo** for ~£2.9 billion (completed 2 October 2025), creating a combined group operating in ~40 countries with ~US$90 billion in annual orders [62][63].
- **Prosus acquired Just Eat Takeaway.com** for €4.1 billion; the company was delisted on 17 November 2025 [64][65].
- **Grubhub was sold to Wonder Group** (January 2025) after Just Eat Takeaway's US$7.3B purchase in 2020 [48].
- Other exits: Deliveroo left Hong Kong (April 2025); Delivery Hero exited Thailand, Denmark, Ghana, Slovakia, and Slovenia; Getir collapsed its UK operations in early 2024; Uber agreed to acquire Getir's Türkiye operations (February 2026) [66][67].
- The competitive environment remains intense in the UK (a "two-horse race" between DoorDash/Deliveroo and Uber Eats post-merger per analysts), and price wars (discounts up to 50% in peak European seasons) compress order margins by 10–15% [17][63].

## 4. Flagship Partnerships and Geographic Differentiators

### 4.1 DoorDash

**Flagship partnerships:**

- **SevenRooms (acquired 2025, ~US$1.2B):** hospitality technology/CRM integration [96].

- **Deliveroo (acquired October 2025):** adds 9 markets (UK, Ireland, France, Italy, Belgium, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Singapore) and ~176,000 merchant partners [62][63][24].
- **Grocery/New Verticals:** DashPass members placed ~75% of Total Orders in US grocery and retail categories; over 25% of user orders come from non-restaurant verticals (Q2 2025) [25][26][42].

**Geographic differentiators:** DoorDash is the dominant US player (60–67% share) [11][40][68], with international expansion now centered on Wolt's European footprint plus Deliveroo. DoorDash's US profitability (first full year of positive GAAP net income in 2025) funds international investment [33][42]. Post-merger, DoorDash announced wind-downs in Qatar, Singapore, Japan, and Uzbekistan (March 2026) — a selective-retreat strategy from marginal markets [93].

### 4.2 Uber Eats

**Flagship partnerships:**
- **Domino's (July 2023):** the largest US restaurant partnership, adding marketplace coverage to the pizza chain's first-party ordering.
- **Instacart (May 2024):** integration of Uber Eats restaurant delivery into the Instacart app, and reciprocal grocery availability.

- **McDonald's, Starbucks, and major QSR chains** across global markets (long-standing anchor partnerships).
- **Trendyol Go (85% stake, US$700M, May 2025):** Türkiye's delivery vertical; **Getir Türkiye operations (February 2026)** extend Uber's Turkish footprint [66][96].
- **Uber One:** surpassed **36 million members in Q2 2025**, generating over 40% of combined Delivery and Mobility gross bookings [38].

**Geographic differentiators:** Uber Eats operates in **45+ countries, 11,500+ cities, with 1M+ restaurant partners** [74][95]. Its strongest delivery growth in 2025 came from the **Europe, Middle East, and Africa (EMEA) region** [39][72]. Uber Eats leverages the broader Uber super-app (mobility cross-selling, Uber One bundling) and was **the first major food delivery aggregator to reach profitability (2023)** [75][95]. In the UK, Uber Eats leads in share of delivery occasions (27.2% per Lumina) [20].

### 4.3 Grubhub

**Flagship partnerships:**
- **Amazon Prime (renewed partnership):** free Grubhub+ for Prime members (unlimited free delivery on orders US$12+) — the single largest subscriber-distribution channel and a key differentiator for Grubhub [88][90].

**Geographic differentiators:** Grubhub is **US-only** (plus Canada) and strongest in the **Northeast and Midwest** — NYC (17.6% local share), Philadelphia, Chicago, Boston [68]. NYC is Grubhub's largest market; the city's commission caps cost Just Eat Takeaway ~US$100 million per year before the sale [48]. Grubhub connects **300,000+ restaurants across 4,000 US cities** [48][74][90]. Its strategy centers on defending dense urban strongholds, Prime-member subscriptions, and first-party products (Grubhub Direct) rather than international scale.

**Flagship partnerships:**
- **Grocery and retail:** grocery reached **18% of group GTV in H1 2025** ("strong double-digit growth"), with **4,000 new grocery/retail merchant sites added** in H1 2025; grocery has scaled to over £1 billion in annual GTV [24][45][46].

Deliveroo exited Hong Kong in April 2025 under pressure from Meituan-backed KeeTa, selling assets to foodpanda [67]. Its differentiators include a **premium brand position, exclusive high-end restaurant partnerships, a rider model that successfully defended self-employed status in UK courts**, and a "Plus-first" subscription strategy targeting mid-teens GTV growth and 4%+ EBITDA margin by 2026 [45][46][91]. As of October 2025, Deliveroo operates as a subsidiary of DoorDash, retaining its London headquarters; Miki Kuusi (Wolt co-founder) leads the combined international business [62][63][93].

## 5. Data Availability and Comparability Notes

- **Market-size methodology gaps:** Market size figures vary by up to 10× across research firms depending on whether they measure platform commission revenue, GMV, meal delivery only, or grocery-inclusive revenue. Each figure in this report is reported with its source and definition.
- **Statista paywalled data:** Several Statista country-level revenue values for 2025/2026 (US, UK, Germany, France, Canada, Mexico) are premium-gated and appear as redacted values in retrieved content; they are flagged where applicable [2].
- **Market share gaps:** No authoritative company-level market share data (by GMV or orders) was located for Canada, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, or Benelux for 2024–2026. The only European country-level share data found is for the UK, with divergent methodologies (Lumina vs. SpartanPac) [20][21].
- **Delivery-time data gaps:** None of the four companies publishes an official average delivery time for 2024–2026. The Intouch Insight study (September 2024) is the only third-party US measurement; Deliveroo was not included [73].
- **Post-merger discontinuities:** Deliveroo's standalone data series ends with the DoorDash acquisition (October 2025). Grubhub's standalone data likewise ends with the Wonder sale (January 2025). Combined entity market shares for DoorDash+Deliveroo in Europe are not yet published.
- **Inflation/order-frequency quantification:** No source provides a precise, uncontested quantitative estimate of inflation-driven changes in average delivery basket size or order frequency in North America/Europe for 2025–2026; the report presents the available directional evidence (USDA price indices, delivery-cost premium analyses, platform order trends).

## Conclusion

The food delivery market in North America and Europe in 2025–2026 is large, growing at high single-digit to low double-digit rates (depending on definition), and undergoing rapid consolidation. North America — led by the United States — remains the largest market, with DoorDash dominant at roughly two-thirds share. Europe is fragmented but consolidating fast through the DoorDash–Deliveroo and Prosus–Just Eat Takeaway transactions, leaving Uber Eats as the principal pan-European challenger. Growth is increasingly driven by grocery and quick-commerce verticals, subscription loyalty programs, and urbanization, while the binding constraints are regulatory (gig-worker classification, commission caps, minimum wages), persistent unit-economics pressure, and intense head-to-head competition in dense urban markets. Grubhub, now under Wonder ownership, has retreated to a US subscription-led niche anchored by Amazon Prime. Collectively, the evidence points to a maturing industry where scale, subscription ecosystems, and regulatory navigation — not growth alone — determine competitive outcomes.
