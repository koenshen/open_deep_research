# Spatial Understanding and Reasoning in Vision-Language Models: A Literature Review (2022–2025)

## 1. Introduction

Spatial understanding—the capacity to perceive, localize, and reason about objects and their geometric, topological, and metric relations in 2D images and 3D scenes—has become a central axis of evaluation for vision-language models (VLMs). It underpins tasks as diverse as object counting, visual grounding and referring expression comprehension, embodied navigation, and general visual reasoning, and it is widely regarded as a necessary component for deploying VLMs in robotics, augmented reality, and embodied AI [1]. This review synthesizes advances from 2022 to 2025 in top-tier AI venues, tracing how both general-purpose and task-specific VLMs acquired spatial capabilities through architectural design, data curation, and targeted benchmarks. We organize the literature into six coherent lines of research: (1) unified sequence-to-sequence architectures that first introduced explicit spatial tokens; (2) grounding-enabled multimodal LLMs that localize referring expressions at box and pixel levels; (3) dedicated spatial reasoning models that inject metric 3D geometry into 2D VLMs; (4) 3D scene-language models spanning datasets, pretraining, and instruction tuning; (5) point-cloud understanding with vision-language alignment; and (6) benchmarks and evaluation methodology. Across all lines, a consistent finding emerges: while models have made dramatic progress on high-level semantic tasks, fine-grained spatial reasoning—especially metric estimation, perspective-taking, and novel spatial compositions—remains a fundamental bottleneck, with a persistent gap of 20–60 percentage points between the best models and human performance.

## 2. Unified Architectures: Location Tokens and Generalist Multimodal Models

The 2022–2023 period consolidated vision-language tasks into unified sequence-to-sequence frameworks, several of which encoded spatial information as first-class tokens. OFA [2] (ICML 2022) unified image generation, classification, captioning, VQA, and visual grounding in a single encoder-decoder transformer, representing objects as sequences of discrete tokens that interleave class labels with bounding-box location tokens. Trained on only 20M image-text pairs, OFA achieved then-state-of-the-art results on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg (94.03 on RefCOCO test-A with the Huge variant) and, crucially, demonstrated through ablations that region-prediction tasks—object detection and visual grounding—are essential for strong multimodal performance, improving captioning by +2.3 CIDEr and VQA by +0.6 accuracy when included. Unified-IO 2 [3] (CVPR 2024) extended this paradigm by tokenizing images, text, audio, and actions into a shared semantic space processed by a single autoregressive encoder-decoder; trained from scratch on a 600TB corpus with gradient-stabilization techniques including 2D rotary encodings, QK normalization, and scaled cosine attention, it achieved state-of-the-art results on the GRIT benchmark spanning grounding, captioning, and VQA.

In parallel, a highly parameter-efficient bridging paradigm emerged. BLIP-2 [4] (ICML 2023) introduced a lightweight Querying Transformer (Q-Former, 188M parameters) that compresses frozen vision-encoder features into 32 learnable "soft visual prompts" for a frozen LLM, outperforming Flamingo80B by 8.7% on zero-shot VQAv2 with 54× fewer trainable parameters. InstructBLIP [5] (NeurIPS 2023) made the key modification of feeding the text instruction into the Q-Former, enabling instruction-aware visual feature extraction that achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot results across 13 held-out datasets. LLaVA [6] (NeurIPS 2023) instead demonstrated the power of visual instruction tuning: using language-only GPT-4 to generate 158K multimodal instruction samples over COCO images, LLaVA connected a CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder to the Vicuna LLM via a simple projection matrix and established the two-stage recipe—feature alignment pretraining followed by end-to-end fine-tuning—that became the default for subsequent multimodal LLMs. Kosmos-1 [7] (NeurIPS 2023) contributed architectural innovations for training multimodal LLMs from scratch, including extrapolatable position embeddings (XPOS) that generalize to longer sequences. Finally, the scaling trajectory continued with InternVL [8] (CVPR 2024), which scaled the vision encoder itself to 6B parameters via a three-stage contrastive, generative, and instruction-alignment pipeline, achieving state-of-the-art results on 32 generic visual-linguistic benchmarks, and Mono-InternVL [9] (CVPR 2025), which explored fully monolithic architectures that integrate visual experts into the LLM through mixture-of-experts, reducing first-token latency by up to 67% while outperforming larger monolithic baselines.

This architectural progression matters for spatial understanding for two reasons. First, the explicit location tokens introduced by OFA established the representational currency—discretized bounding-box coordinates in the shared vocabulary—that later grounding MLLMs would adopt. Second, the efficiency gains from Q-Former-style bridging and visual instruction tuning made it feasible to train generalist models on the large volumes of spatially annotated data that, as discussed below, proved necessary for spatial competence.

## 3. Grounding and Referring Expression Comprehension in Multimodal LLMs

A major line of 2024 work augmented multimodal LLMs with explicit grounding—the ability to localize entities described in text. Kosmos-2 [10] (ICLR 2024) converted bounding boxes into discrete location tokens (discretizing images into P×P bins) and constructed the GrIT corpus of 90M images with 137M grounded objects. In zero-shot grounded phrase grounding, Kosmos-2 achieved 78.7% R@1 on Flickr30k Entities, surpassing fine-tuned VisualBERT by 7.4%, though its referring expression comprehension on RefCOCO (52.32%) remained well below fine-tuned specialists such as OFA. Ferret [11] (ICLR 2024) introduced a hybrid region representation combining discrete coordinates with continuous visual features, processed by a spatial-aware visual sampler that accepts free-form regions (points, boxes, scribbles); trained on the GRIT dataset (1.1M samples with 95K hard negatives), Ferret-13B reached 89.48% on RefCOCO val while reducing object hallucination. GLaMM [12] (CVPR 2024) pushed grounding to the pixel level: integrating a SAM-based pixel decoder with Vicuna-7B, it introduced the novel task of Grounded Conversation Generation and the GranD dataset (7.5M unique concepts anchored in 810M regions across 11M images), reaching 79.5% on RefCOCO val while generating segmentation masks interleaved with free-form language.

A second wave of grounding models optimized the encoder-decoder interface itself. OneRef [13] (NeurIPS 2024) showed that a modality-shared one-tower transformer (BEiT-3) with Mask Referring Modeling—a referring-aware dynamic image masking strategy combining masked image and masked language modeling—could eliminate separate vision/language encoders and special grounding tokens entirely, achieving 92.87% on RefCOCO val with a 0.6B model. RefFormer [14] (NeurIPS 2024) introduced a Query Adaptation module that generates referential queries inside a frozen CLIP backbone, iteratively refining them across multi-level features; with only adapter-level tuning, it reached 86.52% on RefCOCO val (ViT-B) while halving training time relative to prior transformer baselines. Octopus [15] (NeurIPS 2024) proposed decomposing recognition and understanding across LLM layers—DETR-style object queries in lower layers for parallel recognition, sequential reasoning in upper layers—yielding up to 5× speedups on visual grounding tasks. Extending grounding to the temporal dimension, LLaVA-ST [16] (CVPR 2025) introduced Language-Aligned Positional Embedding (LAPE), which embeds coordinate special tokens directly into the visual feature space to bypass cross-modal coordinate alignment difficulty, and a Spatial-Temporal Packer that decouples spatial and temporal feature compression; trained on the ST-Align dataset (4.3M samples across 15 tasks), it achieves 90.1% Acc@0.5 on RefCOCO val while simultaneously localizing events in video.

Collectively, this line shows a clear progression from discrete location tokens appended to text (Kosmos-2), toward hybrid continuous+discrete region representations (Ferret), pixel-level masks (GLaMM), and ultimately grounding mechanisms deeply fused into the backbone (OneRef, RefFormer, Octopus). The RefCOCO family, though created before this window, remains the standard evaluation; the steady climb from roughly 52% (Kosmos-2) to 93% (OneRef) reflects both architectural maturity and the growing availability of grounded instruction data.

## 4. Explicit Spatial Reasoning: From Implicit Cues to Metric 3D Geometry

A pivotal hypothesis, articulated by SpatialVLM [17] (CVPR 2024), is that VLMs' poor spatial reasoning stems not from architectural limits but from the scarcity of 3D spatial knowledge in training data. SpatialVLM's automatic data pipeline lifts 2D images into metric-scale 3D point clouds, generating up to 2 billion spatial VQA examples over 10 million real-world images. Training on this corpus endowed a VLM with quantitative spatial estimates—37.2% of its distance estimates fell within 0.5×–2× of human-annotated ground truth—and, notably, enabled chain-of-thought spatial reasoning (e.g., determining whether three objects on a table roughly form an equilateral triangle) as well as downstream use as a fine-grained reward annotator for robot reinforcement learning.

SpatialRGPT [18] (NeurIPS 2024) attacked the same problem from the architecture side, inserting a flexible depth plugin into the visual encoder of existing VLMs so that relative depth can be injected when available and gracefully omitted when absent. It also introduced an automatic curation pipeline that constructs 3D scene graphs (objects as nodes, spatial relations as edges) from 2D images using open-vocabulary detection and metric depth estimation, yielding the Open Spatial Dataset (1M images, 5M regions, 8.7M template- and LLM-based QA pairs). On the proposed SpatialRGPT-Bench, which tests 3D spatial cognition across indoor, outdoor, and simulated scenes, SpatialRGPT achieved 91.78% average accuracy versus 58.14% for GPT-4V, and 82.3% on BLINK relative depth versus 66.9% for GPT-4V-Turbo, while maintaining general VQA performance.

Diagnostic work sharpened the picture of what remains hard. Eyes Wide Shut [19] (CVPR 2024) identified "CLIP-blind pairs"—images that CLIP perceives as nearly identical despite clear visual differences—and built the MMVP benchmark showing that GPT-4V fails on straightforward questions about nine basic visual patterns; a Mixture of Features approach integrating self-supervised vision features substantially improved visual grounding. CECE [20] (ICLR 2025) showed that caption expansion with NLI-generated contradictions and entailments improves compositional spatial matching without any fine-tuning, raising the Winoground group score from 28.3% (best prior work) to 47.5%.

More recently, ViLaSR [21] (NeurIPS 2025) proposed a fundamentally different paradigm: "drawing to reason in space," in which the VLM performs elementary drawing operations (bounding box annotations, auxiliary lines) in visual space as intermediate reasoning steps. A three-stage training pipeline—cold-start on synthetic drawing data, reflective rejection sampling to reinforce self-correction, then GRPO reinforcement learning with joint answer-and-reasoning rewards—yielded an 18.4% average improvement over strong baselines across five spatial benchmarks (98.2% on MAZE, 63.9% on SpatialEval-Real, 45.4% on VSI-Bench) using only a 7B model, outperforming both significantly larger open-source models and proprietary systems such as GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-Flash. Complementing this, Sparkle [22] (EMNLP 2025 Findings) disentangled 2D spatial reasoning into three atomic capabilities—direction comprehension, distance estimation, and localization—and generated synthetic training data with simplified visual representations for targeted supervision. Fine-tuning InternVL2-8B improved direction comprehension from 35% to 83% and shortest-path solving from 13.5% to 40.0%, with no degradation on standard VLM benchmarks and positive transfer to out-of-distribution real-world spatial tasks. The consistent message across these works is that explicit spatial supervision—whether as depth, 3D scene graphs, or drawing operations—is the most reliable route to spatial competence, and that text-only chain-of-thought is of limited value for spatial problems, which require visual or geometric intermediates.

## 5. 3D Scene-Language Understanding: Datasets, Pretraining, and Generalist Assistants

The 3D counterpart of the field matured rapidly from the foundational ScanRefer dataset [23] (ECCV 2020), which paired 51.5K natural-language descriptions with 11K objects across 800 ScanNet scenes and established the task of 3D object localization from free-form language. Early methods such as 3DVG-Transformer [24] (ICCV 2021) modeled inter-object relations through coordinate-guided contextual aggregation and multiplex cross-modal attention, while Refer-it-in-RGBD [25] (CVPR 2021) extended grounding to single-view RGB-D images where referred objects are often partially occluded. Within the 2022–2025 window, the field moved decisively from task-specific grounding to generalist 3D scene-language assistants. ScanQA [26] (CVPR 2022) introduced 3D question answering with over 41,000 QA pairs across 800 scenes, showing that joint end-to-end training of object localization and answer generation substantially outperforms pipeline approaches (EM@1 of 23.45 versus a human upper bound of 51.6). SQA3D [27] (ICLR 2023) added the crucial dimension of situation: the agent must first understand its position and orientation in the scene from a textual description, then reason about its surroundings to answer questions spanning spatial relations, commonsense, navigation, and multi-hop reasoning. The best model achieved only 47.20% overall versus 90.06% for amateur humans—a gap that defined the 3D reasoning challenge for subsequent work.

On the modeling side, 3D-VisTA [28] (ICCV 2023) demonstrated that a simple unified transformer with vanilla self-attention for both single-modal modeling and multi-modal fusion, pretrained on the ScanScribe corpus (278K scene-text pairs from ScanNet and 3R-Scan, augmented with GPT-3-generated text), transfers strongly across six 3D-VL tasks: it improved ScanRefer by +8.1% (acc@0.5), Scan2Cap by +10.1% (CIDEr@0.25) and +19.2% (CIDEr@0.5), ScanQA by +3.5% EM@1, and SQA3D by +1.9% over prior state-of-the-art. Notably, with only 30% of downstream annotations, the pretrained model outperformed scratch-trained models trained on full data, demonstrating the data-efficiency value of 3D-VL pretraining. 3D-LLM [29] (NeurIPS 2023) injected the 3D world into LLMs by extracting 3D features from rendered multi-view images (via direct reconstruction, feature fusion, or neural fields) and training on roughly one million ChatGPT-generated 3D-language samples spanning captioning, QA, grounding, task decomposition, dialogue, and navigation; it outperformed prior state-of-the-art by 9% BLEU-1 and 10% CIDEr on ScanQA.

The generalist trend accelerated in 2024. SceneVerse [30] (ECCV 2024) contributed the first million-scale 3D vision-language dataset—approximately 68K indoor scenes and 2.5M vision-language pairs generated via scene graphs and LLMs—together with a Grounded Pre-training for Scenes (GPS) transformer that achieves state-of-the-art results across 3D visual grounding and QA benchmarks, with scaling effects in both pre-train/fine-tune and zero-shot settings. PQ3D [31] (ECCV 2024) unified ten 3D-VL tasks through promptable queries over a shared 3D coordinate space, improving ScanRefer by +5.4% (acc@0.5), Multi3DRefer by +11.7% (F1@0.5), and Scan2Cap by +13.4% (CIDEr@0.5). LL3DA [32] (CVPR 2024) introduced visual interactive instruction tuning: the model takes point clouds plus visual interaction prompts (user clicks encoded via 3D Fourier positional embeddings, or box annotations) alongside text instructions, resolving ambiguities that plain text cannot; click prompts improved ScanQA CIDEr by +6.12% at test time, and the generalist model handles dense captioning, QA, embodied dialogue, and planning without task-specific heads. Chat-Scene [33] (NeurIPS 2024) assigned each object proposal a unique identifier token (e.g., `<OBJ013>`), enabling efficient object-level referencing in unified QA-format training; combining Uni3D geometric features with DINOv2 2D semantic features from multi-view projections, it achieved 50.2% acc@0.5 on ScanRefer (+3.7%), 52.4% acc@0.5 on Multi3DRefer (+14.0%), 77.1% CIDEr@0.5 on Scan2Cap (+8.7%), and 54.6% EM on SQA3D. LEO [34] (ICML 2024) extended the generalist paradigm to embodiment: an object-centric, LoRA-tuned Vicuna-7B trained in two stages (3D vision-language alignment, then vision-language-action instruction tuning) performs 3D captioning, 3D QA, embodied reasoning, navigation on MP3D, and robotic manipulation on CLIPort, demonstrating that a single model can perceive, ground, reason, plan, and act in the 3D world. MSQA [35] (NeurIPS 2024) contributed a large-scale multi-modal situated reasoning dataset with 251K QA pairs and interleaved text-image-point-cloud inputs, arguing that single-modality situation descriptions are inherently ambiguous; it also introduced a Multi-modal Next-step Navigation task that evaluates grounding of actions and transitions between situations.

Critically, Beacon3D [36] (CVPR 2025) provided a rigorous object-centric re-evaluation of this progress, identifying three flaws in existing 3D-VL benchmarks: ambiguous referential text, oversimplified metrics vulnerable to language variation, and the isolation of grounding from QA. Its chain-of-analysis paradigm reveals that grounding-QA coherence is fragile—models answer both components correctly in only a small fraction of cases—and, provocatively, that incorporating LLMs into 3D-VL models hinders grounding capabilities while failing to elevate QA, because current 3D QA tasks demand perception and vision-language alignment more than language modeling. This finding echoes SQA3D's conclusion that models attending to the right objects are the ones that answer correctly, and it cautions against treating LLM integration as a panacea for 3D understanding.

## 6. Point Cloud Understanding with Vision-Language Alignment

A parallel line of research adapted 2D vision-language knowledge to raw 3D point clouds. PointCLIP [37] (CVPR 2022) showed that CLIP's zero-shot recognition transfers to point clouds by projecting them into multi-view depth maps, achieving 20.18% zero-shot accuracy on ModelNet40 and substantially better few-shot performance via a lightweight inter-view adapter—an early demonstration that 2D vision-language pretraining carries spatial priors reusable in 3D. Point-BERT [38] (CVPR 2022) introduced Masked Point Modeling, a BERT-style pretraining objective for point cloud transformers that uses a discrete variational autoencoder tokenizer to recover masked point tokens, reaching 92.67% accuracy on ModelNet40 and establishing self-supervised pretraining as the default for 3D encoders. Point-In-Context [39] (NeurIPS 2023) proposed the first general in-context learning framework for point clouds, with a benchmark of 217K samples across reconstruction, denoising, registration, and part segmentation; its joint sampling module and task-aware prompting enable a single model to handle diverse 3D tasks and generalize to out-of-distribution data. Most recently, Point-CSA [40] (CVPR 2025) explored adapting static 3D models to point cloud video via Cross-frame Spatio-temporal Adaptation—grouping points into "point tubes" along spatial and temporal dimensions and applying geometric-constraint temporal adapters with frozen backbones—achieving 95.12% on MSR-Action3D with only adapters and position encoders trainable. These works supply the perceptual front-ends that 3D scene-language models rely on, and they collectively demonstrate the value of transferring 2D foundation-model knowledge into 3D rather than training from scratch.

## 7. Benchmarks and Evaluation Methodology for Spatial Reasoning

The evaluation ecosystem for spatial understanding expanded dramatically during this period, with consistent findings about where models fail. Foundational compositional benchmarks established the baseline before the window: Winoground [41] (CVPR 2022) showed that all tested vision-and-language models perform near chance on matching images to captions that differ only in word order (best model 14.5% group score versus 85.5% for humans); VALSE [42] (ACL 2022) decomposed grounded language understanding into six phenomena—existence, plurality, counting, spatial relations, actions, and coreference—finding that models struggle specifically with counting and spatial prepositions, with VisualBERT performing below a random baseline (48.8%). Earlier datasets including GQA [43] (CVPR 2019), with its 22M compositional questions and scene-graph supervision, and CLEVR [44] (CVPR 2017), with its functional-program annotations for counting and spatial relations, remained widely used diagnostic tools throughout the period, alongside NLVR2 [45] (ACL 2019) for image-pair reasoning.

What's Up? [46] (EMNLP 2023) performed a rigorous diagnosis of spatial preposition understanding, curating three benchmarks—What'sUp (820 tightly controlled photographs of household objects varying only in spatial relation), COCO-spatial (2,687 images), and GQA-spatial (1,451 images)—that isolate spatial reasoning from other skills. All 18 evaluated vision-language models performed poorly: BLIP, near human parity on VQAv2, achieved only 56% accuracy on What'sUp versus 99% for humans, with many models barely above chance. The paper's analysis of the LAION-2B training corpus is damning: spatial prepositions occur in under 0.2% of captions, are often ambiguous or extraneous when present, and can frequently be guessed without looking at the image. This data-scarcity account explains why purely architectural interventions (probability re-normalization, hard-negative captions, fine-tuning) failed to close the gap, and it directly motivated the synthetic-data approaches of SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, and Sparkle discussed above.

More recent benchmarks have raised the ceiling on difficulty. Jigsaw-Puzzles [47] (EMNLP 2025) evaluated 24 state-of-the-art VLMs on 1,100 curated real-world images across five tasks mirroring human cognitive progression—missing piece selection, piece localization, connection verification, anomaly detection, and order restoration. Even Gemini-2.5-Pro reached only 77.14% overall versus 96.36% for humans, and collapsed to 30.00% on open-ended order generation (humans: 94.09%), confirming that multi-step spatial reasoning remains the hardest regime. MINDCUBE [48] (ICCV 2025) tested spatial mental modeling from limited views with 21,154 questions across 3,268 images, finding current VLMs barely above random guessing (37.8%); a "map-then-reason" pipeline with supervised fine-tuning reached 60.8%, and adding reinforcement learning pushed it to 70.7%—again implicating explicit geometric intermediate representations as the key to spatial competence. Emerging benchmarks continue this trajectory: OmniSpatial [49] organizes over 8,400 QA pairs into four cognitive categories (dynamic reasoning, complex spatial logic, spatial interaction, and perspective-taking) and reports that even o3 achieves only 56.33% against 92.63% human performance, with geometric reasoning near random; SR-3D [50] demonstrates that enriching 2D visual features with 3D positional embeddings from monocular depth estimation enables a single-view 2D VLM to reason competitively in multi-view 3D scenes, reaching 62.9% on VSI-Bench and outperforming all open-source models.

## 8. Synthesis and Outlook

Across the 2022–2025 period, four trends are evident. First, spatial grounding moved from a specialized capability of task-specific models to a core competency of generalist MLLMs, enabled by location tokens [10], hybrid region representations [11], pixel decoders [12], and object identifiers [33]. Second, explicit 3D geometric supervision—metric depth, scene graphs, and point clouds—has proven indispensable: every approach that injected such supervision (SpatialVLM's 3D lifting [17], SpatialRGPT's depth plugin [18], SR-3D's 3D positional embeddings [50]) produced large gains, while purely text-driven reasoning failed [21][22]. Third, 3D scene understanding consolidated around LLM-backed generalists (3D-LLM, LL3DA, Chat-Scene, LEO) with object-centric representations and unified QA formats, but rigorous object-centric evaluation [36] warns that apparent QA gains may reflect language priors and that LLM integration can actively degrade grounding. Fourth, benchmarks have become more diagnostic, isolating spatial factors (What's Up?, VALSE), testing mental modeling from limited views (MINDCUBE), and revealing persistent human-model gaps of 20–60 percentage points [46][47][48]. The most promising directions for closing this gap are synthetic spatial data at scale, visual and drawing-based reasoning intermediates, and architectures that treat 3D geometry as a first-class modality rather than an optional plugin.
