# GDPR Enforcement Fines Above €50 Million (Calendar Years 2022–2026)

## Scope, Method and Finality Caveat

This report lists all administrative fines of **more than €50 million** imposed under the GDPR by EU/EEA data protection authorities (DPAs) between 1 January 2022 and 24 August 2026 (the coverage window). The list is based on official DPA decisions and press releases, cross-checked against independent GDPR enforcement trackers (GDPR Enforcement Tracker, GDPRhub, GDPRfine.com, DLA Piper's GDPR Fines and Data Breach Surveys, and the CMS GDPR Enforcement Tracker Report).

**Important finality caveat.** Under the GDPR, a DPA decision is immediately binding even while the fined company exercises its right to appeal. Nearly all fines of this magnitude are currently subject to appeal before national courts or the CJEU; as of 24 August 2026, **none of the fines in the table below has been annulled or reduced**, and none has been paid in full (payment is typically suspended pending appeal). The one major fine set aside in this period — Amazon's €746 million fine (2021) — falls **outside** the 2022–2026 window and is discussed separately below. Every entry below therefore flags its appeal status explicitly, consistent with how GDPR Enforcement Tracker and similar databases record "imposed" fines.

**Excluded items:** proposed/draft fines are not included. In particular, no evidence was found of the reported "€2.5 billion Meta draft fine" or "€520 million Instagram draft fine" — the €2.5 billion figure corresponds to Meta's *cumulative* DPC fines, not a single proposed fine. The CNIL's €325 million Google fine (September 2025) is excluded from the GDPR list because it was imposed under the ePrivacy Directive, not the GDPR (see "Excluded and flagged cases" below).

## Detailed Entries

### 1. Meta Platforms Ireland Limited (Facebook) — €1.2 billion — Irish DPC (2023)

- **Fine amount:** €1,200,000,000 — the **largest GDPR fine ever imposed**, roughly 60% larger than the next-highest figure ([26](https://privacyterms.io/biggest-gdpr-fines-all-time), [29](https://cms.law/en/int/publication/GDPR-Enforcement-Tracker-Report/numbers-and-figures)).
- **Date:** Decision dated **12 May 2023** (DPC reference IN-20-8-1); announced **22 May 2023** ([7](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/decisions/inquiry-concerning-data-transfers-eueea-us-meta-platforms-ireland-limited-its-facebook-service)).
- **Issuing DPA:** Ireland — Data Protection Commission (DPC), after the EDPB's binding decision 1/2023 of 13 April 2023 directed the DPC to impose a fine (the DPC's draft had initially proposed none) ([8](https://iapp.org/news/a/meta-fined-gdpr-record-1-2-billion-euros-in-data-transfer-case)).
- **Infringements:** Article **46(1) GDPR** (transfers subject to appropriate safeguards, Chapter V). The DPC/EDPB found Meta had no valid Article 49 derogation either.
- **Conduct:** Meta Ireland continued transferring EU/EEA Facebook users' personal data to the US after the CJEU's *Schrems II* judgment (July 2020) invalidated the EU-US Privacy Shield. Although Meta used updated 2021 Standard Contractual Clauses with supplementary measures, the EDPB concluded these "did not address the risks to the fundamental rights and freedoms of data subjects" identified by the CJEU, given US surveillance powers under FISA Section 702/PRISM. The infringement was "systematic, repetitive and continuous" and involved "massive" volumes of data; the EDPB found Meta acted with "at least the highest degree of negligence" ([8](https://iapp.org/news/a/meta-fined-gdpr-record-1-2-billion-euros-in-data-transfer-case), [7](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/decisions/inquiry-concerning-data-transfers-eueea-us-meta-platforms-ireland-limited-its-facebook-service), [37](https://gdprlocal.com/metas-e1-2-billion-gdpr-fine-why-it-still-matters-in-2025)). Remedies also included a five-month suspension order and a six-month compliance/storage order ([8](https://iapp.org/news/a/meta-fined-gdpr-record-1-2-billion-euros-in-data-transfer-case)).
- **Status:** Under appeal (Meta filed in the Irish High Court on 26 June 2023); payment suspended. No reduction or annulment as of August 2026; Meta is also challenging related EDPB binding decisions before the CJEU ([37](https://gdprlocal.com/metas-e1-2-billion-gdpr-fine-why-it-still-matters-in-2025), [26](https://privacyterms.io/biggest-gdpr-fines-all-time)).
- **Official DPA source:** [DPC decision page](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/decisions/inquiry-concerning-data-transfers-eueea-us-meta-platforms-ireland-limited-its-facebook-service) ([7](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/decisions/inquiry-concerning-data-transfers-eueea-us-meta-platforms-ireland-limited-its-facebook-service))
- **Independent trackers:** [GDPR Enforcement Tracker — Statistics](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics) ([2](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics)); [DLA Piper GDPR Fines and Data Breach Survey, January 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/01/dla-piper-gdpr-fines-and-data-breach-survey-january-2026) ([28](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/01/dla-piper-gdpr-fines-and-data-breach-survey-january-2026))

### 2. TikTok Technology Limited — €530 million — Irish DPC (2025)

- **Fine amount:** €530,000,000 total — **€485 million** for infringement of Article 46(1) GDPR and **€45 million** for infringement of Article 13(1)(f) GDPR ([19](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-tiktok-eu530-million-and-orders-corrective-measures-following)).
- **Date:** Decision dated **30 April 2025**; announced **2 May 2025** ([19](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-tiktok-eu530-million-and-orders-corrective-measures-following), [20](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/29510-irish-data-protection-commission-issued-fine-of-eur-530-million-against-tiktok-and-ordered-suspension-of-data-transfers-to-china-in-absence-of-gdpr-compliance)).
- **Issuing DPA:** Ireland — Data Protection Commission (DPC).
- **Infringements:** Articles **46(1)** (transfers to China without appropriate safeguards) and **13(1)(f)** (failure to inform data subjects of the third countries of transfer).
- **Conduct:** EEA users' personal data was transferred to China via remote access by ByteDance personnel to data stored in Singapore and the US. The DPC found TikTok failed to verify, guarantee and demonstrate that data remotely accessed in China received essentially equivalent protection; TikTok's own assessment of Chinese law (Anti-Terrorism Law, Counter-Espionage Law, Cybersecurity Law, National Intelligence Law) identified material divergences. Transfer Risk Assessments were not substantively robust; Article 49 derogations were rejected as transfers were systematic, repetitive and continuous. The transparency finding concerned TikTok's October 2021 EEA Privacy Policy, which failed to name China as a destination, covering the period 29 July 2020 – 1 December 2022. In a notable aggravating disclosure, TikTok admitted in April 2025 that limited EEA data had been stored on servers in China, after having previously told the DPC it was not ([19](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-tiktok-eu530-million-and-orders-corrective-measures-following), [23](https://www.arthurcox.com/knowledge/the-tiktok-decision-what-it-means-for-international-data-transfers)).
- **Status:** This is the most litigated of the fines in this list. The Irish High Court, in a judgment of **3 June 2026** ([2026] IEHC 347), largely upheld the DPC's decision, including the €530 million fine, and on 30 June 2026 again upheld the fine; however, the court **vacated and remitted the corrective/suspension orders** to the DPC for reconsideration, and the *quantum* of the fine remains open to TikTok's appeal. A stay on the suspension order was granted in November 2025 and upheld by the Supreme Court on 30 April 2026. As of 24 August 2026, the fine stands but is **not yet fully final as to amount** ([21](https://www.freevacy.com/news/irish-high-court/irish-high-court-upholds-dpcs-tiktok-decision-orders-review-of-corrective-measures/7462), [22](https://acompli.ie/news/tiktok-530-million-fine-upheld-2026), [20](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/29510-irish-data-protection-commission-issued-fine-of-eur-530-million-against-tiktok-and-ordered-suspension-of-data-transfers-to-china-in-absence-of-gdpr-compliance)).
- **Official DPA source:** [DPC press release](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-tiktok-eu530-million-and-orders-corrective-measures-following) ([19](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-tiktok-eu530-million-and-orders-corrective-measures-following))
- **Independent trackers:** [Digital Policy Alert case timeline](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/29510-irish-data-protection-commission-issued-fine-of-eur-530-million-against-tiktok-and-ordered-suspension-of-data-transfers-to-china-in-absence-of-gdpr-compliance) ([20](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/29510-irish-data-protection-commission-issued-fine-of-eur-530-million-against-tiktok-and-ordered-suspension-of-data-transfers-to-china-in-absence-of-gdpr-compliance)); [Arthur Cox briefing](https://www.arthurcox.com/knowledge/the-tiktok-decision-what-it-means-for-international-data-transfers) ([23](https://www.arthurcox.com/knowledge/the-tiktok-decision-what-it-means-for-international-data-transfers))

### 3. Meta Platforms Ireland Limited (Instagram) — €405 million — Irish DPC (2022)

- **Fine amount:** €405,000,000 (including €20 million for the Article 6(1) infringement) ([1](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-instagram-inquiry)).
- **Date:** Final decision adopted **2 September 2022**; announced **15 September 2022** ([1](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-instagram-inquiry)). The GDPR Enforcement Tracker records the announcement as 5 September 2022 ([2](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics)).
- **Issuing DPA:** Ireland — Data Protection Commission (DPC), as lead supervisory authority under Article 56 GDPR, following EDPB Binding Decision 2/2022 of 28 July 2022 ([1](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-instagram-inquiry)).
- **Infringements:** Articles **5(1)(a)** (lawfulness/fairness/transparency), **5(1)(c)** (data minimisation), **6(1)** (no lawful basis — added at the direction of the EDPB), **12(1)** (transparency), **24** (controller responsibility), **25(1)** (data protection by design), **25(2)** (data protection by default), and **35(1)** (DPIA) ([1](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-instagram-inquiry), [3](https://gdprfine.com/dpa/ireland-dpc)).
- **Conduct:** The inquiry concerned the public disclosure of children's email addresses and/or phone numbers via the Instagram "business account" feature, and a **public-by-default** setting for child users' personal accounts during registration. Children's contact details were published in profile HTML source code, and child accounts defaulted to public unless the child knew to change the setting. The DPC found Meta failed to implement appropriate technical/organisational measures and failed to carry out a DPIA despite high risks to children ([1](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-instagram-inquiry), [3](https://gdprfine.com/dpa/ireland-dpc)).
- **Status:** Meta appealed to the Irish High Court in October 2022; as of August 2026 the fine remains standing and under appeal, with no judgment reducing or annulling it ([2](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics), [32](https://measuredcollective.com/the-biggest-gdpr-fines-so-far-2026)).
- **Official DPA source:** [DPC press release](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-instagram-inquiry) ([1](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-instagram-inquiry))
- **Independent trackers:** [GDPR Enforcement Tracker — Statistics](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics) ([2](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics)); [GDPRfine.com — Ireland DPC profile](https://gdprfine.com/dpa/ireland-dpc) ([3](https://gdprfine.com/dpa/ireland-dpc))

### 4. Meta Platforms Ireland Limited (Facebook & Instagram) — €390 million — Irish DPC (2022/2023)

- **Fine amount:** €390,000,000 total — **€210 million for Facebook** and **€180 million for Instagram** ([6](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/data-protection-commission-announces-conclusion-two-inquiries-meta-ireland)).
- **Date:** Two final decisions adopted **31 December 2022**; announced **4 January 2023** ([6](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/data-protection-commission-announces-conclusion-two-inquiries-meta-ireland)).
- **Issuing DPA:** Ireland — Data Protection Commission (DPC), following EDPB binding decision of 5 December 2022 on the consistency mechanism ([6](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/data-protection-commission-announces-conclusion-two-inquiries-meta-ireland), [39](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/meta-ireland-fined-eu390-million-for-unlawful-legal-basis)).
- **Infringements:** Article **6 GDPR** (no entitlement to rely on the "contract" legal basis for personalised/behavioural advertising); transparency breaches under Articles **12**, **13(1)(c)** and **5(1)(a)** (with the EDPB directing an additional finding on the fairness principle) ([6](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/data-protection-commission-announces-conclusion-two-inquiries-meta-ireland), [39](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/meta-ireland-fined-eu390-million-for-unlawful-legal-basis)).
- **Conduct:** Before GDPR applied (May 2018), Meta Ireland changed its Terms of Service for Facebook and Instagram from a "consent" to a "contract" legal basis (Article 6(1)(b)) for most processing, **including behavioural advertising**; users had to click "I accept" to continue using the services. The EDPB found Meta is not contractually obligated to offer personalised ads (the obligation runs to advertisers), that transparency requirements are indispensable to the contract basis, and that Article 21(2)–(3) objections to direct marketing mean such processing is not "necessary" for the contract. The original DPC draft had proposed fines of only €23M (Instagram) and €36M (Facebook); the EDPB ordered significantly increased fines ([6](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/data-protection-commission-announces-conclusion-two-inquiries-meta-ireland), [39](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/meta-ireland-fined-eu390-million-for-unlawful-legal-basis)).
- **Status:** Under appeal **by both sides** — Meta appeals the substance; the DPC itself has brought an action for annulment before the CJEU challenging the EDPB's jurisdiction to direct a fresh open-ended investigation. No reduction or annulment as of August 2026 ([6](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/data-protection-commission-announces-conclusion-two-inquiries-meta-ireland), [32](https://measuredcollective.com/the-biggest-gdpr-fines-so-far-2026)).
- **Official DPA source:** [DPC press release](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/data-protection-commission-announces-conclusion-two-inquiries-meta-ireland) ([6](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/data-protection-commission-announces-conclusion-two-inquiries-meta-ireland))
- **Independent trackers:** [GDPR Enforcement Tracker — Statistics](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics) ([2](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics)); [Stibbe analysis](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/meta-ireland-fined-eu390-million-for-unlawful-legal-basis) ([39](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/meta-ireland-fined-eu390-million-for-unlawful-legal-basis))

### 5. TikTok Technology Limited — €345 million — Irish DPC (2023)

- **Fine amount:** €345,000,000 ([9](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/DPC-announces-345-million-euro-fine-of-TikTok)).
- **Date:** Final decision adopted **1 September 2023**; announced **15 September 2023** ([9](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/DPC-announces-345-million-euro-fine-of-TikTok)).
- **Issuing DPA:** Ireland — Data Protection Commission (DPC), following EDPB Binding Decision 2/2023 of 2 August 2023 ([9](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/DPC-announces-345-million-euro-fine-of-TikTok)).
- **Infringements:** Articles **5(1)(c)** (data minimisation), **5(1)(f)** (security), **5(1)(a)** (fairness — "dark patterns", added by the EDPB), **24(1)** (controller responsibility), **25(1)** (data protection by design), **25(2)** (data protection by default), **12(1)** (transparency), and **13(1)(e)** (information about recipients) ([9](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/DPC-announces-345-million-euro-fine-of-TikTok)).
- **Conduct:** The inquiry covered child users (under 13, under 16 and under 18) between 31 July and 31 December 2020. Failures included: **public-by-default** settings for child accounts (with an option to "skip" making accounts private); the **Family Pairing** feature, which allowed unverified adults to pair with child accounts and send direct messages, posing "severe possible risks to child users"; inadequate transparency wording in privacy notices; "dark patterns" nudging users toward more privacy-intrusive options; and failure to verify ages properly ([9](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/DPC-announces-345-million-euro-fine-of-TikTok), [10](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-66819174)).
- **Status:** TikTok brought legal action in the Irish High Court against the DPC and the Attorney General; the fine remains standing and under appeal as of 2026 trackers ([2](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics), [32](https://measuredcollective.com/the-biggest-gdpr-fines-so-far-2026)).
- **Official DPA source:** [DPC press release](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/DPC-announces-345-million-euro-fine-of-TikTok) ([9](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/DPC-announces-345-million-euro-fine-of-TikTok))
- **Independent trackers:** [GDPR Enforcement Tracker — Statistics](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics) ([2](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/statistics)); [BBC News report](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-66819174) ([10](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-66819174))

### 6. LinkedIn Ireland Unlimited Company — €310 million — Irish DPC (2024)

- **Fine amount:** €310,000,000 (approximately $335 million), comprising three administrative fines ([11](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-linkedin-ireland-eu310-million)).
- **Date:** Decision notified to LinkedIn **22 October 2024**; announced **24 October 2024** ([11](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-linkedin-ireland-eu310-million), [12](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/irish-regulator-fines-linkedin-310-million-euros-for-gdpr-violations)).
- **Issuing DPA:** Ireland — Data Protection Commission (DPC).
- **Infringements:** Articles **6(1)(a)** (invalid consent for third-party data), **6(1)(f)** (unlawful reliance on legitimate interests), **6(1)(b)** (unlawful reliance on contractual necessity), **5(1)(a)** (fairness), **13(1)(c)** and **14(1)(c)** (transparency about lawful bases) ([11](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-linkedin-ireland-eu310-million), [12](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/irish-regulator-fines-linkedin-310-million-euros-for-gdpr-violations)).
- **Conduct:** The inquiry (opened 20 August 2018 following a complaint by French NGO La Quadrature du Net) examined LinkedIn's processing of members' data — both data provided directly and data obtained from third-party partners — for behavioural analysis and targeted advertising. The DPC found LinkedIn had no valid lawful basis for these activities: consent was not freely given, sufficiently informed, specific and unambiguous; LinkedIn's legitimate interests did not outweigh members' rights; and the processing was not necessary for any contract with members ([11](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-linkedin-ireland-eu310-million), [12](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/irish-regulator-fines-linkedin-310-million-euros-for-gdpr-violations)).
- **Status:** Appeal status unclear. Goodwin noted it was unclear whether LinkedIn would appeal; Measured Collective's 2026 ranking lists the fine as under appeal. No court judgment reducing or annulling it has been reported ([32](https://measuredcollective.com/the-biggest-gdpr-fines-so-far-2026), [34](https://legit.eu?post_type=resources&p=5054)).
- **Official DPA source:** [DPC press release](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-linkedin-ireland-eu310-million) ([11](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-linkedin-ireland-eu310-million))

### 7. Uber B.V. / Uber Technologies Inc. — €290 million — Dutch AP (2024)

- **Fine amount:** €290,000,000 — the largest fine ever imposed by the Dutch DPA ([13](https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/current/dutch-dpa-imposes-a-fine-of-290-million-euro-on-uber-because-of-transfers-of-drivers-data-to-the-us)).
- **Date:** Announced **26 August 2024**; GDPRhub records the decision date as 22 July 2024, published 25 August 2024 ([13](https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/current/dutch-dpa-imposes-a-fine-of-290-million-euro-on-uber-because-of-transfers-of-drivers-data-to-the-us), [14](https://gdprhub.eu/index.php?title=AP_%28The_Netherlands%29_-_Uber)).
- **Issuing DPA:** Netherlands — Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (AP), as lead supervisory authority for Uber's EU headquarters.
- **Infringements:** Article **44 GDPR** (transfer of personal data to a third country without appropriate safeguards under Chapter V) ([14](https://gdprhub.eu/index.php?title=AP_%28The_Netherlands%29_-_Uber), [13](https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/current/dutch-dpa-imposes-a-fine-of-290-million-euro-on-uber-because-of-transfers-of-drivers-data-to-the-us)).
- **Conduct:** Uber Technologies Inc. (San Francisco) and Uber B.V. were joint controllers for drivers' data. EEA drivers' personal data (account details, taxi licences, location, photos, payment details, identity documents, and in some cases criminal and medical data) was processed on US servers. Uber had used SCCs but **removed them from its joint controllership agreement on 6 August 2021**, arguing SCCs did not apply to its US processing; the violation ran until Uber certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework in November 2023. The AP rejected Uber's arguments that Article 3 and Chapter V are mutually exclusive, that no "transfer" occurred, and that Article 49 derogations applied (the transfers were systematic, repetitive and ongoing). The complaint originated from more than 170 French drivers via the Ligue des droits de l'Homme ([14](https://gdprhub.eu/index.php?title=AP_%28The_Netherlands%29_-_Uber), [42](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/global-data-hub/2025/eu-case-digest/gdh---dutch-dpa-imposes-record-breaking-fine-of-eur-290-million)).
- **Status:** Under appeal — Uber announced its intention to appeal; Dutch courts are expected to consider questions referred to the CJEU on the Article 3/Chapter V relationship ([14](https://gdprhub.eu/index.php?title=AP_%28The_Netherlands%29_-_Uber), [43](https://www.willkie.com/publications/2024/11/dutch-data-protection-authority-fines-uber-290-million-for-gdpr-data-transfer-violation)).
- **Official DPA source:** [AP press release](https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/current/dutch-dpa-imposes-a-fine-of-290-million-euro-on-uber-because-of-transfers-of-drivers-data-to-the-us) ([13](https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/current/dutch-dpa-imposes-a-fine-of-290-million-euro-on-uber-because-of-transfers-of-drivers-data-to-the-us))

### 8. Meta Platforms Ireland Limited (Facebook) — €265 million — Irish DPC (2022)

- **Fine amount:** €265,000,000 ([4](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-in-facebook-data-scraping-inquiry)).
- **Date:** Decision adopted **25 November 2022**; announced **28 November 2022** ([4](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-in-facebook-data-scraping-inquiry)).
- **Issuing DPA:** Ireland — Data Protection Commission (DPC), with the agreement of all other EU supervisory authorities under Article 60 (no EDPB Article 65 procedure) ([4](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-in-facebook-data-scraping-inquiry)).
- **Infringements:** Articles **25(1)** and **25(2)** GDPR (data protection by design and by default) — lower-tier infringements under Article 83(4) ([5](https://gdprfine.com/cases/meta-265-million-dpc-2022)).
- **Conduct:** The "Facebook data scraping" inquiry. Third parties abused Facebook Search, Facebook Messenger Contact Importer and Instagram Contact Importer to match large lists of phone numbers against user profiles, compiling personal data on **more than 500 million users** (approx. 533 million) — names, Facebook IDs, phone numbers, addresses, birth dates and email addresses — published on a hacking forum in April 2021. The features lacked safeguards against enumeration and bulk matching; the DPC centred the inquiry on design-by-default failures rather than Article 32 security ([4](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-in-facebook-data-scraping-inquiry), [5](https://gdprfine.com/cases/meta-265-million-dpc-2022)).
- **Status:** Meta appealed; in May 2024 the Irish High Court (Justice Garrett Simons) **adjourned the appeal** until the CJEU resolves Meta's separate appeal against the €225 million WhatsApp fine, stating the €265 million fine "will not be legally effective until the EU case is resolved." The fine stands but payment is not legally enforceable pending those proceedings ([30](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/business/meta-appeal-irish-dpc-data-privacy-fine-eu-whatsapp-case-court)).
- **Official DPA source:** [DPC press release](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-in-facebook-data-scraping-inquiry) ([4](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-in-facebook-data-scraping-inquiry))

### 9. Meta Platforms Ireland Limited — €251 million — Irish DPC (2024)

- **Fine amount:** €251,000,000 total, comprising four fines: **€8 million** (Article 33(3)), **€3 million** (Article 33(5)), **€130 million** (Article 25(1)) and **€110 million** (Article 25(2)) ([15](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-meta-eu251-million), [16](https://gdprhub.eu/index.php?title=DPC_%28Ireland%29_-_Meta_%22View-as%22_feature)).
- **Date:** Final decisions adopted **12 December 2024**; announced **17 December 2024** ([15](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-meta-eu251-million)).
- **Issuing DPA:** Ireland — Data Protection Commission (DPC).
- **Infringements:** Articles **33(3)** (failure to include necessary details in breach notification), **33(5)** (failure to document the breach contemporaneously), **25(1)** (data protection by design) and **25(2)** (data protection by default) ([15](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-meta-eu251-million), [16](https://gdprhub.eu/index.php?title=DPC_%28Ireland%29_-_Meta_%22View-as%22_feature)).
- **Conduct:** The September 2018 Facebook "View As" token breach, affecting approximately **29 million accounts globally** (about 3 million in the EU/EEA). A vulnerability in the "View As" feature combined with the "Happy Birthday Composer" function allowed attackers to generate fully permissioned user tokens granting full access to other users' profiles. Affected data included names, email addresses, phone numbers, locations, dates of birth, religion, gender, timeline posts, group memberships and children's data. The DPC found Meta had not implemented appropriate design/default measures (tokens gave unnecessarily broad access) and had failed to notify/document the breach correctly ([15](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-meta-eu251-million), [16](https://gdprhub.eu/index.php?title=DPC_%28Ireland%29_-_Meta_%22View-as%22_feature), [17](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/decisions/inquiries-meta-platforms-ireland-limited-token-breach)).
- **Status:** Meta announced it would appeal the decision (Reuters, 17 December 2024). No court judgment as of August 2026; the fine stands ([15](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-meta-eu251-million)).

### 10. Enel Energia S.p.A. — €79,107,101 — Italian Garante (2024)

- **Fine amount:** €79,107,101 (over €79 million) — at the time the **highest fine ever issued by the Italian Garante** ([38](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/9988921)).
- **Date:** Decision No. 81 of **8 February 2024**; press release dated **29 February 2024** ([38](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/9988921)).
- **Issuing DPA:** Italy — Garante per la protezione dei dati personali.
- **Infringements:** The Garante found serious shortcomings in Enel Energia's information systems used for customer management and service activation, in particular failures to implement measures preventing unauthorised agents from exploiting easy access points into the systems — i.e., violations of the GDPR's security and accountability obligations (the integrity/confidentiality principle in Article 5(1)(f) and the requirements of Articles 24, 25 and 32), together with unlawful processing linked to aggressive telemarketing ([38](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/9988921), [34](https://legit.eu?post_type=resources&p=5054)). The official decision page (in Italian) contains the full legal analysis.
- **Conduct:** Following a Guardia di Finanza investigation that had already produced fines against four companies, the Garante established that Enel Energia had acquired **978 contracts** from companies that were not part of its sales network. Unauthorised agents exploited weak security in Enel's systems for years, generating nuisance calls, service promotions and contract signings; at least **9,300 contracts** were activated as a result ([38](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/9988921)).
- **Status:** The decision was **upheld by the Court of Rome** (judgment No. 12783/2025, published 18 September 2025), which dismissed Enel's objection; a further appeal is currently pending. This is therefore the most "final" of the 2024 fines ([38](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/9988921)).
- **Official DPA source:** [Garante press release](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/9988921) ([38](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/9988921))

## Excluded and Flagged Cases

### Google LLC / Google Ireland Limited — €325 million (CNIL, September 2025) — NOT a GDPR fine

The French CNIL fined **Google LLC €200 million** and **Google Ireland Limited €125 million** (total **€325 million**) on 1 September 2025 for displaying advertisements between Gmail users' emails without prior consent and for collecting invalid consent for advertising cookies during Google account creation. The CNIL expressly stated the proceedings fall under the **ePrivacy Directive** (Article 82 of the French Data Protection Act and Article L.34-5 of the French Postal and Electronic Communications Code), **not the GDPR**, and that the GDPR one-stop-shop mechanism therefore did not apply. For this reason it is **excluded from the GDPR list above**, although several commercial trackers miscategorise it as a GDPR fine. The cookie breach affected more than 74 million accounts ([24](https://www.cnil.fr/en/cookies-and-advertisements-inserted-between-emails-google-fined-325-million-euros-cnil), [41](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/insights-practices-dpc-cnil-imposes-record-325-million-fine)). For the same reason, the CNIL's earlier €150 million Google and €60 million Facebook cookie fines (announced January 2022) are also not GDPR fines and fall outside the window.

### Amazon Europe Core S.à r.l. — €746 million (CNPD Luxembourg, 2021) — annulled March 2026; outside window

The Luxembourg CNPD's €746 million fine of 15 July 2021 (previously the largest GDPR fine ever) was **annulled on 12 March 2026** by Luxembourg's Administrative Court **on procedural grounds only**: the CNPD failed to establish whether Amazon acted with fault (intent or negligence) before fining it, as required by CJEU case law (*Deutsche Wohnen*, C-807/21, and *Nacionalinis*, C-683/21). The court upheld the substantive findings and remanded the case to the CNPD to redo the fault and sanction-selection analysis. The fine is therefore not in effect and no adjusted amount has been set. It is outside the 2022–2026 window in any event ([25](https://cnpd.public.lu/en/actualites/national/2026/03/arret-ca-amazon-cnpd.html), [26](https://privacyterms.io/biggest-gdpr-fines-all-time)).

### Unverified "draft fines": €2.5 billion Meta fine and €520 million Instagram fine

No evidence was found in DPA records, enforcement trackers or news reports of a pending draft DPC decision proposing a **€2.5 billion fine against Meta** or a **€520 million Instagram fine**. The €2.5 billion figure corresponds to the **cumulative total** of fines imposed on Meta Ireland by the DPC (WhatsApp €225M; Instagram €405M; Facebook scraping €265M; Instagram €180M; Facebook €210M; Facebook transfers €1.2B), not a single proposed fine ([31](https://keepabl.com/news/meta-ireland-gdpr-fines)). The €520 million figure appears to be a conflation of the €530 million TikTok fine (2025) and the €405 million Instagram fine (2022). These purported draft fines should not be included in any GDPR fine list.

## Enforcement Context (2022–2026)

- Cumulative GDPR fines since 25 May 2018 reached approximately **€7.1 billion** by January 2026 (DLA Piper's January 2026 survey; CMS's tracker counts €6.11 billion across 2,685 recorded fines as of 1 March 2026) ([28](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/01/dla-piper-gdpr-fines-and-data-breach-survey-january-2026), [29](https://cms.law/en/int/publication/GDPR-Enforcement-Tracker-Report/numbers-and-figures)).
- Roughly **€1.2 billion** in GDPR fines was issued in both 2024 and 2025; 2023 was skewed by the €1.2 billion Meta fine (87% of all EU GDPR fines that year came from the Irish DPC) ([28](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/01/dla-piper-gdpr-fines-and-data-breach-survey-january-2026), [36](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2025/01/dla-piper-gdpr-fines-and-data-breach-survey-january-2025)).
- Ireland's DPC accounts for **€4.04 billion (~57%)** of all GDPR fine value, but only about €20 million (0.5%) has actually been collected because most major fines are under appeal; companies generally comply with corrective orders despite appealing headline amounts ([27](https://www.uniconsent.com/blog/gdpr-enforcement-fines-2026)).
- The EDPB's Article 65 consistency mechanism repeatedly increased fines in this period (Instagram: Article 6 finding added; Meta €390M: from ~€59M draft to €390M; Meta €1.2B: from zero to €1.2B; TikTok €345M: fairness finding added) ([1](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-instagram-inquiry), [6](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/data-protection-commission-announces-conclusion-two-inquiries-meta-ireland), [7](http://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/decisions/inquiry-concerning-data-transfers-eueea-us-meta-platforms-ireland-limited-its-facebook-service)).
