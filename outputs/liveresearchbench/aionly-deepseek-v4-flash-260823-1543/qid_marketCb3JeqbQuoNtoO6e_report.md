# Electric Truck Market Report 2025–2026: Size, Demand, Competitive Landscape, and Strategic Drivers

## Executive Summary

The global electric truck market underwent a structural transformation in 2025: **global electric truck sales doubled year-over-year to exceed 400,000 units for the first time, reaching 9% of all global truck sales** — surpassing the EV sales share for buses and light commercial vehicles [1][2]. Growth was overwhelmingly concentrated in China, which accounted for over 90% of global electric truck sales; one in four trucks sold in China in 2025 was electric, and in December 2025 electric trucks outsold diesel trucks in China for the first time in any month [1][2][3]. Battery-electric powertrains represented 97% of all electric truck sales globally, while hydrogen fuel cell trucks remained commercially marginal after Nikola's Chapter 11 bankruptcy and Hyzon's closure in February 2025 [2][4].

The North American market diverged sharply from this global picture. The US electric pickup segment **contracted 15.6% in 2025 to 90,019 units**, and the competitive lineup was reshaped by three consequential events: **Ford discontinued the F-150 Lightning** (the 2025 segment sales leader), **Stellantis canceled the battery-electric Ram 1500 REV before launch**, and **GM indefinitely suspended its next-generation full-size electric truck program** while continuing to sell current-generation Silverado EV, Sierra EV, and Hummer EV models [5][6][7][8]. Rivian — the fourth named OEM — posted a weak 2025 (42,247 deliveries, −18% YoY) but began the pivotal R2 production ramp in 2026 and delivered 12,194 vehicles in Q2 2026 [9][10].

The regulatory environment in North America turned decisively against mandates in 2025–2026: Congress revoked California's Advanced Clean Trucks and Advanced Clean Cars II waivers under the Congressional Review Act (June 2025), and EPA finalized the rescission of the 2009 greenhouse gas Endangerment Finding in February 2026, repealing federal vehicle GHG standards [11][12][13]. The $7,500 consumer EV credit (30D) and the $40,000 commercial clean vehicle credit (45W) were terminated for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025, under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act [14][15]. Commercial fleet demand has nonetheless remained the sturdiest pillar of adoption, anchored by Amazon's 100,000-van Rivian order (40,000+ deployed), USPS's 66,000-vehicle electrification plan, and a 90% repeat-purchase rate among fleet operators who have already adopted EVs [16][17][18].

## 1. Market Size & Demand, 2025–2026

### 1.1 Global Unit Sales: The 400,000-Unit Inflection

**2025 actuals (IEA Global EV Outlook 2026):**
- Total electric truck sales: **exceeded 400,000 units** (fifth consecutive year of growth; roughly doubled vs. 2024) [1][2].
- Electric heavy freight trucks (HFT, >15 tonnes): **~230,000 units**, up ~174% from ~84,000 in 2024; HFT electric share reached 9% (up from 3%) [2][19].
- Electric medium freight trucks (MFT, 3.5–15 tonnes): **210,000 units**, up 65% from ~127,000; share reached 9% (up from 6%) [2][19].
- Electric trucks reached **9% of all global truck sales**, surpassing the EV share of buses and light commercial vehicles [1][2].
- Battery-electric powertrains: **97% of all electric truck sales** [2][19].

**Historical trajectory (units):**

**2026 outlook and long-term forecasts:**
- IEA: based on current policies, electric trucks reach **at least 20% of global truck sales by 2035** (60% in China); TCO parity with diesel expected in Europe by 2030 [1].
- BloombergNEF: electric vans, trucks, and passenger cars surpass **50% of global sales by 2035**; over two-thirds of car, van, and truck sales electric by 2040 [24].
- JD Power/EV Volumes (July 2026): global EV sales forecast at 23.3 million units in 2026 (+7.8% YoY); electric LCV sales soared 43.0% in Jan–May 2026, with pickups up 11.5% [25].

### 1.3 Segmentation by Vehicle Class

**Light-duty / Class 1–2 pickups (US):**
- Total US electric pickup sales in 2025: **90,019 units, down 15.6% from 2024** (Cox Automotive data) [5].
- Model-by-model: Ford F-150 Lightning 27,307 (−18.5%); Tesla Cybertruck 20,237 (−48.1%); GMC Hummer EV pickup/SUV 15,788 (+12.8%); Chevrolet Silverado EV 11,275 (+51.8%); GMC Sierra EV 7,996 (up from 1,788); Rivian R1T 7,416 (−33.1%) [5].
- Q1 2026 (Cox data): Cybertruck 3,519 (−45% YoY); F-150 Lightning 2,060 (discontinued); Rivian R1T 1,658 (−4%); Silverado EV 1,406 (−41%); Sierra EV 1,288 (+3%) [33][34].
- Class 2B–3 (including heavier EV pickups and vans): **10.8% of Class 2B–3 sales were electric in Q1 2025**, up from 0.1% in 2021 (Atlas EV Hub) [35].

**Medium-duty (Class 3–6) vs. heavy-duty (Class 7–8):**
- Globally (2025): HFT sales ~230,000 units vs. MFT sales ~210,000 units [2][19].
- US (2025): over 95% of the 17,000 US electric truck sales were MFTs, driven by Rivian's ~10,000 Amazon delivery vans [2]. Class 4–8 electric penetration was only ~0.6% in Q1 2025, though a six-fold increase from 2021 [35].
- China (2025, ICCT): ZE heavy trucks and tractor-trailers nearly tripled from 82,300 (2024) to 232,000 units; ZE medium truck sales doubled to ~172,000. Heavy trucks reached 28% battery-electric share (second most popular powertrain behind diesel at 46%); medium trucks reached 22% BEV share [36].
- China full-year NEV heavy-truck registrations: 231,100 units (+182% YoY); NEV share of heavy trucks hit 29% vs. 14% in 2024 [37].
- US Class 8 specifics: Tesla Semi volume production began April 2025 (~50 units/week); the median US battery-electric Class 8 tractor price rose to **$411,200 in MY2025** vs. $172,500 for a diesel equivalent (ICCT) [29][38].

### 1.4 Segmentation by Drivetrain (BEV vs. Hydrogen Fuel Cell)

- **Battery-electric: 97% of global electric truck sales in 2025** [2][19]. BloombergNEF notes electric medium/heavy trucks now outsell fuel-cell trucks by nearly 100-to-1 [39].
- **Hydrogen fuel cell trucks: negligible and contracting in North America.** Nikola filed Chapter 11 on February 19, 2025 (2024: 282 FCEVs produced, 200 shipped) [4]. Hyzon closed in February 2025; Kenworth and Peterbilt delayed fuel-cell T680/579 production indefinitely; **Hyundai is now the only Class 8 fuel-cell tractor supplier in North America**, with only ~14 XCIENT tractors in service (Benore Logistic Systems) [40][41]. The North American hydrogen fuel-cell heavy-duty truck market was valued at just $3.2 million in 2025 (~7 units) [42]. S&P Global Mobility projects fuel-cell trucks at **less than 0.5% of global truck output by 2032** [43].

### 1.5 Segmentation by Region

**China (dominant):**
- >90% of global electric truck sales in 2025; electric HFT share reached 28% (up from 13%), MFT share 24% (up from 16%) [1][2].
- December 2025: electric/new-energy trucks hit ~50% of monthly truck sales; electrive recorded 45,300 NEV heavy-truck registrations at **54% share — the first month electric trucks outsold diesel in China** [3][37].
- ICCT: 2025 ZE-MHDV sales reached **457,000 units (+98% YoY)**; ZE-MHDV share 25% [36].
- Battery-swap-capable truck sales reached ~68,300 units in 2025 (+131%), ~15% of China's electric truck sales [2][36].
- Growth drivers: TCO parity with diesel, a scrappage scheme worth up to ~$20,000 per truck, Stage 4 HDV fuel standards (July 2025), and a national target of **40% new-energy share for heavy trucks by 2030** [2][44].

**Europe:**
- 2025 electric truck sales grew 40% to ~17,000 units (3% share) [2][19]. Germany led with 4,400 (+30%); the UK grew 55%+ to top 3,000; France reached 1,900; Sweden doubled to 1,000 [2][19].
- Electric trucks were 4.2% of EU truck sales in 2025, up from 2.3% in 2024 — but prices remain roughly three times diesel equivalents [45].
- Inflection point: six+ Chinese manufacturers (BYD, Farizon/Geely, Sany, Sinotruk, Windrose, SuperPanther) plan electric HD truck launches in Europe in 2026, priced up to 30% below the ~€320,000 European average [46].

**North America:**
- US electric truck sales (medium/heavy): 17,000 units in 2025 (+25%), despite the ACT repeal and EPA rollbacks; Canada grew 35% to 2,700; North America totaled ~20,000 [2][19].
- US electric pickup (light-duty) sales: 90,019 units (−15.6%) [5]. CALSTART data show cumulative zero-emission truck deployments reached **72,309 units by end of 2025, up 37% YoY**, with ZETs at 4.14% of new truck deployments in H2 2025 [47].
- The overall US EV market contracted sharply after the September 30, 2025 expiration of the federal credit: Q4 2025 EV sales plunged 37% YoY to ~230,000 units (5.7% share); Q1 2026 sales fell 28% YoY to 212,600 units; Q2 2026 improved to 247,226 units (−20.5% YoY) [48][49][50].

### 1.6 Segmentation by Price Tier

- **Entry/work-truck tier ($50K–$56K):** Chevrolet Silverado EV Work Truck ($52,800 fleet; $55,395 consumer); Ford F-150 Lightning ($54,780 base — though 2025 actual base MSRP was ~$49,780–$51,975); 2026 Lightning STX at $63,345 after cuts [6][51][52][53].
- **Mid-tier ($60K–$75K):** GMC Sierra EV Elevation ($62,400); Rivian R1T ($72,990); Silverado EV LT ($69,100) and Trail Boss ($70,000) [51][54].
- **Premium tier ($80K+):** Tesla Cybertruck ($79,990); GMC Hummer EV Pickup ($97,200–$117,470); Rivian R1T Premium/Performance/Quad ($79,990–$115,990); 2025 Sierra EV Denali ($89,900) [51][54][55][56].
- **Class 8:** median US BEV tractor price $411,200 vs. $172,500 diesel (MY2025) — the electric premium widened 27% since MY2020 [38]. Globally, electric truck purchase prices remain 2–3× diesel equivalents [2].
- **Chinese pricing advantage:** 400–600 kWh electric trucks priced $58,000–$85,000 in China vs. Western electric trucks starting near $250,000 — a roughly 3:1 cost advantage [57]. Europe's average electric HD truck price: ~€320,000 [46].

## 2. Customer Segments & Purchase Drivers

### 2.1 Segment Weights

- **Retail buyers represented 54.65% of US electric pickup demand in 2025**, but **commercial/leasing fleets were the fastest-growing end-user segment at 29.43% CAGR** (Mordor Intelligence) [17].
- **Last-mile delivery is the leading global application at 24.2% share** of electric truck demand (Market.us) [21].
- In the US medium/heavy-duty market, fleets are the overwhelming channel: 95%+ of 2025 US electric truck sales were medium freight trucks, dominated by ~10,000 Rivian Amazon vans [2].
- A Cox Automotive analysis found only 14% of fleets operate EVs today, but **87% expect to add EVs within five years**; 90% of current EV-fleet operators plan to acquire additional EVs — and fleet owners project EVs will reach 43% of the average fleet in five years [18].

### 2.2 Commercial Fleets (the primary growth engine)

**Anchor fleet commitments:**

| Fleet | Commitment | Status (as of mid-2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon | 100,000 Rivian EDVs by 2030 | 20,000+ deployed end-2024; 30,000 by end-2025; **40,000+ and 1 billion miles** by Q2 2026 [10][58][59] |
| FedEx | 100% electric global pickup/delivery fleet by 2040; initial BrightDrop orders | 500 EV600s delivered 2021–22; 2,000 additional; potential for 20,000 [60][61] |
| Walmart | 5,000 BrightDrop vans reserved (Jan 2022); zero-emissions logistics by 2040 | 400 Chevrolet BrightDrop vans deployed for InHome in 2024 [60][62] |
| AT&T | Rivian pilot for commercial fleet (Dec 2023) | Expanded to 5G connectivity for R2 (June 2026) [63][64] |
| USPS | 66,000+ BEVs by 2028 (incl. 45,000+ electric NGDVs); 9,250 E-Transits planned | 7,465 E-Transits acquired; ~2,500 NGDVs delivered by Nov 2025 [65][66] |
| Uber | Up to 50,000 autonomous robotaxis on R2 platform; $1.25B equity (March 2026) | Initial $300M expected Q2 2026 [10][67] |
| Duke Energy | 100% of light-duty fleet electric by 2030; 50% of MHD fleet zero-carbon by 2030 | ~600 EVs in fleet [68] |

**Fleet purchase drivers:**
- **TCO:** Electric delivery trucks cut fuel costs up to 60% vs. diesel; maintenance ~$0.11/km vs. $0.15/km diesel; brakes last up to 200,000 km with regenerative braking [21][69]. BrightDrop studies found ~$7,000/year savings per EV600 vs. diesel [60]. A Class 3 delivery van's 8-year TCO: ~$85,000 electric vs. ~$125,000 diesel [70]. ATRI's 2026 cost study shows industry-average truck operating cost hit a record $2.336/mile in 2025 [71].
- **Incentives (pre-September 2025):** 45W commercial credit up to $40,000 for vehicles over 14,000 lbs; state programs (California HVIP: up to $60,000/truck; New York rebates; utility make-ready programs covering 20–40% of EVSE costs) [15][70][72]. The Electrification Coalition urged fleets to sign binding contracts before the September 30, 2025 termination [73].
- **ESG mandates:** Amazon's Climate Pledge (net-zero 2040), FedEx carbon-neutral 2040, Walmart zero-emissions logistics 2040, and the Ceres-led Corporate Electric Vehicle Alliance (~$1 trillion annual revenue, 1.5 million fleet vehicles) [61][74]. Corporate fleet decarbonization mandates are estimated as a +3.5% CAGR market driver [17].
- **Operational fit:** fixed depot routes, overnight parking, predictable duty cycles [18]. Recommended first candidates: last-mile delivery (<100 mi/day), urban service, fixed-route shuttles, high-mileage vehicles [70].
- **Barriers:** acquisition cost, charging infrastructure gaps, working with utilities (12–18-month interconnection timelines), cold-weather range loss (25–50% in extreme cold), and battery replacement costs [18][70][75].

### 2.3 Consumer / Retail Buyers

- Retail is still the majority of US electric pickup demand (54.65%), but contracted sharply in 2025: the segment fell 15.6% [5][17].
- **EV ownership and intent (EPIC/AP-NORC, Oct 2025):** only 7% of Americans own an EV; 37% are at least somewhat likely to purchase one next; 78% of owners would buy another. Cost is the #1 barrier (8 in 10 cite it); range and charging time follow [76].
- **Loyalty is high:** Plug In America surveys show 92% (2025) and 94.3% (2026) of EV drivers say their next vehicle will likely be an EV [77][78]. BCG finds 71% of EV owners intend to stay with EVs; Gen Z/Millennials are the growth cohort, with higher willingness to buy online and lower brand loyalty [79].
- **Price tier exposure:** the end of the $7,500 credit effectively raised prices 7.5–14% for most EVs. EV incentives hit an all-time high of 14.8% of ATP in June 2025; by June 2026 incentives eased to 13% of ATP (~$7,290 per vehicle) [50][80].
- EV pickup consideration (Cox, 2023 baseline): F-150 Lightning led consideration at 67%, followed by Ram 1500 REV and Chevrolet (63%), Hummer and Rivian (48%), Cybertruck (27%) [81].

### 2.4 Government / Municipal Fleets

- **USPS is the single largest government EV commitment**: at least 60,000 NGDVs with 75% BEV proportion (45,000+ electric), 21,000 additional commercial BEVs through 2028, and ~$9.6 billion total investment including $3 billion from the IRA [65][82]. Deployment has lagged: only ~2,500 NGDVs delivered by November 2025, with 6,000+ E-Transits held at storage lots pending charging infrastructure [66].
- **State procurement mandates** remain in force in many states despite federal rollbacks: Connecticut (100% state light-duty EV purchases by 2030), New York (100% passenger fleet ZEVs by 2035; MHD by 2040), Washington (100% passenger/light-duty by 2035), California (50% light-duty ZEV purchases; 15% of heavy vehicles zero-emission by Dec 2025, rising to 30% by 2030) [83][84].
- **CARB Advanced Clean Fleets** still applies to state and local government fleets (CARB withdrew its federal waiver request in January 2025, but state/local application does not require a waiver); the High-Priority and Drayage provisions were formally repealed in September 2025 following litigation settlements [85][86][87].
- Government procurement was dampened in 2025–2026 by the federal purchasing freeze and the termination of 45W, but state-level obligations continue to underpin municipal bus and utility truck orders [88].

## 3. Infrastructure & Regulatory Factors

### 3.1 Public Charging Networks and NACS

- **NACS (SAE J3400) transition is staggered.** As of July 2026, only 32 of 96 tracked EV models ship with native NACS ports; most 2025–2026 electric trucks (Ford F-150 Lightning, GM Silverado/Sierra EV, Hummer EV, Ram ProMaster EV) still use CCS1 with adapters [89][90].
- Native NACS models include the 2026 Rivian R1/R2, Hyundai Ioniq 5 (first non-Tesla native port), and 2026+ GM models only at the Cadillac Optiq level; GM's Equinox/Blazer switch in 2027 [89][90].
- **Tesla Supercharger access** is open to Ford, Rivian, GM, Stellantis, VW, Hyundai/Kia, and most other brands via adapters; Rivian and Ford had free adapter programs (Ford's ended; Rivian's ended), while GM sells its adapter (~$225) [91][92].
- **Tesla's network**: 23,500+ V3/V4 stalls; non-Tesla access began February 2024 (Ford first) [91]. Tesla operates ~54% of US public fast-charging ports [93].
- **Ionna** (JV of BMW, GM, Honda, Hyundai, Kia, Mercedes-Benz, Stellantis, Toyota): 200+ "Rechargeries" built, targeting 1,000+ sites by end of 2025 and 30,000 bays by 2030 [92].

### 3.2 NEVI Program: Freeze, Restoration, and Budget Cuts

- The $5 billion NEVI formula program was **frozen in February 2025** by FHWA guidance rescission; a federal court ordered funding restored in June 2025 (Washington v. USDOT; summary judgment for 20 states and DC in January 2026) [94][95][96].
- **Revised NEVI guidance (August 11, 2025)** gave states flexibility: release of all remaining funds in one solicitation, removal of the 50-mile spacing requirement, expanded site eligibility (including medium- and heavy-duty charging), and elimination of community outreach/Justice40 requirements [97][98].
- **Deployment remains slow**: only 96 NEVI-funded stations were operational at end-2025; states spent just $94 million of $4.4 billion in 2025 (2%); Pennsylvania and Ohio lead [94][99].
- **FY2026 funding cuts**: Congress rescinded **$879 million in unused NEVI funds** and cut $500 million from the program; only $300 million was authorized for 2026 [100][101]. FHWA is also considering raising the Buy America domestic-content threshold for chargers to 100% [102].
- Practical implication: NEVI will contribute little to truck charging in the 2025–2026 window; **private investment is carrying the build-out** (Tesla, Pilot, Greenlane, utility programs) [94].

### 3.3 Depot and Megawatt Charging (MCS)

- **MCS standard (CharIN/SAE J3271)** supports up to **3.75 MW per connector** (1,250V, up to 3,000A), enabling 20–80% charges in under 30 minutes — fitting within mandatory driver rest breaks [103][104]. IEC TS 63379 publication standardized the connector; commercial deployment began 2025–2026 [103].
- **Commercial charge rates**: Tesla Megacharger 1,200 kW (2 operational sites, 64 more "coming soon" as of Feb 2026); ABB 1,200 kW; Huawei 1,440 kW (deployed Aug 2025 in China); Kempower 1,000+ kW (first North American MCS deployment March 2026); Siemens Sicharge Flex up to 1.68 MW [104][105][106].
- **Tesla Semi + Pilot Flying J partnership**: MCS stalls at up to 1.2 MW along I-5/I-10 freight corridors; first sites expected summer 2026; targeting 37 locations in 2026 and 46 by early 2027 [105][107].
- **Greenlane** (Daimler Truck NA / NextEra Energy Resources / BlackRock JV): first corridor on I-15 between Los Angeles and Las Vegas (Colton, Barstow, Baker); flagship Colton site opened April 2026 with 40+ high-speed chargers, future-proofed for MCS; second corridor announced on I-10 (Blythe, CA and Phoenix, AZ) [108][109][110].
- **Grid constraints are the binding bottleneck**: a 100-truck depot needs 20+ MW of grid capacity; grid connection costs $3–8 million for a 20 MW connection with 12–24-month lead times; depot charging will supply 70–80% of truck charging electricity through 2030 [104]. NREL's 80/20 rule: ~80% of fleet energy is satisfied at the depot [111].
- **Demand-charge economics**: unmanaged charging can create 2,800 kW peaks at a 150-van depot costing $42,000+/month in demand charges; managed charging cuts peaks to 20–40% of unmanaged and can reduce electricity costs up to 40% [111]. Over 180 US utilities now offer EV fleet programs and TOU rates [111].

### 3.4 EPA Emissions Rules: 2025–2026 Rollback

- **March 2025**: EPA announced formal reconsideration of the 2009 Endangerment Finding, the MY2027+ light-/medium-duty GHG rule, and Phase 3 heavy-duty GHG standards [112].
- **February 12, 2026**: EPA **finalized the rescission of the 2009 Endangerment Finding** and repealed all subsequent GHG standards for light-, medium-, and heavy-duty vehicles and engines — described as the largest deregulatory action in US history (~$1.3 trillion claimed savings). Legal challenges consolidated in the DC Circuit; effective April 20, 2026 [12][13][113].
- **Phase 3 heavy-duty GHG standards** (originally requiring up to 60% GHG cuts for MY2032 trucks) were repealed as part of this action [12][114].
- Manufacturers no longer have GHG measurement, control, or reporting obligations for MY2012–2027 vehicles; criteria pollutant rules (NOx, PM) remain in force [12][13].

### 3.5 California Waivers and State Mandates

- **Congressional Review Act revocations (June 12, 2025)**: President Trump signed H.J. Res. 87 (Advanced Clean Trucks waiver), H.J. Res. 88 (ACC II waiver), and H.J. Res. 89 (Low-NOx Omnibus waiver) — the first time the CRA was used to void California clean-air waivers [11][115][116].
- **Litigation**: California and 10 states sued (California v. United States, N.D. Cal.); the Ninth Circuit appeal is pending. The DOJ issued cease-and-desist letters to OEMs over the Clean Truck Partnership [117][118]. In March 2026, DOJ and DOT sued California over the "illegal EV mandate" under the Energy Policy and Conservation Act [119].
- **ACT status**: CARB's Advanced Clean Trucks regulation required 5–9% ZEV sales in MY2024 escalating to 55–75% by 2035 (varies by Class); 10 states had adopted ACT. Enforcement is now uncertain pending litigation; several states (Oregon, Vermont, Washington) have paused or stopped enforcement [86][120][121]. CARB announced California met its ZEV truck sales target two years early — before the revocation [122].
- **ACF status**: CARB withdrew its waiver request (Jan 2025); High-Priority and Drayage provisions were repealed (Sept 2025); **state/local government fleet requirements remain in effect**; CARB is developing a new "Drive Forward Fleet" initiative [85][86][87].
- **Supreme Court — Diamond Alternative Energy v. EPA (June 20, 2025, 7–2):** fuel producers have standing to challenge California's waiver reinstatement based on "predictable, commonsense inferences" about regulation-induced market effects — easing future challenges to California's rules [123][124].

### 3.6 Federal Tax Credits: 30D, 45W, 30C

| Credit | Pre-change | Status after OBBBA (signed July 4, 2025) |
|---|---|---|
| 30D new clean vehicle | Up to $7,500 | **Terminated** for vehicles acquired after Sept 30, 2025 (binding contract + payment by that date protects the credit) [14][15] |
| 25E used clean vehicle | Up to $4,000 | Terminated for vehicles acquired after Sept 30, 2025 [15] |
| 45W commercial clean vehicle | Up to $7,500 (light-duty) / $40,000 (over 14,000 lbs) | **Terminated** by Section 70503 of OBBBA for vehicles acquired after Sept 30, 2025; IRS guidance (Aug 21, 2025) clarified "acquisition" via binding contract + nominal down payment [14][15][125] |
| 30C charging infrastructure | 30% of install costs, up to $1,000 (home) / $100,000 (commercial) per port | Still available for property placed in service **by June 30, 2026** [15] |

- A new **auto loan interest deduction** (2025–2028) allows up to $10,000/year deductible interest on new, US-assembled vehicles with income phase-outs [15].
- CBO/JCT estimate: taxpayers would have claimed **$14.4 billion in 45W credits by 2028** absent termination [125].
- State-level incentives remain: California HVIP (up to $60,000/truck), LADWP residential charger rebates, Clean Cars 4 All, and a patchwork of other state rebates [70][72][76].
- **Impact confirmed by data**: Q4 2025 US EV sales plunged 37% YoY after the credit expired; Q1 2026 fell another 28% YoY [48][49]. Cox Automotive: the credit's expiration "will test whether the electric vehicle market is mature enough to thrive on its own fundamentals" [126].

### 4.2 Rivian

**Production & deliveries:**

## 5. Partnerships & Technology Differentiators

### 5.1 Battery Technology

- **LFP adoption**: Ford's **BlueOval Battery Park Michigan** began producing US-built LFP cells in June 2026 — the first automaker-backed LFP plant in the US (~20 GWh annual capacity); LFP cells support Ford's midsize electric trucks and energy storage business [195][196]. BYD and Chinese OEMs use LFP almost exclusively; Chinese LFP pack costs fell to ~$53/kWh in 2024 [197].
- **GM's battery realignment**: GM sold its Lansing Ultium Cells stake to LG Energy Solution (December 2024, ~$1 billion); the BlueOval SK JV was dissolved with SK On taking control of the Stanton, TN plant (May 2026); GM's next-generation EV platform will use **new prismatic cells** debuting with the 2028 Cadillac Escalade IQ [198][199][200].
- **Rivian's cost engineering**: the R2 platform uses large die castings (−32% part cost), a new drive unit (−25%), simplified suspension (−72%), and structural battery integration; the R2's bill of materials is ~50% of the R1's, and at scale the R2 will cost less than half as much to build [145][201].
- **Cell chemistry splitting in Class 8**: most Chinese electric heavy trucks offer 350–500 kWh LFP packs with 200–700 km range [36].

### 5.2 Charging Partnerships

- **Tesla Megacharger + Pilot Flying J**: MCS stalls up to 1.2 MW; first sites summer 2026; 37 locations targeted in 2026 [105][107].
- **Greenlane** (Daimler Truck NA, NextEra, BlackRock): I-15 LA–Las Vegas corridor open; I-10 Phoenix corridor announced; Colton flagship with 40+ chargers; first customer Nevoya piloting the Windrose R700 electric truck with 772 kW dual-gun charging [108][109][110].
- **Ionna**: 7-automaker JV (BMW, GM, Honda, Hyundai, Kia, Mercedes-Benz, Stellantis, Toyota); 200+ Rechargeries; 1,000+ sites target by end 2025; 30,000 bays by 2030 [92].
- **NACS**: Tesla's connector is now SAE J3400; Rivian offers native NACS on 2026 R1/R2; GM/Ford remain CCS1 with adapters through 2026–2027; NEVI 2026 guidance requires dual CCS1 + NACS connectors at new sites [89][90][202].
- **Milence** (Daimler Truck, Traton, Volvo JV): European high-power charging network; demonstrated megawatt charging at Landvetter, Sweden (20–80% in ~30–45 min at up to 1,440 kW) [203].

### 5.3 Software, Autonomy, and Vertical Integration

- **Rivian–Volkswagen Group JV ($5.8 billion)** is the largest technology deal in the space: VW licensed Rivian's zonal electrical architecture and software stack; the JV generated $282M of Rivian's software/services revenue in Q1 2026 and $308M in Q2 2026; first VW models use the technology from 2027 (Scout Motors Traveler/Terra 2027; Audi from ~2028). VW holds 12.3% of Rivian [147][148][204][205]. Reported frictions emerged in late 2025 (integration complexity), but funding milestones continue to be met [206].
- **Rivian–Uber robotaxi partnership** (March 2026): up to 50,000 Level 4 autonomous R2-based robotaxis by 2028; Uber committing up to $1.25B in equity [10][67].
- **Ford Pro software**: ~840,000 paid Ford Pro Intelligence subscriptions (+30% YoY in Q4 2025); 1.6 million total paid customer subscriptions (+50% YoY) by Q2 2026; Ford's Universal EV Platform ("assembly tree" subassembly manufacturing) is the manufacturing differentiator for the 2027 $30K pickup [158][159][160][207].
- **GM's approach**: EV variable-profit-positive status achieved Q4 2024; GM guided EV losses to improve another $1.0–1.5B in 2026; GM's second-generation software-defined vehicle architecture (ICE and EV) debuts in 2028. GM's pivot: double down on profitable ICE trucks (960K full-size pickups sold in 2025, best since 2007) while keeping EVs at the "end game" [169][178][200][208].
- **Chinese OEM cost architecture**: integrated e-axles, simplified architecture, and LFP batteries give Chinese entrants (BYD, Windrose, SuperPanther, Sany, Farizon, Sinotruk) a ~3:1 cost advantage; Windrose's E700 offers a 670-km range, 35-minute charging, at €250,000 — 22% below the European average [46][57].

## 6. Outlook: 2026 and Beyond

**The 2025–2026 period was the electric truck market's high-water mark globally and its stress test in North America.** China's market — now at ~30% NEV share for heavy trucks and on a trajectory toward the 40%-by-2030 national target — is the engine of global volume [36][44]. Chinese OEMs are now exporting that advantage to Europe at scale, threatening incumbent margins [46]. IEA projects electric trucks reach at least 20% of global truck sales by 2035, with China at 60% [1].

In North America, the demand picture is split:
- **Light-duty electric pickups contracted** (−15.6% in 2025) and lost two flagship nameplates (F-150 Lightning ended; Ram 1500 REV BEV canceled). Ford, GM, and Stellantis all publicly walked back EV commitments, with Ford taking a $19.5B write-down and GM suspending its next-gen electric truck program [5][7][8][186].
- **Fleet-oriented medium-duty EVs are growing** — CALSTART recorded a 37% surge in 2025 ZET deployments (72,309 cumulative), and Rivian's commercial van business grew through the R2 transition [47][212].
- **The federal policy tailwind has been removed**: 30D/45W credits are gone, the EPA GHG framework was rescinded, and California's mandates are in litigation limbo. What remains are TCO economics (favorable at depot-charged, high-utilization duty cycles), state-level procurement mandates, corporate ESG commitments, and the used-EV market's maturation (used EV prices within $1,300 of ICE equivalents in Q1 2026) [49][84][111].

The competitive landscape entering 2027: Rivian is scaling R2 and will launch R3/R3X from Georgia in 2028; Ford's next electric truck is a $30,000 UEV-platform pickup in 2027 and an EREV truck with 700+ miles of range; GM continues current-generation Ultium trucks while its next-gen program is suspended (successors no earlier than 2030); Ram's only electrified pickup is the EREV Ram 1500 REV (690-mile range). **The winner in this environment may not be the OEM with the best electric truck — it will be the OEM with the best cost structure, the strongest fleet relationships, and the least exposure to battery-metal and charging-infrastructure risk.**
