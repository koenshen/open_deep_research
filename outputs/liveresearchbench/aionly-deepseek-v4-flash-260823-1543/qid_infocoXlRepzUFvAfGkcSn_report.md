# U.S. Banks Over $50 Billion with Less-than-Satisfactory CRA Ratings (2019–2024)

## Scope and Methodology

The Community Reinvestment Act (CRA) requires federal banking regulators to evaluate whether insured depository institutions are meeting the credit needs of their entire communities, including low- and moderate-income neighborhoods. The four possible CRA ratings are **"Outstanding," "Satisfactory," "Needs to Improve,"** and **"Substantial Noncompliance"** [21]. For this report, "less-than-satisfactory" means either "Needs to Improve" or "Substantial Noncompliance."

This report covers U.S. banks with total assets greater than **$50 billion** supervised by either the **Federal Reserve** (state member banks) or the **Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC)** (national banks and federal savings associations). Official ratings were sourced from the OCC's CRA performance evaluation pages and search tool [4][6], the Federal Reserve's CRA searchable database [5], and the FFIEC's central CRA ratings portal [23]. The evaluation window is 2019–2024, based on the public release of the most recent evaluation in that period.

## 1. Wells Fargo Bank, National Association

- **Regulator:** OCC
- **CRA Rating:** Needs to Improve
- **Evaluation Published:** June 2020 (OCC June 2020 CRA monthly evaluation release) [1]
- **Current Most Recent Rating:** Outstanding (published February/March 2025) [7][8]
- **Assets:** Far above $50 billion; Wells Fargo is one of the largest U.S. banks by assets

Wells Fargo's June 2020 OCC evaluation is listed in the OCC's monthly CRA performance evaluation page for June 2020 [1]. The bank's own announcement explained that although the exam rating was "Outstanding," the final overall rating was downgraded to "Needs to Improve" because of past legal issues [2].

This was not the first time Wells Fargo received a CRA downgrade. In March 2017, the OCC lowered Wells Fargo's CRA rating from "Outstanding" to "Needs to Improve" — the first time since 1994 it had received anything other than Outstanding — following the fake-accounts scandal, a $190 million CFPB settlement, and other legal actions [14]. That earlier downgrade predates the 2019–2024 window, but it provides important context for the 2020 rating.

The OCC's later February 2025 CRA evaluation release (published March 3, 2025) shows Wells Fargo Bank, NA received an **"Outstanding"** CRA rating, including outstanding ratings on the lending, investment, and service tests [7][8]. Thus, while Wells Fargo's only less-than-satisfactory evaluation within 2019–2024 was the June 2020 "Needs to Improve," its current most recent CRA rating is "Outstanding."

- **Official OCC disclosure:** [OCC CRA Performance Evaluations: June 2020](https://www.occ.gov/topics/consumers-and-communities/cra/perfomance-evaluations-by-month/2020/cra-performance-evaluations-jun-2020.html)
- **Bank announcement:** [Wells Fargo Receives 'Outstanding' Community Reinvestment Act Rating](https://newsroom.wf.com/English/news-releases/news-release-details/2020/Wells-Fargo-Receives-Outstanding-Community-Reinvestment-Act-Rating/default.aspx)
- **News coverage:** American Banker covered the earlier 2017 downgrade [14]; the 2020 rating was disclosed by the bank itself [2].

## 2. City National Bank (Los Angeles, California)

- **Regulator:** OCC
- **CRA Rating:** Needs to Improve
- **Evaluation Published:** April 2024 (OCC April 2024 CRA monthly evaluation release) [3]
- **Evaluation Date Range:** March 2023 – January 2024 [3]
- **Assets:** Well above $50 billion; subsidiary of Royal Bank of Canada

City National Bank, headquartered in Los Angeles, is an OCC-regulated national bank and a subsidiary of Royal Bank of Canada. The OCC's April 2024 CRA performance evaluation page lists City National Bank as receiving **"Needs to Improve"** [3]. That monthly page covers 13 evaluations conducted between March 2023 and January 2024, across large bank, small bank, and intermediate small bank examination categories [3]. The exact exam date for City National is not separately stated in the monthly listing; the evaluation was made public in April 2024.

No independent news article specifically covering City National's 2024 "Needs to Improve" rating was identified in the research. The OCC's official monthly disclosure is the authoritative source [3]. Note that **City National Bank of Florida** is a separate institution and is not related to this finding.

- **Official OCC disclosure:** [OCC CRA Performance Evaluations: April 2024](https://www.occ.gov/topics/consumers-and-communities/cra/perfomance-evaluations-by-month/2024/cra-performance-evaluations-apr-2024.html)
- **News coverage:** None identified.

## Why Less-Than-Satisfactory CRA Ratings Are Rare

Less-than-satisfactory CRA ratings are uncommon. A "Needs to Improve" or "Substantial Noncompliance" rating can pause a bank's growth strategy by preventing branch openings, mergers, or acquisitions; it creates significant reputational risk since the rating is public; and it often leads the regulator to shorten the time before the next examination [22]. Under the Bank Merger Act, regulators also consider CRA performance when evaluating merger applications [23]. These consequences help explain why the overwhelming majority of large banks maintain at least a "Satisfactory" rating.

## Data Notes and Verification

- OCC monthly evaluation pages list banks by the month the evaluation was made public. The OCC's searchable CRA database allows filtering by both **Evaluation Date** and **Public Release Date** [4].
- For both qualifying banks, the exact exam date is not stated in the monthly OCC pages; users should use the OCC's CRA search tool to retrieve the individual PDF performance evaluations [4].
- The Federal Reserve database can be filtered by overall rating, asset size, exam year, and exam method for independent verification [5].
- The FFIEC maintains a central portal linking to CRA ratings published by the FDIC, Federal Reserve, and OCC [23].
