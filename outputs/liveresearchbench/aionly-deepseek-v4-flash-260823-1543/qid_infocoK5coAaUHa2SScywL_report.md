# Comprehensive Due-Diligence Report: Client-Facing AI Portfolio-Management and Financial-Planning Copilots Launched by U.S. and Canadian Banks & Broker-Dealers (2023–2026)

## Executive Summary

This report identifies and analyzes U.S. and Canadian banks and broker-dealers that launched production-grade, client-facing AI portfolio-management or financial-planning copilots during the 2023–2026 period. The research focuses exclusively on tools that are directly accessible by end-clients (not advisor-facing or internal-only systems), positioned as AI copilots or assistants for portfolio management or financial planning, and launched in production (not pilot or beta).

Based on comprehensive research across major U.S. and Canadian financial institutions, five client-facing AI copilots meeting the strict criteria were identified: **JPMorgan IndexGPT** (thematic investment baskets, May 2024), **Citi Portfolio Intelligence** (portfolio insights, April 2026), **Citi Sky** (AI-powered virtual assistant, Summer 2026), **Desjardins Alvie** (personal financial management assistant, September 2024), and **RBC NOMI** (personal finance suite with cash-flow forecasting, originally launched 2017 but with new AI features added within the 2023–2026 period). Notably, the vast majority of AI tools launched by major U.S. and Canadian wealth managers during this period are advisor-facing copilots rather than client-facing tools, reflecting a strategic preference for augmenting human advisors rather than directly serving clients.

## 5. Key Findings and Industry Observations

**1. Limited Client-Facing AI Copilots in Wealth Management:** The research reveals that, despite extensive AI investments by major U.S. and Canadian banks, only a small number of truly client-facing AI portfolio-management or financial-planning copilots have reached production during 2023–2026. The vast majority of AI tools in wealth management are advisor-facing, designed to augment human advisors rather than serve clients directly.

**2. Preference for Human-in-the-Loop Models:** Institutions like Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo have explicitly stated that AI should enhance rather than replace human advisors. Bank of America's "bifurcated AI strategy" deliberately avoids generative AI for client-facing tools, using proprietary supervised machine learning with pre-defined answers for reliability, while deploying generative AI aggressively in controlled internal environments.

**3. Canadian Innovation in Client-Facing AI:** Desjardins' Alvie stands out as a notable Canadian innovation, launching within 4.5 months of contract signing and achieving strong early engagement metrics. RBC's NOMI suite, while predating the 2023–2026 window, has been continuously enhanced with new AI features like NOMI Forecast, demonstrating sustained investment in client-facing AI.

**4. Citi's Dual Approach:** Citi is unique among U.S. banks in having launched two distinct client-facing AI tools: Portfolio Intelligence (data-driven portfolio insights) and Citi Sky (conversational AI avatar). This dual approach reflects a broader ambition to serve clients through multiple AI channels.

**5. Regulatory and Compliance Considerations:** The slow adoption of client-facing AI copilots reflects significant regulatory caution. CIRO's Annual Compliance Report 2026 explicitly states that FinOps examiners will ask dealers about AI use and review operational controls. The report notes: "The real risk is client data entering a public AI tool that the firm does not control" and endorses "an approved, governed AI surface plus a clear use policy."

**6. Measurable Impact Where Deployed:** Where client-facing AI tools have been deployed, early results are encouraging. Desjardins reported +30% engagement and 15.3% click-through rates. RBC's NOMI achieved 53% user satisfaction and <1% customer attrition. However, comprehensive KPI data remains limited, particularly for recently launched tools.
