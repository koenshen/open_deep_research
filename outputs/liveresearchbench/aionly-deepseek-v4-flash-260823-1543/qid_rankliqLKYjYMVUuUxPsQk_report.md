# Top 25 Electric Vehicles of 2026: Ranked by EPA Range, 0–60 Acceleration, and Starting MSRP

## Executive Summary

This report ranks the top 25 electric vehicles (EVs) sold in the U.S. market as of August 2026 using a composite score that equally weights three criteria: EPA-estimated driving range (miles), 0–60 mph acceleration time (seconds), and starting MSRP (USD). The dataset covers 42 vehicles with complete, verifiable data across all three metrics, drawn from official manufacturer sites, EPA-derived figures, and instrumented independent testing from Car and Driver, MotorTrend, and Edmunds.

The results reveal a clear hierarchy: **Lucid dominates the top of the rankings**, taking four of the top twelve positions, including the overall #1 — the Lucid Air Grand Touring — which leads the entire field in both range (512 miles) and acceleration (3.0 seconds). The middle of the table is defined by **value-oriented models** like the Tesla Model 3, Mercedes-Benz CLA, Toyota bZ, and Volvo EX30, which score strongly on price. New-for-2026 models — including the BMW iX3, Lucid Gravity, Rivian R2, and Audi A6 e-tron — feature prominently, reflecting a market that has shifted decisively toward 800-volt architectures, native NACS charging, and longer-range base trims.

## Ranking Methodology

### Data Collection and Sources

Data was compiled from official manufacturer websites (Tesla, Rivian, Lucid, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Audi, Toyota, Volvo, Genesis, Subaru, Ford, Chevrolet, Kia, Hyundai, and others), EPA-estimated range figures as reported by manufacturers and corroborated by reputable automotive publications, and independent instrumented 0–60 mph testing from Car and Driver, MotorTrend, and Edmunds. Where manufacturer claims and independent tests diverged materially, the independent instrumented result was used, as it represents the more reliable real-world figure (e.g., Tesla Model Y: 5.9 seconds per Car and Driver vs. 6.8 seconds claimed; Toyota bZ AWD: 4.4 seconds per MotorTrend vs. 4.9 seconds claimed; Porsche Macan Electric: 5.0 seconds per Car and Driver vs. 5.4 seconds claimed).

### Inclusion Criteria

- All EV models sold in the U.S. market as of August 2026 were considered.
- Each vehicle required a complete data triple: EPA-estimated combined range (miles), 0–60 mph time (seconds), and starting MSRP (base trim, before destination fees and incentives).
- Base-trim specifications were used for all three metrics to ensure internal consistency. Where a base trim lacked a published 0–60 time, the lowest-priced trim with a published acceleration figure was used, and this is noted in the caveats.
- Model year is noted for each vehicle; where a 2027 model-year vehicle is already on sale (e.g., Lucid Gravity, BMW iX3), its current pricing is used.

### Composite Score Calculation

Each criterion was normalized to a 0–1 scale using min-max normalization across the full candidate dataset of 42 vehicles:

- **Range score** = (range − 216) / (512 − 216), where 216 miles is the lowest range in the dataset (Mini Countryman SE) and 512 miles is the highest (Lucid Air Grand Touring). Higher range = higher score.
- **Acceleration score** = (9.4 − 0-60 time) / (9.4 − 3.0), where 9.4 seconds is the slowest time in the dataset (Hyundai Ioniq 9 S) and 3.0 seconds is the fastest (Lucid Air Grand Touring). Faster acceleration = higher score.
- **Price score** = ($163,200 − MSRP) / ($163,200 − $28,995), where $163,200 is the highest starting price in the dataset (Mercedes-Benz G 580) and $28,995 is the lowest (Chevrolet Bolt LT). Lower price = higher score.
- **Composite score** = (Range score + Acceleration score + Price score) / 3.

This approach gives equal weight (1/3 each) to all three criteria, as required by the research brief.

## Analysis

### Rationale Behind the Ranking

The composite scoring system rewards vehicles that deliver a balanced combination of long range, strong acceleration, and reasonable pricing. Because all three criteria are equally weighted, no single attribute can carry a vehicle to the top of the table. The Lucid Air Grand Touring's #1 ranking reflects its unique position as the only vehicle in the dataset to achieve the maximum score on both range (512 miles, best-in-class) and acceleration (3.0 seconds, best-in-class); its high $114,900 price is the only drag on its composite score [15][16]. The Air Touring and Air Pure rank #2 and #4 respectively, giving Lucid three of the top four spots — a testament to the brand's efficiency leadership, 900-volt electrical architecture, and class-leading aerodynamic efficiency (0.197 drag coefficient) [14][16].

Value-oriented models climb the rankings through price. The Tesla Model 3 RWD ($36,990) and Model Y RWD ($39,990) score in the top 15 largely because they pair near-best-in-class price scores (0.94 and 0.92 respectively) with respectable 321-mile range and mid-pack acceleration [1][3]. The Mercedes-Benz CLA 250+ is a standout value story: at $47,250, it delivers 374 miles of EPA range — the most of any vehicle in the top 15 except the Lucid Air models — and is the first Mercedes EV with a native NACS port and 320-kW DC fast charging [32][33]. Its 6.6-second 0–60 time is its weakness, but the range-per-dollar value is exceptional.

Performance-focused EVs like the Rivian R2 Performance, Cybertruck, and R1T/R1S rank highly on acceleration (0.6–0.94 normalized scores) but are held back by price and, in some cases, range. The Rivian R2 Performance is the highest-ranked vehicle under $60,000, pairing 330 miles of range with a 3.6-second 0–60 time and a $57,990 starting price — a compelling all-around package [12].

### Notable Trends

**1. Lucid's dominance is unprecedented.** Four of the top twelve vehicles are Lucids. The Air Grand Touring's 512-mile EPA range remains the industry benchmark, and the Gravity SUV — a three-row, seven-passenger SUV with up to 450 miles of range and a 3.4-second 0–60 time — demonstrates that the brand's efficiency advantages translate beyond sedans [17]. The Gravity was named 2026 World Luxury Car of the Year [17].

**2. 800-volt architecture is now mainstream.** The BMW iX3 (Neue Klasse, 800V, 400-kW charging), Audi A6 e-tron (800V), Lucid Gravity, and Mercedes CLA all feature 800-volt electrical systems, enabling faster charging and, critically, longer range from smaller batteries [29][30][17][32]. The iX3's 383-mile EPA range at a $61,500 starting price makes it one of the strongest new entries of 2026 [29].

**3. Value-priced EVs with long range are the market's sweet spot.** The CLA 250+ (374 miles at $47,250), iX3 (383 miles at $61,500), and A6 e-tron (348 miles at $67,900) all rank in the top 14, showing that range-per-dollar is a decisive factor in the composite score [32][29][30]. The Toyota bZ — formerly the bZ4X — jumped to #8 after its 2026 refresh added up to 314 miles of range, 338 hp on AWD models, and a $6,800 price cut [18][19]. MotorTrend's instrumented 4.4-second 0–60 test for the XLE AWD made it one of the quickest value EVs on sale [19].

**4. Acceleration has become table stakes.** Only two vehicles in the top 25 have 0–60 times above 6.0 seconds (the Solterra at ~6.0 and the CLA at 6.6). The slowest vehicle in the full dataset — the Hyundai Ioniq 9 S at 9.4 seconds — ranked near the bottom despite 335 miles of range and a competitive $58,955 price, illustrating how heavily a poor acceleration score penalizes the composite [40].

**5. The federal tax credit's expiration has reshaped pricing.** The $7,500 clean-vehicle tax credit expired on September 30, 2025, meaning all MSRPs in this report are pre-incentive [4][47]. Manufacturers have responded with aggressive pricing: the Chevrolet Bolt returns at $28,995 (the cheapest EV in America), the Nissan Leaf starts at $31,845, and Tesla's Model 3 now starts at $36,990 [8][41][1].

### Caveats and Data Limitations

1. **Incomplete data excluded several notable models.** The Hyundai Ioniq 5, Kia EV6, Chevrolet Equinox EV, Honda Prologue, Nissan Leaf, Chevrolet Blazer EV, Silverado EV, GMC Sierra EV, Hummer EV, Cadillac Lyriq, and Genesis GV60 were excluded from the ranked table because no reliable 0–60 mph time was captured for their base trims in the available research. Several of these — particularly the Ioniq 5 ($35,000, 303 miles) and EV6 ($37,900, 319 miles) — would likely have ranked in the top 25 had complete data been available [43][42][44].
2. **Tesla Model S and Model X status is uncertain.** Multiple third-party sources report that the Model S and Model X were discontinued in 2026, though Tesla's official Model X page was still live during research [4][5][48]. If the Model S Plaid were included, its 2.1-second 0–60 and ~400-mile range would place it near the top of the rankings. The figures above therefore reflect Tesla's confirmed 2026 lineup of Model 3, Model Y, and Cybertruck.
3. **Ford F-150 Lightning discontinued.** Production ended December 2025, so it was excluded; its replacement, the $30,000 Ford Fathom, arrives in 2027 [36].
4. **Base-trim vs. max-range specification variance.** Where a model's base trim had no published 0–60 time, an alternate trim was used (e.g., Toyota bZ XLE AWD rather than the 236-mile base XLE). This creates minor inconsistencies, noted in the table.
5. **Assumptions:** The Rivian R1T's Large-battery range (329 miles) was assumed to match the R1S Dual Large, as the 420-mile figure applies to the more expensive Max battery [11][13]. The Subaru Solterra base 0–60 is a Car and Driver estimate, not an instrumented result [20].
6. **Model-year mixing:** Several 2027 model-year vehicles (Lucid Gravity, BMW iX3, Porsche Macan 2027 base at $80,300) are already on sale in 2026 and were included at their current pricing. The Porsche Macan was ranked using its 2026 MSRP of $82,650; the 2027 base price is $2,350 lower, which would marginally improve its score [24][25].
7. **MSRP excludes destination fees and incentives.** All prices are base-trim MSRP before destination charges and before any dealer or manufacturer incentives. The federal tax credit expired in September 2025 [4][47].
8. **Regional availability:** The Polestar 4 is being withdrawn from the U.S. market, and the Volvo EX30 is discontinued after the 2026 model year, yet both remain on sale as of August 2026 and were included [39][34].
