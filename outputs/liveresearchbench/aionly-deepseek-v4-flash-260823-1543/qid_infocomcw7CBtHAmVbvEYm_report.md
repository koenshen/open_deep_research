# CEO and Board Chair Combined: Fortune Global 500 Companies (as of August 23, 2026)

## Overview and Methodology

The 2026 Fortune Global 500 — the 37th annual ranking of the world's largest corporations by revenue — was published by Fortune on July 28, 2026 (print edition dated August 18, 2026) [1][2]. The list ranks companies on 2025 fiscal-year revenue, which totaled $43.1 trillion across the 500 companies (up 3.2%), with record aggregate profits of $3.39 trillion [2][3]. The United States leads with 141 companies, followed by Greater China with 122, Japan with 40, Germany with 28, and France with 24 [3][4]. The 2026 top 10 includes Amazon (No. 1), Walmart (No. 2), State Grid (No. 3), UnitedHealth Group (No. 4), Saudi Aramco (No. 5), Apple (No. 6), McKesson (No. 7), Alphabet (No. 8), CVS Health (No. 9), and China National Petroleum (No. 10) [3].

This report identifies companies on the 2026 Fortune Global 500 where, as of August 23, 2026, the same executive simultaneously serves as both Chief Executive Officer and Chairman of the Board (or the equivalent top governance role in non-US corporate structures). For each qualifying company, the report provides the executive's full name, the date the dual role was assumed, a link to the most recent proxy statement (DEF 14A) for US filers, and independent news confirmation that the executive currently holds both titles.

**Documentation note:** DEF 14A proxy statements are filed only with the US Securities and Exchange Commission and are therefore available only for US-listed companies. Non-US companies on the Fortune Global 500 do not file DEF 14A; for those companies, the closest equivalent official documentation (governance pages, official biographies, regulatory announcements) is provided in lieu of a DEF 14A, along with independent news confirmation. The SEC EDGAR links provided for US companies route directly to each company's DEF 14A filing history; the most recent proxy statement in each list confirms the combined role by identifying the executive as both Chairman and CEO.

The research confirmed **18 qualifying companies** — 6 based in the United States and 12 based in other countries — where the CEO and Board Chair roles are held by the same person as of the cutoff date. A further set of prominent Global 500 companies was reviewed and excluded because the roles are split between different individuals or the leadership structure changed before the cutoff.

## Governance Context

The combined CEO/Board Chair role remains a significant corporate governance issue. According to a Conference Board/ESGAUGE survey, the percentage of S&P 500 companies combining the roles declined from 49% in 2018 to 44% in 2022, though CEOs serve as board chairs at 55% of companies with annual revenue of $50 billion or more [74]. Among the 2026 Fortune Global 500 companies identified in this report, founder-CEOs (Zuckerberg, Ma, Wang, Lei, Son, Arnault) and long-tenured executives (Dimon, Woods, Pouyanné, Yanai) are the most common profile. Activist shareholders have targeted some of these arrangements: the National Legal and Policy Center filed an exempt solicitation against ExxonMobil's Darren Woods in part because "Woods holds both CEO and Chair titles," and a shareholder group filed a proposal calling on UnitedHealth to separate the roles [22][26].
