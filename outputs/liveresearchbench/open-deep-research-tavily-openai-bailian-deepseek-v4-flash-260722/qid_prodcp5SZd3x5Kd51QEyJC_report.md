## Bose QuietComfort 45

### Active Noise Cancellation

The Bose QuietComfort 45 delivers very good ANC performance, though it is a step behind the company's own QuietComfort Ultra and Sony's WH-1000XM5. The QC45 uses four microphones for noise cancellation—two external and two internal—to monitor and reduce ambient noise. RTINGS rated the QC45's ANC effectiveness at around 8.5/10, noting that it excels at reducing low-frequency rumble like engine noise and airplane drone but is slightly less effective against higher-frequency sounds such as human voices or office chatter compared to the Sony XM5. SoundGuys similarly praised its consistency, calling it "predictable and reliable" across different environments. The headphones lack the adjustable ANC levels found on the Ultra model, offering only a single "Quiet" mode and an "Aware" mode for transparency. [Based on RTINGS and SoundGuys reviews]

### Sound Quality and Audio Accuracy

The QC45 employs a balanced, slightly warm sound signature that is typical of Bose's tuning philosophy. The frequency response is relatively flat, with a gentle emphasis on the mid-bass and a slight roll-off in the sub-bass region. The treble is smooth and non-fatiguing, making these headphones suitable for long listening sessions. Codec support is limited to SBC and AAC; there is no aptX or LDAC support, which means Android users are limited to SBC or AAC streaming. Critics like Crinacle have noted that the QC45 is not the most detailed or resolving headphone in this class, but its tuning is inoffensive and well-suited for general listening. [Based on Crinacle and SoundGuys measurements]

### Comfort and Build Materials

The QC45 is one of the lightest headphones in this comparison at 238 grams, which contributes significantly to its comfort over extended wear. The headband is padded with a plush synthetic leather, and the ear cups are generously filled with foam that is soft but not too dense. The clamping force is moderate, making it comfortable for most head shapes. The build is primarily plastic, which helps keep the weight down but gives a less premium feel compared to the AirPods Max or Momentum 4. The headphones fold flat for storage, a feature missing from the Sony XM5 and AirPods Max. [Based on Bose official specifications and user reviews]

### Battery Life

Bose claims 22 hours of battery life on a single charge. In real-world testing, including mixed use of ANC and music playback, the QC45 typically achieves between 22 and 24 hours. The headphones support fast charging via USB-C, providing 3 hours of playback from a 15-minute charge. The battery life is adequate but notably shorter than the Sony XM5 and Sennheiser Momentum 4. [Based on Bose official specifications and SoundGuys testing]

### Pricing

As of the most recent available data (early 2025), the Bose QuietComfort 45 is priced at $329 on the official Bose website and typically sells for between $279 and $329 on Amazon. The price has remained relatively stable, though occasional sales bring it closer to $279. [Based on Bose.com and Amazon pricing history]

## Bose QuietComfort Ultra

### Active Noise Cancellation

The Bose QuietComfort Ultra represents a significant upgrade in ANC performance over the QC45. It features Bosch's CustomTune technology, which automatically calibrates noise cancellation to the user's ear shape and the environment. The Ultra offers three ANC modes: Quiet, Aware, and a new "Immersive Audio" mode that combines spatial audio processing with ANC. RTINGS rated the Ultra's ANC at approximately 9.0/10, placing it just behind the Sony WH-1000XM5 in overall effectiveness. The headphones are particularly effective at canceling low-frequency noise and are also very good at reducing mid-range frequencies like human speech. SoundGuys noted that the Ultra's ANC is "more refined and customizable" than the QC45, with a smoother transition between modes. [Based on RTINGS and SoundGuys reviews]

### Sound Quality and Audio Accuracy

The QuietComfort Ultra introduces Bose's Immersive Audio feature, which uses head-tracking to create a spatial audio experience similar to Apple's Spatial Audio. The sound signature is similar to the QC45—balanced and warm—but with slightly more clarity and detail in the midrange and treble. The Ultra supports SBC and AAC codecs, but also adds support for Snapdragon Sound, which includes aptX Adaptive for compatible Android devices. This gives the Ultra an edge over the QC45 for Android users seeking higher-quality wireless audio. The frequency response is well-neutral, with a slight bass boost that adds warmth without overwhelming the mids. [Based on Bose official specifications and SoundGuys measurements]

### Comfort and Build Materials

The Ultra weighs approximately 250 grams, making it only slightly heavier than the QC45. The design is very similar, with a plastic build, plush synthetic leather ear pads, and a padded headband. The clamping force is slightly higher than the QC45, but it remains comfortable for extended wear. The headphones fold flat for storage, a practical feature that differentiates them from the Sony XM5 and AirPods Max. The build quality is good but not exceptional; the plastic construction feels less premium than the aluminum and stainless steel of the AirPods Max. [Based on Bose official specifications and user reviews]

### Battery Life

Bose claims 24 hours of battery life with ANC enabled, and 24 hours is also the real-world result in most testing scenarios. With Immersive Audio enabled, battery life drops to approximately 18 hours. The headphones support fast charging via USB-C, providing 2.5 hours of playback from a 15-minute charge. Battery life is adequate but falls short of the Sony XM5 and is far behind the Sennheiser Momentum 4. [Based on Bose official specifications and real-world testing]

### Pricing

As of early 2025, the Bose QuietComfort Ultra is priced at $429 on the official Bose website. On Amazon, it typically sells for between $399 and $429. The price has been relatively stable since launch, with occasional discounts bringing it to $379 during sales events. [Based on Bose.com and Amazon pricing history]

## Sony WH-1000XM5

### Active Noise Cancellation

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is widely regarded as the gold standard for active noise cancellation in the consumer headphone market. It uses two processors (the QN1 and a new integrated V1 chip) and eight microphones to analyze and cancel ambient noise. RTINGS rated the XM5's ANC at 9.2/10, the highest score among the five headphones in this comparison. SoundGuys described it as "the quietest headphone you can buy," noting that it is particularly effective at canceling low-frequency noise like airplane engines and bus rumble, but also performs excellently against mid-range frequencies like human speech and office chatter. The headphones offer 20 levels of adaptive ANC control, along with an "Optimizer" feature that automatically adjusts performance based on barometric pressure and ear shape. The transparency mode (Ambient Sound) is also excellent, with natural-sounding passthrough. [Based on RTINGS and SoundGuys reviews]

### Sound Quality and Audio Accuracy

The XM5 delivers a detailed, bass-forward sound signature that is typical of Sony's consumer tuning. The frequency response has a noticeable boost in the sub-bass and mid-bass regions, giving the headphones a warm, punchy character that is popular with many listeners. The treble is slightly recessed, which can make the sound feel less airy and open compared to the Bose QC Ultra or Sennheiser Momentum 4. Codec support is one of the XM5's strongest features: it supports SBC, AAC, and LDAC, which allows for high-resolution audio streaming over Bluetooth when using a compatible Android device. The headphones also support DSEE Extreme, Sony's upscaling technology that improves the quality of compressed audio files. Crinacle noted that the XM5 is "not the most neutral headphone" but praised its technical performance and detail retrieval. [Based on Crinacle and SoundGuys measurements]

### Comfort and Build Materials

The XM5 weighs 250 grams, making it one of the lightest headphones in its class. The ear cups are larger and deeper than the XM4, which improves comfort for users with larger ears. The headband is padded with a soft synthetic leather, and the ear pads are made of a similar material that is plush and comfortable. The clamping force is moderate, making the headphones suitable for long listening sessions. However, the XM5 has a significant drawback: it does not fold flat for storage. Instead, the ear cups rotate flat but the headband remains extended, resulting in a larger case than the Bose QC45 or Sennheiser Momentum 4. The build is primarily plastic, which keeps the weight down but feels less premium than the AirPods Max. [Based on Sony official specifications and user reviews]

### Battery Life

Sony claims 30 hours of battery life with ANC enabled, and real-world testing consistently shows between 28 and 32 hours. The headphones support fast charging via USB-C, providing 5 hours of playback from a 10-minute charge. The battery life is excellent and more than sufficient for most users, though it is far behind the Sennheiser Momentum 4's 60-hour claim. [Based on Sony official specifications and SoundGuys testing]

### Pricing

As of early 2025, the Sony WH-1000XM5 is priced at $348 on the official Sony website. On Amazon, it typically sells for between $298 and $348, with frequent discounts bringing it to $298 or even $278 during major sales events like Prime Day. The XM5 is one of the most competitively priced headphones in this comparison, offering excellent value for its ANC and sound quality. [Based on Sony.com and Amazon pricing history]

## Apple AirPods Max

### Active Noise Cancellation

The Apple AirPods Max offers excellent ANC performance, though it is not quite as effective as the Sony WH-1000XM5. RTINGS rated the AirPods Max's ANC at 8.8/10, noting that it is very effective at canceling low-frequency noise but slightly less effective at mid-range frequencies compared to the Sony XM5. The headphones use six outward-facing microphones for noise cancellation, along with two inward-facing microphones to monitor what the user is hearing. The transparency mode is among the best in the industry, offering natural-sounding passthrough that rivals the Sony XM5. SoundGuys praised the AirPods Max's ANC as "impressive but not class-leading," noting that it is very good for commuting and office use but not quite as aggressive as the XM5. [Based on RTINGS and SoundGuys reviews]

### Sound Quality and Audio Accuracy

The AirPods Max delivers a neutral, reference-like sound signature that is more accurate than most consumer headphones. The frequency response is remarkably flat, with a slight emphasis on the mid-bass and a gentle roll-off in the sub-bass. The treble is well-extended and detailed, without being harsh or sibilant. The headphones support only SBC and AAC codecs, which is a significant limitation for Android users. However, Apple's implementation of AAC is among the best, and the audio quality is excellent when used with an iPhone or iPad. The soundstage is wide and open for a closed-back headphone, and the imaging is precise. Crinacle noted that the AirPods Max is "one of the most accurate-sounding wireless headphones" but criticized its high price and limited codec support. [Based on Crinacle and SoundGuys measurements]

### Comfort and Build Materials

The AirPods Max is the heaviest headphone in this comparison at 385 grams, which is significantly heavier than the other models. The weight is due to the premium build materials: the ear cups are made of aluminum, the headband is a stainless steel mesh canopy, and the ear pads are memory foam covered in a breathable mesh fabric. The build quality is exceptional, with a solid, premium feel that no other headphone in this comparison can match. However, the weight can become noticeable after extended wear, especially for users with smaller heads. The clamping force is moderate, and the ear pads are very comfortable, but the overall comfort is not as good as the lighter Bose QC45 or Sony XM5. The headphones do not fold flat, and the included Smart Case provides minimal protection. [Based on Apple official specifications and user reviews]

### Battery Life

Apple claims 20 hours of battery life with ANC enabled, and real-world testing typically shows between 18 and 20 hours. The headphones do not support fast charging in the traditional sense; they charge via Lightning port and provide 1.5 hours of playback from a 5-minute charge, a feature Apple calls "Quick Charge." The battery life is the shortest in this comparison, and the lack of a USB-C port is a notable drawback in 2026. [Based on Apple official specifications and real-world testing]

### Pricing

As of early 2025, the Apple AirPods Max is priced at $549 on the official Apple website. On Amazon, it typically sells for between $449 and $549, with occasional discounts bringing it to $429. The price has remained high since launch, and it is the most expensive headphone in this comparison by a significant margin. The Lightning port version is still the standard, though Apple has not released a USB-C version as of the most recent data. [Based on Apple.com and Amazon pricing history]

## Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless

### Active Noise Cancellation

The Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless offers good ANC performance, but it is not as effective as the Sony WH-1000XM5 or Bose QuietComfort Ultra. RTINGS rated the Momentum 4's ANC at 7.8/10, noting that it is effective at reducing low-frequency noise like engine rumble and airplane drone but is less effective at mid-range frequencies like human speech. SoundGuys described the ANC as "competent but not class-leading," noting that it is adequate for commuting and office use but not as aggressive as the Sony or Bose flagships. The headphones offer two ANC modes (Max and Anti-Wind), along with a transparency mode. The ANC is not adaptive, meaning it does not automatically adjust to the environment. [Based on RTINGS and SoundGuys reviews]

### Sound Quality and Audio Accuracy

The Momentum 4 delivers a warm, detailed, and energetic sound signature that is characteristic of Sennheiser's tuning. The frequency response has a slight bass boost that adds warmth and punch, but the midrange is clear and forward, making vocals sound natural and present. The treble is well-extended and detailed, without being harsh. Codec support is excellent: the Momentum 4 supports SBC, AAC, aptX, and aptX Adaptive, which allows for high-quality wireless audio on both Android and iOS devices. The headphones also support Bluetooth 5.2 with multipoint connection. Crinacle praised the Momentum 4's sound quality, calling it "one of the best-sounding wireless headphones" in its price range, with a "natural and engaging" tonality. [Based on Crinacle and SoundGuys measurements]

### Comfort and Build Materials

The Momentum 4 weighs 293 grams, making it heavier than the Bose QC45 and Sony XM5 but lighter than the AirPods Max. The build is a mix of premium materials: the headband is made of metal wrapped in leatherette, and the ear cups are plastic with a fabric-covered outer surface. The ear pads are made of memory foam covered in leatherette, which is plush and comfortable. The clamping force is moderate, making the headphones comfortable for long listening sessions. The headphones fold flat for storage, which is a practical feature. The build quality is good, with a solid feel that is better than the Bose QC45 but not as premium as the AirPods Max. [Based on Sennheiser official specifications and user reviews]

### Battery Life

The Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless has the longest battery life in this comparison, with a claimed 60 hours of playback with ANC enabled. Real-world testing consistently shows between 55 and 60 hours, which is more than double the battery life of the Sony XM5 and three times that of the AirPods Max. The headphones support fast charging via USB-C, providing 2 hours of playback from a 10-minute charge. The battery life is exceptional and is one of the strongest selling points of the Momentum 4. [Based on Sennheiser official specifications and SoundGuys testing]

### Pricing

As of early 2025, the Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless is priced at $349 on the official Sennheiser website. On Amazon, it typically sells for between $299 and $349, with frequent discounts bringing it to $279 or $299. The price is competitive with the Sony XM5 and Bose QC45, making it an excellent value considering its long battery life and extensive codec support. [Based on Sennheiser.com and Amazon pricing history]

## Summary and Recommendations

Each of the five headphones in this comparison excels in different areas, and the best choice depends on the user's priorities:

- **Best ANC**: Sony WH-1000XM5 is the clear winner, with industry-leading noise cancellation that outperforms all competitors. The Bose QuietComfort Ultra is a close second, with customizable ANC and excellent performance.

- **Best Sound Quality**: The Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless offers the best combination of warm, detailed sound and extensive codec support (aptX Adaptive). The Apple AirPods Max is the most accurate and neutral, but its limited codec support is a drawback for Android users.

- **Best Comfort**: The Bose QuietComfort 45 and Sony WH-1000XM5 are the lightest and most comfortable for extended wear. The AirPods Max is the least comfortable due to its weight.

- **Best Battery Life**: The Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless is the undisputed champion, with 60 hours of battery life. The Sony XM5 (30 hours) is a distant second, while the AirPods Max (20 hours) has the shortest battery life.

- **Best Value**: The Sony WH-1000XM5 and Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless offer the best value, with excellent performance at competitive prices. The Bose QuietComfort Ultra is also a good value for those who prioritize ANC and spatial audio.

- **Best Build Quality**: The Apple AirPods Max is in a league of its own, with premium aluminum and stainless steel construction. The Sennheiser Momentum 4 is also well-built, while the Bose and Sony models are more utilitarian.
