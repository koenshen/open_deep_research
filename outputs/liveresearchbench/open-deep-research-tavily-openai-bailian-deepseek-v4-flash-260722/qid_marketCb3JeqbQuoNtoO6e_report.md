# Comprehensive Research Report: The Electric Truck Market (2025–2026)

## Executive Summary

The electric truck market in 2025–2026 is undergoing a profound transformation characterized by slowing consumer demand, significant regulatory reversals, and a strategic pivot by legacy automakers away from pure battery-electric trucks toward extended-range electric vehicles (EREVs). The US electric pickup truck segment sold 90,019 units in 2025, down 15.6% from 2024, while global electric truck sales doubled to over 400,000 units driven overwhelmingly by China. The expiration of the $7,500 federal EV tax credit on September 30, 2025, combined with the EPA's rescission of greenhouse gas standards in February 2026 and the rollback of CAFE penalties, has fundamentally altered the demand landscape. Among the four key competitors analyzed—Rivian, Ford F-150 Lightning, GM Ultium-based trucks, and the Ram 1500 REV—only Rivian remains committed to pure battery-electric truck production with its R2 launch, while Ford and Ram have pivoted to EREV architectures and GM has scaled back its electric truck ambitions.

### 1.3 Demand Trajectory and Key Indicators

**Demand Cooling:** Automakers sold a record number of EVs globally in 2025, but the market is cooling. The Tesla Cybertruck's 20,237 units in 2025 fell far below Elon Musk's initial 500,000-unit target [26][77]. The Ford F-150 Lightning was discontinued due to lackluster sales and unprofitability [26][77]. Stellantis canceled the all-electric Ram 1500 REV, Ford exited the full-electric pickup segment (discontinued Lightning), and automakers are pivoting to extended-range electric vehicles (EREVs) as a compromise [80].

**Reservation Conversion Rates:** The gap between reservations and actual deliveries remains massive. Tesla had 1.5 million Cybertruck pre-orders but sold only 20,237 units in 2025. Slate Auto's affordable electric pickup surpassed 100,000 reservations within two weeks of unveiling in May 2025, reaching 180,000 by mid-2026, with deliveries starting in early 2026 and a production target of 150,000 units by end of 2027 [65][68][69]. Rivian's R2 has "well over 100k" pre-orders [21].

**Fleet Demand Trends:** Despite consumer market cooling, fleet adoption is growing. In 2025, 61% of fleets announcing deployments were doing so for the first time, while nearly half of fleets placing new orders were repeat adopters [7]. Key corporate fleet-decarbonization pledges continue from FedEx, Schneider, PepsiCo, Walmart, and Amazon. Electric truck fleets can save operators up to 30% in fuel costs compared to diesel [10].

### 1.4 Customer Segmentation

**Commercial Fleets vs. Retail Consumers:**

- **Retail consumers** held 54.65% share of US electric pickup truck sales in 2025, but commercial fleets are expected to grow at 29.43% CAGR through 2031 [25][55].
- **Ford F-150 Lightning:** Less than 20% of sales went to commercial Ford Pro fleet customers, while over 80% went to retail buyers [43][47].
- **Rivian:** The Amazon EDV program accounts for substantial fleet volume—Amazon now has over 30,000 custom-built Rivian Electric Delivery Vans, with 100,000 committed by 2030 [39][40].
- **GM:** Silverado EV WT (Work Truck) is specifically designed for commercial fleets, with 2026 pricing starting at $52,800 for the 4WT [43].

**Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Dynamics:** Fleet managers prioritize TCO, which is lower for EVs, especially for short-distance, intra-city routes with predictable daily use. Studies show that by 2030, nearly half of medium- and heavy-duty trucks will be cheaper to own and operate as zero-emission vehicles than diesel [31]. Properly deployed electric pickup trucks could save $1,400 per vehicle annually in fuel and maintenance costs compared to conventional pickups [54].

**Buyer Demographics:** A Carnegie Mellon University study of 534 US pickup truck buyers found that 74% are open to EVs if price, range, and towing capacity are comparable. Respondents fell into three groups: 45% "Tech-Indifferent" (powertrain agnostic), 28% "Electric-Enthusiasts" (prefer plug-in EVs), and 26% "Electric-Opposed" (sharply opposed but open to hybrids) [56][58]. The average age of new Ford F-150 buyers is 55, with household incomes of $82,000–$83,000. Only 15% of full-size pickup owners use their truck primarily for business [Demand Local, 2026].

**Government/Municipal Segment:**

The government segment has been significantly impacted by policy changes:

- **Federal Fleet:** The Biden administration's goal of 100% zero-emission light-duty vehicle acquisitions by 2027 has been effectively abandoned under the Trump administration. The GSA issued Directive 5605.1B on March 3, 2025, stating no new charging station installations are authorized, and federal agencies must provide a written determination documenting a mission-critical need to charge EVs at a GSA-managed facility. By mid-2026, many federal chargers have been deactivated, including 22 at the Denver Federal Building [24].
- **USPS:** The USPS had added 2,600 EVs to its fleet by 2025. However, the Senate's budget reconciliation bill would require the USPS to scrap $1.5 billion in already purchased electric vehicles (7,200 EVs) and $500 million in charging infrastructure [27].
- **State-Level Mandates:** California's Advanced Clean Trucks (ACT) program requires manufacturers to begin selling zero-emission trucks, with 100,000 required by 2030 and 300,000 by 2035 [31][83]. However, in June 2025, Congress revoked California's EPA waivers for the ACT rule via Congressional Review Act resolutions [42]. The Advanced Clean Fleets (ACF) regulation was repealed by CARB on May 8, 2025 [47].
- **California was the leader in ZEV registrations** in Q3 2025 (167 ZEVs), followed by Texas (53), Maryland (47), Virginia (42), and New Jersey (27). The 11 ACT-adopting states accounted for 55% of ZEV registrations [71].
- **Multi-state initiatives** continue: New York (full transition by 2040), Oregon, Illinois, and others have enhanced ACT implementation [34].

## 2. Infrastructure and Regulatory Factors Influencing Adoption

### 2.1 Charging Infrastructure

**Megawatt Charging System (MCS) for Heavy-Duty Trucks:**

The Megawatt Charging System is a new high-power charging standard for heavy-duty electric trucks. MCS chargers are rated for maximum charging rates up to 3.75 MW, 3000A, and 1250 VDC [2]. Most trucks using an MCS charger will be able to go from 20% to 80% SOC in 30–40 minutes, about 10 times the speed of CCS [2].

- **Deployment Timeline:** MCS will transition from concept to commercial deployment in early 2026 [1]. The first public MCS charging session took place in August 2025 in Norrköping, Sweden [3]. The first North American public session occurred in March 2026 [6].
- **Key Providers:** Kempower, ABB, Siemens, and Alpitronic are shipping MCS equipment. Greenlane (a joint venture by Daimler, NextEra, and BlackRock) is building I-15 and I-10 corridors [1]. Tesla has 66 operational or planned 1.2 MW stations for its Semi trucks [2].
- **Economics:** A single MCS station costs approximately $400K; a 10-truck depot costs $7.9M–$15.4M [1]. The global MCS market is projected to grow from $0.99 billion in 2026 to $2.2 billion by 2030, at a CAGR of 22.2% [8].
- **Grid Capacity Constraints:** Grid capacity is the primary constraint. A single MCS charger at 1.2 MW draws as much power as 350–400 average US homes. Interconnection takes 12–24 months [1]. US EV electricity demand could reach 721 terawatt-hours by 2035 [7].

**NACS (North American Charging Standard) Adoption:**

The NACS connector, officially designated SAE J3400, has become the de facto standard in North America. Between May 2023 and February 2024, most major automakers announced plans to adopt NACS [15].

- **Automakers adopting NACS:** Ford, GM, Rivian, Lucid, Hyundai, Genesis, Mazda, Volkswagen Group, Toyota, BMW, Jaguar, Honda, Acura, Nissan, Mercedes-Benz, Volvo, Polestar, and Stellantis—all planning to integrate the port by 2025 and offer adapters in the interim [65].
- **2026 EVs with Native NACS Ports:** Tesla (all models), Hyundai Ioniq 5, Kia EV6/EV9, Rivian R1T/R1S, Lucid Gravity, Cadillac Optiq, Genesis Electrified GV70, Nissan Leaf, Toyota bZ, Subaru Solterra [64].
- **Supercharger Network Access:** As of March 2026, dozens of non-Tesla EVs across many automakers can use Tesla's Supercharger network. Ford was the first to gain access in February 2024, followed by Rivian, GM, Volvo, Polestar, Nissan, Lucid, Mercedes-Benz, Hyundai, Genesis, Kia, and others [68].
- **Network Size:** Tesla's Supercharger network accounts for 60% of the 34,000 fast chargers in the US, with 19,000 NACS ports versus 10,000 CCS ports, and boasts 99% uptime compared to CCS chargers' 72% reliability [72].

**Home Charging:**

Nearly 90% of EV chargers are private, with most EV owners charging at home [15]. The 2026 National Electrical Code update has officially ended the era of DIY installations for Level 2 setups by requiring a qualified person for all installations [1]. A 30% federal tax credit (worth up to $1,000) for home EV charger installation expires June 30, 2026 [1][16]. A complete Level 2 installation in 2026 generally costs between $900 and $2,500 [1].

**Fleet Charging Infrastructure:**

- **Depot charging dominates** current operations for electric trucks [6]. For depot fleets with 6–10 hours of dwell time, managed AC charging (7–22 kW) is strategically superior to DC fast charging due to lower hardware costs and extended battery life [25].
- **Infrastructure costs:** $250,000–$750,000 for a 50-vehicle fleet, with Level 2 depot charging as the foundation, DC fast charging for higher utilization, and smart load management to cut electricity costs by 20–40% [23].
- **Grid upgrades** can cost $500k–$2M+ for a 50-vehicle fleet. Utility upgrades (transformers, distribution) cost $75k–$250k and take 12–24 months [22].
- **Smart load management:** 70% of new charging installations in 2025 utilized dynamic power allocation to manage peak demand charges and reduce grid stress [20].
- **California** may require 151,000 to 156,000 depot and public chargers by 2030, growing to 434,000–460,000 by 2035, with corresponding investment of $7.1–$7.4 billion by 2030 and $16.4–$17.8 billion by 2035 [35].

### 2.2 Regulatory Factors

**EPA Emissions Rules:**

The regulatory landscape has shifted dramatically:

- **March 2024:** EPA issued Phase 3 Greenhouse Gas Standards for heavy-duty vehicles (MY 2027–2032), projecting 67% of new light-duty vehicles would be electric by MY 2032 [27][30]. The Multi-Pollutant Emissions Standards for LDVs and MDVs projected a 68% EV sales share by MY 2032 [31][84].
- **February 2026:** The Trump administration rescinded the Endangerment Finding and all GHG vehicle standards, stating that regulating vehicle emissions is costly and futile [20][21]. The EPA finalized its rescission, repealing all GHG emission regulation for motor vehicles and engines. NHTSA CAFE regulations remain in effect but carry no economic penalties [21][29].
- **July 2026:** The EPA is also proposing to loosen heavy-duty vehicle requirements and delay Tier 4 standards [20].

**Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) Standards:**

- **December 2025:** NHTSA issued a proposed rollback (SAFE Vehicles Rule III), lowering the maximum feasible fuel economy targets by excluding EVs from the standard-setting process. Fuel economy would need to increase by only 0.5% annually for MY 2022–2026 and 0.25% annually for MY 2027–2031, resulting in a projected fleetwide average of about 34.5 mpg by MY 2031 [28][33].
- **July 4, 2025:** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act eliminated civil penalties for CAFE noncompliance by resetting the maximum penalty to $0.00 per vehicle. Previously, penalties were $17 per 0.1 mpg shortfall, resulting in over $1.1 billion in fines from model years 2011–2020 [29].

**Federal Tax Credits:**

- **Expiration:** The federal EV tax credits under the Inflation Reduction Act expired on September 30, 2025. The $7,500 credit for new vehicles, the $4,000 credit for used vehicles, and the Qualified Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit are not available for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025 [73][74][86].
- **Impact:** Ford CEO Jim Farley stated he wouldn't be surprised if EV sales fell to a market share of 5% after the credit ended from around 10–12% [19]. Ellen Hughes-Cromwick of Third Way estimated the EV industry would have reached 50% market share by 2030 with incentives, but now 30% could be ambitious [19].

**State-Level Regulations:**

- **California Advanced Clean Trucks (ACT):** Adopted in 2020, requiring manufacturers to sell increasing percentages of ZEVs starting with the 2024 model year. Targets: 7% in 2025, 10% in 2026, 15% in 2027 for Class 8 tractors, reaching 55% of Class 2b-3 trucks and 40% of Class 7-8 tractors by 2035 [41][46].
- **Legal Challenges:** In June 2025, Congress revoked California's EPA waivers for ACT via Congressional Review Act resolutions [42]. Four major OEMs (Daimler, Volvo, Paccar, Navistar) sued CARB in August 2025 seeking clarity on conflicting federal and state directives. The US Department of Justice sued California in August 2025 to halt enforcement [42].
- **CARB Amendments (July 2025):** CARB approved amendments including a new 'pooling' option allowing manufacturers to transfer surplus ZEV credits between states that have adopted the ACT rule [45].
- **Advanced Clean Fleets (ACF):** Repealed by CARB on May 8, 2025. The rule would have required large fleets to transition to electric trucks, mandating all drayage trucks in California be zero-emission by 2035 [47].
- **States adopting ACT:** Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Washington (MY 2025); Vermont (MY 2026); Colorado, Maryland, New Mexico, Rhode Island (MY 2027) [37].

## 5. Strategic Implications and Market Outlook

### 5.1 The Pivot from Pure BEV to EREV

The most significant trend in the electric truck market is the strategic pivot away from pure battery-electric trucks toward extended-range electric vehicles (EREVs):

- **Ford:** Discontinued the all-electric F-150 Lightning in December 2025. The next-generation "Lightning EREV" will feature 100% electric power delivery, sub-5-second acceleration, 700+ mile range, and "tows like a locomotive" [29][40]. Ford plans a $30,000 midsize EV pickup by 2027 [29][47].
- **Ram:** Canceled the all-electric Ram 1500 REV in September 2025. The Ram 1500 REV (now the name for the range-extended version) uses a 92 kWh battery with a 3.6L V6 generator, offering 145 miles of EV range and 690 miles total [70][71][82].
- **GM:** While not pivoting to EREV, GM scaled back electric truck ambitions by abandoning plans to convert Orion Assembly to EV production, instead pivoting to gas vehicles [36][37]. GM indefinitely suspended plans to refresh its full-size electric truck lineup beyond the current generation in April 2026 [16].
- **Rivian:** Remains the only pure-play EV truck maker continuing with BEV-only strategy. The R2 launched in June 2026, with R3 and R3X planned for future release [93][98].

### 5.2 Market Forecast

- **BloombergNEF:** EVs are now expected to account for 17% of US passenger vehicle sales in 2030, down from earlier projections. All-electric vehicles and plug-in hybrids will be 22% of new sales in the US market in 2026 and 29% in 2027 [36][43].
- **Cox Automotive:** US new vehicle sales projected at 15.8 million units in 2026, down 2.4% from 2025 [50][82].
- **Hybrid sales** are projected to grow by roughly 9% in the first half of 2026, while the overall new-vehicle market is expected to decline by 2.2% [75].
- **Class 8 Truck Market:** Tractor demand is strengthening as of June 2026, with May North American orders up sharply year-over-year. Class 8 backlogs reached a 36-month high in May. Key factors include EPA 2027 planning, falling driver availability, and rising spot and contract rates [51].

### 5.3 Winners and Losers

**Challenges Facing the Industry:**
- Ford lost $3.6 billion on Model e through September 2025, with an estimated $36,000 loss per EV delivered in Q3 2023 [38][48].
- GM recorded $7.6 billion in EV-related charges in 2025 [16].
- Rivian still posting negative automotive gross profit (-$432 million in 2025), though software and services generated $576 million in gross profit [17][66].
- The US EV market share stabilized at 5.8% of new-vehicle sales in Q1 2026, down from the 10.6% peak in Q3 2025 when federal incentives expired [75][76].

**Bright Spots:**
- Global electric truck sales doubled in 2025 to over 400,000 units, driven primarily by China [6][42].
- Fleet adoption is growing, with 61% of fleets announcing deployments in 2025 doing so for the first time [7].
- Rivian's R2 launch provides a more affordable entry point ($44,990) with strong demand (100,000+ pre-orders) [21][98].
- The Ram 1500 REV's EREV approach addresses the core consumer concern of range anxiety, particularly for towing [85].
- GM's Silverado EV 8WT achieved a world record of 1,059.2 miles on a single charge [65].

## 6. Conclusion

The electric truck market in 2025–2026 is at a critical inflection point. The expiration of the $7,500 federal tax credit, the rescission of EPA greenhouse gas standards, and the rollback of CAFE enforcement have fundamentally altered the regulatory and demand landscape. US electric pickup sales fell 15.6% in 2025 to 90,019 units, and the broader US EV market experienced its first annual decline in both sales and market share.

The competitive response has been dramatic: Ford and Ram have abandoned pure battery-electric trucks in favor of EREV architectures, GM has scaled back its electric truck production plans, and only Rivian remains committed to a BEV-only strategy with the June 2026 launch of the R2. The key differentiators shaping competitive advantage are shifting from range and charging speed to total cost of ownership, fleet integration capabilities, and the elimination of range anxiety.

Rivian's partnerships with Volkswagen ($5.8 billion JV), Uber (up to 50,000 robotaxis), and Amazon (100,000 EDVs) provide a strong foundation for growth, while Ford's Ford Pro ecosystem and GM's Ultium platform offer established commercial channels. The Ram 1500 REV's unique EREV positioning may capture demand from consumers who want electric driving without range limitations.

Looking ahead, the market will be defined by the tension between global momentum (doubling of electric truck sales in 2025, driven by China) and US headwinds (regulatory rollback, tax credit expiration, consumer demand cooling). The winners will be those who can deliver affordable, practical electric trucks that meet the real-world needs of both commercial fleets and retail buyers.
