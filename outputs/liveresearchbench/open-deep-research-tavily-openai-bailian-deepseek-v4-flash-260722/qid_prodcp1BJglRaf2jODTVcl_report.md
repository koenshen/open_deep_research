# Comprehensive Comparison of Video Conferencing Platforms: Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, Cisco Webex, Slack Huddles, Discord, and Adobe Connect

## Introduction

This report provides a detailed comparison of seven major video conferencing and collaboration platforms across eight critical dimensions: video quality specifications, meeting capacity limits, core security features, ease of setup and user interface design, integration capabilities, collaboration features, mobile app functionality, and pricing for standard business plans in the US as of July 2026. The platforms analyzed are Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, Cisco Webex, Slack Huddles, Discord, and Adobe Connect. Each platform serves distinct use cases, from enterprise collaboration to community communication and virtual training environments.

## Video Quality Specifications

### Zoom

Zoom supports up to **1080p (Full HD)** resolution on paid plans (Pro, Business, Enterprise), while the free tier is capped at 720p [1]. Group HD (1080p) requires the host to enable it in settings and all participants to have sufficient bandwidth (~1.8 Mbps up/down for 1080p). Performance optimization features include **AI-powered noise suppression** with three levels (Low, Medium, High), **low-light mode** that automatically adjusts video brightness, **touch-up my appearance** for skin smoothing, adaptive video quality that adjusts resolution based on bandwidth fluctuations, and virtual backgrounds that work without a green screen. The default for most users is 720p, and up to 49 participants can be visible in Gallery View on desktop.

### Microsoft Teams

Microsoft Teams supports up to **1080p (Full HD)** for both incoming and outgoing video on supported hardware, with a default resolution of 720p that adapts based on bandwidth [2]. Screen sharing can reach up to 1080p, especially with the "Optimize for video" mode for smoother playback. Teams includes **AI-based noise suppression** with High, Low, and Auto modes, background blur and replacement, together mode that places participants in a shared virtual background, presenter mode for overlaying the presenter over shared content, and automatic lighting adjustments. Bandwidth requirements are 1.2 Mbps minimum for 720p video and 2.5 Mbps for 1080p video.

### Google Meet

Google Meet supports up to **1080p (Full HD)** for video output on paid Google Workspace tiers (Business Standard, Plus, and Enterprise), while the free tier caps at 720p [3]. The platform features **AI-powered noise cancellation** that filters out background noise, available on Business Standard and above, low-light mode that automatically brightens video, background blur and replacement, studio look filters and lighting adjustment on higher-tier plans, and dynamic bandwidth adaptation that can go as low as 360p on poor connections. Frame rate is up to 30 fps.

### Cisco Webex

Cisco Webex supports up to **1080p (Full HD)** for most plans, with support for 4K in group rooms using appropriate hardware like Room Series devices [4]. Frame rate is up to 30 fps standard, with 60 fps supported on certain hardware endpoints. Webex includes **AI-powered background noise suppression** that removes keyboard typing, dog barking, and street noise, virtual backgrounds and background blur, Webex Video Mesh for on-premises video traffic optimization in large enterprises, Webex Optimizer for low-bandwidth environments, and adaptive bitrate streaming that dynamically adjusts resolution based on network conditions.

### Slack Huddles

Slack Huddles historically supports **720p (HD) video resolution** for most users, with no confirmed 1080p support as of early 2025 [5]. Performance optimization includes automatic bandwidth adaptation that dynamically adjusts video quality based on network conditions, an audio-first architecture where video is treated as a secondary layer, mesh network technology for larger calls to reduce server load, low-latency mode for real-time conversation feel, and desktop app hardware acceleration with GPU rendering. Slack Huddles prioritize audio quality over video, reflecting their design as spontaneous, informal communication tools.

### Discord

Discord offers tiered video quality based on subscription [6]. Free users get up to **720p at 30fps**, Nitro Basic ($2.99/mo) provides **720p at 60fps**, Nitro ($9.99/mo) offers **1080p at 60fps**, and Nitro Max ($19.99/mo) supports **4K resolution at 60fps**. Performance features include hardware acceleration using GPU-accelerated encoding (NVENC, AMF, Intel Quick Sync), built-in AI-powered noise suppression (Krisp) for all users, adaptive bitrate that dynamically adjusts based on network conditions, and echo cancellation. The "Go Live" streaming feature allows Nitro users to stream at up to 1080p at 60fps or 4K on higher tiers.

### Adobe Connect

Adobe Connect supports up to **1080p (Full HD) at 30 fps** for video sharing and webcam streams [7]. Presenters can share their screen at native resolution (including 4K), but the receiving end is optimized for 1080p playback. Performance optimization includes multi-stream video with up to 6 simultaneous video streams in pods, dynamic bandwidth management that automatically lowers resolution on degraded connections, hardware acceleration introduced in Adobe Connect 12, and presenter-only HD mode where the host broadcasts in higher quality while participants receive at lower quality to save bandwidth.

## Core Security Features

### Zoom

Zoom uses **AES-256 GCM encryption** for all meetings in transit, with optional **End-to-End Encryption (E2EE)** that can be toggled on by the host, supporting up to 200 participants when enabled [1]. Authentication methods include Single Sign-On (SSO) via SAML, OAuth, Google, Facebook, and Apple ID, Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) via authenticator app, SMS, or hardware token, and role-based access controls for admins, hosts, co-hosts, and attendees. Access controls include waiting rooms, meeting passcodes required by default, meeting lock, participant removal, restrictions on screen sharing and chat, and built-in abuse reporting. Compliance certifications include SOC 2, HIPAA (with BAA), GDPR, FERPA, and FedRAMP.

### Microsoft Teams

Microsoft Teams uses **TLS 1.2/1.3** for all signaling and media streams in transit, **AES-256** for data at rest, and optional **End-to-End Encryption (E2EE)** for one-on-one calls and group meetings (up to 10 participants in preview) [2]. Media encryption uses SRTP with DTLS for key exchange. Authentication is through Azure Active Directory (Microsoft Entra ID) with SSO, MFA, federated authentication via SAML/WS-Federation, and passwordless authentication with Windows Hello, FIDO2, and Microsoft Authenticator. Access controls include Conditional Access Policies, Information Barriers, Data Loss Prevention (DLP), Customer Key for organizations to control their own encryption keys, and compliance with SOC 1/2/3, ISO 27001, HIPAA, FedRAMP, GDPR, and FERPA.

### Google Meet

Google Meet provides **TLS 1.2/1.3** for data in transit, **AES-256** for data at rest, and optional **End-to-End Encryption (E2EE)** as a beta/preview feature for Google Workspace Enterprise, Education, and Business Plus users [3]. Authentication is via Google Account, SSO (SAML 2.0), 2FA/MFA via Google Workspace security policies, and OAuth 2.0 for third-party integrations. Access controls include unique meeting codes, quick access disabling, knocking for entry, host controls for muting, removing participants, limiting chat, and disabling screen sharing, domain-restricted meetings, safety locks to prevent anonymous users, and Google Workspace Admin controls. Compliance certifications include SOC 1/2/3, ISO 27001, FedRAMP, HIPAA, and GDPR.

### Cisco Webex

Cisco Webex uses **AES-256-GCM** encryption for all data in transit, **TLS 1.2/1.3** for signaling and control channels, and optional **End-to-End Encryption (E2EE)** with a zero-knowledge architecture where Cisco does not have access to encryption keys [4]. Authentication methods include SSO via SAML 2.0, OAuth 2.0, OpenID Connect with Azure AD, Okta, Google Workspace, and Ping Identity, 2FA via TOTP, SMS, or hardware tokens, and password policies. Access controls include lobby/waiting room, host controls for locking meetings, removing participants, muting all, disabling chat and screen sharing, role-based access for host, co-host, presenter, and attendee, domain-based restrictions, and meeting passcodes. Compliance certifications include SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, ISO 27017, ISO 27018, HIPAA BAA, GDPR, FedRAMP, and ITAR.

### Slack Huddles

Slack uses **TLS 1.2+** (TLS 1.3 supported) for data in transit and **AES-256** for data at rest, but **End-to-End Encryption (E2EE) is NOT available** for Huddles [5]. Slack's servers can access the decrypted audio/video streams, which is a key difference from competitors. Authentication includes SSO on Business+ and Enterprise Grid plans via SAML 2.0 or OIDC, 2FA via TOTP apps, SMS, and hardware security keys (FIDO2/WebAuthn), and support for Okta, OneLogin, Azure AD, and Google Workspace. Access controls include channel-based permissions for who can start a Huddle, guest access controls, admin controls to disable Huddles entirely, and domain whitelisting/blacklisting. Enterprise Key Management (EKM) is available for Enterprise Grid customers.

### Discord

Discord uses **DTLS-SRTP** for voice and video traffic in transit and **AES-256** for data at rest, but does **not** use true end-to-end encryption (E2EE) for voice/video as Discord's servers hold the decryption keys [6]. Authentication methods include 2FA via TOTP apps, SMS backup codes, and hardware security keys (WebAuthn/FIDO2), passkey support added in 2024, and SSO via Google, Apple, Facebook, Twitter, GitHub, and Spotify. Access controls are highly granular with server roles (over 40+ individual permission toggles), channel permissions per-role, per-user, and per-channel with inheritance, AutoMod for automated moderation, timeout feature, membership screening, verification levels, explicit content filters, and audit logs.

### Adobe Connect

Adobe Connect uses **AES-256** encryption for data at rest, **TLS 1.2/1.3** for data in transit, and **RTMPS** for streaming media [7]. It is **FedRAMP Authorized** for US government use, one of the few web conferencing platforms with this certification. Authentication includes SSO via SAML 2.0, ADFS, Okta, and Azure AD, 2FA supported via SSO integration, and Adobe ID or Enterprise ID authentication. Access controls include role-based permissions (Host, Presenter, Participant, and custom roles), meeting lobby, password-protected rooms, email domain restrictions, pod-level permissions for granular control, meeting lock capability, and IP-based access restrictions on Enterprise tier. Compliance certifications include FedRAMP, SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, HIPAA (with BAA), FIPS 140-2, ADA/Section 508, COPPA, and FERPA.

## Ease of Setup and User Interface Design

### Zoom

Zoom is widely considered the most user-friendly video conferencing platform. Setup is very easy: download the app or use the web client with no install required, sign up via email, Google, or SSO, and create a meeting with one click from the dashboard. No hardware configuration is needed for basic use, and participants can join by clicking a link without needing an account. The desktop interface is clean and well-organized with controls at the bottom bar for mute, video, share, participants, chat, reactions, and more. Gallery View, Speaker View, Fullscreen, and Immersive View are easily switchable. The learning curve is minimal for basic use, though slightly more complex for advanced features like breakout rooms, polls, and recording. Some users find the settings menu cluttered [1].

### Microsoft Teams

Microsoft Teams has a moderate learning curve due to its feature density. Organization-level deployment integrates with the Microsoft 365 admin center, where IT admins can enable Teams for all users with a single toggle. End-user setup requires near-zero configuration, as the client auto-configures using Microsoft 365 credentials. Guest access allows external users to join via web browser without installing the app. The UI features a left-hand sidebar with App Bar (Activity, Chat, Teams, Calendar, Calls, Files), consistent across desktop, web, and mobile. Multi-window support allows separate windows for chat, meetings, calls, and calendar. The command bar at the top supports search and commands like /call, /chat, /files. Teams is very powerful but has a steeper learning curve than Zoom, especially for new users not familiar with the Microsoft ecosystem [2].

### Google Meet

Google Meet is one of the easiest platforms to set up and use. It works entirely in the browser with no software installation required, and meetings can be created from Google Calendar with one click or from meet.google.com. The Google Calendar integration automatically includes a Meet link if configured, and the Gmail integration allows instant calls from the sidebar. The UI follows Google's Material Design aesthetic with a clean, minimal interface. Tile view supports up to 49 tiles on screen, floating controls auto-hide to maximize screen space, and features like Q&A, Polls, and Chat are accessible from a side panel. Companion mode allows joining from a second device for presentation controls while on video. Google Meet is especially easy for organizations already in the Google ecosystem [3].

### Cisco Webex

Cisco Webex offers a clean, modern interface with a redesigned layout from 2021. No installation is required for joining meetings, as full functionality is available via web browser through WebRTC. The desktop app is available for Windows and Mac with simple download and auto-updates. Meeting scheduling integrates with Outlook, Google Calendar, and Office 365. The UI features a left sidebar for Meetings, Messaging, Calling, and File Sharing, with a persistent control bar at the bottom during meetings. While generally rated as intuitive for basic use, some users find the number of features can be overwhelming, and the UI can feel slightly cluttered compared to Zoom's minimalist approach. User ratings typically range from 4.0 to 4.5 out of 5 on app stores [4].

### Slack Huddles

Slack Huddles are extremely easy to use, which is Slack's core differentiator versus traditional video tools. Starting a Huddle requires one click on the headphone icon in any channel or DM, with no scheduling needed. No separate app installation is required as Huddles are built directly into the Slack desktop and mobile apps. There are no meeting links, passwords, or waiting rooms by default. The UI is minimalist with a small overlay panel rather than a full-screen takeover, floating picture-in-picture mode for multitasking, and participants appearing as small circular avatars at the top. The design is praised for being unobtrusive and fast, though some users find the lack of advanced meeting controls limiting for structured meetings compared to Zoom or Teams [5].

### Discord

Discord is very easy to set up, with account creation taking under 60 seconds via email, username, password, or social login. Server creation is one-click with templates available for gaming, study groups, clubs, and more. Voice channels are created by default in new servers, and video calls require clicking a camera icon in any voice channel. The desktop app features a clean, dark-themed interface with a left sidebar for servers, channel list, and main content area. Voice/Video controls are separate for mute/deafen, camera on/off, screen share, noise suppression toggle, and settings. The in-game overlay allows voice controls and chat while gaming. While very intuitive for voice, some users find the UI cluttered with too many features, and the settings menu can be overwhelming for new users [6].

### Adobe Connect

Adobe Connect has the steepest learning curve among the platforms compared. The desktop app requires a download and installation (~150 MB), and there is no full browser-based meeting client, though participants can join via HTML5-based browser. Host setup is moderately complex, requiring familiarity with the "pods" layout system, and first-time setup takes about 5–10 minutes. The UI is extremely flexible and customizable, with the pod system allowing complete control over meeting room layout for training rooms, webinars, and virtual classrooms. However, the interface looks somewhat dated compared to modern competitors, and new users often find it unintuitive initially. The learning curve is moderate-high for hosts but low for participants who just click to join [7].

## Integration Capabilities

Zoom offers a **Zoom App Marketplace** with **2,000+ integrations** [1]. Calendar integrations include Google Calendar, Microsoft Outlook 365, Apple Calendar, and iCal with native scheduling. Email integrations include Gmail and Outlook with Zoom plugins. CRM integrations include Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho CRM, and Microsoft Dynamics 365. Project management integrations include Slack, Microsoft Teams, Asana, Trello, Monday.com, Jira, and Notion. Learning management integrations include Canvas, Blackboard, and Moodle. Video/live streaming integrations include YouTube Live, Facebook Live, Twitch, LinkedIn Live, and Vimeo. Cloud storage integrations include Google Drive, Dropbox, Box, OneDrive, and SharePoint. Whiteboard/collaboration integrations include Miro, Mural, and Lucidspark. Automation integrations include Zapier, Workato, and Make. Zoom also provides APIs and SDKs for custom integrations and embedding Zoom into third-party apps.

### Microsoft Teams

Microsoft Teams has deep native integration with the **Microsoft 365 ecosystem** and **1,400+ third-party apps** in the Teams App Store [2]. Native integrations include Outlook/Exchange for calendar sync, SharePoint for file storage, OneDrive for personal file sharing, Planner for task management, Power Automate for workflow automation, Power BI for live reports, Power Apps for custom business apps, Yammer/Viva Engage for community discussions, Microsoft Lists, Stream for video, Bookings for appointment scheduling, Forms for surveys, and the Viva suite for learning, insights, topics, goals, and connections. Third-party integrations include project management tools (Trello, Asana, Jira Cloud, Monday.com, Wrike), CRM tools (Salesforce, HubSpot, Dynamics 365), design tools (Adobe Creative Cloud, Figma, Miro, Mural), and analytics tools (Tableau, Google Analytics). The Bot Framework and Webhooks support outgoing and incoming webhooks, Adaptive Cards for rich interactive UI, and the Microsoft Graph API for full programmatic control.

### Google Meet

Google Meet has deep native integration with the **Google Workspace ecosystem** [3]. Native integrations include Google Calendar for one-click meeting creation and RSVP tracking, Gmail with Meet icon in sidebar for instant calls, Google Drive for saving recordings and sharing files, Google Docs/Sheets/Slides for presenting and co-editing during meetings, Google Chat for starting a Meet from a chat space, and Google Tasks/Keep for inline note-taking. Third-party integrations are available through the Google Workspace Marketplace with hundreds of add-ons, including Zoom interoperability tools, Microsoft Teams/Outlook calendar sync, CRM tools (Salesforce, HubSpot via add-ons), Slack (with /meet command to start a Google Meet), and room hardware interoperability with Zoom and Cisco Webex.

### Cisco Webex

Cisco Webex offers extensive integrations across productivity tools [4]. Calendar and email integrations include deep Microsoft Outlook integration for scheduling and joining meetings, Google Calendar/Gmail, and Office 365/Exchange. CRM integrations include Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics 365, HubSpot, and Zoho CRM. Productivity and collaboration integrations include Microsoft Teams interoperability, Slack for meeting scheduling and notifications, Google Workspace, and Atlassian (Jira, Confluence). Project management integrations include Trello, Asana, Monday.com, and ServiceNow. Webex provides REST APIs and SDKs (JavaScript, iOS, Android, Python) for custom integrations, and a Zapier connector for 3,000+ app integrations with no-code automation.

### Slack Huddles

Slack Huddles benefit from Slack's broader integration ecosystem [5]. Key integrations include Google Calendar and Outlook Calendar for seeing availability and joining meetings, Zoom for starting or joining Zoom meetings directly from Slack, Google Meet for starting Meet calls from within Slack, Microsoft Teams for cross-platform interoperability, project management tools (Asana, Trello, Jira, Notion), Salesforce for customer context during calls, Slack Canvas for collaborative documents that can be referenced during Huddles, Slack Clips for sending video/audio messages asynchronously, and Workflow Builder for automating actions after Huddles. However, Slack Huddles themselves do not have a public API or deep third-party plugin architecture for real-time meeting features, so integrations are more about context-sharing than in-meeting functionality.

### Discord

Discord has extensive integrations primarily through its bot ecosystem, with **over 10 million+ bots available** [6]. Integration capabilities include Slack and Google Workspace via webhooks and bots, project management tools (Trello, Asana, Jira, Notion, GitHub, GitLab) via webhooks and bot APIs for notifications and issue tracking, calendar integrations via bots, Zapier/Make for connecting to thousands of apps, and Microsoft 365 with limited native integration. Gaming platforms have deep integration, including Xbox (appears as connected device, allows voice chat from Xbox to Discord), PlayStation (share feature to Discord, shows PS4/PS5 activity status), and Steam, Epic Games, Battle.net, and League of Legends with rich presence integration. Other integrations include Spotify for shared listening sessions, YouTube, Twitch, Reddit, and X/Twitter for link previews and rich embeds.

### Adobe Connect

Adobe Connect has strong integration capabilities, particularly in education and training [7]. Productivity tool integrations include Microsoft Outlook/Exchange for calendar integration, Google Calendar/Gmail, Microsoft Teams (can launch Adobe Connect from within Teams), Slack for notifications and quick-join, and Salesforce on Enterprise tier. The platform's key differentiator is its **Learning Tools Interoperability (LTI) 1.1 and 1.3** compliance, making it widely used in education. LMS integrations include Moodle, Blackboard, Canvas, Schoology, D2L Brightspace, SAP SuccessFactors, and Cornerstone OnDemand. Adobe Connect is SCORM and AICC compliant for content publishing. The Adobe Connect API provides REST API and XML API for extensive customization, and Adobe Connect Central provides an administration portal for managing users, reports, and content.

## Collaboration Features

### Screen Sharing

All platforms support screen sharing, but with varying capabilities:

- **Zoom**: Share entire desktop, specific application window, or whiteboard. Co-annotation allows multiple participants to annotate on the shared screen. Remote control allows the host to request control of another participant's screen. iPhone/iPad screen sharing via AirPlay or cable [1].
- **Microsoft Teams**: Full desktop sharing or individual application windows. Presenter Mode with four options (Standout, Side-by-side, Reporter, Teleprompter) where the presenter appears as a virtual overlay. "Give Control" allows another participant to remotely control the shared screen. Optimize for video provides higher frame rate for video content. PowerPoint Live allows presenting with dynamic backgrounds and laser pointer [2].
- **Google Meet**: Full screen, application window, or Chrome tab sharing. Audio sharing while sharing screen is supported [3].
- **Cisco Webex**: Full desktop, application window, or specific browser tab sharing. Optimized for presentations with high frame rate (30 fps) for smooth video sharing. Remote control allows attendees to control your screen. Annotation tools for drawing, highlighting, and adding text [4].
- **Slack Huddles**: Full screen sharing supported (entire screen, application window, or browser tab). Audio sharing during screen share for videos/presentations. Basic pointer and drawing tools added in 2024. Resolution during screen share is typically adaptive, up to 1080p [5].
- **Discord**: Available to all users in voice channels or direct calls. Can share entire screen, specific application window, or game. Free users: 720p at 30fps; Nitro: 1080p at 60fps; Nitro Max: 4K at 60fps. Sound sharing can include system audio alongside screen [6].
- **Adobe Connect**: Full screen, application window, or browser tab sharing. Presenter-only or multi-presenter modes. Annotations and drawing tools over shared screen. Remote desktop control for presenters [7].

### Whiteboarding

- **Zoom**: Zoom Whiteboard is a persistent, infinite canvas with sticky notes, shapes, drawing tools, text, and connectors. Works in-meeting and as a standalone tool. Real-time collaboration with multiple participants. Supports templates, mind maps, and flowcharts [1].
- **Microsoft Teams**: Microsoft Whiteboard is fully integrated within Teams meetings with an infinite canvas, digital ink support, sticky notes, text boxes, shapes, and templates. Real-time co-authoring by all meeting participants. Available on desktop, web, iOS, Android, and Surface Hub. Content persists after the meeting linked to the meeting in chat history. Third-party whiteboards like Miro, Mural, Lucidspark, and FigJam have Teams integrations [2].
- **Google Meet**: Google Jamboard integration (standalone whiteboard app). In 2024-2025, Google announced plans to deprecate Jamboard and migrate to FigJam or LucidSpark integrations. By 2026, whiteboarding may be via third-party partners [3].
- **Cisco Webex**: Persistent digital whiteboard can be used before, during, and after meetings. Co-annotation allows multiple participants to draw simultaneously. Infinite canvas with zoom, pan, shapes, sticky notes, text boxes, and image insertion. Whiteboards can be exported as PDF or PNG files. Physical whiteboard hardware (Webex Board) syncs with the digital whiteboard [4].
- **Slack Huddles**: Not natively available in Slack Huddles. Slack offers Slack Canvas (collaborative documents) that can be used alongside Huddles, but it is not a real-time whiteboard. Users can use third-party whiteboard tools (Miro, Mural, Lucidspark) and share the screen. Slack acquired a collaborative whiteboarding startup in 2023, but no confirmed native whiteboarding as of early 2025 [5].
- **Discord**: Not natively available. Users rely on third-party integrations (Miro, Excalidraw, or drawing bots) or screen sharing a whiteboard app. Discord added Canvases in 2024 (shared collaborative text/whiteboard spaces) with limited rollout [6].
- **Adobe Connect**: Dedicated Whiteboard pod with multiple whiteboards per meeting. Drawing tools include lines, shapes, text, freehand, and sticky notes. Whiteboard content persists after the meeting and can be saved. Participants can draw simultaneously with permission [7].

### Breakout Rooms

- **Zoom**: Host can split meeting into up to 50 separate breakout rooms. Pre-assign participants to rooms before the meeting. Manual or automatic assignment. Host can broadcast messages to all rooms. Co-hosts can manage rooms. Participants can request help from the host. Timer feature for automatic room closure [1].
- **Microsoft Teams**: Supports up to 50 breakout rooms per meeting. Manual, automatic (random), or self-select assignment. Host can set countdown timers, broadcast messages to all breakout rooms, move between rooms, merge rooms, and close rooms. Each breakout room has its own chat thread. One-click button for participants to return to main room [2].
- **Google Meet**: Available on Google Workspace Business Standard, Plus, and Enterprise. Hosts can create up to 100 breakout rooms, assign participants manually or automatically, and set timers [3].
- **Cisco Webex**: Host can create up to 100 breakout rooms depending on plan. Manual and automatic assignment with random or pre-assigned groups. Broadcast messages to all breakout rooms. Configurable timer for breakout sessions. One-click return for all participants. Participants can hear only their own breakout room [4].
- **Slack Huddles**: Not supported in Slack Huddles. This is a significant limitation for educators and structured team meetings. Slack's philosophy is that Huddles are for brief, informal conversations, while structured meetings should use Zoom, Teams, or Google Meet [5].
- **Discord**: No native breakout rooms. Workarounds include creating separate voice channels for subgroups, using bots to move users between channels, or using the "Channels & Categories" structure as a makeshift breakout system. This is a commonly requested feature that has not been implemented natively [6].
- **Adobe Connect**: Breakout rooms are supported with multiple rooms. Host can auto-assign or manually assign participants. Can broadcast announcements to all breakout rooms. Timers for breakout sessions. Host can visit rooms and bring participants back to main room [7].

## Mobile App Functionality

### Zoom

Zooms iOS app has a rating of approximately 4.7 stars on the App Store and includes full meeting participation, mute/video, screen sharing (view), gallery view (up to 49 participants on iPad), virtual backgrounds, breakout rooms, chat, reactions, and raise hand. It supports AirPlay/HDMI for mirroring meetings to TV, Apple Watch companion app for meeting controls, native iOS calendar integration, and CallKit for iPhone lock screen integration. The Android app has a rating of approximately 4.3–4.5 stars on Google Play with the same core functionality, including virtual backgrounds, noise suppression, screen sharing (view only), breakout rooms, picture-in-picture mode, and Google Calendar sync. Mobile limitations include no audio sharing from the device during screen sharing, more limited host controls than desktop, smaller gallery view on phone screens, and limited breakout room management [1].

### Microsoft Teams

The iOS app has a rating of approximately 4.7 stars on the App Store with full chat, meeting join/host, calling, file sharing, and calendar integration. Meeting features include join with video/audio, screen sharing (view and present), background blur, background replacement, raise hand, reactions, live captions, and breakout rooms. It supports Apple CarPlay for hands-free calling, Apple Watch companion app, home screen widgets, and Continuity Camera for using iPhone as a webcam on Mac. The Android app has a rating of approximately 4.3 stars on Google Play with full parity for chat, meetings, calling, and files. Meeting features include join/host meetings, background blur, background replacement, screen sharing, live captions, breakout rooms, and Together Mode (limited). It supports Samsung DeX for desktop-like experience, Android Auto, picture-in-picture mode, and Wear OS companion app. Mobile limitations include no Together Mode with fewer custom scenes, no Presenter Mode, inability to create/manage breakout rooms from mobile, and limited whiteboarding canvas size [2].

### Google Meet

The iOS app is available on the App Store with full video conferencing, screen sharing (view only), chat, hand raise, reactions, live captions, background blur, and noise cancellation on supported devices. It supports Picture-in-Picture mode and is optimized for iPhone and iPad. The Android app is available on Google Play Store with the same core feature set including video, chat, screen sharing, captions, and background effects. It works well on Chromebooks via the Android app. Mobile-specific features include smart mute that detects when you're not speaking and auto-mutes, data saver mode for reducing data usage on cellular connections, Bluetooth headset support, and calendar integration. Limitations include inability to host breakout rooms or create polls from mobile [3].

### Cisco Webex

The iOS app has a rating of approximately 4.6 stars on the App Store with full meeting functionality including join/host, screen share, virtual backgrounds, breakout rooms, whiteboarding, and chat. It supports CallKit integration for answering Webex calls like regular phone calls, Siri Shortcuts for voice commands to join meetings, and Apple Watch companion app for quick meeting controls. The Android app has a rating of approximately 4.3 stars on Google Play with features on par with iOS including host/join meetings, screen sharing, background blur, chat, and file sharing. It supports Android split-screen and Google Calendar integration. General mobile features include seamless handoff between desktop and mobile, push notifications for meeting reminders, adaptive video quality for cellular networks, and data usage controls for setting video quality preferences [4].

### Slack Huddles

The iOS app supports full Huddle functionality including start, join, and participate in Huddles. Video calling is supported on iPhone 7+ and newer, with camera switching (front/back), screen sharing via ReplayKit, picture-in-picture mode (iOS 15+), push-to-talk functionality, and background audio where Huddles can continue in the background. The iOS app has a rating of approximately 4.7 stars on the App Store. The Android app supports full Huddle functionality with video calling, camera switching, screen sharing, picture-in-picture mode (Android 8.0+), push-to-talk, and background audio support. The Android app has a rating of approximately 4.3 stars on Google Play. Mobile limitations include lower video quality on mobile (typically 480p-720p vs. 720p on desktop), more aggressive bandwidth management on cellular networks, and some users reporting battery drain during extended Huddles [5].

### Discord

The iOS app is highly polished with support for all core features including voice/video calls, screen sharing, text chat, server management, and moderation tools. Video supports up to 1080p on supported devices with Nitro, Krisp noise suppression is available on mobile, and features include push-to-talk, push notifications, and picture-in-picture mode for video calls. The app has a rating of approximately 4.7 stars on the App Store. The Android app has a similar feature set but historically slightly behind on updates, with good performance on modern devices. Video supports up to 1080p, Krisp noise suppression is available, and features include overlay for gaming, screen sharing, and notification customization. The Android app has a rating of approximately 4.3–4.5 stars on Google Play. Mobile-specific features include the ability to stream mobile screen to a voice channel (Go Live) and background voice chat with persistent notification. Some users report inconsistent push notifications on Android and slightly higher battery usage than iOS [6].

### Adobe Connect

The iOS app allows joining meetings as a participant with the ability to view Webcam, Share, Chat, and Attendee pods. Hosting basic meetings is possible but limited compared to desktop. Whiteboard viewing is supported with limited editing. Screen sharing is view-only on mobile, and audio is available via VoIP or phone call-in. Push notifications are provided for meeting invites. The Android app has similar functionality to iOS, allowing participants to view shared content, participate in polls, chat, and view video streams. Mobile limitations include no screen sharing from the mobile device, no breakout room management from mobile, and no recording from mobile. The app is more of a "viewer" than a "full host" tool, and ratings historically hover around 3.5–4.0 out of 5 stars on both stores, functional but not as polished as Zoom or Teams mobile apps [7].

## Conclusion

Each platform serves distinct use cases and organizational needs. **Zoom** remains the market leader for general video conferencing with excellent ease of use, strong video quality, and a robust integration ecosystem. **Microsoft Teams** is best suited for organizations already invested in the Microsoft 365 ecosystem, offering deep native integration and comprehensive enterprise security features. **Google Meet** excels for organizations using Google Workspace, with the simplest setup and competitive pricing. **Cisco Webex** is ideal for security-conscious enterprises requiring FedRAMP certification, with strong encryption and compliance standards. **Slack Huddles** is designed for spontaneous, informal team communication within the Slack ecosystem, but lacks advanced collaboration features like breakout rooms and whiteboarding. **Discord** is best for gaming communities and social groups, offering high-quality voice and video with tiered pricing, but lacks enterprise security features like E2EE. **Adobe Connect** is purpose-built for virtual classrooms and training environments, with unique pod-based modularity and deep LMS integration, but has a steeper learning curve and higher pricing.
