# GDPR Fines Exceeding €50 Million (2022–2026): Research Report

## Introduction

This report addresses the research brief to identify all organizations fined more than €50 million under the GDPR during the calendar years 2022 through 2026 (inclusive, up to July 22, 2026). The research attempted to retrieve data from official Data Protection Authority (DPA) websites and independent GDPR enforcement trackers, but all web search tool calls failed due to API usage‑limit errors. Consequently, no live data could be gathered from external sources. The findings below are drawn from the researcher’s pre‑existing knowledge (training data cutoff) and the reflections recorded during the research session. Only fines that are known to be final decisions (not proposed or appealed) and that fall within the requested date range are included. The report provides the organization name, exact fine amount, date of final decision, concise infringement summary, and suggested links to the official DPA decision and an independent tracker. Because the links could not be verified live, they are marked as “suggested” and may have changed since the knowledge cutoff. Fines from 2024–2026 could not be confirmed due to the lack of live search results.

## Fines Confirmed (2022–2023) – Final Decisions

### 1. Meta Platforms Ireland Limited (Instagram) – €405 Million

- **Fine Amount:** €405 million
- **Date of Final Decision:** 5 September 2022
- **Authority:** Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC)
- **Infringement Summary:** Instagram allowed children (ages 13–17) to operate business accounts, which made their email addresses and phone numbers publicly visible. The default privacy settings for minors were not age‑appropriate, and the platform’s registration process lacked sufficient safeguards. The DPC found violations of Articles 5(1)(c) (data minimisation), 5(1)(f) (integrity and confidentiality), 12, 13 (transparency), and 25 (data protection by design and default) of the GDPR.
- **Suggested DPC Decision Link:** `dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/` (search for “Instagram fined 405 million euro for children's data”)
- **Suggested Independent Tracker Link:** `gdprhub.eu/DPC_v._Meta_Platforms_Ireland_Limited_(Instagram)_(2022)`

### 2. Meta Platforms Ireland Limited (Facebook & Instagram) – €390 Million

- **Fine Amount:** €390 million (€210 million for Facebook, €180 million for Instagram)
- **Date of Final Decision:** 4 January 2023
- **Authority:** Irish DPC (following binding decision of the EDPB under Article 65)
- **Infringement Summary:** Meta relied on the “contractual necessity” legal basis (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR) to serve behavioral advertising, rather than obtaining valid consent. The EDPB overruled the DPC’s original lower fine, requiring a higher penalty. The practice forced users to accept personalised ads as a condition of using the service.
- **Suggested DPC Decision Link:** `dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/` (search for “DPC announces decision in Meta Ireland (Facebook and Instagram) inquiries”)
- **Suggested Independent Tracker Link:** `gdprhub.eu/DPC_v._Meta_Platforms_Ireland_Limited_(January_2023)`

### 3. Meta Platforms Ireland Limited (Facebook) – €1.2 Billion

- **Fine Amount:** €1.2 billion
- **Date of Final Decision:** 22 May 2023
- **Authority:** Irish DPC
- **Infringement Summary:** Meta continued transferring EU user data to the United States after the *Schrems II* ruling invalidated the Privacy Shield. The Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) used were deemed insufficient to protect data from US surveillance. The DPC found a violation of Article 46(1) GDPR (inadequate safeguards for international transfers). Meta was also ordered to suspend data transfers to the US within five months and delete unlawfully transferred data within six months.
- **Suggested DPC Decision Link:** `dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/` (search for “Data Protection Commission announces decision in Meta Ireland inquiry”)
- **Suggested Independent Tracker Link:** `gdprhub.eu/DPC_v._Meta_Platforms_Ireland_Limited_(May_2023)`

### 4. TikTok Technology Limited – €345 Million

- **Fine Amount:** €345 million
- **Date of Final Decision:** 15 September 2023
- **Authority:** Irish DPC
- **Infringement Summary:** TikTok violated children’s privacy rules by setting default public profiles for users under 16, using inadequate age‑verification mechanisms, and failing to provide transparent information to young users. Issues were also found with the “family pairing” feature. Violations included Articles 5(1)(c), 5(1)(f), 12, 13, 25, and 35 GDPR.
- **Suggested DPC Decision Link:** `dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/` (search for “TikTok fined 345 million euro”)
- **Suggested Independent Tracker Link:** `gdprhub.eu/DPC_v._TikTok_Technology_Limited_(2023)`

## Conclusion

Based on the research conducted (limited by the failure of all web searches), the only final GDPR fines exceeding €50 million that can be confirmed for the period 2022–2026 (up to July 22, 2026) are the four decisions issued by the Irish DPC in 2022 and 2023:

| Organization | Fine Amount | Date | Infringement |
|--------------|-------------|------|--------------|
| Meta (Instagram) | €405 million | 5 Sep 2022 | Children’s data privacy (default public accounts, lack of safeguards) |
| Meta (Facebook & Instagram) | €390 million | 4 Jan 2023 | Forced consent to personalised advertising (contractual necessity) |
| Meta (Facebook) | €1.2 billion | 22 May 2023 | Unlawful EU-US data transfers (Schrems II) |
| TikTok | €345 million | 15 Sep 2023 | Children’s data handling (default public profiles, age verification) |

No other fines from other DPAs (e.g., CNIL, CNPD, Garante, AP, AEPD) or from non‑tech sectors (finance, retail, healthcare) could be identified due to the inability to perform live searches. The links provided above are suggested based on the researcher’s knowledge; actual URLs should be verified by visiting the official DPA websites and independent trackers. For a complete and up‑to‑date list, direct access to these sources is recommended.
