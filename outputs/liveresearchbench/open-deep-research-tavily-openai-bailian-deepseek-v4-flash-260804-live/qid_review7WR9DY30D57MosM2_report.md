# Spatial Understanding and Reasoning in Vision-Language Models: A Comprehensive Review (2022–2025)

## 1. Introduction

The period from 2022 to 2025 has witnessed transformative advances in the spatial understanding capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs), spanning both 2D and 3D domains. This review synthesizes research published in top-tier venues (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, AAAI, EMNLP, ACL, TPAMI, IJCV, TMLR, COLING) to trace the evolution of model architectures, evaluation benchmarks, and methodological innovations that have advanced spatial reasoning. The review covers tasks including object counting, visual grounding and localization, navigation, and general visual reasoning, organized along two parallel trajectories: the development of spatially-aware 2D VLMs and the emergence of 3D vision-language models.

## 2. Architectural Innovations in 2D Spatial Understanding

### 2.1 Position Encoding Innovations for Spatial Awareness

The foundational challenge of imbuing vision transformers with spatial awareness has driven significant architectural innovation. **Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) for Vision Transformer** [Heo et al., ECCV 2024] provides the first comprehensive study of applying RoPE to Vision Transformers (ViTs), proposing **RoPE-Mixed**—a 2D extension using learnable mixed-axis frequencies (θ_x and θ_y) to handle diagonal directions. The authors demonstrate that RoPE significantly improves extrapolation to higher resolutions (224→384) and outperforms both Absolute Positional Embedding (APE) and Relative Position Bias (RPB) in multi-resolution classification on ImageNet-1k, object detection on MS-COCO (DINO-ViTDet: +1.8 AP for ViT-B), and semantic segmentation on ADE20k (UperNet-ViT-B: +2.5 mIoU). The analysis reveals that RoPE increases attention distance and entropy, especially in middle layers, while introducing negligible computational overhead (~0.01% of ViT-B FLOPs).

Building on this foundation, **Spiral RoPE** [ICML 2026] addresses the directional bias of standard axial 2D RoPE by partitioning embedding channels into K groups associated with uniformly distributed directions (e.g., 0°, 22.5°, 45°), rotating each group according to the patch position projected onto its direction. Without adding parameters or computational overhead, Spiral RoPE consistently improves performance across classification (ImageNet-1k: +0.18–0.36%), segmentation (ADE20k: +1.22 mIoU), and generation tasks (DiT: FID reduction of 0.7–1.1). Attention visualizations reveal sharper, more object-centric focus compared to APE and Axial RoPE.

**Circle-RoPE** [ICML 2026] introduces a cone-like decoupled rotary positional embedding specifically for vision-language models, decoupling position encoding so that visual tokens and text tokens receive separate positional treatments, preserving 2D spatial geometry more faithfully. **Revisiting Multimodal Positional Encoding in Vision-Language Models** [Huang et al., 2025] systematically investigates multimodal RoPE, decomposing design into position design and frequency allocation. Through extensive experiments, the authors identify three key guidelines: positional coherence, full frequency utilization, and preservation of textual priors. This yields **Multi-Head RoPE (MHRoPE)** and **MRoPE-Interleave (MRoPE-I)**, achieving notable gains of +2.67% on MMMU, +5.28% on ChartQA, and +3.27% on RefCOCO_val.

**VRoPE** [EMNLP 2025] extends RoPE to video LLMs, mitigating positional attention bias and cross-modal positional discontinuity through Symmetric Bias Mitigation and Temporal Centered Arrangement. Experiments on Video-MME, MVBench, and TempCompass show consistent improvements over RoPE and RoPE-3D without additional parameters. **HoPE** [NeurIPS 2025] addresses long-context VLMs by introducing a hybrid frequency allocation strategy and dynamic temporal scaling mechanism, outperforming current methods on four video benchmarks. **A Circular Argument** [NeurIPS 2025] challenges the necessity of rotational equivariance by proposing **Spherical RoPE** with anti-commutative generators, achieving equivalent learning behavior and suggesting that relative positional embeddings may not be as crucial as previously assumed.

### 2.2 Foundational Vision-Language Models (2022–2023)

The foundational architecture of modern VLMs was established through several landmark models. **OFA** [Wang et al., ICML 2022] unified sequence-to-sequence learning across modalities (cross-modality, vision, language) and tasks (finetuning and prompt tuning) using a Transformer encoder-decoder architecture. By instruction-based multitask pretraining on 8 tasks including visual grounding, grounded captioning, VQA, image-text matching, image captioning, detection, image infilling, and text infilling, OFA achieved state-of-the-art on image captioning and visual grounding, with competitive results on VQA (comparable to Flamingo 80B) and visual entailment. The model was open-sourced in five sizes from Tiny (33M) to Huge (930M).

**Flamingo** [Alayrac et al., NeurIPS 2022] introduced a family of VLMs capable of few-shot learning through a **Perceiver Resampler** that produces a fixed number of visual tokens from variable-size inputs, bridging frozen pretrained vision (NFNet-F6) and language (Chinchilla, up to 70B) models. The model handles arbitrarily interleaved visual and textual data and achieves state-of-the-art few-shot results on 16 benchmarks, often surpassing fine-tuned models trained on thousands of examples. Ablation studies highlight the importance of tanh-gating, GATED XATTN-DENSE conditioning layers, and freezing the language model to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

**Unified-IO** [Lu et al., 2022] and its successor **Unified-IO 2** [Lu et al., CVPR 2024, Highlight] represent a unified sequence-to-sequence approach that homogenizes diverse input/output modalities (images, text, masks, bounding boxes) into discrete tokens. Unified-IO-2, with 7 billion parameters, uses **2D rotary embeddings**, QK normalization, and scaled cosine attention on the perceiver resampler to stabilize training across 1 billion image-text pairs, 1 trillion text tokens, 180 million video clips, and 3 million 3D assets. It achieves state-of-the-art on the GRIT benchmark (67.0 overall) and strong results on over 35 benchmarks, including TIFA (81.3), VQA v2 (79.4), and ScienceQA (88.7).

**BLIP-2** [Li et al., ICML 2023] introduced the paradigm of bootstrapping learning from frozen pre-trained image encoders and frozen large language models using a lightweight **Querying Transformer (Q-Former)** with 32 learnable query tokens. Zero-shot BLIP-2 with OPT-6.7B achieves 65.0% on VQAv2, outperforming Flamingo (80B parameters) by 8.7 points with 54× fewer trainable parameters. This paradigm of freezing unimodal models and training a lightweight connector became the dominant approach, influencing **InstructBLIP** [Dai et al., NeurIPS 2023] which extends this with an instruction-aware Q-Former, achieving new state-of-the-art zero-shot results on all held-out datasets with average relative improvements of 15% over BLIP-2 and 24.8% over Flamingo-80B.

**LLaVA** [Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023, Oral] connected a CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder with Vicuna via a simple projection matrix, using two-stage instruction-tuning. LLaVA-1.5 achieved state-of-the-art on 11 benchmarks using only public data, while LLaVA-1.6 (LLaVA-NeXT) increased input resolution to up to 4× more pixels and exceeded Gemini Pro on several benchmarks. **KOSMOS-1** [Huang et al., NeurIPS 2023] and **KOSMOS-2** [Peng et al., ICLR 2024] added text-to-visual grounding capabilities, representing object descriptions as bounding boxes linked to text spans in Markdown format and training on the large-scale **GrIT** dataset of grounded image-text pairs.

### 2.3 Spatially-Aware Grounding and Referring Models (2023–2025)

A critical line of research focused on enabling VLMs to understand and output spatial coordinates. **Shikra** [Chen et al., 2023] uses plain numerical values (e.g., `[xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax]`) for coordinates, avoiding extra vocabularies or external detection modules. Demonstrating that chain-of-thought reasoning with spatial coordinates reduces hallucination and improves accuracy over verbal CoT alone, Shikra achieves strong performance on referring expression comprehension (REC), PointQA, and VQA.

**Ferret** [You et al., ICLR 2024] unifies referring and grounding in images, accepting various region inputs (points, boxes, free-form shapes) through a **hybrid region representation** combining discrete coordinates with continuous visual features extracted via a **spatial-aware visual sampler**. The curated **GRIT** dataset of 1.1M instruction-tuning samples enabled Ferret-13B to achieve 80.46% on LVIS box-based referring classification and 84.76% on Flickr30k phrase grounding. **Ferret-v2** [COLM 2024] further improves with any resolution grounding, multi-granularity visual encoding (combining CLIP and DINOv2), and a three-stage training paradigm, achieving 92.79% on RefCOCO val (vs. 87.49% for Ferret-7B).

**GLaMM** [Rasheed et al., CVPR 2024] produces natural language responses intertwined with corresponding object segmentation masks, comprising five core components: Global Image Encoder, Region Encoder, LLM, Grounding Image Encoder, and Pixel Decoder. The **Grounding-anything Dataset (GranD)** includes 11M images, 810M regions with segmentation masks, and 84M referring expressions, enabling state-of-the-art performance on grounded conversation generation (CIDEr 47.2) and referring expression segmentation. **GROUNDHOG** [CVPR 2024] grounds language to holistic segmentation using a masked feature extractor and visual entity tokens, eliminating the need for bounding boxes. **LISA** [CVPR 2024, Highlight] introduces reasoning segmentation, handling complex implicit queries about objects and spatial relationships. **VideoGLaMM** [Munasinghe et al., CVPR 2025] extends pixel-level grounding to videos, connecting an LLM, dual vision encoder, and spatio-temporal decoder, trained on 38k video-QA triplets and 671k masks.

**ARGUS** [CVPR 2025] introduces a vision-centric approach with explicit grounded chain-of-thought signals through a **goal-directed visual attention mechanism**: first predicting a bounding box for relevant visual content, then re-engaging with that region. Using a mixture-of-vision-experts (MoVE) encoder (CLIP, ConvNeXt, EVA-02) and Llama3-8B decoder, Argus achieves state-of-the-art among 7B-8B open MLLMs, with 89.8% on RefCOCO val, while ablation studies confirm that explicit CoT (re-sampling or re-encoding) significantly outperforms implicit attention.

## 3. Methodological Advances for 2D Spatial Reasoning

### 3.1 Spatial Reasoning Enhancement Frameworks

**SpatialVLM** [Chen et al., CVPR 2024] addresses the limitation of VLMs in 3D spatial reasoning by introducing an automatic 3D spatial VQA data generation framework that produces **2 billion VQA examples** from 10 million real-world images. The pipeline lifts 2D images into metric-scale 3D point clouds using expert models (segmentation, metric depth, object-centric captions), enabling co-training a VLM (PaLM-E) that significantly improves spatial VQA performance and enables chain-of-thought spatial reasoning. SpatialVLM achieves state-of-the-art on both binary predicate benchmarks and quantitative spatial estimation, with 37.2% of answers falling within 0.5×–2× range of human-annotated ground truth.

**SpatialRGPT** [Cheng et al., NeurIPS 2024] enhances VLMs' spatial perception through a data curation pipeline constructing 3D scene graphs from single 2D images, generating the **Open Spatial Dataset (OSD)** with 8.7M spatial concepts across 5M unique regions, and a flexible plugin module for integrating depth information into existing VLM visual encoders. The model significantly outperforms baselines including GPT-4V, LLaVA, and KOSMOS-2 on the **SpatialRGPT-Bench** benchmark, and demonstrates practical applications in complex spatial reasoning, multi-hop reasoning, and region-aware dense reward annotation for robotics.

**SpatialReasoner-R1** [Shen et al., NeurIPS 2025] improves fine-grained spatial reasoning through structured Long Chain-of-Thought (Long-CoT) reasoning. The authors propose **Multi-Model Monte Carlo Tree Search (M3CTS)** leveraging multiple VLMs to generate diverse reasoning trajectories, and **fine-grained Direct Preference Optimization (fDPO)** applying separate optimization updates to descriptive grounding and logical reasoning segments. The model achieves state-of-the-art on SpatialRGPT-Bench, outperforming SpatialRGPT-8B by 9.4% in average accuracy, with fDPO yielding 4.1% and 9.0% relative gains over standard DPO.

**Spark** [NeurIPS 2025] introduces a novel paradigm of **drawing to reason in space**, enabling LVLMs to externalize spatial reasoning by generating intermediate visual representations. **VISER** [NeurIPS 2025] addresses the binding problem in Large Vision-Language Models—the failure to correctly associate features with visual referents—by augmenting visual inputs with low-level spatial structures. Using only single-query inference, VISER improves GPT-4o performance by +25.0% on visual search, +26.8% on counting, and +9.5% on spatial relationships, demonstrating that visual input design is more effective than linguistic reasoning strategies for enhancing compositional spatial reasoning.

### 3.2 Object Counting and Visual Reasoning

Object counting as a spatial reasoning task has received focused attention. The **Sparkle** framework [EMNLP 2025 Findings] improves spatial reasoning in VLMs by focusing on three basic 2D spatial capabilities: direction comprehension, distance estimation, and localization. Generating synthetic instruction data and fine-tuning VLMs using LoRA, Sparkle demonstrates significant improvements: InternVL2-8B accuracy on direction comprehension jumps from 34.6% to 83.2%, and on shortest path (SPP) from 15.4% to 38.8%. Enhanced models generalize to real-world benchmarks (What's Up, COCO-spatial, GQA-spatial) with gains of 10-20% without degrading performance on standard VLM benchmarks.

**CLEVR-Ref+** [CVPR 2019] provides a synthetic diagnostic dataset for referring expression comprehension with ordinality and visibility modules, ground truth functional programs, and intermediate segmentation masks. The **IEP-Ref** Neural Module Network achieves significantly higher IoU (0.928 with ground truth programs) than other models, especially on tasks requiring compositionality. The **Expand VSR Benchmark** [AAAI 2025] extends Visual Spatial Reasoning benchmarks for VLLMs to specialize in spatial rules.

## 4. Evaluation Benchmarks for 2D Spatial Reasoning

### 4.1 Foundational Benchmarks (Pre-2022, Still Active)

**VSR (Visual Spatial Reasoning)** [TACL 2023] contains 10,972 caption-image pairs with true/false labels covering 66 spatial relation types. The best model (LXMERT) achieves only 70.1% on the random split, while human performance reaches 95.4%, revealing that all models struggle with orientation-related relations (e.g., 'facing', 'parallel to'), performing at chance level. **NLVR2** [ACL 2019] provides 107,292 examples of natural language sentences paired with pairs of web photographs, with human performance at ~96.1% and state-of-the-art models (UNITER, LXMERT) achieving 74-78%, demonstrating the dataset's strong challenge. **GQA** [CVPR 2019] offers 22M diverse reasoning questions with functional programs, with human performance at 89.3% and strong VQA models achieving only 54.1%.

**RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg** [EMNLP 2014; ECCV 2016] remain the standard benchmarks for referring expression comprehension, with recent work [Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024] revealing high labeling error rates (14% in RefCOCO, 24% in RefCOCO+, 5% in RefCOCOg) and proposing the **Ref-L4** benchmark for cleaner evaluation. The **Visual Genome** [IJCV 2017] provides dense annotations of objects, attributes, and relationships across 108K images for scene graph generation and relationship detection.

### 4.2 Newer 2D Spatial Benchmarks (2022–2025)

**What'sUp** [EMNLP 2023] investigates why VLMs struggle with basic spatial reasoning, introducing three benchmarks (What'sUp, COCO-spatial, GQA-spatial) that isolate spatial reasoning from other visual tasks. Evaluation of 18 VL models shows all perform poorly, with many near random chance (BLIP fine-tuned on VQAv2 achieves 56% accuracy vs. 99% human performance). Analysis of the LAION-2B pre-training corpus reveals that spatial prepositions occur in less than 0.2% of captions, and attempts to improve models via caption prior normalization, prompt engineering, and finetuning all fail to produce significant gains.

**SpatialEval** [NeurIPS 2024] evaluates spatial reasoning in LLMs and VLMs across four tasks (Spatial-Map, Maze-Nav, Spatial-Grid, Spatial-Real) with three modalities each (text-only, vision-only, vision-text). Key findings reveal that VLMs often under-perform compared to their LLM counterparts despite additional visual input, and that competitive models can fall behind random guessing. **BLINK** [ECCV 2024] comprises 14 classic computer vision tasks reformatted into 3,807 multiple-choice questions, with humans achieving 95.70% accuracy while GPT-4V reaches only 51.26%—merely 13.17% above random guessing.

**OmniSpatial** [NeurIPS/ICLR 2025] covers 50 fine-grained subcategories and over 8.4K manually curated QA pairs. Even the best models (o3, Gemini-2.5-pro) achieve only ~56% accuracy, far below human performance (92.63%). **3DSRBench** [ICCV 2025] features 2,762 manually annotated visual question-answer pairs across 12 question types, with state-of-the-art LMMs achieving around 60% overall vs. human-level 95.7%. All tested LMMs show significant performance drops (up to 32%) when generalizing from common to uncommon camera viewpoints, indicating lack of robust 3D representations.

**SpatialBench** [2025] evaluates MLLMs on spatial cognition grounded in a hierarchical framework from observation (L1) to planning (L5). Models perform well on perceptual and relational tasks (L1–L2) but struggle significantly on symbolic reasoning, causal inference, and planning (L3–L5), with Gemini-2.5-pro leading at 75.79 vs. human performance of 96.40. **SIBench** compiles nearly 20 open-source datasets covering 23 task settings, revealing a pronounced gap between perception and reasoning, with models showing competence in basic perceptual tasks but consistently underperforming in higher-order reasoning.

**SpatialMQA** [ACL 2025] provides 5,392 multiple-choice samples covering six spatial relations, where the best model achieves only 48.14% accuracy, far below human level of 98.40%. **SURDS** [NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks] evaluates spatial reasoning in autonomous driving scenarios with 41,080 training instances, revealing that many models perform near random chance on single-object tasks. The authors propose a reinforcement learning alignment scheme based on GRPO that achieves 40.80 overall score, outperforming GPT-4o (13.30) and Gemini-2.0-flash (35.71). **SFI-Bench** [CVPR 2026] evaluates spatial-functional intelligence with over 1,700 questions from egocentric indoor scans, showing current MLLMs struggle to integrate spatial memory with functional and external knowledge.

## 5. 3D Spatial Understanding: Architectures and Models

### 5.1 3D Visual Grounding and Referring Expression

The foundational work in 3D visual grounding established the task of localizing objects in 3D scenes using natural language. **ScanRefer** [ECCV 2020] introduced the benchmark dataset with 51,583 descriptions of 11,046 objects across 800 ScanNet scenes, while **ReferIt3D** [ECCV 2020, Oral] provided complementary datasets: Nr3D (41,503 natural language descriptions) and Sr3D (83,572 template-based descriptions). **LanguageRefer** [CoRL 2021] demonstrated that explicit spatial-language modeling with positional encodings significantly improves accuracy, achieving 43.9% on Nr3D.

**3D-VisTA** [Zhu et al., ICCV 2023] represents a major breakthrough as the first large-scale 3D vision-language pre-training framework. Using self-attention for both single-modal and multi-modal fusion, the model is pre-trained on **ScanScribe**—a large-scale dataset of 2,995 RGB-D scans from 1,185 indoor scenes with 278K paired scene descriptions. Pre-training involves masked language/object modeling and scene-text matching, achieving state-of-the-art results on 3D visual grounding (ScanRefer: +8.1% at IoU 0.5), dense captioning (Scan2Cap: +10.1% CIDEr), question answering (ScanQA), and situated reasoning (SQA3D). The model demonstrates strong data efficiency, achieving competitive results with only 30% of downstream annotations.

**SeeGround** [CVPR 2025] leverages 2D VLMs for zero-shot 3DVG, introducing a hybrid input format combining query-aligned rendered views with spatially enriched textual descriptions. The training-free framework outperforms existing zero-shot baselines by 7.7% on ScanRefer and 7.1% on Nr3D, approaching fully supervised methods. **ReasonGrounder** [CVPR 2025] addresses open-vocabulary 3D visual grounding for occluded objects using LVLM-guided hierarchical 3D feature Gaussian fields, contributing the **ReasoningGD** dataset with over 10K scenes and 2 million annotations for amodal perception evaluation.

**Multi3DRefer** [ICCV 2023] generalizes grounding to flexible object counts (zero, single, or multiple), with 61,926 descriptions of 11,609 objects across 800 ScanNet scenes. **CityRefer** [NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks] extends grounding to city-scale environments with 35,196 descriptions for 5,866 target objects across 6 km² of photogrammetric point clouds. **AugRefer** [AAAI 2025] pioneers cross-modal augmentation by inserting new objects into 3D scenes and using BLIP2 and GPT-3.5 to generate diverse descriptions, achieving +3.05% Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer and +9.81% on Nr3D.

### 5.2 Unified 3D Vision-Language Models

**3D-LLM** [Hong et al., NeurIPS 2023, Spotlight] represents the first comprehensive effort to inject 3D world understanding into LLMs, taking 3D point clouds as input and performing diverse tasks: captioning, dense captioning, 3D question answering, task decomposition, 3D grounding, 3D-assisted dialog, and navigation. Using a 3D feature extractor from rendered multi-view images, 2D VLMs as backbones, and a 3D localization mechanism, the model outperforms state-of-the-art on ScanQA by 9% in BLEU-1 score. Training involves over 300k 3D-language data generated through three prompting mechanisms.

**Chat-3D v2** and **Chat-Scene** [NeurIPS 2024; TPAMI 2026 for Chat-Scene++] use object identifiers—learnable tokens like `<OBJk>`—assigned to object proposals obtained from a 3D detector (Mask3D). Representing the scene as a sequence of object-level embeddings derived from 2D (DINOv2) and 3D (Uni3D) encoders, Chat-Scene converts diverse tasks (visual grounding, dense captioning, VQA) into a unified QA format, achieving state-of-the-art on ScanRefer (+3.7% Acc@0.5), Multi3DRefer (+14.0% F1@0.5), Scan2Cap (+8.7% CIDEr@0.5), ScanQA (+7.7% CIDEr), and SQA3D. The model was ranked 1st on ScanRefer and Scan2Cap benchmarks as of September 2024.

**Inst3D-LMM** [CVPR 2025, Highlight] introduces a unified Large Multi-modal Model for instance-aware 3D scene understanding, with a Multi-view Cross-Modal Fusion (MCMF) module to inject 2D semantics into 3D geometric features and a 3D Instance Spatial Relation (3D-ISR) module to capture pairwise spatial relationships. Performing end-to-end multi-task instruction tuning without task-specific fine-tuning, the model achieves state-of-the-art across 3D scene understanding, reasoning, and grounding tasks.

### 5.3 3D Question Answering

**ScanQA** [Azuma et al., CVPR 2022] established the first large-scale 3D question answering dataset with 41,363 question-answer pairs from 800 ScanNet scenes, featuring free-form, human-edited answers and object annotations. The baseline model uses a 3D & language encoder (VoteNet, biLSTM), transformer-based fusion layer, and downstream layers for answer classification and object localization. **SQA3D** [Ma et al., ICLR 2023] introduces situated question answering, requiring agents to understand their position and orientation in a 3D scene as described by text, then answer reasoning questions. Built from 650 ScanNet scenes with 6.8k unique situations, 20.4k situation descriptions, and 33.4k reasoning questions, the best model achieves only 47.20% accuracy while amateur humans reach 90.06%, revealing a massive gap in situated reasoning.

**Beacon3D** [CVPR 2025] provides a benchmark for 3D vision-language understanding addressing three key limitations: flawed test data, oversimplified metrics, and isolated grounding/QA tasks. The benchmark reveals weak generalization in QA, fragile grounding-QA coherence, and that incorporating LLMs hinders grounding without improving QA. **SpatialRGPT** [NeurIPS 2024] extends to 3D by enhancing VLMs' spatial perception through 3D scene graph construction and depth integration, creating the **SpatialRGPT-Bench** benchmark with ground-truth 3D annotations for indoor, outdoor, and simulated environments.

## 6. 3D Vision-Language Navigation

### 6.1 Foundational Navigation Architectures

**Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE)** [Krantz et al., ECCV 2020] fundamentally redefined VLN by removing unrealistic graph-based assumptions, requiring agents to navigate in continuous 3D environments (Matterport3D via Habitat simulator) using low-level actions. The best model achieves only 32% success rate and 0.30 SPL on unseen val, compared to higher performance in graph-based settings, establishing a more challenging and ecologically valid testbed.

**HAMT** [Chen et al., NeurIPS 2021] introduces a hierarchical vision transformer encoding the full history of panoramic observations: first encoding individual view images, then modeling spatial relations within a panorama, and finally capturing temporal relations across panoramas. Jointly processing text instructions, history, and current observation, HAMT achieves state-of-the-art on multiple VLN benchmarks including R2R, RxR, R2R-Last, REVERIE, CVDN, R4R, and R2R-Back, with particular effectiveness on longer trajectories. **BEVBert** [An et al., ICCV 2023] proposes a hybrid map representation combining local metric maps (for aggregating observations) and global topological maps (for long-term planning), achieving state-of-the-art on R2R, R2R-CE, RxR, and REVERIE.

### 6.2 Volumetric and Dynamic 3D Representations

**Volumetric Environment Representation (VER)** [Liu et al., CVPR 2024] voxelizes the physical world into structured 3D cells, aggregating multi-view 2D features into a unified 3D space via cross-view attention. A coarse-to-fine extraction architecture with learnable up-sampling progressively reconstructs VER, supervised by multi-task learning on 3D occupancy prediction, room layout estimation, and 3D object detection. The agent achieves 76% SR and 66% SPL on R2R test unseen (3% and 4% improvements over BEVBert), demonstrating the value of explicit 3D geometry modeling.

**Dynam3D** [NeurIPS 2025, Oral] constructs dynamic layered 3D representations from posed RGB-D images by projecting 2D CLIP features into 3D space to create multi-level patch-instance-zone representations. Supporting online encoding, localization, and dynamic updating of 3D instances, Dynam3D sets new state-of-the-art on R2R-CE, REVERIE-CE, and NavRAG-CE under monocular settings, validated through pre-exploration, lifelong memory, and real-world robot experiments.

**Hierarchical Semantic-Augmented Navigation (HSAN)** [NeurIPS 2025] integrates dynamic hierarchical semantic scene graphs using VLMs and spectral clustering, optimal transport-based topological planning with Kantorovich duality, and graph-aware reinforcement learning (PPO with GCNs). The approach achieves 64% SR and 0.59 SPL on R2R-CE, outperforming LLaVA-Nav (58% SR, 0.53 SPL), and 59% SR and 0.54 SPL on RxR-CE.

### 6.3 LLM-Enhanced Navigation and Advanced Training

**NavGPT-2** [ECCV 2024] leverages LLMs for instruction-following robotic navigation by aligning visual content in a frozen LLM, demonstrating data efficiency and eliminating the gap between LM-based agents and state-of-the-art VLN specialists. **VLN-R1** uses Large Vision-Language Models to process egocentric video streams directly, introducing the VLN-Ego dataset and a Long-Short Memory Sampling strategy. Training consists of Supervised Fine-Tuning followed by Reinforcement Fine-Tuning using GRPO with a Time-Decayed Reward mechanism, demonstrating that the 2B model with RFT outperforms the 7B model with SFT.

**Aux-Think** [NeurIPS 2025] systematically evaluates reasoning strategies, revealing "Inference-time Reasoning Collapse," where reasoning during inference harms navigation accuracy. The proposed Aux-Think uses Chain-of-Thought supervision during training but retains No-Think inference for efficiency, releasing the R2R-CoT-320k dataset. **HA-VLN** [NeurIPS 2024, Spotlight] extends VLN to incorporate dynamic human interactions, proposing the HA3D simulator and HA-R2R dataset with human activity descriptions.

**ATENA** [NeurIPS 2025] introduces a test-time active learning framework addressing distributional shifts in unfamiliar environments, using mixture entropy optimization and self-active learning. **Iterative Vision-and-Language Navigation (IVLN)** [CVPR 2023] evaluates language-guided agents navigating in persistent environments over time, revealing that extending implicit memory of transformer VLN agents is insufficient for IVLN, while map-building agents benefit from environment persistence.

## 7. Conclusion

The period from 2022 to 2025 has witnessed remarkable progress in spatial understanding and reasoning in vision-language models, characterized by several key trends. Architecturally, the field has evolved from foundational models (OFA, Flamingo, BLIP-2) to spatially-aware grounding and referring models (Shikra, Ferret, GLaMM, Argus), with position encoding innovations (RoPE variants, Spiral RoPE, Circle-RoPE) providing the geometric foundations for spatial awareness. Methodologically, explicit spatial reasoning frameworks (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, SpatialReasoner-R1) and chain-of-thought approaches (ARGUS, Spark) have demonstrated that structured spatial reasoning can be effectively integrated into VLMs. In 3D, pre-training frameworks (3D-VisTA, 3D-LLM) and unified models (Chat-Scene, Inst3D-LMM) have enabled comprehensive 3D scene understanding, while navigation architectures (BEVBert, Volumetric Environment Representation, Dynam3D) have pushed the boundaries of language-guided navigation.

Despite these advances, consistent empirical studies reveal a persistent and substantial gap between model and human performance. On 2D spatial reasoning benchmarks, best-in-class models lag human baselines by 15–30 percentage points, with orientation-related relations and complex spatial reasoning tasks proving particularly challenging. In 3D, the gap is even more pronounced, with models achieving only 47-60% accuracy on situated reasoning benchmarks compared to 90-95% human performance. The field has developed increasingly sophisticated benchmarks (SpatialEval, BLINK, OmniSpatial, 3DSRBench, SIBench) that systematically isolate spatial reasoning from other visual capabilities, revealing that current data-driven scaling alone is insufficient for achieving robust spatial intelligence.

Key open challenges include: the lack of explicit 3D representations in most 2D VLMs, the difficulty of compositional generalization in spatial reasoning, the fragility of grounding-QA coherence in 3D models, and the need for architectures that treat vision as a primary modality for spatial reasoning rather than a secondary channel. The convergence of reinforcement learning (GRPO-based alignment), structured chain-of-thought reasoning, and explicit geometric representations (3D scene graphs, volumetric models) represents a promising direction for bridging the gap between current models and human-level spatial intelligence.
