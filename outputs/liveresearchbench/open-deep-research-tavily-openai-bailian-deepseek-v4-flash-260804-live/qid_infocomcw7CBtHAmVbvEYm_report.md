# CEO/Chair Duality in the Fortune Global 500 (as of August 5, 2026)

## Overview

Based on the 2026 Fortune Global 500 list (published July 28, 2026), which ranks the world’s 500 largest corporations by fiscal year 2025 revenue, a significant minority of CEOs also serve as Board Chair. The practice of CEO duality – where one person holds both the chief executive and board chair roles – is most common among large-cap companies. In the S&P 500, 42% of companies combined the roles in 2025, down from 47% in 2020, according to a May 2026 report from The Conference Board published on the Harvard Law School Forum on Corporate Governance [[2]](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/05/13/ceo-chair-leadership-when-and-why-boards-combine-or-separate-the-roles). The trend is even more pronounced among very large firms: 55% of companies with over $50 billion in revenue combine the roles, compared to only 25% of those with under $100 million [[7]](https://www.thecorporatecounsel.net/blog/2022/07/board-governance-dual-chair-ceos-on-the-decline.html).

The following companies on the 2026 Fortune Global 500 have been confirmed – through independent news articles and official governance documents – to have a CEO who also serves as Board Chair as of August 5, 2026. Companies where the CEO previously held both titles but no longer does (e.g., Berkshire Hathaway, where Greg Abel became CEO on January 1, 2026, while Warren Buffett remains Chair) are excluded. This list is based on the available research and is not exhaustive; a complete audit of all 500 companies would require direct access to every company’s proxy statement.

## Key Observations

- **Prevalence:** Approximately 42% of S&P 500 companies combine the CEO and chair roles, and the rate is higher among the largest firms. This suggests that roughly 200–250 of the Fortune Global 500 companies may have CEO duality, though the proportion varies by region (much lower in Europe and Asia).  
- **Founder-CEOs:** Many of the confirmed dual-role CEOs are founders or long-tenured leaders (e.g., Zuckerberg, Benioff, Dimon). Founder-CEOs are more likely to retain the chair role.  
- **Recent Trends:** Several large companies, including Berkshire Hathaway and AIG, have recently split the roles. The global trend is toward separation, but many powerful companies still maintain the combined structure.  
- **Data Gaps:** This report is based on publicly available research and does not cover all 500 companies. A complete list would require systematic analysis of each company’s proxy statement or governance documents, particularly for non-U.S. companies where DEF 14A filings may not be available.
