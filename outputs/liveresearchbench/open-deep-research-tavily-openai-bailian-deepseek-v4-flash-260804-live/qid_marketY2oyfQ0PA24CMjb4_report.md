# Comprehensive Research Report: Food Delivery Market in North America and Europe (2025–2026)

## Executive Summary

The global online food delivery market continues its robust growth trajectory through 2025–2026, driven by entrenched consumer habits, urbanization, and platform expansion into grocery and retail verticals. The market is valued between USD 257–320 billion globally in 2025, with North America accounting for approximately 31–34% of revenues and Europe representing 24–27% [1][2][3]. Both regions face structural challenges including profitability pressures, evolving labor regulations, and intense competition, yet platforms are adapting through diversification, subscription models, and technological innovation. DoorDash dominates the U.S. market with 65–68% share, while Uber Eats holds 23–25% and Grubhub has declined to 7–9% [4][5][6]. In Europe, consolidation is reshaping the landscape: Prosus acquired Just Eat Takeaway for €4.1 billion, and DoorDash acquired Deliveroo for £2.9 billion [7][8].

## 3. Demand Drivers

### 3.1 Urbanization

Urbanization remains a fundamental structural driver of food delivery demand:

- Over 75% of Europe's population lives in urban areas [36].
- 57.8% of the global population lives in cities, with cities generating 80% of GDP [37].
- Urban residents lead food delivery app usage: 39.2% of urban residents use apps weekly, compared to only 15.3% in rural areas [38].
- The number of megacities quadrupled from 8 in 1975 to 33 in 2025 [39].
- High population density enables favorable delivery economics: China's urban areas have 2,426 people per square kilometer (almost 8 times the U.S.), enabling delivery costs of about $1 compared with $5 in the U.S. [40].
- The South region of the U.S. holds the largest regional market share at 28.7%, reflecting demographic tailwinds from population migration to Texas, Florida, and Georgia [14].

### 3.2 Consumer Convenience and Post-Pandemic Behavioral Shifts

The COVID-19 pandemic created a permanent structural shift in consumer behavior:

- 77% of U.S. consumers state that convenience is a critical component in their purchasing decisions [16].
- 52% of U.S. consumers say food delivery is an "essential" part of their lifestyle [41].
- 68% of pandemic-era users still order weekly [42].
- 67% of Millennials and 63% of Gen Z rely on food delivery regularly [41].
- Gen Z leads in using food delivery apps: 2.3 orders per week versus 1.1 orders for older generations [43].
- Nearly 75% of all restaurant traffic now happens off-premises (takeout, drive-thru, delivery) [44].
- 73% of consumers prioritize faster delivery, making it the number one factor when choosing a delivery service [43].
- 40% of diners prefer to order delivery and takeout through a restaurant's website and app [43].
- The average American spends over $1,566 annually on food delivery services, with each order costing about $35.42 [45].
- 60% of U.S. consumers order at least once a week [33].

### 3.3 Inflation Pressures and Consumer Spending

Inflation is reshaping consumer behavior in complex ways:

- Food away from home rose 4.1% year-over-year (January 2026), nearly double the grocery inflation rate [46].
- Over the past five years, food and labor costs for the average restaurant have each climbed 35% [46].
- Delivery now costs nearly 80% more than pickup, adding an average of $9.30 per order [47].
- 91% of diners have noticed menu price increases; 80% use BOGO deals or real-time specials to save [48].
- 82% of consumers have changed shopping behavior due to high prices [49].
- 44% of consumers cite high delivery costs as a concern [21].
- Average basket value for delivery fell 6% and spend per unit dropped 12% [50].
- Pickup orders grew 14% while delivery orders fell 6% and spending dropped 12% [50].
- Gen Z's spending at quick-service restaurants has dropped faster than any other generation, down 19 percentage points over two years [46].

### 3.4 Technological Adoption

Technology is transforming every aspect of food delivery:

- 95% of restaurants use AI-assisted inventory management, menu optimization, reservations, and/or other forms of AI [43].
- AI-powered logistics systems improved delivery route efficiency by 31% [21].
- The integration of AI for demand forecasting has enabled platforms to reduce food waste by an estimated 18% while simultaneously increasing order frequency per active user by approximately 22% year-over-year [51].
- 52% of U.S. diners are comfortable with restaurants or delivery apps using AI to provide personalized food recommendations [52].
- 45% of professional kitchens are expected to adopt AI tools by 2025 for inventory forecasting and demand planning [53].
- Drone deliveries are projected to handle 8% of orders by end of 2025 [43].

**Cloud Kitchens / Ghost Kitchens:**

- The U.S. dark kitchen market was valued at approximately USD 3.54 billion in 2023, expected to reach USD 104.3 billion by 2030, growing at a CAGR of 62.1% [55].

## 4. Challenges

### 4.1 Profitability Issues

Despite market growth, profitability remains elusive for most players:

- Most restaurants lose money on aggregator orders due to 15–30% commissions, unchanged dine-in pricing, and hidden costs [46].
- The average restaurant net profit margin is between 3% and 9%, so a 30% delivery commission exceeds the restaurant's entire profit margin by 3–10× [58].
- For a typical $50 order, true platform cost is 41–43.5%, leaving restaurants with only $28–30 before cost of goods sold and labor [59].
- 72% of restaurants say high commission fees are their most significant challenge with delivery platforms [43].
- "An order that leaves 68 cents on the dollar in the dining room may leave 12 or less through an aggregator" [46].
- China's urban areas have 2,426 people per square kilometer (almost eight times the comparable U.S. population density), enabling deliveries in China to cost about $1, compared with $5 in the U.S. [40].

**Platform Profitability Strategies:**
- DoorDash's approach rests on four pillars: DashPass subscriptions, DoorDash Drive (white-label logistics), DashMart (owned virtual convenience stores), and DoorDash for Work (corporate catering) [60].
- DoorDash reported GAAP net income of $935 million in FY2025, compared to $123 million in 2024 [61].
- Uber's delivery segment became EBITDA profitable in 2023, with Delivery EBITDA margin reaching 4.6% in Q4 2025 [62][63].
- Deliveroo achieved its first-ever profit of £3 million in 2024 [64].

### 4.2 Labor Regulation Changes

Labor regulation is the most significant regulatory challenge facing the industry:

**European Union — Platform Work Directive:**
- The EU Platform Work Directive (Directive 2024/2831), adopted October 2024, requires EU Member States to transpose its provisions into national law by December 2, 2026 [65].
- The directive has four pillars: (1) a legal presumption of employment, shifting the burden of proof to the platform; (2) restrictions on algorithmic management; (3) human review of automated decisions; and (4) enhanced data sharing and enforcement rights [65].
- As of mid-2026, transposition is progressing unevenly, with France, Germany, Italy, Spain, and the Netherlands moving on schedule [65].
- The European Labour Authority reports 28.3 million EU platform workers existed in 2021, with 5.5 million potentially misclassified [66].

**United States — Federal and State Developments:**
- The U.S. Department of Labor published a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on February 26, 2026, refining criteria for determining independent contractor vs. employee status [67].
- California's Proposition 22 was upheld by the California Supreme Court in July 2024 [68].
- New York City implemented a minimum wage rule requiring app-based delivery drivers to be paid at least $17.96 per hour, with a scheduled increase to $19.96 per hour by April 2025 [68].
- Seattle requires ride-share and app-based delivery companies to provide a valid reason before deactivating a worker [68].
- The FTC published an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on April 16, 2026, seeking public comment on a potential rule to address unfair or deceptive fees charged by online food delivery platforms [69].

### 4.3 Competition and Market Consolidation

The competitive landscape is undergoing rapid consolidation:

- DoorDash holds 65–68% of the U.S. food delivery market, Uber Eats holds 23–25%, and Grubhub has declined to 7–9% [4][5][6].
- Together, DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub control 80% of the U.S. market [33].
- The top five platforms globally (Meituan, Uber Eats, DoorDash, Just Eat Takeaway, Delivery Hero) account for approximately 61% of global gross merchandise volume [70].

**Major M&A Activity (2024–2025):**
- **February 2025**: Prosus acquired Just Eat Takeaway for €4.1 billion [7].
- **May–October 2025**: DoorDash acquired Deliveroo for £2.9 billion ($3.9 billion), completing on October 2, 2025 [8].
- **January 2025**: Wonder Group acquired Grubhub from Just Eat Takeaway for $650 million—a 91% discount on the $7.3 billion paid in 2020 [71].
- **May 2024**: Uber Eats acquired Foodpanda in Taiwan for $950 million [72].

- DoorDash became the leading third-party marketplace in U.S. grocery and retail order volume in 2025 [30].
- Uber Eats expects non-restaurant deliveries to hit $12.5 billion annual run rate by end of 2025 [31].
- Deliveroo's grocery now represents 18% of group GTV [73].

### 7.4 Technology and AI

- 95% of restaurants use AI-assisted inventory management, menu optimization, reservations, and/or other forms of AI [43].
- DoorDash and Uber Eats are investing heavily in AI-powered logistics, personalized recommendations, and demand forecasting [87][93].

## 8. Conclusion

The food delivery market in North America and Europe is experiencing robust growth through 2025–2026, with the global market projected to reach USD 350–470 billion by 2026. North America accounts for approximately 31–34% of global revenues, while Europe represents 24–27%. The market is being driven by entrenched consumer convenience preferences, urbanization, and platform expansion into grocery and retail verticals.

However, the industry faces significant challenges: profitability remains elusive for many players, labor regulations are tightening on both sides of the Atlantic, and competition is intensifying through consolidation. DoorDash's dominance in the U.S. (65–68% market share) is being reinforced through its acquisition of Deliveroo and expansion into grocery, while Uber Eats leverages its rideshare integration and global scale. Grubhub has been marginalized, and Deliveroo is now part of DoorDash's global footprint.

The key competitive battlegrounds are shifting from restaurant delivery alone to multi-category platforms offering restaurant, grocery, convenience, and retail delivery. Subscription models, advertising revenue, and technological innovation are becoming critical differentiators. The next phase of growth will depend on platforms' ability to achieve sustainable profitability while navigating regulatory challenges and expanding their total addressable market.
