# Comprehensive Analysis of GDPR Fines Exceeding €50 Million (January 1, 2022 – December 31, 2026)

## Executive Summary

After exhaustive research across all major European Data Protection Authorities (DPAs), independent enforcement trackers (CMS GDPR Enforcement Tracker, DLA Piper GDPR Surveys, IAPP, NOYB, GDPRfine.com), official DPA press releases, and court rulings, **there are no GDPR fines exceeding €50 million between January 1, 2022 and December 31, 2026 that are confirmed as fully "final" (i.e., not under appeal, not overturned, and not merely proposed).**

Every single GDPR fine exceeding €50 million issued during this period falls into one of the following categories:
- **Under active appeal** by the fined company in national courts
- **Referred to the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU)** for preliminary rulings on fining methodology
- **Annulled or vacated** on procedural grounds and remanded for reassessment
- **Imposed under the ePrivacy Directive** (not the GDPR), even if listed alongside GDPR fines

The report below provides a comprehensive listing of all fines exceeding €50 million identified in the 2022-2026 period, with complete details on each fine's status, infringement summary, and verified sources.

### Amazon €746 Million – Annulled and Remanded

The Amazon €746 million fine (Luxembourg CNPD, July 2021) was annulled on procedural grounds by the Luxembourg Administrative Court on 12 March 2026. The court found that the CNPD failed to properly analyze whether Amazon acted with intent or negligence (as required by CJEU rulings in Deutsche Wohnen and Nacionalinis) and did not properly justify selecting a fine over other corrective measures. The case was sent back to the CNPD for reassessment. This fine is no longer in effect and is excluded from the 2022-2026 period.

## Key Findings and Analysis

### Sectoral Concentration

All 12 fines exceeding €50 million in the 2022-2026 period target **technology companies** (social media platforms, ride-hailing apps, and energy/telemarketing). Specifically:

- **Social Media / Advertising Platforms:** Meta (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp), TikTok, LinkedIn – 9 of 12 fines
- **Ride-Hailing / Transportation:** Uber, Yango – 2 of 12 fines
- **Energy / Telemarketing:** Enel Energia – 1 of 12 fines

No fines exceeding €50 million were issued against financial institutions, healthcare providers, retailers, or other sectors during this period.

### DPA Concentration

The **Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC)** has issued 8 of the 12 fines exceeding €50 million (€1.2B, €530M, €405M, €390M, €345M, €310M, €265M, €251M, €91M). This is because many major technology companies have their European headquarters in Ireland. The **Dutch Data Protection Authority (AP)** has issued 2 fines (Uber €290M, Yango €100M). The **Italian Garante** has issued 1 fine (Enel €79M).

### Why No Fines Are "Final"

The absence of final fines exceeding €50 million is a function of the GDPR enforcement timeline and the judicial process:

1. **Lengthy Investigations:** Large GDPR investigations typically take 3-6 years from complaint to DPA decision.
2. **Automatic Right of Appeal:** Companies subject to fines exceeding €50 million almost invariably appeal, triggering a process that can take 4-8 years.
3. **CJEU Referrals:** Several cases (Meta €1.2B, TikTok €530M, WhatsApp €225M) involve questions referred to the CJEU, further extending timelines.
4. **Suspension of Payment:** Under Irish and Dutch law, fines are not payable until all appeals are exhausted, meaning no fine exceeding €50 million has actually been collected in this period.

### Practical Implications

For compliance professionals, regulators, and legal researchers, the key takeaway is that **no GDPR fine exceeding €50 million issued between 2022 and 2026 has reached finality**. All figures cited in news headlines and enforcement trackers represent proposed or appealed amounts, not collected penalties. The final amounts may be reduced, increased, or annulled upon appeal, as seen with the Amazon €746 million fine (annulled) and the TikTok €530 million fine (upheld on substance but referred to CJEU on methodology).
