# Investment Philosophies Compared: Duan Yongping, Warren Buffett, and Charlie Munger

## Introduction

Duan Yongping (段永平), Warren Buffett, and Charlie Munger represent three of the most influential value investors of the modern era, linked by a direct line of intellectual descent. Duan, widely called "China's Warren Buffett," paid $620,100 in 2006 to have lunch with Buffett — the first Chinese investor to do so — and credits that meeting with reshaping his investment framework around the concept of "business model" [ScienceNet report](https://news.sciencenet.cn/htmlnews/2025/1/537045.shtm). Buffett and Munger, partners for nearly six decades, built Berkshire Hathaway into a roughly $1 trillion enterprise through a philosophy that evolved from Benjamin Graham's "cigar butt" bargains to Munger's "wonderful businesses at fair prices" [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/academy/trading/warren-buffett-investment-strategy-rules-fortune).

This report compares the three investors across six dimensions: core principles, business quality and moats, valuation and timing, risk and concentration, the role of character, and notable similarities and differences. The research draws on primary sources including Duan's public writings on Xueqiu and his 2025–2026 interviews, Buffett's shareholder letters and meeting transcripts, Munger's speeches compiled in *Poor Charlie's Almanack* and his Daily Journal annual meeting Q&As, and reliable secondary analyses.

## 1. The Three Investors at a Glance

### Duan Yongping (段永平)

Duan was born in 1961 in Jiangxi Province, studied at Zhejiang University and Renmin University, and in 1989 took over a failing factory that became "Little Tyrant" (小霸王), which grew to over ¥1 billion in annual sales and 80% of China's home gaming market. In 1995 he founded BBK Electronics (步步高), which later incubated OPPO, vivo, OnePlus, and Little Genius — companies that together became China's second- and third-largest smartphone suppliers [投中网](https://www.chinaventure.com.cn/news/80-20251117-388888.html). He retired from operations in 2001, moved to Palo Alto, and turned to full-time investing, reportedly turning about $1 million into roughly $15 billion over 24 years [Binance Square](https://www.binance.com/en/square/post/26131627562946). His most famous investment was NetEase in 2002 — a ~$2 million position at under $1 per share that returned roughly 100x [Sina Tech](https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-11-14/doc-infxkhuv1570059.shtml). He is also a major philanthropist whose H&H Evergreen Foundation held over $4 billion in assets, and he mentored Pinduoduo founder Colin Huang, whom he brought to the 2006 Buffett lunch [投中网](https://www.chinaventure.com.cn/news/80-20251117-388888.html). Since 2010 he has posted publicly on Xueqiu under the handle 大道无形我有型 ("The Great Way is Formless, but I Have Form"), with over a million followers [Xueqiu profile](https://xueqiu.com/n/%E5%A4%A7%E9%81%93%E6%97%A0%E5%BD%A2%E6%88%91%E6%9C%89%E5%9E%8B).

### Warren Buffett

Buffett, born in 1930 in Omaha, studied under Benjamin Graham at Columbia and ran the Buffett Partnership from 1957 to 1968, compounding at 31.6% annually versus 9.1% for the Dow [Learning from Buffett's letters](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/decoding-warren-buffetts-annual-letters-key-takeaways-for-investors). Under Munger's influence he shifted from buying cheap, mediocre companies to buying wonderful companies at fair prices — a transition crystallized by the purchase of See's Candies in 1972 and Coca-Cola in 1988 [The Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2024/08/warren-buffett-why-i-transitioned-from-cigar-buts-to-quality-companies). Berkshire Hathaway has compounded at over 20% annually since 1965, roughly double the S&P 500 [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/academy/trading/warren-buffett-investment-strategy-rules-fortune). He stepped down as Berkshire's CEO at the end of 2025, with Greg Abel succeeding him [Business Insider](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/warren-buffetts-company-took-kraft-181728165.html).

### Charlie Munger

Munger (1924–2023), a lawyer turned investor, ran Wheeler, Munger & Company from 1962 to 1975, compounding at 19.8% annually versus 5.0% for the Dow [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Munger). He joined Berkshire as vice chairman in 1978 and served as chairman of the Daily Journal Corporation from 1977 to 2022, where his concentrated portfolio of bank stocks and BYD produced extraordinary returns [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/charlie-munger.asp). His speeches, compiled in *Poor Charlie's Almanack*, articulate a multidisciplinary "latticework of mental models" approach to investing and life [Graham Mann](https://grahammann.net/book-notes/poor-charlies-almanack-charles-munger). He died in November 2023 with an estimated net worth of $2.6 billion, the vast majority in Berkshire Hathaway stock [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Munger).

## 2. Core Investment Principles and Value-Investing Approach

### Duan Yongping: "Buying a stock is buying the company"

Duan's entire philosophy rests on one sentence: **"买股票就是买公司"** — buying a stock is buying the company — which he learned from Buffett and Buffett learned from Graham. He extends it: "买公司就是买其未来现金流折现" ("Buying a company is buying the discounted present value of its future cash flows") [Sina Finance 2025 dialogue](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml). He has said that if even 1% of people truly understood this phrase it would be remarkable; executing it is harder still [文学城](https://blog.wenxuecity.com/myblog/82458/202506/25380.html).

His corollary formulations include:

- **"投资就是价值投资"** — Investment IS value investing. "If you're not investing in value, what are you investing in?" [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml)
- **"投机像狩猎，投资像种田"** — speculation is zero-sum hunting; investment is steady, non-zero-sum farming [上海证券报](https://paper.cnstock.com/html/2025-09/15/content_2121369.htm)
- **"慢即是快"** (slow is fast) — setting return targets leads to foolish actions; process correctness matters [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml)
- **The "punch card" discipline** — Duan follows Graham/Buffett's 20-hole punch-card approach and says he has used fewer than 10 of his 20 holes in a lifetime; his major investments number fewer than ten: NetEase, Yahoo (for Alibaba access), Apple, Berkshire Hathaway, Moutai, Tencent, and Pinduoduo [投资界](https://news.pedaily.cn/202511/557256.shtml)

Duan describes himself as a "满仓主义者" (full-position investor): "Holding cash is a very uncomfortable thing" [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml). He holds essentially three core stocks — Apple, Tencent, and Moutai: "我就三只股票：苹果、腾讯、茅台" [投资界](https://news.pedaily.cn/202511/557256.shtml). He advises ordinary investors to simply buy the S&P 500 index rather than trade, warning that ~80% of retail investors lose money in both bull and bear markets [Sina Tech](https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-11-14/doc-infxkhuv1570059.shtml).

### Warren Buffett: From cigar butts to wonderful companies

Buffett's philosophy evolved through two distinct phases. In his early career, following Graham, he practiced **"cigar butt" investing** — buying beaten-down companies below liquidation value for "one last puff." His 1989 shareholder letter defined it: "A cigar butt found on the street that has only one puff left in it may not offer much of a smoke, but the 'bargain purchase' will make that puff all profit" [The Investor's Podcast](https://www.theinvestorspodcast.com/articles/cigar-butt-investing).

Charlie Munger changed this. Buffett wrote: "Charlie shoved me in the direction of not just buying bargains, as Ben Graham had taught me... It took a powerful force to move me on from Graham's limiting views. It was the power of Charlie's mind" [Investment Masters Class](http://mastersinvest.com/newblog/2016/6/9/evolution-of-a-value-manager). The result was Buffett's most famous formulation: **"It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price"** [TQI Capital](https://valueb9b.substack.com/p/buffetts-biggest-mistake-from-cheap).

Buffett's foundational tenets include: "Rule No. 1: Never lose money. Rule No. 2: Never forget Rule No. 1"; "Our favourite holding period is forever"; "Never invest in a business you cannot understand"; and "The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient" [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/academy/trading/warren-buffett-investment-strategy-rules-fortune). He defines intrinsic value as "the present value of all cash that can be extracted from a business during its remaining economic life" and insists on being "fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful" [Picture Perfect Portfolios](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/how-warren-buffett-uses-intrinsic-value-to-buy-stocks).

### Charlie Munger: Latticework, inversion, and "sit on your ass investing"

Munger's philosophy is built on **multidisciplinary thinking** — a "latticework of mental models" drawn from psychology, mathematics, biology, engineering, and economics, with 80–90 models carrying "about 90% of the freight" toward wisdom [Farnam Street, 1994 USC speech](https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom). His signature device is **inversion** — solving problems backward: "All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there" [ModelThinkers](https://modelthinkers.com/mental-model/inversion). "It is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have gotten by trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent" [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom).

His investing doctrine is memorably simple: **"The big money is not in the buying or the selling, but in the waiting"** — what he called "sit on your ass investing": find a few great companies and hold them for decades [Picture Perfect Portfolios](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/the-art-of-sit-on-your-ass-investing-lessons-charlie-munger). He argued that "all intelligent investing is value investing — acquiring more than you are paying for" [YAPSS](https://www.yapss.com/blogs/collection-charlie-munger/the-real-money-is-in-great-companies-charlie-mungers-investment-philosophy-final-interview-with-cnbc-2023-c-c-m-327). In his final CNBC interview (2023), he explained that Graham's approach of buying cheap businesses worked for 40–50 years but became crowded; the real money was in great companies that "carried you up and up and up and up and up" [YAPSS](https://www.yapss.com/blogs/collection-charlie-munger/the-real-money-is-in-great-companies-charlie-mungers-investment-philosophy-final-interview-with-cnbc-2023-c-c-m-327).

Munger rejected the efficient-market view and favored a pari-mutuel racetrack metaphor for markets — inefficient enough for disciplined bettors to win by betting "very seldom" on mispriced opportunities [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom).

## 3. Business Quality, Competitive Advantage (Moats), and Management

### Duan Yongping: Business model as the first filter

Duan's single most important evolution came from the 2006 Buffett lunch. He asked Buffett what he valued most in investing; Buffett answered **"business model"** — "if the business model is wrong, I don't look further. He has a filter." Duan says it took him about ten years to truly internalize this [ScienceNet](https://news.sciencenet.cn/htmlnews/2025/1/537045.shtm). His system became a two-filter approach: business model + corporate culture [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml).

- **Moat definition**: A good business model has a wide moat — strong profits and net cash flow that competitors find extremely difficult to take away "even over long periods" [投中网](https://www.chinaventure.com.cn/news/80-20251117-388888.html). He warns that "technical advantages rarely form moats — the culture creating the technology does" [上海证券报](https://paper.cnstock.com/html/2025-09/15/content_2121369.htm).
- **Differentiation**: "The greater the degree of product homogenization, the higher the likelihood of tough business." He rejects price wars as "nuclear weapons" and quips that "cost performance is an excuse for insufficient performance" [Sina Tech](https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-11-14/doc-infxkhuv1570059.shtml). "Brand is a concentration of differentiation" [文学城](https://blog.wenxuecity.com/myblog/82458/202506/25380.html).
- **Management can't save a company**: "管理救不了一家公司" — citing Nokia, which refused Android and chased market share over user experience [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml). He called his 2008 purchase of GE a mistake by his current standards — a bad business model and culture — and sold when the company removed integrity from its homepage [Zhihu 100条思考](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/498129247).
- **Corporate culture is paramount**: He praises Apple for being "very user-oriented, not very business-oriented," citing Tim Cook's remark that making devices usable by blind people doesn't involve "the damned return on investment." Culture, he says, is a company's "north star" — a good culture ensures mistakes are recognized and corrected [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml).
- **Case examples**: He treats Moutai as a "long-term bond," distinguishing it from all other baijiu — "白酒分两种，茅台和其他" ("Baijiu comes in two types: Moutai and everything else") — and calls Tencent a "non-sale item" unlikely to be surpassed within ten years [Sina Tech](https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-11-14/doc-infxkhuv1570059.shtml). In July 2026 he began buying Pop Mart after reviewing its annual report, saying "there is a high probability that I will not sell it within 10 years" [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/business/china-business/article/3350454/chinas-warren-buffett-calls-pop-mart-long-term-buy-hints-labubu-maker-stake). His "clarity test": a good company can be described clearly in one sentence; if the business model is muddy, it's not an investment [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml).

### Warren Buffett: The moat as the primary criterion

Buffett calls the durability of a firm's competitive advantage — its **moat** — "the most important thing" in evaluating a business: "trying to find a business with a wide and long-lasting moat around it... protecting a terrific economic castle with an honest lord in charge of the castle" [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/05/07/warren-buffett-believes-this-is-the-most-important-thing-to-find-in-a-business.html). He identifies two types of advantage: being the low-cost producer (GEICO's direct-to-consumer model) and owning a powerful product franchise or brand people will pay up for (Coca-Cola, See's) [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/05/07/warren-buffett-believes-this-is-the-most-important-thing-to-find-in-a-business.html). "A truly great business must have an enduring moat that protects excellent returns on invested capital" [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/academy/trading/warren-buffett-investment-strategy-rules-fortune).

The canonical lesson is **See's Candies** (1972): acquired for $25 million against just $8 million of net tangible assets, it earned ~$4 million pre-tax on that base (~50% pre-tax return on tangible capital), raised prices 10% annually "and nobody cared," and has generated over $2 billion of cumulative cash flow with only $32 million of incremental reinvestment over 50 years [Quartr](https://quartr.com/insights/edge/tasting-quality-berkshires-defining-bet-on-sees-candies). Buffett wrote in the 2007 letter: "Without See's, there would have been no Coca-Cola" [25iq](https://25iq.com/2016/11/25/a-dozen-things-warren-buffett-and-charlie-munger-learned-from-sees-candies). The 1988 Coca-Cola purchase — over $1 billion at roughly 25x earnings — tested the principle that pricing power and global distribution justify premium prices for economic goodwill [Investment Masters Class](http://mastersinvest.com/newblog/2016/6/9/evolution-of-a-value-manager). Berkshire's 2016 entry into Apple came after Buffett reframed it not as a tech stock but as a consumer franchise with extraordinary loyalty and pricing power [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/academy/trading/warren-buffett-investment-strategy-rules-fortune).

On management, Buffett looks for **three attributes**: "Intelligence, energy, and integrity. And if they don't have the last one, don't even bother with the first two" [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/warren-buffett-the-three-things-i-look-for-in-a-person). He warns: "When a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for bad economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact" [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/academy/trading/warren-buffett-investment-strategy-rules-fortune). His Kraft Heinz experience — which he admitted was a case of overpaying, with Berkshire later writing down the stake by billions — demonstrates that even great investors err on management and price [Business Insider](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/warren-buffetts-company-took-kraft-181728165.html).

### Charlie Munger: Economic castles, incentives, and the four filters

Munger defined a moat as "an intrinsic characteristic that gives the business a durable competitive advantage," and made it the first of his four essential investment filters: (1) a business with a moat, (2) a business you can understand, (3) management with integrity and talent, and (4) a price with a margin of safety — "no matter how wonderful it is, it's not worth an infinite price" [25iq on Moats](https://25iq.com/2012/12/06/charlie-munger-on-moats-first-of-the-four-essential-filters). He distinguished durable moats (brand, network effects, cost advantages, regulation, patents, switching costs, culture) from fleeting ones, warning that Kodak and General Motors "wiped out their shareholders" [Investment Masters Class](http://mastersinvest.com/moats).

- **The See's lesson**: "We found out fairly quickly that we could raise the price every year 10% and nobody cared" — the discovery that pricing power is the ultimate test of a brand [SF Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/bayarea/article/charles-munger-sees-candy-berkshire-hathaway-18520703.php). Munger said the See's purchase enabled the Coca-Cola investment: "I don't think Warren would have made all the money that Berkshire made in Coca-Cola if he hadn't bought See's" [25iq](https://25iq.com/2016/11/25/a-dozen-things-warren-buffett-and-charlie-munger-learned-from-sees-candies).
- **Incentives as the root of management quality**: "Maybe the most important rule in management is 'Get the incentives right'"; "Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome" [Farnam Street, Psychology of Human Misjudgment](https://fs.blog/great-talks/psychology-human-misjudgment). He admired BYD founder Wang Chuanfu for rewarding executives with his own stock — a practice Munger copied at Daily Journal [Kingswell](https://www.kingswell.io/p/charlie-munger-q-and-a-2023-daily).
- **High-quality businesses make easy decisions**: "The difference between a good business and a bad business is that good businesses throw up one easy decision after another" [25iq](https://25iq.com/quotations/charlie-munger). His deepest conviction: "We've really made the money out of high quality businesses... The big money's been made in the high quality businesses" [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/charlie-munger-how-not-invest).

## 4. Valuation, Margin of Safety, and When to Buy/Sell

### Duan Yongping: Margin of safety is understanding, not cheapness

Duan's most distinctive reformulation of value investing doctrine concerns the margin of safety. He writes: **"老巴的安全边际不是指这个，安全边际指的是你对公司有多懂... 不是说有多便宜，便宜的东西可以更便宜的"** — "Buffett's safety margin doesn't mean cheapness; it refers to how well you understand the company. Cheap things can get cheaper." Understanding is "the greatest safety cushion" [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml).

- **Valuation is "kung fu"**, not formula: value is the discounted present value of future cash flows over the company's entire life, but P/E ratios are backward-looking history; ROE is a better screen but insufficient alone. "Time is the friend of excellent companies and the enemy of mediocre ones" [上海证券报](https://paper.cnstock.com/html/2025-09/15/content_2121369.htm).
- **Buy when no one cares**: "Buy where no one cares, sell where there's a crowd." His NetEase logic: the stock traded near $0.40–$0.80 while the company held over $2 of cash per share; after hiring lawyers to assess litigation risk, he concluded any risk was survivable. He bought ~$2 million worth in April 2002; the stock rose 20x in six months and he ultimately earned ~100x [Sina Tech](https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-11-14/doc-infxkhuv1570059.shtml). His regret, characteristically: "如果我看懂了，我应该把整个网易买下来" ("If I truly understood it, I should have bought all of NetEase") [Tina He Substack](https://fakepixels.substack.com/p/tao-and-the-art-of-investing-in-modern).
- **Selling discipline**: "Buy and hold" doesn't mean never selling — opportunity cost matters, and "if you can earn 10+ points per year, you may not need Apple." But he emphasizes finding errors and correcting them immediately: "买错股票要立即抛掉" (sell a wrongly bought stock immediately; don't wait for it to recover) [ScienceNet](https://news.sciencenet.cn/htmlnews/2025/1/537045.shtm). He also advises: "if you can't withstand a stock falling 50%, you shouldn't buy it" [投资界](https://news.pedaily.cn/202511/557256.shtml).
- **On price drops**: "If the fundamentals of a company are sound, a price drop is an opportunity" — he added to Moutai through a ~50% drawdown and bought Tencent repeatedly during 2022–2023 [Sina Tech](https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-11-14/doc-infxkhuv1570059.shtml). He also notes the market typically takes about three years to reflect a stock's true value [Futu](https://www.futunn.com/en/learn/detail-duan-yongping-dont-do-what-you-don-t-understand-when-investing-1259-2109010075).

### Warren Buffett: Owner earnings and "roughly right" valuation

Buffett's valuation framework is anchored by **owner earnings**, defined in the 1986 shareholder letter as reported earnings plus non-cash charges (depreciation, amortization) **minus** maintenance capital expenditures — the cash truly available to owners. He stressed that this figure "must be a guess," and that GAAP "cash flow" numbers that ignore maintenance capex are "absurd" [Wikipedia: Owner earnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owner_earnings). He never uses precise DCF outputs: "It's not cheap enough if you need a calculator to figure it out" — a formulation Duan explicitly endorses [Futu](https://www.futunn.com/en/learn/detail-duan-yongping-dont-do-what-you-don-t-understand-when-investing-1259-2109010075).

- **Margin of safety**: "We insist on a margin of safety in our purchase price," a precept learned from Graham that "will never be obsolete" [Mesirow](https://www.mesirow.com/wealth-insights-hub/timeless-lessons-from-warren-buffett).
- **When to buy**: "A climate of fear is your friend when investing; a euphoric world is your enemy" [The Financial Club](https://financial.scaclub.org/sites/financial.scaclub.org/files/documents/2025/Warren%20Buffett%20Philosophy%20for%20Investing.pdf). The 2008 Goldman Sachs deal — $5 billion of 10% preferred stock plus warrants, made during "an economic Pearl Harbor" — exemplifies buying quality at crisis prices; Berkshire ultimately earned roughly $3.1–3.7 billion on the package [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/our-firm/history/moments/2008-buffett-investment) [Quartz](https://qz.com/67052/heres-how-warren-buffett-made-3-1-billion-on-his-crisis-era-bet-on-goldman-sachs). He refused dot-com tech stocks in 1999 despite underperforming, because he could not model their cash flows [Picture Perfect Portfolios](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/how-warren-buffett-uses-intrinsic-value-to-buy-stocks).
- **When to sell**: True sells are rare. Berkshire has held Coca-Cola since 1988 and American Express since the 1960s [GOBankingRates](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/warren-buffett-invested-5b-goldman-195420885.html). He sold all airline stocks in 2020, judging the industry structurally unattractive [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/02/warren-buffett-says-he-is-disappointed-in-kraft-heinz-split.html). In 2026, with Berkshire holding record cash, he said: "I have no idea what the stock market's going to do... I do think I've got a reasonable idea of what a business is worth" [The Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2026/03/warren-buffett-on-markets-apple-and-why-hes-holding-cash).

### Charlie Munger: Great business at a fair price, and the opportunity cost lens

Munger's valuation principle is the inverse of Graham's: **"A great business at a fair price is superior to a fair business at a great price"** [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/12099614-a-great-business-at-a-fair-price-is-superior-to). His reasoning is mathematical: "If the business earns 6% on capital over 40 years and you hold the stock for 40 years, you're not going to make much different than a 6% return — even if you originally buy it at a huge discount" [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/charlie-munger-how-not-invest). Conversely, an 18% return on capital justifies an expensive-looking price [The Investor's Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vD2JIWO0osQ).

- **Margin of safety**: A Graham precept "that will never be obsolete" — borrowed from engineering; a survival buffer against error, with sizing flexibility (10–20% for stable businesses, 40–60% for volatile ones) [Mahavak latticework notes](https://mahavak.github.io/models). He also said: "If you can't stomach 50% declines in your investment, you will get the mediocre returns you deserve" [Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/education/charlie-munger-quotes-on-investing-and-life).
- **Opportunity cost as the true measure**: Munger's biggest regrets were errors of omission — seeing Google's value through GEICO's advertising data and failing to buy, which he called "many, many, many billions" of opportunity cost [Investment Masters Class](http://mastersinvest.com/newblog/2016/6/9/evolution-of-a-value-manager).
- **Holding forever, ignoring price**: "I'm no good at exits. I don't like even looking for exits. I'm looking for holds" [Investment Masters Class](http://mastersinvest.com/newblog/2023/12/13/charlie-munger-10-quotes). He held Costco at 40x earnings — "a perfect damn company... I'm never gonna sell a share" [Kingswell](https://www.kingswell.io/p/charlie-munger-q-and-a-2023-daily). Notably, he said he owns assets "I would never buy at their current prices but I am quite comfortable holding them" [Fundoo Professor](https://fundooprofessor.wordpress.com/2017/01/02/paradox).

## 5. Risk, Diversification, and Concentration

### Duan Yongping: Three golden "do nots" and extreme concentration

Duan's risk framework is a set of prohibitions he calls his golden rules:

1. **No short selling** — he lost ~$200 million shorting Baidu, calling it "a stupid act of losing composure" [上海证券报](https://paper.cnstock.com/html/2025-09/15/content_2121369.htm).
2. **No borrowing, no margin, no leverage** — "even Apple's stock has fallen 40%+ at least 10 times in 20 years; leverage risk can wipe out investors" [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml).
3. **Don't touch what you don't understand** (不懂不做) — understanding requires analyzing business models and corporate culture [Futu](https://www.futunn.com/en/learn/detail-duan-yongping-dont-do-what-you-don-t-understand-when-investing-1259-2109010075).

He defines risk as a function of understanding: "Value investing risk should be no greater than driving a car, while speculation risk is like gambling" [文学城](https://blog.wenxuecity.com/myblog/82458/202506/25380.html). "In investing, don't fear missing opportunities; the most important thing is not stepping on landmines" [ScienceNet](https://news.sciencenet.cn/htmlnews/2025/1/537045.shtm).

On diversification, Duan is dismissive — he calls it "wealth management, not true investing," and "撒胡椒面" (scattering pepper). He manages only two to three companies at a time, versus "Buffett and Munger's dozen" [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml). His 13F portfolio illustrates this: at Q2 2026, nearly 90% sat in his top five holdings (Apple 41.05%, Berkshire Hathaway 24.18%, Pinduoduo 9.99%, Tesla 7.44%, NVIDIA 6.58%) [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/duan-yongpings-stock-portfolio-the-billionaires-top-5-recent-buys). His options strategy is the notable nuance: he actively sells puts and covered calls on companies he deeply understands and intends to hold, likening it to "running an insurance company" or "running a casino" — while insisting he never buys options (that's gambling) and never uses margin [Zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com/en/article/1907809487298986356). In 2025 he sold large numbers of options during the tariff crisis, using several hundred million in margin, earning ~¥20 million in premiums [Zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com/en/article/1907809487298986356).

### Warren Buffett: Risk as permanent loss; diversification as protection against ignorance

Buffett defines risk not as volatility but as **the possibility of permanent loss of capital** — "Risk comes from not knowing what you're doing" [Picture Perfect Portfolios](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/how-warren-buffett-uses-intrinsic-value-to-buy-stocks). He famously said: "Diversification is protection against ignorance. It makes little sense if you know what you are doing" [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/031115/what-did-warren-buffett-mean-when-he-said-diversification-protection-against-ignorance-it-makes.asp). Berkshire's portfolio is nevertheless highly concentrated: as of Q2 2026, its top five holdings (Apple 22.0%, American Express 17.1%, Coca-Cola 10.9%, Alphabet 9.4%, Bank of America 9.2%) represented 68.7% of the $299.3 billion portfolio, with a concentration score of 88/100 [InvestorLens](https://www.investorlens.capital/investors/warren-buffett). "Over time, it takes just a few winners to work wonders" [WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/us/insights/blog/buffetts-2024-vision-what-investors-can-learn-from-six-decades-of-letters).

Buffett's approach to cash differs sharply from Duan's discomfort with it: Berkshire ended 2025 with record cash and Treasuries of roughly $348 billion, which Buffett frames as strategic waiting, not idleness — "We will always hold a boatload of cash and U.S. Treasury bills" [Mesirow](https://www.mesirow.com/wealth-insights-hub/timeless-lessons-from-warren-buffett). He wants Berkshire "financially impregnable and never dependent on the kindness of strangers" [WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/us/insights/blog/buffetts-2024-vision-what-investors-can-learn-from-six-decades-of-letters). He has also repeatedly noted that a modest correction does not necessarily create opportunity — "recent market moves have not created compelling value" [The Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2026/03/warren-buffett-on-markets-apple-and-why-hes-holding-cash).

### Charlie Munger: "Excessive diversification is madness"

Munger was the most unsparing critic of conventional diversification among the three: **"The idea of excessive diversification is madness... it's a confession that you don't really understand the businesses you own"** [LinkedIn/Hiten Sampat](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hitenps_riskmanagement-concentratedinvesting-behaviouralbias-activity-7320085008864485376-PUUP). His full view: "The whole secret of investment is to find places where it's safe and wise to non-diversify... Diversification is for the know-nothing investor; it's not for the professional" [Investment Masters Class](http://mastersinvest.com/diversificationquotes). He mocked modern portfolio theory and beta as "witchcraft" that "none of it makes any sense to me" [25iq](https://25iq.com/quotations/charlie-munger).

Munger practiced what he preached. His personal fortune sat almost entirely in three things: Berkshire Hathaway (~90% of net worth at the time of *Poor Charlie's Almanack*), Costco shares, and Daily Journal stock [The Investor's Podcast](https://www.theinvestorspodcast.com/charlie-munger). At the Daily Journal, he deployed $20.4 million of accumulated cash on March 9, 2009 — the exact day the S&P 500 bottomed at 666 — into Wells Fargo and Bank of America, a "once-in-forty-years opportunity" that grew to over $130 million within six years [Wealthy Retirement](https://wealthyretirement.com/retirement-planning/charlie-munger-one-best-investments-all-time). His BYD stake — a $3.3 million wager that returned 15-fold — was funded with what he called "a little leverage," something he generally warned against: "The three things that ruin people are ladies, liquor and leverage" [Kingswell](https://www.kingswell.io/p/charlie-munger-q-and-a-2023-daily). On volatility, he was blunt: "If you're going to be in this game for the long pull... you better be able to handle a 50% decline without fussing too much about it" [Novel Investor](https://novelinvestor.com/quote-author/charlie-munger).

## 6. Business, Investing, and Character/Values

### Duan Yongping: 本分 (benfen) and 平常心 (a calm mind)

Duan's life philosophy, which he applies equally to business and investing, is built on two Chinese concepts:

- **本分 (benfen)** — "doing what one is supposed to do and not doing what one is not supposed to do." His full definition: "Benfen means doing the right things and doing things right; a calm mind means being able, at any time — especially when tempted — to exclude all external distractions, return to the essence of things, discern right from wrong, and know what the right thing is" [vocus](https://vocus.cc/article/69172ba0fd89780001c63e69).
- **平常心 (pingchangxin)** — rationality; "returning to fundamentals"; dampening ego and panic. "Our secret is simply: benfen + a calm mind" [投中网](https://www.chinaventure.com.cn/news/80-20251117-388888.html).

His central formulation — **"做对的事情，把事情做对"** ("do the right things, do things right") — he credits to Peter Drucker and learned in his junior year at university. "In the process of doing the right things and doing things right, mistakes are acceptable. But the consequences of doing wrong things are inexcusable" [ScienceNet](https://news.sciencenet.cn/htmlnews/2025/1/537045.shtm). His Xueqiu profile adds: "We became who we are largely because of the things we didn't do" [Xueqiu](https://xueqiu.com/n/%E5%A4%A7%E9%81%93%E6%97%A0%E5%BD%A2%E6%88%91%E6%9C%89%E5%9E%8B).

The practical expression is his **"Stop Doing List" (不为清单)** — the "secret" to running a good company is not what you do but what you don't do: no OEM manufacturing (he famously rejected a million-unit VCD order from Walmart), no interest-bearing debt, no price haggling, no acquisitions, no blind expansion, no attacking competitors, no delayed supplier payments [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-10-20/doc-infunaua6001976.shtml). "Know where you'd die; don't go there" [文学城](https://blog.wenxuecity.com/myblog/82458/202506/25380.html). Related maxims include "敢为天下后" (dare to be behind the world — almost all successful companies started by imitation, citing Google, Apple, Microsoft) [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml) and "走捷径是最快的迷路方式" ("taking the shortcut is the fastest way to get lost") [ScienceNet](https://news.sciencenet.cn/htmlnews/2025/1/537045.shtm).

On character in investing, Duan says: **"投资实际上是在跟他做朋友"** — "investing is actually making friends with the person." This is why he respects Elon Musk's abilities but dislikes him, and why he regrets selling Tesla on character grounds despite calling it a financial mistake [Sina Tech](https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-11-14/doc-infxkhuv1570059.shtml). He calls investing "not a smartness contest but an honesty contest": "不自欺，就能长期" ("If you don't deceive yourself, you can last long") [投中网](https://www.chinaventure.com.cn/news/80-20251117-388888.html). "Truly great people rely not on talent but on doing their part"; "cleverness isn't valuable; clarity is" [投中网](https://www.chinaventure.com.cn/news/80-20251117-388888.html). He describes himself as "胸无大志" (without grand ambitions) and "just an ordinary person... I do what I should do" [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml).

### Warren Buffett: The inner scorecard and reputation

Buffett's most cited framework for character is the **inner scorecard vs. outer scorecard**, from Alice Schroeder's *The Snowball*: "The big question about how people behave is whether they've got an Inner Scorecard or an Outer Scorecard. It helps if you can be satisfied with an Inner Scorecard." His thought experiment: would you rather be the world's greatest lover but have everyone think you're the worst — or the worst but have everyone think you're the greatest? [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/the-inner-scorecard). This explains behaviors like refusing to move Berkshire's tax domicile to Bermuda despite saving tens of billions [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/the-inner-scorecard).

His most famous quote on reputation: **"It takes 20 years to build a reputation and five minutes to ruin it. If you think about that, you'll do things differently"** [BrainyQuote](https://www.brainyquote.com/quotes/warren_buffett_108887). The 2008 crisis demonstrated how reputation becomes an economic moat: CEOs called Buffett because he had credibility, not just cash — "If Buffett backed you, you were good" [William Stern video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GvCihrqjYXE). And on life's ultimate measure: "If you get to my age in life and nobody thinks well of you, I don't care how big your bank account is, your life is a disaster" [Thrive Street](https://www.thrivestreetadvisors.com/blog/warren-buffetts-scorecards).

His management ethics — the "three attributes" of integrity, intelligence, and energy — apply to himself as much as to others. Buffett's own letters are notable for candidly admitting mistakes: Berkshire Hathaway itself as a "cigar butt" mistake, overpaying for Kraft, missing Google and Amazon [TQI Capital](https://valueb9b.substack.com/p/buffetts-biggest-mistake-from-cheap) [Business Insider](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/warren-buffetts-company-took-kraft-181728165.html).

### Charlie Munger: Deserved trust and the psychology of misjudgment

Munger's ethical system centers on worthiness and rationality: **"The safest way to try to get what you want is to try to deserve what you want"** [TIME](https://time.com/6340573/charlie-munger-how-to-lead-a-successful-life). He described his approach to business as "extended Confucianism" — treating shareholders, suppliers, and customers like extended family to create "a seamless web of deserved trust" [HBO Kunhardt interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6s2HAzMeL0). "Remember that reputation and integrity are your most valuable assets and can be lost in a heartbeat" [Duncan Williams](https://www.dwassetmgmt.com/blog/remember-that-reputation-and-integrity-are-your-most-valuable-assets-and-can-be-lost-in-a-heartbeat-charlie-munger). In one famous incident, he paid $230,000 for something offered at $130,000 because he considered the bargain unethical in a partnership context [Investment Masters Class](http://mastersinvest.com/newblog/2016/6/9/evolution-of-a-value-manager).

His **"iron prescription"** guards against ideological self-deception: "I'm not entitled to have an opinion unless I can state the arguments against my position better than the people who are in opposition" [TIME](https://time.com/6340573/charlie-munger-how-to-lead-a-successful-life). And his core epistemology: "Above all, never fool yourself, and remember that you are the easiest person to fool" [Graham Mann](https://grahammann.net/book-notes/poor-charlies-almanack-charles-munger). His 1995 speech on the **Psychology of Human Misjudgment** — 25 cognitive tendencies from incentive-caused bias and social proof to envy and denial — is his magnum opus on why character and self-awareness matter more than IQ in investing [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/great-talks/psychology-human-misjudgment). "A lot of people with high IQs are terrible investors because they've got terrible temperaments. And that is why we say that having a certain kind of temperament is more important than brains" [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/author/quotes/236437.Charles_T_Munger). He warned against envy, resentment, and especially self-pity — "Self-pity is always counterproductive... you get a great advantage over everybody else" when you avoid it [TIME](https://time.com/6340573/charlie-munger-how-to-lead-a-successful-life).

## 7. Similarities and Differences Across the Three Investors

### 7.1 The shared foundation

All three investors share a remarkably consistent core worldview:

- **Buying stocks is buying businesses.** Duan's "买股票就是买公司" is literally Buffett's maxim, which Buffett credited to Graham; Munger called this "the most useful idea that Ben Graham ever had — have the mindset of somebody buying into a business planning to hold for the long pull" [HBO Kunhardt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6s2HAzMeL0).
- **Circle of competence.** All three insist on investing only in what you understand. Duan: "不懂不做" — Buffett: "Never invest in a business you cannot understand" — Munger: "Knowing where you're competent and where you aren't... is the single most important thing" [Futu](https://www.futunn.com/en/learn/detail-duan-yongping-dont-do-what-you-don-t-understand-when-investing-1259-2109010075) [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom).
- **No leverage, no shorting.** Buffett's "Rule 1: don't lose money" is echoed in Duan's three golden rules and Munger's "Three L's" warning. Duan explicitly lists the pillars he took from the Buffett lunch: never invest in what you don't understand, never borrow to invest, and never short stocks [Pandaily](https://pro.pandaily.com/p/who-is-chinas-warren-buffett).
- **The punch card.** Duan follows the Graham/Buffett 20-hole punch-card discipline and has used fewer than ten holes [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/05/28/billionaire-warren-buffett-teaches-this-20-slot-rule-to-getting-rich-at-business-schools.html).
- **The fat pitch / patience.** Buffett's Ted Williams metaphor, Munger's "sit on your ass investing," and Duan's "慢即是快" all express the same idea: extreme patience combined with extreme decisiveness [Wide Moat Research](https://www.widemoatresearch.com/p/wait-for-your-pitch).
- **Temperament over IQ.** Buffett: "The most important quality for an investor is temperament, not intellect." Munger: "rationality" is the one-word secret. Duan: "The most important thing in investment is not IQ but emotional control" [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml).

### 7.2 Duan Yongping vs. Buffett-Munger: Where he aligns

**Duan is a direct intellectual descendant of Buffett and Munger.** He watched Buffett's 1998 University of Florida speech at least ten times [Bilibili collection](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV11Y4y117DS), paid $620,100 for the 2006 lunch, and brought Colin Huang — who later credited Duan with his core business philosophy [ScienceNet](https://news.sciencenet.cn/htmlnews/2025/1/537045.shtm). Buffett, in turn, credited Duan: at the 2022 Berkshire meeting he introduced Duan to Munger and Bill Gates, saying, "This is Ping, he told me Apple's business model is better than Coca-Cola's, so I bought a lot of Apple!" [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/money/fund/etf/2025-01-05/doc-inecwxky9645808.shtml). Duan's argument had been that while Buffett drank Pepsi when it cost half as much as Coke, real Apple users don't care about Android's price difference — and Buffett replied, "I got it!" [ScienceNet](https://news.sciencenet.cn/htmlnews/2025/1/537045.shtm).

He also follows Buffett-Munger doctrine on moats, corporate culture, and long-termism with unusual rigor. His statement that "he manages only 2–3 companies at a time versus Buffett and Munger's dozen" indicates his self-conscious positioning within the tradition [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml). His Berkshire holdings (24.18% of his 13F portfolio at Q2 2026) are a direct expression of admiration for Buffett [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/duan-yongpings-stock-portfolio-the-billionaires-top-5-recent-buys).

### 7.3 Duan Yongping vs. Buffett-Munger: Key differences

Despite the alignment, Duan diverges in several meaningful ways:

1. **Margin of safety as understanding, not price.** Duan's reformulation — "safety margin refers to how well you understand the company... cheap things can get cheaper" — is more radical than Buffett's and Munger's Grahamian framing of buying below intrinsic value [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml). It collapses valuation into epistemology.
2. **Full-position posture vs. cash piles.** Duan is "uncomfortable holding cash" and effectively always fully invested, while Buffett famously sits on a record ~$348 billion and Munger praised "the courage to sit there with all that cash and do nothing" [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/stockzmt/2025-01-06/doc-inecyfep3065663.shtml) [The Financial Club](https://financial.scaclub.org/sites/financial.scaclub.org/files/documents/2025/Warren%20Buffett%20Philosophy%20for%20Investing.pdf).
3. **Options selling.** Duan actively sells puts and covered calls — "running an insurance company" — a practice Buffett generally avoids entirely. Analysts note the strategic ambiguity and control costs of options are likely why Buffett abstains; Duan's approach also reflects his smaller capital base relative to Berkshire [Zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com/en/article/1907809487298986356).
4. **Entrepreneur's lens.** Duan built BBK and incubated OPPO/vivo, so he reads businesses through an operator's eyes — "I come from the gaming industry, I know games well" — giving him an operating-experience advantage that pure investors lack [投中网](https://www.chinaventure.com.cn/news/80-20251117-388888.html). His "Stop Doing List" is a management doctrine applied to investing.
5. **Public engagement.** While Buffett and Munger communicated through annual letters and meetings, Duan engages in real-time public Q&A on Xueqiu with over a million followers, including famously blunt exchanges with critics [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2025-03-04/doc-inenncvp9973263.shtml).
6. **Market context.** Duan invests across US, Chinese (A-share, H-share, ADR) markets, holding Moutai, Tencent, and Pinduoduo — and his framework explicitly accounts for Chinese corporate governance and SOE dynamics (e.g., Moutai's state ownership preserving quality standards) [Sina Tech](https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-11-14/doc-infxkhuv1570059.shtml).
7. **Philosophical language.** Duan's 本分/平常心 vocabulary — "do the right things, do things right" — is a distinctive synthesis of Confucian self-cultivation with Graham-Buffett investing that neither Buffett nor Munger expressed [vocus](https://vocus.cc/article/69172ba0fd89780001c63e69).

### 7.4 Buffett and Munger: A partnership with distinct voices

The differences between Buffett and Munger are subtler but real:

- **Munger moved Buffett from Graham to "wonderful companies."** Buffett was explicit: "Charlie understood this early; I was a slow learner" [The Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2024/08/warren-buffett-why-i-transitioned-from-cigar-buts-to-quality-companies). Munger's 1965 instruction was to "add to it wonderful businesses purchased at fair prices and give up buying fair businesses at wonderful prices" [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/charlie-munger-how-not-invest).
- **Munger's disciplinary range is broader.** His latticework spans psychology, mathematics, biology, and history; Buffett's reading is deeper but narrower, focused on businesses and markets [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom). Munger's psychology of misjudgment is his unique contribution — Buffett called every interaction with Munger a learning experience [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/06/29/heres-how-buffett-partner-charlie-munger-applies-psychology-to-economics.html).
- **Sharper public positions.** Munger was famously anti-crypto ("rat poison"), anti-EBITDA ("substitute the word 'bullshit' earnings"), and willing to invest in China (BYD, Alibaba) in ways that stretched Berkshire's traditional circle [Kingswell](https://www.kingswell.io/p/charlie-munger-q-and-a-2023-daily). He also used a little leverage at Daily Journal — something Buffett never does.
- **Temperament and candor.** Munger's bluntness ("I'm rational" as his life's secret) contrasts with Buffett's folksy aphorisms, but the two never had a serious argument: "We never had a fight" [HBO Kunhardt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F6s2HAzMeL0).

---

## 8. Conclusion

Duan Yongping, Warren Buffett, and Charlie Munger form a coherent intellectual lineage: Graham's foundation, Munger's quality-first evolution, Buffett's institutional embodiment, and Duan's Chinese adaptation. All three agree that investing is buying businesses at a discount to their lifetime cash-generating ability; that understanding is the ultimate risk control; that concentration, patience, and temperament beat IQ and activity; and that character — integrity, rationality, and knowing what not to do — is inseparable from investment success.

The most instructive differences lie in Duan's innovations: his definition of margin of safety as depth of understanding rather than cheapness, his full-position comfort with only two to three companies, his operator's eye for business models and corporate culture, and his translation of Buffett-Munger doctrine into the Chinese philosophical vocabulary of 本分 and 平常心. Meanwhile, the Buffett-Munger partnership itself shows how a shared philosophy can accommodate distinct temperaments — Munger's multidisciplinary inversion and Buffett's institutional patience — and still compound for six decades.

For investors, the three profiles together offer a complete syllabus: Buffett for the discipline of valuation and moats, Munger for the psychology and mental models, and Duan for the translation of these principles into concentrated, understanding-driven practice in both US and Chinese markets.
