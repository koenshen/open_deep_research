# Comprehensive Research Report: Manufacturing Technology Options for Hollow Motor Shafts in New Energy Vehicle Electric Drive Units

**Date: August 24, 2026**

## 1. Executive Summary

The global tempered steel hollow shaft market was valued at **$8.9 billion in 2025** and is projected to reach **$14.2 billion by 2034**, growing at a **CAGR of 5.3%** [15]. With 14.1 million EVs sold in 2025 and projections of 28.4 million units by 2034, demand for optimized driveline components is accelerating rapidly [15].

Hollow motor shafts offer fundamental advantages over solid shafts: when the ratio of inner to outer diameter is 0.5, the outer diameter of a hollow shaft needs to be only about 2% larger than that of a solid shaft to achieve the same load-bearing performance, while reducing weight by more than 20% [23]. This weight reduction translates directly to improved energy efficiency, extended driving range, and enhanced vehicle dynamics. However, the hollow shaft design demands specialized manufacturing processes that can increase production costs by 15-25% compared to conventional motor designs [17].

This report provides a comprehensive analysis of all current manufacturing techniques for hollow motor shafts, compares them across critical criteria, and identifies the most suitable manufacturing routes for NEV electric drive unit applications.

## 2. Background: Why Hollow Motor Shafts for NEVs

When a shaft transmits torque, **maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface and minimum stress at the center**. The material at the center of a solid shaft contributes very little to torque resistance. Thus, for the same weight, a hollow shaft is stronger; for the same strength, a hollow shaft is lighter [16]. Strength depends on how material is distributed, not merely how much material is used [16].

Hollow shaft motors offer several key advantages for NEV applications:

- **Direct drivetrain integration** with reduced component count
- **Weight savings** of 20-50% compared to solid shafts
- **Enablement of coolant oil flow** through the shaft cavity, increasing heat transfer and enabling higher power density [1][8][17]
- **Reduced rotational inertia**, enabling faster acceleration and deceleration
- **Improved NVH characteristics** when properly designed and balanced

Current development goals for lightweight hollow shaft motors focus on achieving **power-to-weight ratios exceeding 5 kW/kg** while maintaining efficiency levels above 95% across the operational range [1].

## 3. All Current Forming and Manufacturing Techniques for Hollow Motor Shafts

### 3.1 Rotary Swaging / Radial Forging

**Process Description:** Rotary swaging is a high-precision cold-forming process in which several die segments oscillate radially and simultaneously strike the workpiece in rapid succession at frequencies exceeding 1,000 strokes per minute, with each stroke producing an incremental forming step of 0.25 to 1.5 mm [7]. This cold-forming technique reshapes metal tubes, rods, or wires without removing material, making it efficient for applications where precision, material integrity, and cost are important [9].

**Research Evidence:** A 2025 study published in the *Journal of Cleaner Production* (Volume 495) investigated rotary swaging technology for manufacturing lightweight hollow motor shafts for railway electric motors [6]. The study combined FEM simulations and experimental investigations on hot rotary swaging of hollow motor shafts. A detailed four-pass rotary swaging process analysis examined stress, strain, forming force, and damage. The maximum damage was 0.8, confirming process feasibility. Validation experiments showed stable outer diameter consistency, while the inner diameter increased toward the end face along the axial direction. Microstructural observations revealed that the recrystallization fraction in the rotary-swaged region reached 72.29%. Quenching and tempering treatment increased the yield strength in the rotary-swaged region by 27.5% compared to the undeformed region, also improving plasticity. A successful trial production fabricated a hollow motor shaft with a length of 635 mm and a maximum outer diameter of Φ105mm, achieving a weight reduction of over 30%. The billet material was forged 42CrMo steel rod (Φ106mm outer diameter), processed through deep hole drilling, multiple RS passes, and final precision machining [6].

**Key Advantages:** Cold-forming via rotary swaging can increase fatigue strength by up to 30%, enabling significant downsizing of cross-sections [7]. Hollow shafts and lightweight structures can be produced with weight reductions of up to 50% compared to conventional machining [7]. The process accommodates a wide range of external and internal geometries and offers considerable design freedom for both visible and structural parts [7]. Programmable kinematics, high geometric flexibility (stepped, tapered, polygonal, spline-like geometries), short cycle times, low tooling effort, and excellent repeatability are key benefits [7].

**Suppliers:** Felss Group (Generation e4.0 energy-saving rotary swaging machines, HA series, FR series) [7].

**Suitable Materials:** Forged 42CrMo steel rod [6]; micro-alloyed steel tubes [10].

**Subsequent Processing:** Deep hole drilling (pre-forming), multiple RS passes, heat treatment (quenching and tempering), final precision machining [6].

**Hyundai Patent Application:** A patent (US20160305469A1) by Hyundai Motor Co describes a hollow drive shaft manufacturing method involving: (1) swaging one of two hollow tubes of the same diameter to reduce its outer diameter; (2) assembling the swaged tube into the other tube to form a hollow shaft; and (3) joining first and second stems to both sides via friction welding. Torsion torque can be applied to the tubes in the same or different directions at various stages to enhance physical properties. Swaging increases physical properties like surface hardness and tensile strength [10].

### 3.2 Flow Forming / Shear Spinning

**Process Description:** Flow forming produces cylindrical workpieces from hollowed tubes or preforms using three or more rollers that move axially to stretch and thin the wall material, maintaining consistent material volume [14]. Flow forming is a chipless forming process that deforms metals through targeted pressure without cutting, eliminating material loss [13]. Both metal spinning and flow forming are considered cold forming processes. Cold working positively affects the workpiece's mechanical properties, producing improved grain structures and increased tensile strength [14].

**One-Piece Type Rotor Shafts (WF Maschinenbau):** WF Maschinenbau produces one-piece type rotor shafts and hollow shafts for electric motors, designed for demanding automotive applications up to **20,000 rpm**. The manufacturing process: the shaft is first flow-formed (with flow forming integration of internal cooling fins and/or forming of the external stop collar) achieving **radial run-out tolerances of less than 0.05 mm**, then the shaft end is drawn in (with integration of an internal cooling tube if required) [11]. Machine series used: VSTR (Vertical flow forming machines) produce lightweight elements with less material and at the same time higher strength; VUD (Vertical all-purpose machine) are all-in-one machines that combine almost all chipless forming processes [11].

**NETFORM Two-Piece Hollow Rotor Shaft:** NETFORM has created a patented two-piece hollow rotor shaft design for EV motors made of two flow-formed half shafts that are laser-welded together at the center. The flow forming process increases material strength by **2-3 times the base material** due to work hardening during the forming process itself, allowing for reduced wall thickness and overall weight reduction. For applications requiring higher stiffness, an optional center support component can be added, providing axial and radial support while still allowing coolant flow. Additionally, inserts can be added to the center support to control coolant flow and prevent pooling [8].

**LEIFELD FFC Series:** The LEIFELD FFC Series is a CNC-controlled vertical Flow Forming Center by Leifeld Metal Spinning, designed for chipless forming of hollow bodies used in drive components such as clutch housings, gearbox housings, disc carriers, and hollow shafts. Key benefits include up to **50% reduced setup time**, up to **100% longer tool life** via a driven tailstock, optimized three-sided accessibility, multi-process capability (flow forming, splitting, tooth profiling, hub preforming), and full automation through robotic integration. The system enables near-net-shape production of complex geometries within tight tolerances [12]. Leifeld Metal Spinning GmbH has over 130 years of experience, 147 patented developments, and operates a globally unique application center [13].

**Suitable Materials:** Aluminum, steels, copper, brass, Inconel, stainless steel, titanium, and more [14].

**Subsequent Processing:** Depending on the design, the flow-formed shaft may require heat treatment, laser welding (for two-piece designs), final machining, and balancing [8][11].

### 3.3 Deep Hole Drilling from Solid Bar Stock

**Process Description:** EV driving motors require lightweight shafts with excellent dynamic balance, leading to hollow shafts that need deep hole drilling. Shafts are becoming hollow to reduce their weight, and it is becoming necessary to handle deep hole drilling in recent shaft operations [3]. Gun drilling is a drilling method to achieve the highest straightness and is applied to meet the high dynamic balance requirement of the shaft [3].

**Tungaloy Tooling Solutions:** Tungaloy provides comprehensive solutions for deep hole drilling, chip control, and efficient use of CBN inserts. Key processes include: (1) External Turning using TungTurn-Jet holders with internal coolant for chip control and T9200 CVD grade inserts; (2) External Threading with high-precision ground TungThread inserts; (3) Combination Drilling with Chamfering (Pre hole for deep drilling) using DrillMeister heads; (4) Deep Drilling using DeepTri-Drill indexable insert gun drills with chip splitters for high productivity; (5) Slot Milling using TungMeister VEH heads with anti-vibration technology (variable helix, variable pitch); (6) Hard Turning using WavyJoint CBN inserts with large brazing area for heat dissipation combined with TungTurn-Jet holders [3].

DeepTri-Drill indexable insert gun drills provide "remarkably higher productivity than conventional brazed gun drills" [3].

**Suitable Materials:** Low carbon steel (which presents chip control problems); machining after hardening is also required [3].

**Subsequent Processing:** As the shaft is machined from solid bar, it requires turning, threading, slot milling, and potentially hard turning after heat treatment [3].

### 3.4 Seamless Tube Forming and Machining

Ultra-high-strength seamless tubes are relevant for automotive applications [19]. **Suitable Materials:** Seamless steel tubes (various grades), stainless steel tubes [19][20].

### 3.5 Friction Welding of Shaft Sections

**Process Description:** To reduce both shaft weight and manufacturing cost, a hollow shaft structure was developed that is assembled from a pipe and solid parts by ultraprecision friction welding [6][7][8][9]. The motor shaft has complicated figures at both shaft ends in order to connect to bearings or a drive shaft. Traditionally, these shafts with complicated structures have been made from single solid shafts by cutting using machine tools, making the shaft weight heavy and the cost high [6][7][8][9].

**Research Evidence (IEEE 2016):** A 2016 IEEE conference paper (ICRERA) by Tetsuya Akiyama, Takuro Honda, and Yoshitaka Nakanishi demonstrated that friction welding of lightweight motor shafts for EVs successfully produced hollow shafts that agreed with the quality required for EV motor shaft (up to 0.10 mm or less tolerance) by changing welding condition or work cramping method. Strict inspection on weld defects via microscopic observation in the weld zone confirmed that these hollow shafts without any weld defect were usable for practical applications. The weight reduction of the motor shaft is very effective in improving driving performance because it reduces inertia of rotor and enhances the control responsiveness of the induction motors [6][7][8][9].

**USUI Co., Ltd.:** USUI's hollow motor shaft combines hollow pipe material and forged material using friction welding, making it possible to support various sizes and reduce weight while maintaining the required strength of the shaft. USUI proposes the optimum motor shaft for electric vehicles that require weight reduction to increase efficiency [7].

**Hyundai Patent:** The Hyundai patent (US20160305469A1) also describes friction welding of first and second stems to both sides of the assembled hollow shaft [10].

**Suitable Materials:** Steel pipe and forged steel end sections [6][7][8][9][10][7].

### 3.6 Cross Wedge Rolling (CWR)

**Process Description:** Cross-wedge rolling (CWR) is an incremental forming method for producing rotationally symmetric components such as a basic shape of gear shaft or a preform before impression die forging [1]. CWR is a rotary forming process based on the principles of metal plastic deformation. Under continuous rolling, a single shift can produce thousands of parts, with efficiency **5-20 times higher** than traditional machining processes [4]. CWR applies radial compressive forces and axial tensile stresses to achieve plastic deformation, producing shafts with steps and tapers [4].

**Modified CWR for Hollow Shafts:** Conventional CWR cannot be used to produce hollow shapes. A modified CWR process was presented in *Procedia Manufacturing* (2018) by Dirk Landgrebe, Jürgen Steger, Uwe Böhmichen, and Markus Bergmann, which enables the manufacturing of hollow shafts. The method introduces two mandrels which act concurrently with the tooling wedges in an elaborate kinematic system. During processing, generated shear stresses induce fracturing located along the billet axis (based on the **Mannesmann effect**), and the actively driven mandrels use the fracture and penetrate axially into the workpiece [1]. LASCO Umformtechnik GmbH offers a Cross Wedge Roll machine (Type: QKW) designed for preforming of round material and ideally suited for the finish-forming of gear shafts, stepped shafts, as well as hollow shafts. The roller diameter ranges from 350 to 1,400 mm [2].

**CWR Process Advantages:** Material utilization reaches **80-90%**, representing an improvement of approximately 30-40% compared with machining processes (40-60%) [4]. Dimensional tolerance of **±0.1 mm**, surface roughness **Ra 1.6-3.2 μm**, die life of tens of thousands of parts [4]. Three process types exist: (1) Convex-surface synchronous roll (for Ø6-150 mm, L40-1200 mm shafts), (2) Fixed concave + rotating convex (for asymmetric/special shafts), (3) Opposing flat wedges with linear motion (for medium-small batch slender shafts) [4].

**Comparative Analysis (2026 Study):** A 2026 paper published in *Advances in Science and Technology Research Journal* (20(1), 423-435) compared three CWR methods: flat wedge tools, convex wedge tools (two rolls), and concave wedge tools. The study used numerical simulations in Simufact.Forming software on a 67.85 kg machine shaft (Ø125 mm max, 1146 mm long) made of C45 steel at 1,180°C billet temperature with a 28-second forming cycle. Key findings: All three methods produced correctly shaped products without defects. The lowest radial force was achieved with the two-roll method (2,091.4 kN), and the highest with concave tools (2,626.7 kN). The lowest tangential force was with the two-roll method (368.2 kN), and the highest with concave tools (453.2 kN). Flat wedge rolling had the lowest energy consumption (1,873.6 kJ, 100%), followed by two-roll (1,973.0 kJ, 105.3%), and concave tools (2,128.1 kJ, 113.6%). The two-roll method produces the lowest forces, and flat wedge rolling is most energy-efficient. The concave segmented tool method is not justified for standard applications but may be useful for special products like bimetallic or toothed parts [3].

**Microstructure Evolution (Aluminum Alloy):** A 2022 study in the *Journal of Central South University* (Volume 29, pages 807-820) by Yu et al. investigated how microstructure evolves during CWR of aluminum alloy hollow shafts without a mandrel using Deform-3D simulation. The average grain size of the rolled piece increases with the increase of the rolling temperature, decreases with the increase of the sectional shrinkage, and decreases first and then increases with the increase of the spreading angle, and changes little with the increase of the forming angle [5].

**Suitable Materials:** C45 steel [3], aluminum alloys [5], high-strength steels, titanium alloys [4].

**Subsequent Processing:** Heat treatment, finish machining, surface finishing [4].

### 3.7 Cold Extrusion / Forward/Backward Extrusion

**Process Description:** Cold extrusion is a forging operation which has the typical advantages of material savings, work-hardening (strengthening), and grain flow or directional strengthening [12]. The process involves forcing a billet of material through a die at room or slightly elevated temperatures, producing a continuous product with constant cross-section [13]. Types include: forward extrusion (metal flows in same direction as punch, used for bolts, screws, stepped shafts), backward extrusion (metal flows upward surrounding the descending punch, producing cup-shaped parts), and upsetting (metal gathers perpendicular to tooling motion, often combined with other methods) [13].

**Cold Extrusion Die Design for Hollow Shafts (2022 Study):** A research article published in *Materials Today: Proceedings* (2022) presented the design and finite element analysis of a cold extrusion die for forming hollow shafts. The study used medium carbon steel (JIS G3445) for the hollow shaft and D2 carbide steel for the die material, with analysis conducted using Abaqus simulation software. The methodology involved designing dies with multiple taper angles (n=1,2,3,4) and subdividing the extrusion process into three stages. Through 126 analyses, the optimal die configuration was identified: Die 1 with a 15° taper angle, Die 2 with a 20° taper angle, and Die 3 with a 15° taper angle, at a velocity of 1000 mm/s. This configuration successfully produced a hollow shaft with good formability, acceptable stress/strain distribution, and without fracture. Key conclusions: (a) Forming a shaft using a single extrusion die with multiple taper angles is challenging; (b) Stress-strain results from FEA support the use of subdivided cold extrusion dies; (c) The study successfully demonstrated the use of extrusion as a viable process for stepped shaft forming, which is a novel approach compared to traditional methods like rolling, forging, and cross-wedge forming [11].

**Process Limits:** Forward extrusion (open-die): 30-35% reduction in area; closed-die: up to 70-75%. Backward extrusion: 20-25% minimum to 70-75% maximum reduction; maximum hole depth = 3× hole diameter. Lateral extrusion: up to 60% reduction. Draw ironing: normally limited to 30% [12]. Maximum extrusion ratios: 40 for Al alloy 1100, 5 for AISI 1018 steel, 3.5 for type 305 stainless steel. Punch pressure ranges from 34.5 MPa (soft metals) to 3,100 MPa (alloy steel) [12].

**Equipment:** Mechanical presses (most common, higher production rates, used for large volumes); Hydraulic presses (longer work strokes, versatile, lower speeds, for smaller volumes). Horizontal mechanical presses: up to 1,000 tons, 150 parts/min. Vertical mechanical presses: 1,000-2,000 tons, 25 parts/min [12].

**Tooling Materials:** Punch materials: M2/M4 high-speed tool steel (62-66 HRC), tungsten carbide (up to 2,750 MPa). Die materials: D2, M2, M4 tool steel (58-64 HRC), tungsten carbide (up to 3,100 MPa). Shrink rings: H11/H13 die steel (46-48 HRC). A tool life of 100,000 pieces is likely to be considered above average for consumable tools [12].

**Steel Quality:** Steels up to 0.3% C are easily extruded; high-C steels up to 0.5% C can be extruded with limited ratios and spheroidize annealing. Killed steels (especially Al-killed) are preferred for homogeneous structure. Sulphur limited to 0.02% typically. For surface preparation of steel slugs, phosphate coating for cold extrusion is almost universal practice [12].

**Kaneta Kogyo (Japan):** Kaneta's hollow shafts are plastic processed in-house from coil material and bar material via cold forging and deep hole forging. Compared to cutting processing, it can reduce material costs by up to 15%. Processing record of one-shot hole diameter x 5 times, multi-stage 10D or more. This reduces welding of divided parts and realizes high rigidity and cost reduction [25].

### 3.8 Powder Metallurgy / Sinter Forging

**Process Description:** Powder metallurgy (PM) is a manufacturing process covering a wide range of ways in which materials or components are made from metal powders. PM processes can reduce or eliminate the need for subtractive processes in manufacturing, lowering material losses and reducing the cost of the final product [17]. The basic "press and sinter" process consists of three steps: powder blending (or pulverization), die compaction, and sintering [17].

**Forged Powder Metallurgy (GKN):** GKN Powder Metallurgy's Forged Powder Metallurgy (FPM) technology combines engineered metal powders with forging to produce high-performance components with superior durability, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness. The process involves four key stages: (1) Clean alloy selection and preform design using advanced simulations, (2) Powder compaction and sintering to create a high-integrity preform, (3) A single hot forging stroke that refines grain structure and eliminates porosity, and (4) Post-processing including heat treatment (up to Rockwell C 60), minimal machining, and quality inspection. Key benefits include up to **15-20% mass reduction**, **15-30% greater durability** than wrought steels, near-net shape precision eliminating machining waste, and design flexibility for complex geometries. Applications include automotive drivetrain components (differential gears, connecting rods, dog clutches), aerospace/industrial machinery, and specialized engineering. Sustainability benefits include up to **100% recyclability** of metal powders and reduced material waste and energy usage [16].

**Powder Metallurgy Market:** The global PM market was valued at $7.49 billion in 2020 and is projected to reach $12.63 billion by 2028, growing at a CAGR of 6.77% [18]. The typical US light-duty vehicle contains about 37 pounds of PM parts [18]. The automotive industry is the primary customer for PM parts [18].

**Key PM Processes:** Powder Forging (preform hot forged to full density for as-wrought properties), Hot Isostatic Pressing (HIP - powder filled into a mould, evacuated, sealed, and heated under high gas pressure ~100 MPa to full density), Metal Injection Moulding (MIM - fine powder mixed with binder, injection moulded, binder removed, then sintered with ~18% shrinkage to 95-99% density), Additive Manufacturing (using metal powders for laser sintering/melting) [17].

### 3.9 Additive Manufacturing Approaches

**Process Description:** Additive manufacturing (AM) constructs components layer by layer, offering design freedom for complex geometries, material efficiency (35-80% reduction in material usage compared to traditional methods), performance enhancements through multi-material construction, and improved sustainability [23]. AM enables the fabrication of complex geometries such as hollow windings, integrated cooling channels, and optimized magnetic flux paths [23].

**Metal AM for Electrical Machines (Review Article):** A comprehensive review published in *Energies* (2022) by Ahmed Selema, Mohamed N. Ibrahim, and Peter Sergeant from Ghent University examined metal AM for electrical machines. Four main metal AM techniques are discussed and compared: (1) Powder Bed Fusion (PBF) - most widespread, uses laser/electron beam to fuse metal powder; (2) Binder Jetting - deposits liquid binder on powder bed, then sintering; (3) Directed Energy Deposition (DED) - material fed into heat source, fastest for large parts; (4) Joule Printing - uses resistance heating, lowest energy consumption. In conventional metal manufacturing, the required energy to machine 1 kg of titanium exceeds 40 kWh, whereas AM needs only less than 1 kWh for the same weight [9].

**Applications in Electrical Machines:**
- **Rotors:** 6-slot rotor achieving 2.3T flux density using Fe-Co powder; skewed rotors with honeycomb structures reducing torque ripple by ~45%; interior magnet rotors with 53% weight reduction, 9% faster acceleration, and 90% cogging torque reduction; 3D-flux PM machines with 40% higher power density and 15% lower losses [9].
- **Windings:** Shaped profile coils reducing AC losses; hollow conductors integrated with cooling channels achieving 20 A/mm² current density; hairpin windings for e-traction motors delivering 30% more torque; multi-material coils with integrated heat exchangers [9].
- **Thermal Management:** 3D-printed ceramic heat exchangers reducing winding temperature rise by 44%, achieving 35.7 A/mm² continuous current density; cooling jackets with unique designs improving thermal resistance by 25% and reducing weight by up to 40% [9].

**ORNL Research on 3D Printing Rotors:** Oak Ridge National Laboratory's Manufacturing Demonstration Facility, led by researcher Amiee Jackson, researched 3D printing rotors for electric motors using laser powder bed fusion. Conventional rotors are made from iron-silicon alloy sheets that are laminated and wound. Increasing silicon content (up to 6%) improves motor performance but reduces ductility, making winding difficult. Additive manufacturing offers design freedom and eliminates the need for winding, potentially allowing use of higher-silicon alloys. Test results: The printed rotors were less efficient than conventional ones — requiring ~10x the current, had audible losses, and couldn't reach the same speed at the same voltage (likely due to eddy current losses which laminations normally reduce). However, the printed rotors did spin, did not overheat quickly, and showed promise for further development. Next steps include iterating on material formulations to improve efficiency and simulate lamination-like properties [6][7].

**3D Printed Tooling for EV Motor Machining:** Kennametal used additive manufacturing (laser powder bed fusion) to create a lightweight, large-diameter boring tool for EV motor housing machining. Two versions were developed: an all-steel 3D-printed version at 10.7 kg (23.5 lbs) — roughly 40-50% lighter than a conventional tool; and a hybrid version combining 3D-printed steel cutting sections with a carbon fiber composite central shaft at 9.5 kg (21 lbs). The tool incorporates internal coolant passages that act as a hydrostatic bearing, guiding the tool during precision cutting. The single tool machines three different features in one pass: the bearing surface for the rotor mount (~20 micron precision), the long central stator bore, and the recessed surface for the cover plate. This replaces at least three separate conventional tools. Kennametal is currently implementing an enhanced version of the steel/CFRP hybrid tool into serial production for an automotive OEM customer [22].

**Hollow 3D Printing Innovations:** Innovations in hollow 3D printing across FDM, SLA/DLP, and SLS processes show that hollow strategies routinely cut material usage by up to 50%, reduce print time by ~30%, and trim material spend by 20-40%. Aerospace parts can achieve up to 70% weight reduction. For metal-bound filaments, consistent wall thickness, 2-3 millimeter gas and binder escape holes, and smooth internal fillets reduce cracking and slumping while improving densification [8].

### 3.10 Other Emerging Techniques

**Warm Forming Processes (Cross Rolling and Spin Extrusion):** A research article published in the *Journal of Materials Processing Technology* (Volume 119, Issues 1-3, 2001) by R. Neugebauer, M. Kolbe, and R. Glass presents new warm forming processes for producing hollow shafts using incremental rolling techniques. Two methods are described: cross rolling and spin extrusion, which can be used individually or combined. Mass reduction in passenger cars can be achieved up to 1.7 kg; in transport cars up to 7 kg. The common advantages of these partially shaping bulk forming methods are lower forming force allowing for a reduction of machine sizes, considerable reduction or even omission of costly and environmentally unfriendly lubricants and pre and post work, and good forming conditions even for materials which are difficult to shape. In spin extrusion, the hollow shape is formed by the partial pressure of the pressure rollers which roll off along the outer casing of the unmachined part together with a forming spike acting simultaneously and synchronously in axial direction. Both cold and hot forming methods can be applied to spin extrusion [18][24].

**Rolling-Extrusion:** A theoretical analysis of the rolling-extrusion process for manufacturing hollow axi-symmetrical parts found that including a calibrated zone on the profiled rolls and optimizing the roll velocities and pusher displacement stabilized the process and reduced distortions in the formed part cross-sections. The proper combination of tool geometries and kinematic parameters is necessary to ensure a stable rolling-extrusion process for hollow parts [15].

**Near-Net Shape Forging (Bharat Forge Patent):** Patent US9446445B2 assigned to Bharat Forge Ltd describes a method for manufacturing near-net shaped hollow shafts, particularly for high-power gearboxes. The method uses a near-net shape forging process starting from a concast bloom (rectangular or round) or as-cast round ingot, with specific reduction ratios (>5 for concast blooms, >3 for round ingots). The process involves: heating, upsetting, and drawing operations using flat dies; booster heating steps; final upsetting and punching in hollow dies to form the near-net shape; heat treatment (normalizing, hardening, double tempering); proof machining and stress relieving. Key advantages: Input weight reduced by ~30%, machining time reduced by ~41%, improved grain flow, microstructure, and mechanical properties, higher and more consistent safety factors, enables production of hollow shafts with multiple-diameter bores and continuous grain flow lines [14][22].

**Hollow Forging (AHD Steel - Wind Turbine Application):** A 2025 case study comparing hollow forged Air Hardening Ductile (AHD) steel rotor shafts with cast iron for wind turbines found that the AHD steel hollow forged rotor shaft has a mass of 10.2 t, which is 37% lighter than the cast shaft (16.2 t). The hollow forged shaft (€90,000-120,000) is 50-70% more expensive than the cast shaft (€60,000-70,000), primarily due to high forging surcharges (160% of final shaft mass vs. 32% for casting). For cost parity, forging surcharges must be reduced to approximately 50%. Using green steel and omitting heat treatment (AHD steel air hardens), the manufacturing GWP of the hollow forged shaft (20.7 tCO₂eq) is comparable to casting (20.2 tCO₂eq) [21].

**Hybrid Shaft (CFRP + Steel):** A hybrid shaft approach combining carbon fiber-reinforced plastic (CFRP) with stainless steel, using dry filament winding and centrifugal casting, can achieve up to 50% weight reduction compared to traditional steel shafts, with a production capacity of 18,000 parts/year [2]. BYD has developed proprietary lightweight hollow shaft motor technology utilizing a carbon fiber reinforced polymer (CFRP) hollow shaft construction [1]. NIDEC Corp. uses aluminum alloy with integrated cooling channels, achieving 25% weight reduction [1].

**Load-Optimized Hollow Rotor Shaft (Patent):** A patent-pending design (DE102022118352A1) describes a load-optimized hollow rotor shaft with varying inner diameter [1]. Another patent (WO2022253383A1) describes a hollow shaft made of rotationally symmetrical components joined by high-temperature soldering [1].

**General Motor Shaft Materials:**

- **4140 (42CrMo4):** For deep hardenability and fatigue resistance. Yield strength 655-900 MPa Q&T, fatigue endurance limit ~480 MPa (60% higher than 1045) [1]. Used for high-power motors, quenched and tempered [3].
- **8620 (20NiCrMo2):** For carburizing gears and splines, case hardness 58-62 HRC, core 30-45 HRC [1].
- **38CrMoAlA:** Used for high-speed, high-precision motors [3].
- **45# Steel:** Most common for general motor shafts [3].
- **40Cr:** Alloy steel for high-power motors, quenched and tempered [3].

### 4.3 Required Subsequent Processing Steps

**Heat Treatment Types:**
- **Quenching and Tempering (through-hardening):** Increases strength, promotes desirable microstructure. For rotary swaged 42CrMo, Q&T increased yield strength by 27.5% [6].
- **Induction Hardening:** Selectively hardens specific areas like bearing journals. Lower distortion, localized size change. Surface hardness HRC 58-62 for 4140 steel, hardening depth 2-6mm [2]. For 1045 steel, induction-hardened to 50-55 HRC at journals [1].
- **Carburizing:** Creates hard surface layer with tough core via high-temperature carbon diffusion. Higher distortion risk, almost always requires post-heat-treatment grinding [12]. For gears/splines, case hardness 58-62 HRC, core 30-45 HRC [1].
- **Nitriding:** Low-temperature process introducing nitrogen into surface. No quenching required, minimal distortion, very small size change. Suitable for shafts with tight tolerance requirements. Performed at 500-600°C, case depth 0.2-0.7mm [2][12].
- **Stress Relief (Annealing):** Eliminates residual stresses [11].
- **Normalizing, Hardening, Double Tempering:** Used in Bharat Forge near-net forging process [14][22].

**Machining Operations:**
- Rough turning, semi-finishing, finish turning/grinding [14]
- Deep hole drilling (gun drilling) [3]
- External turning, external threading, slot milling [3]
- Hard turning (using CBN inserts) after heat treatment [3]
- CNC machining with precision tolerances of ±0.005 mm [14]

**Surface Finishing:**
- Surface finish Ra 0.4-0.8 µm for typical motor shafts [4]
- Surface finish Ra 1.6-3.2 μm for CWR [4]
- Grinding, polishing [2]

**Balancing:**
- Dynamic balancing is critical for high-speed shafts (up to 20,000+ RPM) [13]
- ISO 1940-1 establishes balance quality grades for rotating rigid rotors, with hollow shaft motors typically falling under quality grades G2.5 to G6.3 depending on application requirements [13]
- Automated balancing systems integrated with manufacturing processes can reduce per-unit balancing costs by 70-85% while maintaining consistent quality standards [13]
- Balancing equipment costs: traditional static balancing $15,000-$50,000, advanced dynamic balancing systems $100,000-$500,000, field balancing portable analyzers $25,000-$75,000, laser interferometry systems $200,000-$800,000 [13]

**Recommended Process Flow for High-Precision Shafts:** Rough machining → heat treatment → finish grinding or precision turning → final inspection [12].

## 5. Most Suitable Manufacturing Routes for NEV Hollow Motor Shafts

### 5.1 Recommended Primary Manufacturing Routes

#### Route A: Flow Forming (One-Piece Construction) — Recommended for High-Volume Production

**Process:** Flow form a single-piece shaft from a tubular preform, integrating internal cooling features and external stop collars [11].

**Justification:**
- WF Maschinenbau has demonstrated radial run-out tolerances of less than 0.05 mm, critical for high-speed operation up to 20,000 rpm [11]
- Achieves near-net shape with less material and higher strength [11]
- Flow forming increases material strength by 2-3 times base material, enabling thinner walls and weight reduction [8]
- Multi-process capability (flow forming, splitting, tooth profiling, hub preforming) in a single CNC machine with full robotic automation enables high throughput [12]
- Up to 50% reduced setup time and up to 100% longer tool life (LEIFELD FFC Series) [12]

**Suitable for:** High-volume production (thousands to millions of units/year).

**Materials:** Steels (42CrMo, 4140, 1045), depending on strength requirements.

**Subsequent processing:** Heat treatment (Q&T or induction hardening), finish machining, dynamic balancing (G2.5-G6.3 per ISO 1940-1) [13].

#### Route B: Rotary Swaging — Recommended for Medium-Volume Production with Complex Geometry

**Process:** Start with a deep hole drilled billet, perform multiple rotary swaging passes to reduce diameter and form steps [6].

**Justification:**
- A 2025 *Journal of Cleaner Production* study demonstrated successful production of a 635mm hollow motor shaft with over 30% weight reduction using 42CrMo steel [6]
- Rotary swaging increases fatigue strength by up to 30% [7]
- Accommodates a wide range of external and internal geometries (stepped, tapered, polygonal, spline-like) [7]
- Quenching and tempering increased yield strength by 27.5% in the swaged region [6]

**Suitable for:** Medium-volume production with complex stepped geometries.

**Materials:** 42CrMo, micro-alloyed steels.

**Subsequent processing:** Heat treatment (Q&T), final precision machining, balancing.

#### Route C: Friction Welding of Sections — Recommended for Large-Diameter or Multi-Material Shafts

**Process:** Friction weld forged solid end sections (with bearing seats, splines, etc.) to a central seamless tube [6][7][8][9][7].

**Justification:**
- IEEE research demonstrated successful production of EV motor shafts with quality up to 0.10 mm tolerance [6][7][8][9]
- USUI Co. has commercialized this approach for EV motor shafts [7]
- Enables different materials for different sections (e.g., forged high-strength steel ends with a lighter tube center)
- Hyundai's patent validates this approach with added torsion torque treatment for bidirectional durability [10]

**Suitable for:** Large-diameter shafts, shafts requiring different properties at ends vs. center, multi-material designs.

**Materials:** Forged steel ends + seamless steel tube.

**Subsequent processing:** Friction welding, heat treatment, finish machining, balancing, strict weld zone inspection.

#### Route D: Near-Net Shape Forging + Punching — Recommended for High-Volume with Stepped Internal Bores

**Process:** Forge from concast bloom or ingot with upsetting, drawing, and punching in hollow dies to achieve near-net shape with continuous grain flow [14][22].

**Justification:**
- Bharat Forge patent demonstrates 30% input weight reduction, 41% machining time reduction [14][22]
- Improved grain flow and mechanical properties [14][22]
- Enables multiple-diameter bores with continuous grain flow lines [14][22]

**Suitable for:** High-volume production where continuous grain flow along stepped bores is critical.

**Materials:** Carbon steels, alloy steels.

**Subsequent processing:** Heat treatment (normalizing, hardening, double tempering), proof machining, stress relieving.

### 5.2 Engineering Justification for NEV Applications

**High Torque Transmission Requirements:**
For NEV applications, the shaft must transmit high torque while being lightweight. Hollow shafts offer superior strength-to-weight ratios compared to solid shafts — for the same weight, a hollow shaft is stronger; for the same strength, a hollow shaft is lighter [16]. When the ratio of inner to outer diameter is 0.5, the outer diameter of a hollow shaft needs to be only about 2% larger than that of a solid shaft to achieve the same load-bearing performance, while reducing weight by more than 20% [23].

**High-Speed Rotation (up to 20,000+ RPM):**
WF Maschinenbau has demonstrated one-piece flow-formed rotor shafts capable of withstanding up to 20,000 rpm with radial run-out tolerances less than 0.05 mm [11]. The lower rotational inertia of hollow shafts enables faster acceleration and deceleration [16]. Dynamic balancing per ISO 1940-1 (grades G2.5 to G6.3) is essential [13]. Proper heat treatment (induction hardening or nitriding) provides better dimensional stability for high-speed precision [12].

**Weight Reduction Needs:**
Every kilogram saved in motor weight translates to improved energy efficiency and extended driving range [1]. The rotor shaft accounts for approximately 30% of total rotor weight (steel, ~7850 kg/m³) [2]. Rotary swaging achieves over 30% weight reduction [6]; flow forming enables 2-3x strength increase allowing thinner walls [8]; hybrid CFRP+steel shafts achieve up to 50% weight reduction [2].

**NVH (Noise/Vibration/Harshness) Requirements:**
The hollow shaft design creates unique thermal and vibration challenges [17]. Radial run-out tolerances of less than 0.05 mm (achievable via flow forming) are critical for NVH control [11]. Gun drilling achieves the highest straightness for deep hole drilling, which is necessary for meeting the high dynamic balance requirement of the shaft [3]. Dynamic balancing at operational speeds (up to 6,000 RPM demonstrated by FANUC) is required [13]. Automated balancing systems can reduce per-unit balancing costs by 70-85% while maintaining consistent quality standards [13].

**Thermal Management:**
Direct oil cooling through the hollow shaft cavity can reduce thermal resistance by up to 40% [1]. Flow forming enables integration of internal cooling fins during the forming process [11]. NETFORM's two-piece design allows for coolant flow through the center with optional center support components controlling coolant flow [8].

### 5.3 Recommended Manufacturing Route Combinations

For a typical NEV electric drive unit hollow motor shaft, the most suitable manufacturing route would be a **combination of flow forming and finish machining**:

1. **Primary Forming:** Flow forming from a tubular preform, integrating internal cooling features and external stop collar [11]. This provides the best combination of strength, precision, and production efficiency for high-volume NEV applications.

2. **Heat Treatment:** Induction hardening of bearing journals (for wear resistance) combined with Q&T of the shaft body (for strength and fatigue resistance). Induction hardening provides lower distortion than carburizing, while nitriding offers minimal distortion for tight tolerance requirements [12].

3. **Finish Machining:** CNC turning and grinding to achieve final tolerances, with gun drilling for cooling channels if required [3].

4. **Dynamic Balancing:** Per ISO 1940-1 at G2.5 quality grade, integrated into the production line with automated balancing systems [13].

For shafts requiring **maximum weight reduction**, the hybrid CFRP+steel approach (dry filament winding + centrifugal casting) offers up to 50% weight reduction but is limited to 18,000 parts/year production capacity [2].

For shafts requiring **stepped internal bores with continuous grain flow**, the near-net shape forging method (Bharat Forge patent) is recommended, offering 30% input weight reduction and 41% machining time reduction [14][22].

## 6. Conclusion

The manufacturing of hollow motor shafts for NEV electric drive units involves a diverse range of forming technologies, each with distinct advantages and limitations. The selection of the optimal manufacturing route depends on production volume, geometric complexity, material requirements, cost constraints, and specific performance criteria.

For high-volume production (the dominant scenario for NEV applications), **flow forming** emerges as the most suitable primary forming technique due to its combination of high precision (radial run-out < 0.05 mm), excellent mechanical properties (2-3x strength increase), production efficiency (fully automated, multi-process capability), and ability to integrate cooling features directly during forming. **Rotary swaging** offers a strong alternative for medium-volume production with complex geometries, while **friction welding** provides advantages for large-diameter or multi-material shaft designs. **Near-net shape forging** is optimal for shafts requiring stepped internal bores with continuous grain flow.

The trend toward higher power densities, faster rotational speeds, and more stringent weight reduction targets will continue to drive innovation in hollow shaft manufacturing. Emerging technologies such as additive manufacturing and hybrid CFRP+steel designs offer promising pathways for further weight reduction and performance optimization, though they currently face limitations in production throughput and cost for high-volume applications.
