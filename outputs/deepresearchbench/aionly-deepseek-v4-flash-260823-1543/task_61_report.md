# Chub Mackerel (*Scomber japonicus*) in Pacific Rim Markets: Price Dynamics, Biological Variability, and Oceanographic Drivers — An Integrated Analysis

## Executive Summary

Chub mackerel (*Scomber japonicus*) is one of the most economically consequential small pelagic species in the Pacific Rim, supporting major fisheries in Japan, South Korea, China, Russia, the United States, Mexico, Peru, and Chile, with global trade in mackerel products valued at well over US$13 billion annually ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/mackerel-market)). Over the past decade, Pacific Rim chub mackerel markets have experienced extraordinary price volatility: Tokyo wholesale prices rose from roughly 450 yen/kg in mid-2024 to a record 864 yen/kg in December 2025 and remained above 600 yen/kg through mid-2026; South Korean wholesale prices jumped 60% year-on-year in January 2026 and Korean mackerel exports surged 247% in the first half of 2026; and FAO GLOBEFISH recorded a 98% increase in the small-pelagics price index between January and September 2022 alone ([FAO GLOBEFISH](https://www.fao.org/in-action/globefish/species-analysis/small-pelagics/en), [GLOBEFISH Highlights 2022](https://infofish.org/v4/media/attachments/2023/03/09/gh-4-2022-updated.pdf)).

These price dynamics cannot be understood without reference to the biological and oceanographic context. Chub mackerel exhibit pronounced interannual and decadal variability in body size, condition, and recruitment — driven by density-dependent competition (especially with Japanese sardine), sea surface temperature (SST) anomalies, the Kuroshio Current's path and transport, ENSO/PDO regime shifts, and marine heatwaves. This report synthesizes market price data, fisheries stock assessments, and oceanographic research across the Pacific Rim to trace the causal chain: **ocean conditions → biological resource quality/quantity → market supply and pricing → fishery economics**.

## 1. Price Dynamics in Major Pacific Rim Aquatic Markets

### 1.1 Global Market Context

The global mackerel market was valued at US$13.48 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach US$17.45 billion by 2030 (5.31% CAGR), with Europe holding the largest share (36.85% in 2024) and Asia-Pacific the fastest growth (6.41% CAGR). Atlantic mackerel dominates species-level value (38.18% share in 2024), but chub mackerel (*Scomber japonicus*) is the key species across Pacific Rim domestic markets, canning industries, and West African export trade ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/mackerel-market)). The canned mackerel segment alone is valued at roughly US$0.99 billion (2025), projected to reach US$1.97 billion by 2036 ([Future Market Insights](https://www.futuremarketinsights.com/reports/canned-mackerel-market)).

FAO GLOBEFISH's 2025–2026 quarterly small-pelagics analyses document that "Asia remained the largest regional market for frozen small pelagics in 2025," with trade flows increasingly distorted by historically high Atlantic mackerel prices and reduced Norwegian availability ([FAO GLOBEFISH Small Pelagics](https://www.fao.org/in-action/globefish/species-analysis/small-pelagics/en)). The 2022 GLOBEFISH Highlights reported that small pelagic prices rose 98% between January and September 2022, driven by lower mackerel and herring quotas and a 6.7% reduction in global fishmeal supply following poor Peruvian anchoveta catches ([GLOBEFISH Highlights 2022](https://infofish.org/v4/media/attachments/2023/03/09/gh-4-2022-updated.pdf)). Fishmeal prices peaked at US$1,600/tonne in April 2022 and fish oil at US$3,700/tonne in September 2022 — nearly double year-earlier levels — demonstrating how forage-fish environmental shocks propagate through the entire aquatic protein economy.

### 1.2 Japan: The World's Most Important Chub Mackerel Market

**Market structure.** The Toyosu wholesale market (opened October 2018, replacing Tsukiji) is the world's largest wholesale fish market; seafood volume has declined from ~800,000 tonnes (1988) to ~330,000 tonnes recently, yet Toyosu's share of the national market rose to 26% ([Wikipedia: Toyosu Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyosu_Market), [Tokyo Suisan Shinko](https://lib.suisan-shinkou.or.jp/column/ryutsu-korekara/vol06.html)). A critical structural feature: contrary to the market's auction image, nearly 90% of fish by monetary value at Toyosu are traded via negotiated (relative) transactions, including mackerel, sardines, and saury. Prices are determined by landing volume, origin, size, and quality, with premium fishing methods (e.g., single-line "ippon-zuri") commanding 5× or higher prices ([Jiji Press](https://www.jiji.com/jc/v8?id=20220410toyosunews)).

**Wholesale price series.** The monthly average wholesale price of mackerel (サバ) at Toyosu, compiled by GD Freak from Tokyo Metropolitan Central Wholesale Market statistics, shows a dramatic escalation beginning in late 2024 ([GD Freak Toyosu price series](https://jp.gdfreak.com/public/detail/jp011013999600100250/3)):

| Period | Monthly average wholesale price (yen/kg) |
|---|---|
| 2024 range | 466 (May) – 651 (December) |
| 2025 range | 445 (June) – 864 (December) |
| 2026 Jan–Jun | 811, 693, 701, 647, 617, 637 |
| 5-yr average (2021–2025) | 433 (June) – 650 (December) |

June 2026's 637 yen/kg was ~43% higher than June 2025, marking 12 consecutive months above the two-year average and a record June since 2021. Prices show strong seasonality: based on the 2021–2025 average, December (the autumn fatty-mackerel peak) runs ~50% above the June trough, with prices typically rising 14% from June to July and a further 6% into August ([GD Freak Toyosu price series](https://jp.gdfreak.com/public/detail/jp011013999600100250/3)). Daily market data across 16 Japanese markets on August 21, 2026, showed a mid-range wholesale price of 514 yen/kg, with a 30-day range of 252–1,419 yen/kg and wide market-level dispersion (Sapporo mid 648 yen/kg; Toyosu mid 756; Osaka mid 580; Kobe cheapest at 375) ([魚相場ナビ](https://sakana-souba-navi.jp/fish/%E3%82%B5%E3%83%90)).

**Producer (landed) prices.** Japan's domestic landed prices for mackerel ranged from JPY 79 to JPY 110 per kg over the 2013–2022 decade — an order of magnitude below Toyosu wholesale prices — with the wholesale-to-landed margin widening substantially after 2022 ([SeafoodSource](https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/supply-trade/japan-a-leading-producer-of-mackerel-is-also-norway-s-most-important-market)). The Fisheries Agency White Paper documents that weighted-average producer prices for all fishery products rose from 131 yen/kg (2009) to 162 yen/kg (2019), while mackerel specifically saw both increased catches and rising prices after 2018, driven by growing demand for canned goods ([Japan Fisheries Agency White Paper](https://www.jfa.maff.go.jp/j/kikaku/wpaper/r01_h/trend/1/sankou_2_9.html), [Fisheries White Paper 2019 – Management Trends](https://www.jfa.maff.go.jp/j/kikaku/wpaper/r01_h/trend/1/t1_2_2.html)).

**Import prices and the Norway factor.** Japan is Norway's most important mackerel market (140,000 tonnes in 2022, >40% of Norway's total exports). Critically, Norwegian Atlantic mackerel import prices stood at ~200 yen/kg from 2010–2020 but tripled to ~600 yen/kg by 2025; one Tokyo retailer reported a 75% increase from 437 to 765 yen/kg between 2025 and February 2026 ([Toyo Keizai](https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/907381?display=b), [Plastic Pallet](https://plastic-pallet.co.jp/iran_japan-canned-fish-explained-202606)). Historically, Japanese domestic mackerel commanded a premium over Norwegian imports; that ranking has now reversed ([Toyo Keizai](https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/907381?display=b)).

**The canned mackerel crisis (2022–2026).** The most visible downstream manifestation of raw-material scarcity is the collapse of Japanese canned mackerel ("saba-kan") production — from ~42,000 tonnes five years ago (roughly) to **21,000 tonnes in 2024**, a 7% year-on-year decline and roughly half of 2019 levels. The national mackerel catch in fiscal 2023 (July 2023–June 2024) was ~271,000 tonnes, halved in five years; the Tokyo wholesale price reached 560 yen/kg in 2024 (+30% vs. five years earlier); and the Fisheries Agency's 2025 TAC for chub + spotted mackerel was cut ~40% to ~350,000 tonnes ([Asahi Shimbun](https://www.asahi.com/articles/ASTBH1V52TBHUTIL020M.html), [Zhoushan Aquatic Products Association](http://www.zsea.cn/index.php?m=content&c=index&a=show&catid=19&id=12187)). Manufacturer price increases cascaded through the system: Maruha Nichiro raised canned mackerel prices +11–23% in April 2023 ("record poor catch"); Kyokuyo cited record poor domestic catches and global meat-price inflation; Hagoromo Foods announced +7.3–29.4% increases in June 2026; and the premium "Sava-kan" line (launched 2013 to celebrate recovery from the 2011 earthquake) ceased production entirely in 2026 ([Minato Shimbun](https://www.minato-yamaguchi.co.jp/minato/e-minato/articles/130589), [Kyokuyo](https://www.kyokuyo.co.jp/news/002600.html), [Plastic Pallet](https://plastic-pallet.co.jp/iran_japan-canned-fish-explained-202606)).

The 2026 canned-fish crisis has four interacting drivers: (1) Red Sea shipping disruptions adding 10–15 days to Europe–Asia voyages; (2) Norway's quota cuts (a 22% reduction to 165,298 tonnes, with record purchase prices of 640–650 yen/kg); (3) fuel-cost-driven fleet tie-ups across Southeast Asia (Thailand's fishing association predicted up to 70% of commercial boats could halt from mid-April 2026); and (4) Japan's structural disadvantage — a 7% import tariff on Norwegian mackerel versus South Korea's 0%, causing Japan to "lose the bidding war" for raw material ([Plastic Pallet](https://plastic-pallet.co.jp/iran_japan-canned-fish-explained-202606)). Japanese imports from Norway fell from ~50,000 to under 30,000 tonnes.

### 1.3 South Korea: From Domestic Staple to Global Export Powerhouse

**Market structure.** The Busan Cooperative Fish Market (BCFM) is Korea's largest fish market and handles roughly **80% of the nation's mackerel distribution** ([Busan Ilbo](https://www.busan.com/view/busan/view.php?code=2025112315290083885)). Korea's price-monitoring infrastructure is sophisticated: the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries launched an integrated supply-demand information system in March 2019 covering mackerel, hairtail, squid, pollock, and others, with daily monitoring of production, origin, wholesale, and consumer prices ([MOF Korea](https://www.mof.go.kr/doc/ko/selectDoc.do?docSeq=25356&menuSeq=971&bbsSeq=10)). Busan also operates a "Big-Data Wave" fisheries dashboard integrating production-port auction data, wholesale data, and retail data ([Busan Big-Data Wave](https://data.busan.go.kr/bdip/analysis/rtlMntrng.do)).

**Auction and wholesale prices.** BCFM data show strong seasonality and a sharp upward trend:

- **January 2026:** ~10,880 tonnes auctioned at an average of **2,495 won/kg — nearly 60% above January 2025 (1,565 won/kg)** — attributed to a weaker won, rising overseas demand, and reduced domestic catches ([Stripes Korea](https://korea.stripes.com/food-drink/mackerel-busan.html)).
- **H1 2026 Noryangjin wholesale market:** **4,334 won/kg, +21% year-on-year**, double the overall seafood inflation rate of 11% ([Chosun](https://www.chosun.com/english/industry-en/2026/07/28/2YHO4U6X55HLVMYKATGMJE6LI4)).
- **BCFM official monthly data:** July 2026 unit price 3,259 won/kg (10,540 t); March 2026 highest volume at 2,405 won/kg (20,419 t); December 2025 highest total value (68.0 billion won across 25,487 t, unit price 2,669 won/kg) ([BCFM monthly sales](http://www.bcfm.co.kr/ndata/read_fish.jsp)).
- **2023 annual record:** BCFM generated 323 billion won — highest in 7 years — with mackerel alone at 67,000 tonnes/113 billion won (+16% from 2022's 58,000 t/90.9 billion won), driven by favorable weather (more fishing days, fewer typhoons) and surging exports to Africa and China ([Yonhap](https://www.yna.co.kr/view/AKR20231228100900051)).
- **November 2025 record volume:** ~220,000 boxes (~5,500 tonnes) were auctioned on November 22, 2025 — 1.5–2× the peak-season average and the largest quantity in 30 years, with unusually large East Sea (rather than Jeju) catches attributed to climate-driven shifts in fishing grounds ([Busan Ilbo](https://www.busan.com/view/busan/view.php?code=2025112315290083885)).

**Export boom.** Korean mackerel exports reached **US$200 million in 2025 (+128.8% year-on-year)**, with Africa taking roughly half. In H1 2026, exports surged **247% to US$181.7 million**, propelling mackerel from Korea's 12th-largest seafood export (2024) to 3rd place. China became Korea's largest seafood importer (US$391.8M in H1 2026, overtaking Japan), with purchases of medium-to-large mackerel increasing **36-fold year-on-year** and European canning demand for small mackerel rising 23-fold. At a July 17, 2026 Busan auction, ~80 wholesalers competed for 30,000 boxes of mackerel; one export company swept two-thirds of the volume for ~1.1 billion won ([Chosun](https://www.chosun.com/english/industry-en/2026/07/28/2YHO4U6X55HLVMYKATGMJE6LI4)). The driver is Norway's production collapse — reports describe Norway's 2026 quota at 81,000 tonnes (about half of the prior year) per [Chosun](https://www.chosun.com/english/industry-en/2026/07/28/2YHO4U6X55HLVMYKATGMJE6LI4), while other reporting cites a 22% cut to 165,298 tonnes ([Plastic Pallet](https://plastic-pallet.co.jp/iran_japan-canned-fish-explained-202606)); the discrepancy reflects different quota components, but the direction is unambiguous. Global demand has shifted toward Korean-caught chub mackerel as a substitute for Atlantic mackerel.

**Product-form differentials (February 2018 reference):** At Garak Wholesale Market (Seoul), fresh mackerel top grade traded at 30,000 won/box, domestic frozen top grade at 19,558 won/box, imported frozen top grade at 22,755 won/box; regional live/fresh mackerel from Wando commanded 21,000 won/kg ([FRESH HONG blog](https://blog.naver.com/seafoodsmall/221207153751?viewType=pc)).

### 1.4 China: Rising Importer, Global Supplier, and Price Maker for West Africa

China is among the fastest-growing markets for Atlantic mackerel globally, with consumption growing 35% between 2020–2024 to 12,000 tonnes, and pan-fried/ready-to-cook products increasingly popular among younger consumers; prices were expected to surge 20–30% in the 2025–26 season following the ICES September 2024 recommendation of a 22% reduction in the 2025 Northeast Atlantic mackerel TAC to 577,000 tonnes ([Foodmate](https://fsc.foodmate.net/show.php?itemid=718792)). Norway/Atlantic mackerel prices above US$2,700/tonne CFR West Africa have turned "what was once a large-volume business into a niche market" ([New Marine Ltd](https://www.facebook.com/newmarineai/posts/the-mackerel-market-is-seeing-significant-variations-across-regions-for-atlantic/1024049979732882)).

For *Scomber japonicus* specifically, China's 2024 season opened at US$1,250–1,300 CFR to West Africa for medium sizes; Japan offered ~US$1,300 for 200–400g B-quality; Korea offered 200–400g and 300–500g at US$1,350–1,400; Mexico offered limited quantities (fat >15%) at US$1,250–1,350; Morocco and Mauritania were reliable suppliers at US$1,300–1,350 ([New Marine Ltd](https://www.facebook.com/newmarineai/posts/the-mackerel-market-is-seeing-significant-variations-across-regions-for-atlantic/1024049979732882)). These CFR prices — roughly US$1.25–1.40/kg — define the global commodity benchmark for frozen whole chub mackerel.

Domestically, China's main distribution channel for fresh chub mackerel from its largest fishery (Zhoushan) is the Dàshìjiè Seafood Wholesale Center in Ningbo, which handles fresh boat catches in evening auctions (4–9 PM), supplemented by dried-goods and live-tank sections ([Traveler Mac](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UlhXiFY9uuA)); the Zhoushan Aquatic Products Association closely monitors Japan's mackerel crisis, reporting the 2024 Tokyo wholesale average of 560 yen/kg and the halving of Japanese canned production ([Zhoushan Association](http://www.zsea.cn/index.php?m=content&c=index&a=show&catid=19&id=12187)). JD.com's dominant domestic format is ready-to-eat water-boiled canned mackerel in 150g cans, alongside frozen Norwegian fillet boxes ([JD.com](https://www.jd.com/chanpin/11757704.html)).

### 1.5 Russia (Far East)

Vladivostok wholesale prices for frozen ungutted mackerel (скумбрия) stood at 115–140 rubles/kg in 2022; by 2026 listings had risen to 370–400 rubles/kg, with cold-smoked mackerel at ~257 rubles/kg ([fishery.ru](https://fishery.ru/prices/vladivostok?pid=399), [farpost.ru](https://www.farpost.ru/vladivostok/food/seafood/+/%D0%A1%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BC%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%8F)). These Russian Far East prices mix Atlantic mackerel and Pacific chub mackerel traded interchangeably, and ruble depreciation complicates dollar comparisons; the ~2.5–3× ruble appreciation over 2022–2026 nevertheless signals genuine tightening in Far Eastern supply, consistent with the broader northwest Pacific resource trends described in Section 2.

### 1.6 United States (West Coast): Low-Value, Environmentally Driven Fishery

US Pacific mackerel (chub mackerel) is the classic "boom-bust" small pelagic: commercially landed volume and value are small by Pacific Rim standards but highly variable. In 2024, commercial landings totaled 3.3 million pounds (≈1,500 tonnes) valued at US$800,000 — an average ex-vessel price of ~**US$0.24/lb (~$0.53/kg)**. The stock is not overfished and not subject to overfishing, but remains at relatively low historical abundance "primarily due to oceanographic conditions" ([NOAA Pacific Mackerel](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/pacific-mackerel)). In the 2023–24 fishing year, only ~778 mt of the 7,871 mt harvest guideline was taken (ex-vessel value ~$406,702), reflecting both low abundance and limited market demand; the 2025–26 harvest guideline is 9,143 mt based on an estimated biomass of 61,737 mt ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/25/2025-16233/fisheries-off-west-coast-states-coastal-pelagic-species-fisheries-biennial-specifications-2025-2026), [NOAA Catch-Only Assessment](https://www.pcouncil.org/documents/2025/05/catch-only-stock-assessment-of-pacific-mackerel-scomber-japonicus-for-u-s-management-in-the-2025-26-and-2026-27-fishing-years.pdf)). Management history matters: a 1970 moratorium followed the 1960s collapse, a 1985 moratorium was triggered when biomass fell below the 18,200 mt cutoff, and the 2000 federal Coastal Pelagic Species FMP now sets Harvest = (Biomass − 18,200 mt) × 30% × 70% distribution factor ([Crone & Hill 2015](https://www.mafmc.org/s/8_Pacific_mackerel_assessment2015.pdf)). By contrast, the separate Atlantic chub mackerel fishery (GARFO) had used only 1.75% of its 4.5-million-pound 2026 quota by mid-July ([NOAA GARFO](https://apps-garfo.fisheries.noaa.gov/quota-monitoring/squid-mackerel-butterfish/chub_qm.html)).

### 1.7 Peru, Chile, and Mexico: The Humboldt and Eastern Pacific Producers

**Peru** is the largest South American producer. Chub mackerel (caballa) represents **15.7% of total hydrobiological resources entering Peruvian wholesale fish markets** and contributes 1.1% of GDP. The average wholesale market price is **S/4.1 per kg (≈US$1.10/kg)**. Landings are predominantly directed to frozen and canning industries for export, with a smaller share to fresh wholesale markets. The 2025 global quota was 79,013 tonnes (30,104 t large-scale + 48,909 t artisanal); January–October 2025 landings were 38,000 tonnes (-30.6% year-on-year), following a declining annual trajectory: 99,000 t (2020), 99,000 (2021), 91,000 (2022), 81,000 (2023), 57,000 (2024) ([PRODUCE Caballa Ficha](https://www.producempresarial.pe/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/602-5.-Ficha-Recurso-Caballa_OCT-2025.pdf)). Exports in Jan–Oct 2025 were 10,500 t worth US$15.3 million FOB (unit value ≈ **US$1.46/kg**), primarily to Côte d'Ivoire (67%), Spain (8%), Italy (5%), and Ghana (4%) — the African frozen-whole market again dominating. Seasonality peaks January–March, but monthly landings are extremely volatile (from 1,672 t in October 2024 to 28,534 t in February 2024) ([PRODUCE Caballa Ficha](https://www.producempresarial.pe/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/602-5.-Ficha-Recurso-Caballa_OCT-2025.pdf)). Historically, Peru's record chub mackerel catch was **177,507 tonnes in 1997, surpassed by ~380,000 tonnes in 1998–99** at the height of the 1997–98 El Niño — a striking demonstration of warm-phase abundance ([IHMA](https://ihma.org.pe/caballa)). El Niño's role in 2026 is again evident: a "super" El Niño forced Peru to cancel its April–July anchovy season and doubled jack mackerel and corvina prices in domestic markets ([Grist](https://grist.org/economics/el-nino-scrambling-fisheries-throughout-the-pacific)).

**Mexico** is a modest but growing exporter: 2024 exports were 8,689 tonnes valued at US$15.95 million (unit value US$1.84/kg), with unit values volatile across years — US$2.39/kg (2020), US$1.23/kg (2021), US$1.19/kg (2022), US$1.41/kg (2023) — reflecting volume-price tradeoffs and product mix ([Selina Wamucii Mexico](https://www.selinawamucii.com/insights/prices/mexico/mackerel)).

**Chile** manages chub mackerel (caballa) as a fishery with closed access to new operators; the resource is primarily processed into fishmeal/oil with secondary canned, frozen, and eviscerated lines, with main export markets for fishmeal in China, Japan, Taiwan, and Germany ([SUBPESCA](https://www.subpesca.cl/portal/616/w3-article-828.html)). No public price series was recovered for Chilean chub mackerel, reflecting its reduction-fishery orientation.

## 2. Interannual Variations in Body Weight and Length

### 2.1 Species Baseline

*Scomber japonicus* reaches a maximum of 64 cm TL (common length ~30 cm FL), maximum weight 2.9 kg, and maximum age 18 years; length at first maturity is ~26.1 cm; it prefers subtropical waters of 10–27°C, and spawns in batches at 15–20°C ([FishBase](https://fishbase.se/summary/Speciessummary.php?id=117)). Across the Pacific Rim, each major stock exhibits distinct but parallel stories of density-dependent size decline, strong year-class effects, and environmental forcing.

### 2.2 Japanese Pacific Stock: The Strong 2013 Year Class and Density-Dependent Shrinkage

The Pacific stock off Japan collapsed from a spawning stock of 0.45–1.60 million tonnes (1970–1985) to ~0.1 million tonnes in the late 1990s, with recruitment overfishing of the dominant 1992 and 1996 year classes preventing recovery ([Kawabata et al.](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/fishsci1994/68/sup1/68_sup1_93/_pdf)). Recovery came after the **strong 2013 year class**: the population grew from 153,000 tonnes (2001) to a massive 6,808,000 tonnes (2019) per larval-density surveys in the northern Satsunan area ([Frontiers 2021 Satsunan study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2021.725227/full)). This extraordinary biomass expansion triggered density-dependent growth suppression, documented by Kamimura et al. using 18,302 fish sampled at northern Pacific-coast ports (2006–2019):

- **Body condition, growth, and habitat temperature all decreased with increasing conspecific and Japanese sardine abundance.** Mean annual growth rates were positively correlated with body condition in all four quarters (p < 0.01).
- Structural equation modeling showed that **direct food competition (conspecific + sardine abundance) affects body condition more strongly than indirect temperature effects** — the "direct effects" pathway dominates.
- Abundance ranges were extraordinary: age-1+ chub mackerel abundance ranged 8.5×10⁹ (2007) to 141.2×10⁹ individuals (2017); Japanese sardine abundance ranged 5.2×10⁹ (2008) to 313.6×10⁹ (2018).
- Length–weight relationship: **W = 1.09 × 10⁻⁶ L³·⁴¹**; von Bertalanffy Linf ranged 340–441 mm and K from 0.25–0.55, confirming highly variable growth ([Kamimura et al. 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.03.25.436928.full)).

The baseline trend is equally important: Watanabe & Yatsu showed that from 1970–1998, as abundance declined, the proportion of mature females at each age **increased** and length/age at 50% maturity **decreased** — a classic density-dependent growth acceleration during stock decline, modulated by spawning-ground SST for age-1 fish ([Watanabe & Yatsu 2006](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165783606000154)); and length-at-age interannual variation is jointly driven by density dependence and SST in the Kuroshio–Oyashio area ([Watanabe & Yatsu 2004](https://spo.nmfs.noaa.gov/content/effects-density-dependence-and-sea-surface-temperature-interannual-variation-length-age)). Region-wide, Lin & Ito documented **two distinct periods of declining fish weight in the western North Pacific — the 1980s and the 2010s — with the 2010s decline reaching levels similar to the 1980s lows**, attributed to (1) inter/intra-species competition (Neocalanus copepod depletion by sardine; the 2010s driven by recovering sardine + chub mackerel), (2) bottom-up effects of stronger stratification and reduced carrying capacity, and (3) temperature-size rule effects ([Lin & Ito, PICES ECCWO5](https://meetings.pices.int/Publications/Presentations/2023-ECCWO5/GP/GP-P19.pdf)).

### 2.3 Tsushima Warm Current Stock (East China Sea / Sea of Japan / Yellow Sea)

The Tsushima stock biomass fluctuated around 1 million tonnes (1973–1996), peaked at 1.37 million (1996), fell to a low of 370,000 tonnes (2013), and recovered to 654,000 tonnes (2018), with SSB at 240,000 tonnes (2018). Growth is comparatively fast: 25–28 cm FL at age 1, 29–32 cm at age 2, 33–35 cm at age 3, 36 cm at age 4; maturity 60% at age 1, 85% at age 2, 100% at age 3+ ([Tsushima Stock Assessment 2019](https://www.fra.go.jp/shigen/fisheries_resources/meeting/peer_review_meeting/files/2020/assess_masaba_t.pdf)). Notably, Chinese catches (up to 500,000 t since 2010) are not included in the Japan–Korea joint assessment — a major uncertainty in estimating total mortality and hence body-size trends for this stock ([Tsushima Stock Assessment 2019](https://www.fra.go.jp/shigen/fisheries_resources/meeting/peer_review_meeting/files/2020/assess_masaba_t.pdf)).

### 2.4 Korean Waters: Alarming Miniaturization of the Catch

The most striking morphometric trend in the region comes from Korea. Busan Cooperative Fish Market landing data show the proportion of small-sized (≤250 g, ≈29.7 cm TL — below the L50 of ~31.4 cm) immature individuals in the catch rose **from 65% in 2015 to 88% in 2024**. A 2026 assessment using CMSY++ and Bayesian state-space models classified the stock as overfished (B/BMSY = 0.63 and 0.59 in 2024), with 87.6–95.3% posterior probability of remaining in the "overfished and overfishing" red quadrant due to sustained high fishing mortality since the early 2000s. Korean catch fell from a 1996 peak of 415,003 tonnes to a historical low of 77,603 tonnes in 2020, recovering only to 124,928 tonnes in 2024; the large purse-seine fleet accounts for 82.2% of domestic catch. Critically, nominal fleet reduction (342 vessels in 1992 → 106 in 2024) was offset by horsepower growth (827 → 1,471 EHP/vessel), so effective effort increased ([Kwak, Kim & Lee 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1814806/full)).

### 2.5 Northwest Pacific High Seas (Chinese Light Purse Seine Fishery)

Chinese high-seas operations (35–45°N, 145–165°E) provide the longest continuous biological time series for the oceanic component of the Pacific stock. From 38,123 fishing operations (2016–2021) and 4,440 biological samples:

- **Fork length ranged 102–400 mm; mean length varied from 212.07 mm (2016) to 268.28 mm (2018)** — a ~56 mm swing driven by year-class composition.
- Growth parameters: Linf 36.0–42.35 cm; K 0.31–0.65; Z (total mortality) 1.01–1.64; F (fishing mortality) 0.23–0.79.
- CPUE declined year-on-year (r = −0.489, p < 0.01), with high values 2016–2018 and low values 2019–2021; the fishing-ground centroid moved northwestward, consistent with warming.
- LBB assessment found no overfishing during 2016–2021 (exploitation rate 0.17–0.32), but the exploitation rate was rising sharply, reaching **0.48 by 2021** ([Zhao, Zhang & Tang 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-2615/15/8/1135)).

A more recent integrated LBB + Catch-MSY assessment (2019–2024, 6,061 specimens) shows the situation deteriorating: the stock was within sustainable limits (B/BMSY ≥ 1.0) in 2019, 2021, and 2022, but suffered overfishing in 2020, 2023, and 2024; pooled B/BMSY dropped to **0.51 in 2023–2024**, with F/M > 1.5 in recent years, and the final year diagnosed as recruitment overfishing. The authors recommend a TAC of 211,300–281,700 t (60–80% of the current baseline of 352,100 t) ([Huang et al. 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-3888/11/5/276)).

### 2.6 East China Sea / Yellow Sea Coastal Component

Cai et al.'s growth-heterogeneity study (2016–2020, 2,686 specimens from the Zhejiang coastal area and high seas near Japan) found fork lengths of 40–294 mm (mean 181.91 mm), body weights 0.8–311.8 g (mean 73.27 g), and length–weight relationship **W = (1.41 × 10⁻⁶) × FL³·³⁷**. Year-to-year variation was the **largest source of growth heterogeneity** (exceeding regional and seasonal effects), most pronounced for fish >200 mm FL. The lowest relative condition factor occurred in **2017**; fork length was significantly greater in 2020 than all other years; and fish in the high-seas area (median 220 mm) were much larger than in the Zhejiang coastal area (median 150 mm). Otolith aging (0–5 years) showed individual growth slower than in previous studies ([Cai et al. 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/10/2/301)). The longer East China Sea record (1999–2011 lighting-purse-seine catch data) showed a declining abundance trend, especially after 2008, with the abundance index falling from 1.22 (2008) to 0.82 (2011) ([Wang et al. 2014](http://china-fishery.cn/en/article/doi/10.3724/SP.J.1231.2014.48737)).

### 2.7 California Current Stock

The US/Mexico Pacific mackerel stock shows the same boom-bust morphology. Survey fish (2012–2022, n=1,762 aged) spanned 53–402 mm FL and ages 0–7, with **age-0 fish comprising 46%** of the sample; CDFW port sampling (2008–2022, n=9,035) spanned 84–429 mm FL and ages 0–9, with ages 0–3 representing 96% of the catch. Length at 50% maturity is 274 ± 1.26 mm FL; age at 50% maturity is ~1.01 years — all females mature by 2.5 years ([NOAA Life History Report 2023](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/52097/noaa_52097_DS1.pdf)). The 2023 benchmark assessment estimated 2021 total biomass at 93,823 mt (87% from ages 0–2), with projected SSB of 46,167 mt (2023) and 50,372 mt (2024) ([Kuriyama et al. 2023](https://www.pcouncil.org/documents/2023/06/assessment-of-pacific-mackerel-scomber-japonicus-for-u-s-management-in-the-2023-24-and-2024-25-fishing-years.pdf)). The 2025 catch-only assessment estimated biomass at 61,737 mt (2025–26) and 67,954 mt (2026–27), with high recruitment in model years **2011, 2016, and 2018** — a pulse pattern tied to oceanographic events detailed in Section 3 ([NOAA Catch-Only Assessment](https://www.pcouncil.org/documents/2025/05/catch-only-stock-assessment-of-pacific-mackerel-scomber-japonicus-for-u-s-management-in-the-2025-26-and-2026-27-fishing-years.pdf)). Notably, this stock's SSB declined from 26,676 mt (2008) to 5,822 mt (2016) before recovering to 7,512 mt (2018) — an extraordinarily tight coupling to the 2014–2016 marine heatwave and its aftermath ([Crone et al. 2019](https://www.pcouncil.org/documents/2019/06/agenda-item-f-3-attachment-1-stock-assessment-report-pacific-mackerel-stock-assessment-for-u-s-management-in-the-2019-20-and-2020-2021-fishing-years-full-report-electronic-only.pdf)).

### 2.8 Peruvian (Humboldt) Stock

Peruvian chub mackerel (*S. japonicus peruanus*) in 2025 showed a **main size mode of 35 cm TL with juveniles (<29 cm) at just 0.01% of the catch** — a healthy adult-dominated size structure, contrasting with the high juvenile incidence observed in 2011, 2013, 2015, and 2018–2023 for jack mackerel ([SPRFMO SC13-ECO05](https://www.sprfmo.int/assets/Meetings/02-SC/13th-SC-2025/Ecosystems/SC13-ECO05-Abundance-and-biological-aspects-of-Jack-mackerel-and-other-species-of-the-Peru-Current-in-the-Humboldt-System.pdf)). Chub mackerel sizes in Peruvian waters generally range 20–43 cm TL (modes 15–33 cm), with adult fish concentrated along thermal fronts between Subtropical Surface Waters and Cold Coastal Waters ([SPRFMO SC8-HM03](https://www.sprfmo.int/assets/Meetings/SC/8th-SC-2020/SC8-HM03-Jack-and-Chub-Mackerel-2011-2020.pdf)). The 2024 summer survey found adult chub mackerel unusually predominant in the southern area, reflecting post-Coastal-El Niño habitat reorganization ([SPRFMO SC12-HM05](https://www.sprfmo.int/assets/Meetings/02-SC/12th-SC-2024/Habitat-Monitoring/SC12-HM05-PER-SPRFMO-Habitat-for-Jack-and-chub-mackerel-Peru-2022-2024.pdf)).

### 2.9 Summary of Morphometric Drivers

Across all Pacific Rim stocks, interannual variation in body weight and length is governed by four interacting factors: **(1) year-class effects** (strong cohorts like 2013 in Japan, 2011/2016/2018 in California, or 2016/2017 in Peru dominate mean size for years); **(2) density-dependent growth** (Kamimura et al.'s SEM: competition suppresses condition and growth; Watanabe & Yatsu: accelerated maturity at low abundance); **(3) interspecific competition with sardine** (the sardine–mackerel "alternation"); and **(4) temperature** (warmer SST reduces length-at-age in the western North Pacific, tends to increase condition in the Humboldt where chub mackerel prefer warmer equatorial waters). These biological realities mediate how oceanographic signals become market-price signals.

## 3. Oceanographic Correlations

### 3.1 SST Thresholds and Spawning Habitat

Chub mackerel spawning is tightly constrained by temperature. The temperature suitability index (TSI) literature assumes a Gaussian spawning-temperature response with **mean 18°C and variance 2.0°C** (suitable range roughly 15–22°C); TSI in the East China Sea and Tsushima Strait showed abrupt regime shifts in the late 1990s for the Tsushima stock and in the late 1970s for the Pacific stock, with abundance lagging TSI by 1–2 years (the 0–2-year age structure of the catch) ([Frontiers TSI 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.996626/full)). For the Pacific-cohort recruitment, GLM/GAM analysis (1980–2016) identified optimal ranges of **SST 20.2–20.6°C, SSH 62–65 cm, and SSS 34.72–34.83** on the spawning grounds, with recruitment at high levels when ln(SSB) > 6.0 ([Wu & Chen 2020](https://www.china-fishery.com/jfcen/article/abstract/20180811426?st=article_issue)).

Fishing-ground habitat is cooler and more constrained: the optimal SST range for the Chinese high-seas light-purse-seine fishery is **14–18°C (~16°C ideal)**, with optimal chlorophyll-α of 0.3–0.8 mg/m³; ocean warming has shifted migration timing earlier and displaced the fishing-ground centroid **0.92° east and 1.57° north between 2014 and 2019** ([Li et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-3888/9/8/323)). Russian waters similarly show mackerel concentrations at 12–17°C, with the 8°C isotherm as the northern/deep habitat limit ([TINRO/PICES 2020](https://meetings.pices.int/Publications/Presentations/PICES-2020/VW6-ePoster-15001-Ustinova.pdf)). In northeastern Taiwan, peak catch rates occur at much warmer SST (29°C), chlorophyll 0.25 mg/m³, salinity 33.7 psu, and SSH 0.575 m, with the Western Pacific Oscillation the most significant climatic driver ([Mondal et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0141113624004641)). In Peru, chub mackerel are fished at SST of 18–24°C (vs. 18–25°C for jack mackerel), with salinity 34.9–35.4 psu and chlorophyll <4 µg/L — always associated with the coastal-oceanic front ([SPRFMO SC12-HM05](https://www.sprfmo.int/assets/Meetings/02-SC/12th-SC-2024/Habitat-Monitoring/SC12-HM05-PER-SPRFMO-Habitat-for-Jack-and-chub-mackerel-Peru-2022-2024.pdf)).

### 3.2 The Kuroshio Current: Conveyor Belt and Obstacle

The Kuroshio is the dominant physical feature for Japan's Pacific stock. It acts as a larval conveyor belt from southern spawning grounds (Izu Ridge, Kii Peninsula, Shikoku, Kyushu) to the productive Kuroshio–Oyashio transition nursery. A coupled ocean–biophysical model comparing three Kuroshio path modes found that **active larval swimming (32 cm/s with diel vertical migration) reduces transport distances by up to 473 km and shifts larvae toward higher-zooplankton nursery areas**, with the large-meander (TLM) path producing the widest larval distribution due to pronounced meandering and eddy activity; nearshore non-large-meander paths produced narrower distributions ([Deng & Li 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3263/16/6/212)). The largest Kuroshio large meander on record — LM2017, beginning August 2017 and persisting more than 7.5 years ([Hirata et al. 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10872-025-00753-z)) — ended in spring 2026, an event that will restructure coastal warming and larval retention patterns ([Plastic Pallet](https://plastic-pallet.co.jp/iran_japan-canned-fish-explained-202606)).

The Igeta et al. study on Japanese jack mackerel provides a causal template: a rising sea level west of Kyushu caused by the **Kuroshio's northward shift** strengthened the Tsushima Warm Current throughflow, diverting larvae that had previously reached the Pacific coast into the Sea of Japan, contributing to the 2000–2017 recruitment decline ([Igeta et al. 2023](https://kyushu-u.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/effect-of-interannual-variations-of-kuroshiotsushima-warm-current)). ARDL-ECM modeling of chub mackerel further shows Kuroshio volume transport positively correlates with chub mackerel abundance in winter but negatively in summer; Oyashio area significantly affects fish under 2 years old; and SST anomalies significantly affect all age groups in both short and long term ([Li et al. 2025](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025JSR...20302561L/abstract)).

### 3.3 ENSO, PDO, and the Sardine–Mackerel Alternation

The classic framework of Chavez et al. remains foundational: Pacific sardine and anchovy (and by extension chub mackerel) populations alternate on ~50-year timescales, synchronized across Japan, California, Peru, and Chile, tracking basin-scale thermocline depth and productivity. Warm "sardine regimes" (1925–1950, 1975–mid-1990s) alternate with cool "anchovy regimes"; the 1976–77 and 1988–89 regime shifts are the most consequential for modern Japanese fisheries ([Chavez et al. 2003](https://www.ruirosalab.com/uploads/4/3/7/0/43701679/chavez_et_al_2005.pdf)). Lindegren et al.'s demographic reconstruction (1661 onward) quantified the mechanism: environmentally sensitive stock-recruitment models explained **77.2% of sardine and 70.9% of anchovy recruitment variance**, with sardine benefiting from warm SST and anchovy from cool SST, at dominant periodicities of ~60 (anchovy) and ~80 (sardine) years ([Lindegren et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3746886)).

For chub mackerel in the East China Sea, the PDO operates with a **counterintuitive sign**: during warm PDO phases, vertical water temperatures on the fishing ground were *cooler* (a PDO-induced redistribution of the water column), preferred habitat shifted southeastward, and suitable habitat shrank; during cool PDO phases, habitat expanded northwestward ([Yu et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11802-021-4760-x)). Russian fishery research attributes the post-2014 en-masse reappearance of sardine and mackerel in the Sea of Okhotsk/Kuril waters to a positive PDO phase with relatively cold conditions in the reproduction zone and a large Kuroshio meander ([TINRO/PICES 2020](https://meetings.pices.int/Publications/Presentations/PICES-2020/VW6-ePoster-15001-Ustinova.pdf)). The current PDO "regime" is unusual: the 2020s negative phase is "new" — the Northeast Pacific is only relatively cool compared to an extremely warm central/western North Pacific — amplifying marine heatwave activity ([Climate Impact Company](https://climateimpactcompany.com/northwest-u-s-hydro-report-new-vs-old-pacific-decadal-oscillation-2)).

### 3.4 Marine Heatwaves: The Blob and the Northwest Pacific Record

The 2013–2016 Northeast Pacific "Blob" — 711 days, peak anomaly +2.6°C in July 2015 — fundamentally restructured the California Current forage base (krill collapse, pyrosome blooms, 62,000 murre deaths, record toxic algal blooms) ([NOAA Fisheries: Looking Back at the Blob](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/looking-back-blob-record-warming-drives-unprecedented-ocean-change), [Scripps MHW FAQ](https://scripps.ucsd.edu/research/climate-change-resources/californias-marine-heatwaves)). For Pacific mackerel, the heatwave and the 2015–16 El Niño coincided with the stock's lowest SSB (5,822 mt in 2016) but also set up the 2016 and 2018 recruitment pulses that drove the subsequent recovery ([Crone et al. 2019](https://www.pcouncil.org/documents/2019/06/agenda-item-f-3-attachment-1-stock-assessment-report-pacific-mackerel-stock-assessment-for-u-s-management-in-the-2019-20-and-2020-2021-fishing-years-full-report-electronic-only.pdf)).

In the western North Pacific, a dedicated 2014–2021 marine-heatwave analysis found:

- CPUE was **significantly lower during marine heatwaves (p < 0.001)**; the Pearson correlation between heatwave frequency and CPUE was **r = −0.72 (p < 0.001)**.
- Heatwave intensity peaked at 3.5–4.5°C in 2016; durations exceeded 90 days in the central region during 2020–2021 (up from 10–25 days in earlier years).
- GAM analysis showed heatwave intensity explains 33.9% of CPUE deviance; CPUE dropped rapidly once intensity exceeded ~1.5–2.3°C.
- The CPUE centroid shifted east (~151°E → ~153°E) and north in summer — poleward displacement to escape thermal stress ([Ji et al. 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-3888/11/1/13)).

### 3.5 Humboldt Current: Upwelling, El Niño, and the Chub vs. Jack Mackerel Flip

The Humboldt Current is the world's most productive eastern boundary current (18–20% of global marine fish catch), driven by year-round coastal upwelling off Peru, with an oxygen minimum zone that compresses pelagic habitat toward the surface ([Wikipedia: Humboldt Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Current)). El Niño events deepen the thermocline, suppress upwelling, and trigger ecosystem flips. The Peruvian time series shows a striking bimodal alternation within the mackerel complex itself: **cold-to-normal years (2011–2014) favored jack mackerel** (peak catch 208,000 t in 2011), while **warm El Niño years (2015–2017) favored chub mackerel** (peak catch 140,000 t in 2016; geostatistical abundance >800,000 t in August 2016), with chub mackerel — a warmer-water species — distributing further north and over the shelf ([SPRFMO SC8-HM03](https://www.sprfmo.int/assets/Meetings/SC/8th-SC-2020/SC8-HM03-Jack-and-Chub-Mackerel-2011-2020.pdf)). Chub mackerel ecological niche modeling confirms 95% of records from 1997–2018 occurred at 16.12–26.15°C ([Torrejón-Magallanes et al. 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0079661121001567)). The 2026 "super" El Niño again disrupted Peruvian fisheries: anchovy moved below purse-seine reach (>100 m), and jack mackerel/corvina prices doubled in markets ([Grist](https://grist.org/economics/el-nino-scrambling-fisheries-throughout-the-pacific)). Characterization-factor research confirms ENSO indices (especially sea-level anomalies) correlate weakly-to-moderately with Peruvian stock productivity, including chub mackerel ([Manrique-Muñante et al. 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11367-026-02626-z)).

### 3.6 Climate Projections: A Northward-Shifting Resource

Under RCP 8.5, the western North Pacific is projected to warm 0.90–1.42°C across the habitat by the 2050s; spawning grounds (SGI > 0.7) currently extending from northern Taiwan through the Korea Strait to the Sea of Japan will shift northward, and nursery-ground biomass will redistribute from the Japan/East Sea toward the Korea Strait and Yellow Sea ([Go, Lee & Jung 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-3888/10/1/20)). Larval IBM projections show overall larval biomass increasing 8.88% by the 2050s, with a 0.72°N centroid shift; East China Sea share declines from 77.3% to 71.3%, while Korea Strait share more than doubles from 7.0% to 16.0% ([Go & Jung 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2025.1648650/full)). Chub mackerel will become less strongly influenced by the Kuroshio as spawning shifts north ([Global Seafood Alliance](https://www.globalseafood.org/advocate/projecting-the-shift-of-chub-mackerel-spawning-grounds-from-climate-change-in-the-western-north-pacific-ocean)). The California Current analog projects sardine poleward displacement of ~500–800 km by end of century, with catch increasing 50–70% in the northern CCS and decreasing 30–70% in the south ([Fiechter et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2021.685241/full)); chub mackerel should follow similar thermal habitat shifts.

## 4. Integrated Analysis: Environment → Biology → Supply → Price → Fishery Economics

The Pacific Rim chub mackerel system provides one of the clearest available demonstrations of the full causal chain from ocean physics to supermarket prices. The following case studies trace these linkages quantitatively.

### 4.1 Japan: The 2022–2026 Price Crisis as a Compound Environmental-Management Shock

**Environment → biology:** The Kuroshio large meander (2017–2026), record marine heatwaves (2014–2021), and the 2015–2025 regime-scale recovery of Japanese sardine combined to suppress chub mackerel body condition and recruitment. Marine-heatwave frequency correlated with CPUE decline at r = −0.72 ([Ji et al. 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-3888/11/1/13)); Kamimura et al. demonstrated density-dependent declines in body condition and growth as sardine abundance rose from 5.2×10⁹ to 313.6×10⁹ individuals ([Kamimura et al. 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.03.25.436928.full)); Lin & Ito showed the 2010s weight decline rivaling the 1980s ([Lin & Ito](https://meetings.pices.int/Publications/Presentations/2023-ECCWO5/GP/GP-P19.pdf)).

**Biology → supply:** The national catch halved from ~540,000 t (2018) to ~271,000 t (fiscal 2023), a five-year halving; the Fisheries Agency responded with a ~40% TAC cut for 2025 (~350,000 t combined), including a 60% cut in the Pacific-stock quota (from ~350,000 to 139,000 t) ([Asahi](https://www.asahi.com/articles/ASTBH1V52TBHUTIL020M.html), [Huang et al. 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-3888/11/5/276)).

**Supply → price:** Toyosu wholesale prices rose from 466 yen/kg (May 2024) to 864 yen/kg (December 2025), with June 2026 at 637 yen/kg (+43% Y/Y; 12 consecutive months above the two-year average) ([GD Freak](https://jp.gdfreak.com/public/detail/jp011013999600100250/3)). Canned production halved to 21,000 t, and retail canned prices rose ~10% in a single year (March 2025–March 2026) ([Asahi](https://www.asahi.com/articles/ASTBH1V52TBHUTIL020M.html)). MAFF's econometric analysis quantifies the price-quantity relationship: for mackerel, the year-over-year correlation between production-area prices and landing volumes is **−0.544** — a strong inverse price-quantity relationship — and the consumption-market/retail price correlation is **+0.837**, meaning wholesale shocks transmit almost one-for-one to consumers ([MAFF Price Analysis](https://www.maff.go.jp/j/tokei/bunseki/report/attach/pdf/suisanbutu_kakaku-2.pdf)).

**Price → economics:** Mackerel's demand elasticity is high (−2.85 to −2.51), so retail prices rising faster than incomes induces demand destruction (household per-capita consumption fell from 1,100 g in 2015 to 815 g in 2021 even before the crisis) ([SeafoodSource](https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/supply-trade/japan-a-leading-producer-of-mackerel-is-also-norway-s-most-important-market), [MAFF](https://www.maff.go.jp/j/tokei/bunseki/report/attach/pdf/suisanbutu_kakaku-2.pdf)). Japan's structural disadvantage (7% mackerel import tariff vs. Korea's 0%) means Japanese canners lose raw-material auctions to Korean and Chinese buyers — an economic transmission of environmental scarcity across borders ([Plastic Pallet](https://plastic-pallet.co.jp/iran_japan-canned-fish-explained-202606)).

### 4.2 South Korea: Export-Led Price Inflation as a Substitute for Norwegian Supply

Korea demonstrates the globalized transmission of a single environmental-management event (Norway's quota cuts). Norway's reductions — a 20% cut in 2024, 22% in 2026 to 165,298 t with record purchase prices of 640–650 yen/kg ([Plastic Pallet](https://plastic-pallet.co.jp/iran_japan-canned-fish-explained-202606)) — removed ~50,000+ tonnes from the global frozen-mackerel market. Korea's response was a **247% surge in H1 2026 exports to US$181.7M**, with China's medium-large mackerel purchases up 36-fold and European canning demand up 23-fold ([Chosun](https://www.chosun.com/english/industry-en/2026/07/28/2YHO4U6X55HLVMYKATGMJE6LI4)). Domestic consumers bore the cost: wholesale prices rose 21% (Noryangjin H1 2026) and auction prices 60% (Busan January 2026) ([Chosun](https://www.chosun.com/english/industry-en/2026/07/28/2YHO4U6X55HLVMYKATGMJE6LI4), [Stripes Korea](https://korea.stripes.com/food-drink/mackerel-busan.html)). The biological foundation is fragile: with 88% of Busan landings now immature (≤250 g) and B/BMSY at 0.59–0.63, Korea is exporting a depleting resource ([Kwak et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1814806/full)). This is a textbook case of **global quota scarcity bidding up the price of a substitutable Pacific species, with domestic consumers as the residual claimant.**

### 4.3 Peru: El Niño as a Double-Edged Sword

Peru's history inverts the Japanese pattern: warm events *increase* chub mackerel availability — the record 380,000-tonne catch came during the 1997–98 El Niño ([IHMA](https://ihma.org.pe/caballa)) — while simultaneously destroying the anchoveta basis of the fishmeal industry (2022 anchoveta oil yields fell to 2% vs. a normal 3%, and the 2026 El Niño forced cancellation of the April–July anchovy season, with jack mackerel and corvina prices doubling in markets) ([GLOBEFISH Highlights 2022](https://infofish.org/v4/media/attachments/2023/03/09/gh-4-2022-updated.pdf), [Grist](https://grist.org/economics/el-nino-scrambling-fisheries-throughout-the-pacific)). The economic net effect depends on which product chain dominates: for chub mackerel, warm events boost the frozen/canned export-oriented fishery (average export unit value ≈ US$1.46/kg FOB; domestic wholesale ≈ S/4.1/kg ≈ US$1.10/kg), but El Niño-driven fishmeal/fish-oil price spikes (US$1,600/t and US$3,700/t in 2022) raise feed costs globally, including for aquaculture competitors and canned-fish supply chains ([PRODUCE](https://www.producempresarial.pe/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/602-5.-Ficha-Recurso-Caballa_OCT-2025.pdf), [GLOBEFISH Highlights 2022](https://infofish.org/v4/media/attachments/2023/03/09/gh-4-2022-updated.pdf)). Peru also illustrates how management responds to environment-driven variability: the 2024 quota was adjusted to a complementary 5,775 t for large-scale vessels after IMARPE advised the total catch limit should not exceed 63,000 t across all fleets (F = 2.0× the 2023 level, below MSY) ([Ministerial Resolution 092-2024-PRODUCE](https://lapatria.pe/elperuano/523-44/resolucion-ministerial-n-000092-2024-produce)).

### 4.4 United States: Where Environmental Variability Shows Up in Quantity, Not Price

The US West Coast presents a contrasting transmission mode: Pacific mackerel ex-vessel prices are persistently low (~US$0.53/kg) and unresponsive to scarcity because landings are tiny and demand is thin; environmental variability (the 2014–16 heatwave, ENSO-driven recruitment in 2011/2016/2018) transmits almost entirely through **quantity**, not price ([NOAA Pacific Mackerel](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/pacific-mackerel), [Crone et al. 2019](https://www.pcouncil.org/documents/2019/06/agenda-item-f-3-attachment-1-stock-assessment-report-pacific-mackerel-stock-assessment-for-u-s-management-in-the-2019-20-and-2020-2021-fishing-years-full-report-electronic-only.pdf)). The fishery's economic footprint (778 mt harvested, ~$407,000 ex-vessel in 2023–24) is so small that even a 10-fold biomass increase would barely register in US seafood markets — the species is a "boom-bust" ecological player, not a commercial anchor, in North America. This contrast underscores that **the price transmission of oceanographic variability is mediated by market structure, trade orientation, and the existence of a processing/canning industry** — not by biology alone.

### 4.6 A Regional Synthesis: Oceanic Regimes as the Common Driver

The synthesis across the Pacific Rim reveals a coherent pattern. Decadal-scale oceanographic regimes (PDO/ENSO and the sardine anchovy alternation) set the background level of chub mackerel productivity in each region — favorable in the Humboldt during warm phases, unfavorable in Japan during the current warm/sardine phase, and pulsing in California with recruitment events. Superimposed on these regimes, marine heatwaves and Kuroshio path variability drive interannual spikes and slumps. These biological fluctuations propagate to markets through a **management and trade amplifier**: quota systems (Norway's conservative TACs, Japan's historically under-utilized TACs, Korea's overfished stock, Peru's scientific quotas) and trade policies (Japan's 7% tariff vs. Korea's 0%) determine which region's consumers or processors absorb the scarcity. The Japanese price series — a >80% wholesale increase in 18 months with near-unity pass-through to retail — and the Korean export boom are the observable market fingerprints of this chain. As climate models project a northward shift of chub mackerel spawning (0.72°N centroid shift; doubling of Korea Strait nursery share by 2050s), the geographic distribution of fishing opportunities — and hence the relative price positions of Japan, Korea, and China — will continue to shift ([Go & Jung 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2025.1648650/full), [Go et al. 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-3888/10/1/20)).

## 5. Conclusion

Chub mackerel across the Pacific Rim exemplifies the tightly coupled dynamics of marine environmental variability, biological resource quality, market pricing, and fishery economics. The key findings are:

1. **Prices have entered a structurally elevated regime (2022–2026).** Tokyo wholesale prices (~637 yen/kg in June 2026, +43% Y/Y), Korean wholesale prices (~4,334 won/kg H1 2026, +21% Y/Y), and global CFR export offers (US$1,250–1,400/t for *S. japonicus*; >US$2,700/t for Atlantic mackerel) all reflect supply-side scarcity propagated through trade substitution ([GD Freak](https://jp.gdfreak.com/public/detail/jp011013999600100250/3), [Chosun](https://www.chosun.com/english/industry-en/2026/07/28/2YHO4U6X55HLVMYKATGMJE6LI4), [New Marine](https://www.facebook.com/newmarineai/posts/the-mackerel-market-is-seeing-significant-variations-across-regions-for-atlantic/1024049979732882)).

2. **Biological trends are unambiguous and regionally differentiated:** Korean landings shifted to 88% immature individuals (2015→2024); Japanese Pacific stock growth and body condition declined under density-dependent competition with a recovering sardine stock; the northwest Pacific high-seas stock slipped from sustainable to overfished status (B/BMSY 0.51 in 2023–24); the California stock oscillates with ENSO-driven recruitment; Peru's stock is adult-dominated and warm-phase-favorable ([Kwak et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1814806/full), [Kamimura et al. 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.03.25.436928.full), [Huang et al. 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-3888/11/5/276), [SPRFMO SC13-ECO05](https://www.sprfmo.int/assets/Meetings/02-SC/13th-SC-2025/Ecosystems/SC13-ECO05-Abundance-and-biological-aspects-of-Jack-mackerel-and-other-species-of-the-Peru-Current-in-the-Humboldt-System.pdf)).

3. **Oceanographic drivers are quantifiable:** SST thresholds (18°C spawning optimum; 14–18°C fishing grounds), marine-heatwave effects (r = −0.72 with CPUE), Kuroshio path/transport effects on larval delivery, and ENSO/PDO regime shifts collectively explain much of the interannual variability in recruitment, growth, and distribution ([Frontiers TSI 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.996626/full), [Ji et al. 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-3888/11/1/13), [Chavez et al. 2003](https://www.ruirosalab.com/uploads/4/3/7/0/43701679/chavez_et_al_2005.pdf)).

4. **Integrated chain transmission is measurable:** MAFF's −0.544 (price–landing) and +0.837 (wholesale–retail) correlations, Japan's −2.85 to −2.51 demand elasticity, and the Korean export-price passthrough provide quantitative hooks for bioeconomic modeling. Management and trade policy determine who ultimately bears the cost of oceanographic scarcity.

From a policy perspective, the current crisis argues for: precautionary TACs that account for climate-driven recruitment failure (as Norway has done, and as Japan's 2025 cuts begin to do); reductions in juvenile catch (Korea's 88% immature proportion is unsustainable); and trade-policy reform (Japan's tariff disadvantage). From a research perspective, the Pacific Rim chub mackerel system should be treated as an integrated socio-ecological unit: advances in seasonal-to-decadal ocean prediction (ENSO, PDO, marine-heatwave forecasts) could be linked directly to fisheries-management and price-forecast models to anticipate — and potentially buffer — the kind of price shocks documented here.
