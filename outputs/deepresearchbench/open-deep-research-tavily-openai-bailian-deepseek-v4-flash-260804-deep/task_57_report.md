# Global AI Investments, Initiatives, and Outputs by Major International Consulting Firms: A Comprehensive Overview

## Executive Summary

The global consulting industry has undergone a profound transformation driven by artificial intelligence. Between 2023 and 2026, the world's ten largest consulting firms—Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG, Accenture, McKinsey & Company, Boston Consulting Group, Bain & Company, IBM, and Capgemini—have collectively invested tens of billions of dollars in AI capabilities, launched extensive proprietary platforms, forged strategic alliances with technology leaders, and implemented massive workforce upskilling programs. Key findings from this research include:

- **Collective AI investment exceeds $30 billion** across these firms, with Deloitte investing $3 billion in AI training, Accenture committing $3 billion to its AI practice, and EY investing $1.4 billion in its AI platform [1][2][3].
- **Proprietary AI platforms have become a competitive differentiator**, with firms launching platforms like EY.ai, KPMG Workbench, Deloitte's Enterprise AI Navigator, Accenture's GenWizard, and McKinsey's Lilli [4][5][6].
- **Strategic partnerships with technology leaders** have become central to AI strategy, with OpenAI, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Google Cloud, and AWS emerging as the most critical alliance partners [7][8][9].
- **Talent development is a defining priority**, with firms training hundreds of thousands of employees in AI fundamentals and creating specialized AI academies [10][11].
- **AI revenue is growing rapidly**, with Accenture reporting $2.7 billion in generative AI revenue in FY2025, BCG reporting 25% of revenue from AI-related work, and EY seeing 30% AI-related consulting revenue growth [12][13][14].

## 1. Deloitte

### 1.1 AI-Driven Products and Services

Deloitte has developed a comprehensive suite of AI products and platforms designed to support clients across the entire AI adoption lifecycle. The firm's core AI service capabilities include AI Strategy, AI Insights & Engagement, Analytics & Data Modernization, and Robotics & Intelligent Automation [15]. Deloitte's approach is centered on the concept of "the power of 'with'"—combining human intelligence with cutting-edge technologies to help businesses reach their full potential [15].

**Deloitte's Enterprise AI Navigator** guides organizations through identifying value, defining future state, and building solutions. China Widener, Vice Chair and US Technology, Media & Telecommunications industry leader at Deloitte Consulting LLP, explains that the key is not technology but connecting AI to broader corporate strategy, reimagining workflows from scratch, and taking a disciplined end-to-end approach [16].

**Deloitte's Generative AI Services** encompass three featured offerings: Generative AI Readiness, Acceleration, and Advantage. The firm outlines 17 key areas where it assists clients, including business strategy, tax opportunities, proof-of-concept experiments, workforce innovation, data foundations, operating model governance, solution deployment, process reimagination, risk management, cost optimization, app modernization, business model transformation, continuous improvement, training, governance, and managed services [17]. Deloitte states: "Generative AI turns unstructured data into logical results, makes mundane tasks creative, and transforms code into art, but it needs human input for purpose and trust" [17].

**Deloitte's TrueServe Platform** has been deployed in over 100 projects for more than 60 unique clients across multiple industries, accelerating AI-powered service transformation [18]. The firm's **AI and Analytics Services** emphasize a phased approach: AI readiness (ambition, value demonstration, foundation building), AI acceleration (scaling solutions, talent development, governance), and AI advantage (sustaining value, innovation, and risk management) [19].

**Deloitte's Tech Trends 2026 Report**, published December 10, 2025, examines how organizations are shifting from AI experimentation to real business impact. The report identifies five interconnected trends: AI goes physical, the agentic reality check, the AI infrastructure reckoning, the great rebuild, and the AI dilemma. The core message is: "The question used to be 'What can we do with AI?' Now it's 'How do we move from experimentation to impact?'" [20].

**Deloitte's State of AI in the Enterprise 2026 Report**, based on a survey of 3,235 business and IT leaders across 24 countries, finds that AI workforce access has grown to approximately 60% but usage lags at under 60% of those with access. Only 25% of organizations have moved 40% or more of AI experiments into production. Most firms see operational gains (efficiency, cost reduction) but only 20% report revenue growth from AI [21][22]. Agentic AI adoption is aggressive—23% already use it, 74% expect to within two years, but only 21% have mature governance for autonomous agents [21].

**Deloitte's 3R Framework for AI ROI**, presented in the 2026 Global Human Capital Trends report, offers a strategic approach: the first R is **Reimagine**—redesigning business models, processes, and workflows before pursuing return. The second R is **Redistribute** tasks between human and agentic digital workforces. Only then does the third R—**Return on Investment**—materialize through optimal coupling of human and digital work [23].

**Deloitte's CFO-targeted AI Agents** are being launched on AWS Marketplace to streamline financial reporting [21]. The firm is also pivoting to an outcome-based pricing model for 2026, sharing risk and value with clients rather than traditional hourly billing. Deloitte Consulting CEO Jason Salzetti stated: "We're trying to get our clients to shift to more of, 'How do we share in the value that we create?' So that's our value-creation, outcome-based model" [21].

**Partnerships**: Deloitte has alliances with AWS, Google, Oracle, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, and Workday [25]. Deloitte is a top AWS partner and was named NVIDIA partner of the year [17].

**Acquisitions**: Deloitte has made 139 acquisitions from 2008 through 2026 according to Tracxn data [28]. Recent acquisitions include:

Offerings include GenAI-focused programs, AI fluency for all, AI leadership for managers, AI bootcamp for professionals, and AI strategy for executives [35].

**Deloitte's Workforce Research**: Deloitte surveyed 1,874 workers (65% early career) from the US, Canada, India, and Australia. 79% of early career workers are excited about AI opportunities (vs. 66% of tenured workers). 75% believe AI will create new job opportunities, and 77% believe AI will help them move up in their careers [17]. However, early career workers also express anxiety about reduced learning opportunities, job displacement, and skill atrophy. 82% of early career tech workers question their career path due to AI [17].

## 4. KPMG

### 4.1 AI-Driven Products and Services

KPMG has developed a comprehensive suite of AI platforms and solutions, anchored by its **KPMG Workbench**, launched on June 17, 2025. This foundational multi-agent AI platform is built on Microsoft Azure AI Foundry Services and is part of KPMG's multi-billion dollar investment in AI and agentic transformation. The platform features a network of 50 AI assistants (agents) and chatbots, with nearly 1,000 in development, designed to work as digital teammates across multiple LLMs and alliance partners (Oracle, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workday) [79][80][81].

KPMG Workbench enables client delivery platforms like KPMG Digital Gateway (Tax), KPMG Velocity (Advisory), and KPMG Clara (audit). It includes built-in data sovereignty for client control and regulatory compliance. KPMG is the first organization in the world to achieve BSI/ISO 42001 certification for AI Management Systems, and every agent on Workbench receives a "Trusted AI stamp" based on a 10-pillar framework [79][80][81].

**KPMG Clara (Smart Audit Platform)**: KPMG Clara is a global cloud-based AI audit platform used by over 95,000 audit professionals globally across 140+ countries. The platform uses machine learning, generative AI, and agent-based automation to deliver a risk-based and data-driven audit. It leverages Azure AI Foundry, Azure App Service, .NET 8, Azure Cosmos DB, and advanced AI services to enable agentic AI capabilities, allowing auditors to move from traditional sampling to whole-dataset analysis [82][83][84].

**KPMG Velocity (AI-Enabled Business Transformation Platform)**: Launched in February 2025, KPMG Velocity is an AI-enabled business transformation platform designed to help enterprises navigate constant change by integrating AI, industry expertise, and strategic consulting. The platform offers five core solution areas: Grow (market share with AI insights), Evolve (operating models), Operate (productivity via AI agents), Innovate (modernized tech), and Trust (resilience and compliance) [85][86].

**KPMG Digital Gateway (Tax Platform)**: KPMG Digital Gateway is the firm's global client delivery platform for tax and legal services, now powered by Claude (Anthropic) through the May 2026 global alliance. It enables real-time agentic workflows, with the ability to build AI agents to help clients adjust to changing tax regulations in minutes instead of weeks [87][88][89].

**KPMG aIQ (Artificial Intelligence + Intelligence Quotient)**: KPMG aIQ combines the power of artificial intelligence with KPMG's human intelligence quotient. The lowercase 'a' shows how AI plays an enabling role to the capitalized 'IQ', representing human intelligence that ultimately drives capabilities and future advancements [90][91].

**KPMG Trusted AI Framework**: A 10-pillar framework built on three core principles: Values-driven (guided by KPMG's ethical standards), Human-centric (augmenting human capabilities), and Trustworthy (embedding ethics across the AI lifecycle). The ten ethical pillars are: Reliability, Security, Safety, Privacy, Sustainability, Explainability, Data, Transparency, Fairness, and Accountability [92][93].
